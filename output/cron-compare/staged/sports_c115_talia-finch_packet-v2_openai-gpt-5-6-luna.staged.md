Harry Hayward is the name I keep hearing in San Antonio, even with the television turned low.

At the corner, the room is all small sounds: a chair dragged across the floor, a bottle set down hard, somebody laughing before the replay has reached the shot. The Oaks had been down two after three quarters in Philadelphia. Then they scored 40 in the fourth, beat the 76ers 120-106 and made the fourth win in a row feel less like a clean procession than a door kicked open.

Hayward went through that door. The second-round rookie scored a career-high 19 points, adding seven rebounds and two blocks in 22 minutes. The number travels differently from a star's number. It gets repeated with a little surprise attached, as if the people here are still testing how it sounds.

“I’m just trying to do my part and help the team win,” Hayward said. “The fans seem really excited, and it’s great to see them enjoying the games. We feel the energy when we’re on the court, and it’s a good vibe. I haven’t really had a chance to talk to many fans directly, but I’ve seen the signs and heard the cheers. It’s nice to know they’re supporting us.”

The signs are the part people in this room understand. They are not proof that everybody has chosen the Oaks. They are evidence of somebody choosing them, which is how a second club gets started: one name remembered, then another, then an argument over who had been watching first.

The room’s excitement has an edge because the win did not arrive without a wobble. Oakland opened the game 31-17, surrendered the lead, and entered the fourth quarter behind. The comeback gave the people here something to hold besides a final score. They watched the team lose control and then take it back.

Adash Stanley’s name comes up next. He scored 23, made five of seven three-pointers and handed out seven assists. His neighbors, he said, are catching the buzz, too.

“My neighbors seem to be catching the buzz around the Oaks’ success,” Stanley said. “I came home after a win and found they’d left a case of something cold on my porch. We swapped stories about what was going on around the block near the courthouse, and the vibe was positive. They know I’m part of the team, so I think they’re happy to see us doing well.”

Austin Reaves, whose night in Philadelphia was quieter, described the larger sound anyway: “The fans are excited, and you can feel it. We’ve been playing well, and the energy’s been building. I’m not surprised, though — Oaks fans are loyal and passionate. When we’re clicking, the crowd gets behind us, and it’s a great feeling.”

Nobody here is using the word settled. The Oaks are 8-1, but the question remains local and immediate: who is going to keep showing up when the game stops handing them a perfect ending?

For now, the replay reaches the fourth quarter. The room leans forward. Somebody taps the table in time with the crowd noise, and Hayward’s name rises again.
