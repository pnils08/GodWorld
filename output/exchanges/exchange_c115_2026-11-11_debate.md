# Exchange — debate (Cycle 115)

- participants: POP-00143 Clarissa Dane | POP-00784 Isaac Martinez
- topic: Ivy Hill Community Support Initiative (INIT-014) — vote at cycle 116 (initiative)

---

**Clarissa Dane:** Isaac, I've spent enough time walking these blocks to know that the empty storefronts aren't just eyesores — they're a signal. This initiative keeps the landlords who held the line on rent in place instead of letting another wave of displacement hollow out Grand Lake or West Oakland. We've watched what happens when we don't anchor these spaces: the community loses its footing, and the people who've held on get priced out. That's why I'm backing it.

**Isaac Martinez:** I hear you on the empty storefronts. I walk past three of them every day on my way to a job, and yeah, they're a drag on the whole block. But I gotta ask — what's this actually doing for the folks who are already here? My neighbor's been fighting a rent hike for six months, and I don't see how keeping a landlord in place helps her out. If the money's going to hold the line on rent, great. If it's just keeping the building from changing hands, that's a different story.

**Clarissa Dane:** That's the right question, and here's the concrete part: the stabilization fund isn't just about who owns the building, it's about who gets to stay in it. When a landlord holds the line on rent instead of flipping the property, your neighbor's fight doesn't start over with a new owner who wants market rate the day they close. It keeps the pressure off her while we work the housing side, and it keeps the block from turning over so fast that nobody has a chance to organize.

**Isaac Martinez:** I get that, and I won't pretend stability isn't worth something. But I've seen this before — a landlord "holding the line" until the pressure's off, then selling to the highest bidder anyway. My buddy Mateo's whole building got flipped two years ago, and every tenant had to move in under three months. If this fund's got teeth, if it actually locks the rent for years, not just a cycle or two, then I'm with you. If it's just a band-aid, we're all just waiting for the next shoe to drop.
