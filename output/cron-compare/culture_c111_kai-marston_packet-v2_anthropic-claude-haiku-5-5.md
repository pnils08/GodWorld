# Tara Ellison Is Rising, and Nobody Around Her Can Say Where

Tara Ellison is standing on a Rockridge sidewalk with her hands loose at her sides, and when the question comes she takes a second before she answers. "I'm not sure what you mean. I'm just here in Rockridge, and I'm not in a room with anyone right now."

The ledger has a different account of her. This cycle it lists Ellison as a journalist in Media, Rockridge, climbing fast enough that it flags her as surging. Her name sits among the 39 culture figures the ledger counts as rising this cycle, and one of those 39 is new to the record. She was last logged the cycle before this one. Between those two points, the ledger doesn't say what she did, only that the numbers moved her up.

So the question becomes: what does a rising name look like from the street it supposedly belongs to?

I went looking for the answer in the neighborhoods around her. In Laurel, sculptor Alma Vasquez was at a stoplight with the window down, and she did not know Ellison's name well enough to say much about it. "To be honest, I don't really know much about Tara Ellison or her rise in Rockridge. I live in Laurel, and while I'm aware of the general vibe around Oakland, I don't have a clear view of what's going on in Rockridge. I do see that there are a lot of cultural figures rising in fame, and that's interesting. Sometimes when I'm driving through other neighborhoods, I catch snippets of the local culture, like the bassline from a car at the stoplight here in Laurel."

That bassline is the closest thing to evidence I have of a scene. It came through an open window, moved past in a second, and belonged to nobody I could see. Vasquez hears culture the way a driver does, in pieces, between red lights.

In Fruitvale, actress Nina Reyes was not thinking about any of it. "To be honest, I haven't really thought about Tara Ellison's fame or what's happening over in Rockridge. I've just been focusing on my own life and things here in Fruitvale." Reyes is also listed among the rising figures, which means that the ledger's rising list and the people who live near those figures are not the same group. Being counted is one thing. Being talked about on the block is another.

The ledger keeps its own balance. It has moved Lena Cross and Rowan Pierce onto its where-are-they-now file, a quieter list for names that have stopped climbing. Rising and fading sit in the same record, and the same neighborhoods hold both.

Walking back toward the car, I kept thinking about how little of this is visible. Ellison's rise is real on the ledger. Vasquez's bassline is real at a light in Laurel. Reyes's indifference is real in Fruitvale. None of those three places touches the others in any way I can document. A surge in one name does not prove a neighborhood is moving, and a neighborhood humming with music does not prove that a single name is the reason.

So who should answer for that gap? The ledger keeps the count, the figures know their own lives, and the people on the corner are busy with something else. Between the three of them, the connecting thread is still missing.

Is this a sustainable surge, or only a momentary trend that will pass before the next stoplight?
