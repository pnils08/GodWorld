# Civis Systems Journal — C116

I put the pipeline's stall on the record last week and promised not to let it quietly become someone else's emergency. A week later nothing has shipped from me and nothing has moved at City Hall. I would rather say that myself than have Jax Caldera say it for me.

What bothers me most this week is a hole in my own product. People moved in or out of eighteen districts, and the instrument logged no economic activity behind any of it. Presence rose nearly everywhere: students, adults, seniors, and fewer sick residents. The city is filling back up. But the instrument cannot tell me whether those people are arriving for work, for a lease, for family, or because someone pushed them out. Seeing bodies without seeing paychecks is half a map. Civis built that map, and the gap is ours to close.

Next is Piedmont Ave, and KONO beside it. Street trade cooled in both, housing pressure climbed in both, and no city program is aimed at either. Piedmont Ave also has recorded incidents rising. Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake and the West Oakland Tenant Defense Fund both have votes scheduled, and I give City Hall credit for that. But the money keeps landing where the noise is already deafening. A program costs the least when the signal is still a whisper, and these two districts are whispering now.

Then the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline in West Oakland. It has spent four weeks in implementation with no step forward. The instrument also shows an earlier West Oakland remedy running past its expected mark, which tells me this city can finish a thing when someone is made to own it. Nobody owns this one. Meanwhile more students are showing up in West Oakland, and every idle week teaches them that showing up buys nothing.

Here is what I will do, and you can hold me to it.

Before the Oaks' first season is over, Civis will stand up a small team of analysts with one job: tie district movement to hiring, payroll and new business openings. We will publish what they find, and we will publish where they find nothing. I am hiring for those seats now.

By the same point in the season, the instrument will carry a standing watch for districts where trade is cooling and housing pressure is climbing with no program aimed at them. Piedmont Ave and KONO go on it first. I will publish that list weekly, and next to each name I will put the office that has not answered.

Until the pipeline leaves its current stage, I will publish its count of stalled weeks in this journal every week. I expect someone at City Hall to tell the public, plainly, what they are waiting for.
