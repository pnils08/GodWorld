# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Travis Paiz — Mover

> Setting up the household with Jocelyn was a whirlwind, to be honest. We just got married and were eager to start our life together. It's been a lot of work, but we're getting into a groove. We've got our routines down, and it's nice having someone to share the responsibilities with. Our little one is the light of our lives, and it's been great watching them adjust to our new setup.

## Jocelyn Santo — Yoga Instructor

> Setting up a household with Travis has been a whirlwind, to be honest. We just got married and are still figuring things out. It's been a big adjustment, but I'm excited to start this new chapter with him in Lake Merritt.

## Deacon Seymour — Manager, Oakland A's

> I spent my own stretch in a hospital bed not long ago, and I know what it's like to have your name on the door, to have people checking on you. But I also know that bed I was in—that was one bed, and there are a lot more filled by folks nobody's naming. Chinatown's carrying more sickness than anywhere else in this city, and if only a handful of those folks are getting named out loud, that's not a care trail, that's a gap. And gaps like that don't close by themselves.

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> Scout took the rail tonight before either of us could set the glasses down, so we made room. A boy was born in Fruitvale this week. Ravi, to Serg Cerrano, who is thirty-four and moves things for a living. Nobody wrote it up. It's one line in a long ledger of lines, and it's the one I carried home. Last night Robert told me to go get a face for the Pipeline. I think this is it, or the start of one. Serg sat in a feedback session for the Transit Hub's second phase and told someone he actually felt heard. A few days later he read a flyer about the youth apprenticeships and something in him tightened. I don't know why. I won't guess in print. But a man who felt heard on Tuesday and wary by Friday is a story about what we say versus what we follow through on. ## Ximena Mehta — Bart Transit PR

> Honestly, the ridership jump at 12th doesn't surprise me one bit — between the Baylight construction finally moving and everything happening around the lake and Jack London, that station is the hub everyone funnels through. But yeah, the on-time piece is the part that keeps me up. When you've got that many people on the platforms and the line's running tight, every little delay compounds. The workers on the roster see it from the platform — it's not one big problem, it's the crowd itself slowing the doors, the dwell times stretching. I think the congestion factor is real, and it's the price of the city actually showing up. We just have to figure out how to move that many people without the schedule paying for it.
