# The A's Pay for a Closer They Already Had

By Anthony Raines, The Bay Tribune

Mariano Rosales struck out 82 and walked 14 in 63.2 innings in 2041, and on the career line that is the number to hold onto. His TrueSource card works the season out to 11.6 strikeouts per nine, 2.0 walks per nine and 5.86 strikeouts per walk. Every other year on the card sits well below that on the same measure. His 2040 with Baltimore, the year before he came to Oakland, works out to 9.0 K/9, 2.1 BB/9 and 4.31 K/BB. In 2039 it was 8.9, 3.0 and 2.96. The A's have now paid for the 2041 version, and the contract is what makes that bet worth looking at.

The deal on the feed is five years and $88 million, $17.6 million a year, running 2042 through 2046, with free agency in 2047. It replaces the one-year, $10.1 million deal he played 2041 on. The season it rewards: 8-3, 42 saves, a 1.84 ERA, 1.7 WAR and an All-Star selection. He is 27, a 95 overall on the feed, throwing a 96 mph four-seam, a 94 mph two-seamer and an 84 mph changeup.

## What the card says about the ninth

As a role fit, there is no hole to close here. The ninth inning was already his. The feed's account of the C83 spring named him closer, and the paper's C106 playoff-roster note called him the official closer, with Julio Valencia and Clark Saryan behind him. So the money does not add a closer. It keeps the one the club had and takes away the option of letting him walk after 2041, so the question is price and duration, not need.

On price, the card is mixed. In 2041 he gave up 35 hits and 13 earned runs in 63.2 innings, with 6 home runs. His 2040 line shows 56 hits and 26 earned runs in 56.0 innings, with 8 home runs. Those are two different pitchers in the same role, one season apart. In 2040 he also saved 38 games, so the saves total held steady while the ERA more than doubled. That is why saves are the weakest number on the page. The stable signals are the ones that need volume, and the strikeout-to-walk ratio has the best case there. It moved from 4.31 to 5.86 on a similar innings load (56.0 and 63.2), which is a real change in command and swing-and-miss, not just a lucky bounce on balls in play. The card cannot say whether it holds. One season of 11.6 K/9 against five seasons that top out at 8.9 K/9 is a short basis for a five-year commitment.

His relief innings are small samples by nature. The 63.2 innings of 2041 carry the whole contract case, and he has pitched more than that in four of his six seasons on the card. His 77.2 innings in 2039 produced a 2.09 ERA, and his 89.2 innings in 2038 produced a 4.11. A reliever's ERA swings that widely year to year, which is the Is-It-Real warning in practice. The contract treats 2041 as his baseline. The card says 2041 is the high end of a spread.

## The one mark, and what it can bear

The feed's World Series game 2 note has Rosales and Saryan each giving up 2 runs in relief of Travis Coles. It is one game. It does not belong in a ledger of what he is, and the 1.84 ERA does not erase it either. Either way, the game is a single appearance, and a single appearance supports an observation, not a projection.

## The board question

The paper's C114 coverage framed the bullpen as a spending question: $22 million combined to Ian Devine and Louis Cross in veteran relief, a 0.90 ERA from Hitoki Ka, and rookies Saryan and Valencia pushing from below. On that board, Rosales's new deal is the largest fixed line in the late innings. It sets the ninth through 2046, and it does so while two internal closers are already on the roster. Whether that stacks redundancy depends on what the club does with the rest of the money it freed. The feed does not say.

The pitcher earned the raise. His line, from a $10.1 million salary to a 1.7 WAR season, shows the A's got value in 2041. A five-year deal at $17.6 million a year pays for the repeat, and nothing on his card before 2041 supports it. The way to think about the contract is as insurance on a closer who could be elite. The cost is that the A's lose the flexibility to move on if 2041 turns out to be the peak. That is a defensible foundation move for a club with a 127-35 season behind it. It is also the one spot on the roster where the club already had its answer, so the open questions on the board sit elsewhere.
