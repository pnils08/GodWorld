# Earlier in the Tribune: Grand Lake Leads the Incident Table at 21% Clearance. Who Answers for the Other Four in Five?

Twenty-five Cycles ago, in Cycle 90, Police Chief Rafael Montez signed the dispatch integration agreement with the civilian-led Oakland Alternative Response Initiative following an executive deadline from Mayor Avery Santana, establishing protocols to route non-violent crisis calls to co-responders while keeping armed patrol officers available for backup. Twenty-two Cycles ago, in Cycle 93, Montez committed in writing to conducting four fifty-hour department training cohorts on de-escalation, mental health literacy, and dispatch protocols. Eighteen Cycles ago, in Cycle 97, Montez formalized that division of labor at dispatch across all nine council districts, while counseling department restraint during routine crime fluctuations and warning against inflating statistical variance into crisis. Thirteen Cycles ago, in Cycle 102, Montez shifted visible patrols to five commercial districts flagged as declining, but when violent crime simultaneously dropped sharply across five other neighborhoods, he declined to claim credit for a decline his deployments had not produced.

Four Cycles ago, in Cycle 111, municipal initiative records listed the \$2.5 million Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake as legislation-filed, though the initiative carried no disbursements or active funding. Across subsequent cycles, Grand Lake was reported as a settled lake-ring retail and theater district gaining modest residential arrivals, with new streetlights installed and long-standing community figures providing neighborhood continuity. Earlier published coverage contained no reporting on incident clearance, police response times, or custody holding figures for Grand Lake, and prior editions contained no reporting on Deputy Chief of Operations Kimberly Cho.
