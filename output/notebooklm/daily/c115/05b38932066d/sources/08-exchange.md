# Vinnie Keane and Amara Keane, talking

A conversation between two Oakland citizens, in their own words.

**Vinnie Keane:** Amara, hey. I was just thinking about you—walking the lake this morning and remembering how we used to grab coffee before my early workouts. How's your day shaping up?

**Amara Keane:** Hey, you. That's a sweet memory—those early mornings were something else. My day's shaping up nicely, actually; I've got a quiet afternoon with some sheet music and maybe a walk to Port Grind if I can tear myself away from the piano.

**Vinnie Keane:** Port Grind sounds good—I could use a decent cup and some of that quiet myself. You know, I was over there yesterday and overheard folks talking about the Commercial Stabilization vote. Seems like the whole neighborhood's got an eye on it.

**Amara Keane:** I know—everyone's got opinions on it, and I've heard more than a few conversations at the market. I just hope whatever they decide keeps the neighborhood's heart intact, you know?

**Vinnie Keane:** That's the thing, isn't it? Grand Lake's got a heart that can't be bought or sold. I've been thinking about that a lot lately—how some things are worth more than what's on paper.

But listen, I don't want to get heavy. How's the piano treating you these days? Still working on something new?

**Amara Keane:** The piano's been good—I've been playing around with a softer arrangement, something that feels like early morning light on the lake. It's still a work in progress, but I'm enjoying the quiet company of it.
