# Oakland Casino Town is down 3 jobs, and it is the second straight cycle of losses

**By Jordan Velez, Economics & Labor**

Oakland Casino Town lost three jobs in Cycle 117. The Business Ledger puts its payroll at 94, down from 97 a cycle earlier. The manager says none of the three were layoffs and calls it "normal churn." The ledger shows a longer slide than one cycle.

**The count, going back.** The Tribune reported 99 employees at the casino in C115, one fewer than the cycle before, which makes 100 at C114. The ledger says 94 now, after a loss of 3. That puts C116 at 97, a loss of 2. Put together:

| Cycle | Headcount | Change |
|---|---|---|
| C114 | 100 | — |
| C115 | 99 | −1 |
| C116 | 97 | −2 |
| C117 | 94 | −3 |

That is six jobs in three cycles, about 6% of the payroll, and each cycle's loss has been bigger than the one before. The C115 figure of 99 comes from our own earlier coverage. The C116 figure is arithmetic from this cycle's ledger, not a number I pulled directly.

The ledger's annual growth rate for the casino reads −1.5%. It was −2.3% in C115. The rate improved by about a point this cycle even as headcount fell. I wouldn't read that as a recovery, because the rate is a modeled yearly figure and the headcount is a count. The ledger lists no annual revenue for the casino, so I can't tell you whether the business is shrinking or only trimming.

**Who held the jobs.** The ledger doesn't name them. Casino Manager Philly Rodriguez, who lives in West Oakland, gave this account:

"Three jobs came off our payroll this cycle, and I can tell you straight: none of them were layoffs. We had two folks who moved on — one took a job down in San Jose, the other went back to school — and the third spot was a position we just didn't backfill when the person retired."

I haven't independently confirmed any of the three departures. The ledger records headcount only, not reasons. A retirement that isn't backfilled is still a lost job, whatever the cause. At the ledger's average salary of $70,000, three jobs come to roughly $210,000 a year in payroll that is no longer there. That is my estimate and not a ledger line.

**Is it a trend?** Rodriguez says no. "If it happens again next cycle, then we've got something to talk about," he said. "But right now? I'm not worried. We're holding steady." He added that "the floor's still running full shifts."

By the ledger, it has already happened again. Headcount has fallen three cycles running. I'm not calling it distress. The reasons he gave are ordinary ones, and a 94-person floor isn't gutted. But "if it happens again" has already been answered. The open question is whether the vacancies get filled.

**What's going on around it.** The Baylight District's other employers are mostly adding jobs. DigitalOcean (801 employees) and the Baylight Construction Authority (451 employees) are both hiring or holding steady, and Discord (350) and Anthropic (86) are flat to up. The district's median income is $139,000 and its trajectory is steady. The casino is the nightlife business in a district built around construction and tech payrolls. In the C117 ripple ledger, the casino's loss is logged as a "sector-impact" event, with a magnitude of −8.

On the betting side, the Casino Ledger shows 17 undocked wagers and 14 sports wagers placed this cycle. Only 2 settled and 15 voided. The house float is $246,000. Whatever that says about how the floor is doing, it isn't a payroll measure.

**What I'm watching.** If C118 shows 94 or higher, Rodriguez's churn story holds. If it shows 91 or lower, four cycles of decline is a trend. I'll also ask whether the retired position is posted. A job the casino has stopped filling and a job it hasn't filled yet look the same in the ledger, but they aren't the same.
