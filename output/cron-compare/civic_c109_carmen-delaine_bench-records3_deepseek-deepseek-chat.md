**West Oakland Stabilization Fund: A Slow Drip of Relief at Month Eight**

Outside the ThriftMic on Mandela Parkway, a late afternoon breeze carried the faint hum of construction cranes from the north. The flats were built out, their glass facades catching the last light of the day, but here, south of the tracks, the streetscape told a different story. Peeling paint, boarded windows, and the occasional vacant lot marked the edges of a neighborhood rapidly becoming a relic of its own history. The West Oakland Stabilization Fund, approved nine months ago to the tune of $28 million, was meant to stem that tide. Yet as Cycle 109 closes, just $400,000 has been disbursed this month, reaching only two households.

The fund’s charter, signed by Mayor Avery Santana in Cycle 78, was clear: stabilization grants for long-tenure West Oakland residents priced out as the neighborhood transformed. Not a hood-wide relief effort, but targeted aid for those the original text called "the remnant." Money is moving — $4.2 million disbursed in 47 checks under Deputy Mayor Okoro’s oversight — but slowly. At this rate, the fund’s remaining $23.4 million won’t reach its intended recipients until well after the neighborhood’s transformation is complete.

The vote that birthed the fund passed 6-3 in Cycle 78, with Councilmember Leonard Tran voting no and Ramon Vega voting yes. Mayor Santana, whose approval rating hovers at 69%, has framed the initiative as a critical piece of her economic equity agenda. Still, questions linger about the pace of disbursement. Why $400,000 this month, and only to two households? Why the sluggish rollout when the flats rise faster each day?

The next inflection point comes at month nine, with a scheduled disbursement review in Cycle 110. Deputy Mayor Okoro’s office has declined to comment on the current pace, pointing instead to the fund’s charter and the complexity of verifying long-tenure claims. But on the ground, the clock ticks louder than any policy explanation. The ThriftMic’s neon sign flickers; the flats gleam in the distance. What remains of West Oakland waits.
