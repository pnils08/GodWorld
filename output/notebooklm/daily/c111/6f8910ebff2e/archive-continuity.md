### **Section 1: Housing Stabilization & Public Accounting**
*Current Leads: `Ninety-One Households, One Ledger` / `The Pipeline Is Running. The Count Isn't Moving.` / `The Ledger Closes Before the Ink Is Dry`*

#### **1. Direct Published Facts (Cycles Y2C109–Y2C110)**
* **Legislative Origin & Vote:** Proposed by **Mayor Avery Santana**, the **West Oakland Stabilization Fund** was signed into law in **Cycle 78** on a narrow **6–3 City Council vote** (with Councilmembers **Ramon Vega** voting yes, **Leonard Tran** voting no, and **Elliott Crane** voting yes remotely) [1, 2].
* **Disbursement History:**
  * **Cycle 109 Baseline:** Four weeks into implementation, residents on the ground—West Oakland Community Bakery worker **Daniel Cloak**, coffee shop barista **Irene Fay**, bakery regular **Darius Clark**, and resident **Elena Patel**—reported seeing zero visible financial arrival or change on their blocks [1, 9–12].
  * **Cycle 110 Month-Nine Audit:** Fund Director **Marcus Webb** reported Month-Nine data to the City Council Finance Committee, logging **79 households stabilized in nine months** at a **23-day average processing window** (\$400,000 approved for Month-Nine) [1-4].
* **The Financial Ledger Arithmetic:** The fund carried an initial envelope of **\$28 million** [1, 5]. By the end of Cycle 109, the ledger recorded **\$81,188 disbursed in direct household grants**, leaving **\$23 million remaining** in Cycle 110 [1, 5].
* **Institutional Positions:**
  * **Mayor Avery Santana:** Stated that *"relief without bureaucracy keeps neighbors in their neighborhoods"* [3, 6].
  * **Deputy Mayor Brenda Okoro:** Stated that *"targeted relief that flows to tenants before displacement does"* [6].
  * **District 1 Councilmember Denise Carter:** Argued that *"when storefronts go quiet, families go next—so we're taking the Stabilization Fund to where the need is moving"* [5, 7].
* **Cycle 111 Advancement:** The Month-Ten disbursement review updates the official ledger count from 79 to **91 households stabilized**.

#### **2. Analytical Inferences & Unresolved Storylines**
* **Analytical Inferences:** Editor **Mags Corliss** and civic reporter **Carmen Delaine** infer an unresolved arithmetic discrepancy between the \$28 million initial envelope, \$81,188 disbursed in household grants through C109, and \$23 million remaining [5].
* **Unresolved Storyline:** Whether the Month-Ten review reconciles the ledger accounting gap, and whether stabilizing 91 households is sufficient to prevent displacement before private commercial and tech development (**Oakmesh Systems**, **Civis Systems**, **Coastline Construction**) outpaces public aid [2, 28–31, 48, 60].
