# Nineteenth Street Filled Up, and the Rows Do Not Say Why

On the record for Cycle 115, 19th St Oakland carried 16,076 riders. The previous cycle's row had 10,752. The new row also reads "above-average ridership" and "game day crowds" in its notes. The row's on-time figure is 81%, one point better than C114. The gain in riders was 5,324, the largest of any station. The platform took the added people without losing its timetable.

Coen Cartier drives for AC Transit. He did not feel a jump on his own runs, and he said so. "Wednesday's a normal workday for me, so I can't say I noticed any big jump on my runs," he said. "But I did see more folks waiting at 19th than I'd expect on a regular afternoon — that station's been busier lately, and with the council vote and the game downtown, people were moving around more than usual. Most of them got on heading toward the Lake Merritt and downtown stops, not so much out to the edges."

That is a bus operator reading a rail platform from the curb. It is useful testimony because it is modest. He saw a crowd and a direction of travel. He did not see a cause, and the rows do not give him one.

## One station, then eight

The 19th Street number is the loudest, but it is not alone. Eight stations are on the record this cycle, and every one rose:

- 12th St Oakland City Center: 14,526 riders, up 4,515. On-time was 86%, up 9 points.
- West Oakland: 9,519, up 3,491. On-time was 77%, up 3 points.
- Fruitvale: 8,943, up 3,880. On-time was 87%, up 15 points.
- Coliseum: 7,776, up 2,980. On-time was 81%.
- Lake Merritt: 6,780, up 2,684.
- Rockridge: 5,684, up 2,397.
- MacArthur: 4,634, up 1,828.

Together the stations carried 73,938 riders against 46,839 the cycle before. That is roughly 58 percent more people on the same platforms.

The corridors moved in step. The metrics sheet gives I-580 East a traffic index of 89, up 51 from C114. I-880 South reads 98, up 44. I-880 North and I-980 each read 100. Grand Ave, International Blvd and Telegraph Ave all rose. When the freeways and the platforms climb together, the explanation is probably not a platform problem.

## What the factors say, and what they leave out

Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for BART's Oakland division, offered the stacking theory. "It's not one big thing — it's a bunch of little things stacking up," she said. "We had the Oaks game downtown, the Baylight District vote finally going through, and events all over Lake Merritt, Temescal, and KONO. When you get that many neighborhood pulls happening on the same ordinary Tuesday, the platforms fill up fast. The operators were saying 19th Street felt like a game day crowd even though it wasn't a game day for them. It's just what happens when the whole city decides to move at once."

The 19th Street row backs most of that list: a downtown game day, events in Downtown, Lake Merritt, Temescal and KONO, and Baylight District work marked construction-active. It also lists crowded platforms. The Oaks beat the Magic 125–109 at KONO on a 94 percent house.

The other stations do not all share those causes. Fruitvale's row names no game. It lists events in East Oakland, twice, and the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II visioning, marked design-phase. Elena Soria Dominguez, the hub's planning lead, would not credit the planning work. "I can't say the visioning is what's pulling those extra riders in — we're still in the design phase, no shovels in the ground," she said. She pointed to East Oakland's events and crowds instead. She added that the construction workers on their breaks under the overpass "see it from the platform every day."

Two things in the record cut against a tidy story. The two quotes name different days, a Tuesday and a Wednesday. The metrics rows carry no day name at all. The C114 row for 19th Street also lists "weekend" among its factors, and the C115 row lists none. This cycle is marked an ordinary weekday with no citywide cut. Comparing a weekday to a weekend is a plausible part of the gap. The record does not say how much of it, so this is a reading, not a finding.

## The rows are quiet where it matters

No chaos event touched BART or AC Transit this cycle. No stalled train is on any of these rows, and no station reports a short crew. Fruitvale's on-time figure rose 15 points while its riders rose by 3,880. That is the opposite of strain.

The record carries one reading of the crowding itself: the congestion figure reads 0.12 on every station row. That is below the 0.30 on 19th Street's C114 row. A larger crowd on a calmer system is an odd pairing, and the rows do not explain it.

## What no row can name

If every station moved together on an ordinary weekday, what changed in the city that no single station row can name? The rows give games, events and a council vote, each as a separate pull. They do not say whether the 73,938 is a one-cycle pile-up or a new floor. 19th Street's earlier rows swing between roughly 5,000 and 17,000. If the next ordinary weekday lands near 16,000, the platforms are carrying a different city than the one they were scheduled for. On-time performance will show it first.
