### **Temescal’s Ledger Is Growing — The Question Is Who It’s Growing For**
*By Jordan Velez, Business Desk*

In Temescal, every named business on the local ledger closed the cycle in the black, but neighborhood storefronts and corporate offices are moving at vastly different speeds. At **Da Dough**, sourdough baker **Melton Neilon** works a five-person counter where revenue added \$128 to reach \$371,000. A few doors away at **OakTown Social**, baristas **Vanessa Treary** and **Vanessa Ramas** added \$308 on \$521,000 in sales. 

By contrast, technical firms and contractors posted substantial gains:
*   **Gridiron Analytics** (founded by Daniel Yoon and Christine Nakamura) added \$16,000 to reach \$2.9 million in revenue—a 27.9 percent growth rate led by data analysts **Ariana Reyes** and **Jade Soy**, alongside sports analytics leads **Dawson Nguyen** and **Wesley Clark**.
*   **Calderon-Nishi** added \$14,000 to reach \$9 million in revenue with 34 employees, including plumber **Jett Walker** and electricians **Ronald Silk** and **Cristina Norwood**.
*   **Temescal Community Health Center** holds 14 employees—including physical therapist **Clearence Leighton**—and reports \$45.1 million in revenue.

Project Director **Bobby Chen-Ramirez** emphasized that top-line ledger growth means little if neighborhood needs remain unaddressed: *"Look, I run a health center project, not a spreadsheet... the numbers on the wall don't mean a damn thing if Mrs. Okafor still can't get her blood pressure checked without taking two buses. What creates accountability for me is simple: I've got 14 employees on my roster, and every one of them lives in this neighborhood... If growth just means the ledger gets fatter while the sidewalk stays the same, then we're building a ghost town with good accounting"*.

Across town in Ivy Hill, **Ivy Hill Market** provided a stark contrast, shedding two employees as its growth dropped to negative 3.9 percent.

### **The Quiet Platform Still Has a Job**
*By Trevor Shimizu, Transit Desk*

Citywide weekend transit ridership dropped across eight stations and 10 corridors to 46,520 passengers, but platform reliability and schedule performance varied significantly station by station. **12th St Oakland City Center** experienced the largest drop, losing 4,821 boardings (down to 9,341) while on-time performance stood at 84 percent. Meanwhile, **19th St Oakland** maintained a 90 percent on-time rate despite falling by 4,777 riders to 10,012. **Coliseum** (5,595 riders, 82% on-time) and **Fruitvale** (81% on-time) also absorbed weekend declines.

Station operators and drivers noted how lighter weekend crowds shift the daily rhythm:
*   **Eleanor Rivera** (Station Manager, 12th St): *"The platform echoes more. You hear the announcements bounce off the tile instead of getting swallowed up by the crowd. And you see the regulars more clearly... We just adapt, keep things moving, make sure the trains run on time"*.
*   **Evan Lewis** (AC Transit Driver): *"When the system runs light on a weekend, you feel it in your bones... 12th Street is the heart of downtown... When it's quiet there, it's not just empty platforms. It's the whole city catching its breath"*.
*   **Coen Cartier** (AC Transit Operator): *"Riders don't see that part. They just see the bus showing up. But for us, it's the difference between moving a crowd and moving people"*.

### **In Temescal, a School the District’s Growth Passed By**
*By Angela Reyes, Education Desk*

Temescal’s school quality index sits at 6.43 on the district table—the lowest among the 22 ranked neighborhoods. While enrollment expanded in six other neighborhoods (West Oakland +3, Fruitvale +2, Chinatown +2, Glenview +2, East Oakland +2, Piedmont Ave +1), Temescal’s student count held flat at 313. The neighborhood records an 85 percent graduation rate, a 55 percent college-readiness rate, a 6.88 teacher quality score, and \$11,500 in per-student funding—compared to top-ranked Rockridge (8.58 index, 94% graduation, 76% college readiness, 9.09 teacher quality, \$15,000 funding, 247 students).

Ground-level reflections reveal how students and educators experience the gap:
*   **Patricia Nolan** (Retired Teacher): *"I've watched that building for thirty years... when I was teaching, we had the same struggles, but there was a sense that the neighborhood was pulling for us. Now the district grows everywhere else, and Temescal just... sits. The teachers there are still good people, still trying. But you can't pour from an empty cup"*.
*   **Rick Walker** (K-8 Teacher, Fruitvale): *"When the district grows, the money and the attention follow the growth. Temescal isn't growing, so it's just... flat... That gap isn't just numbers on a table. It's the difference between a kid getting a new textbook or a worn-out one from three years ago"*.
*   **Salma Navarro** (Student, Temescal): *"Nobody's really bragging about stats or anything. It's more like, are we gonna be okay? That's what comes up when we talk"*.

### **Morton’s 285 At-Bats Are the Evidence the Outfield Math Starts From**
*By Anthony Raines, Tanya Cruz, & P Slayer, Sports Desk*

With **Danny Horn** locked in as starting center fielder and **Darrin Davis** under contract at designated hitter, Athletics General Manager **Mike Paulson** faces an offseason logjam of five outfielders competing for three starting spots. Newsroom analysis focuses on the three left fielders on the roster:

*   **Desmond Morton** (.291 AVG, 7 HR, 44 RBI, 28 SB, .348 OBP across 285 ABs in 80 games): Morton provides the most complete full-season evidence, pairing team-leading speed with proven everyday durability.
*   **Frank Reyna** (.317 AVG, 14 HR, 34 RBI, .683 SLG across 164 ABs in 46 games): Reyna boasts impressive power rates (1 HR every 11.7 ABs), but his numbers stem from a smaller, platoon-heavy sample size.
*   **JR Rosado** (.298 AVG, 7 HR, 27 RBI across 184 ABs in 54 games): Rosado sits between Morton and Reyna across rate metrics, creating direct positional redundancy in left field.

The front-office decision hinges on whether Paulson relies on Morton’s established full-season workload or Reyna’s high-slugging potential.
