# The Lives of C113

**Elijah Roberts** moved into a place of their own in West Oakland this cycle, renting. The household ledger holds the move in a single line. The record does not say what the place is, which street it is on, or what the rent is.

Roberts is a construction laborer and has been on the record for thirty-two entries. Two of them from C112 sit close to the move. In one, it was the third late night that week, with the ceiling still there at four. In the other, Roberts spent time sharpening a skill that might matter later. Then, in C113: "worked through another weekend and called it fine."

The record has not reconciled those lines, and this page will not do it for them. A person can be short of sleep and short of a lease in the same week. A person can also call a weekend fine because the key is in their pocket. All the log says is that the move and the strain arrived together.

Other lines from the ledger show how Roberts moves through the neighborhood. In C110, Roberts helped a neighbor through the West Oakland Stabilization Fund portal because the interface was confusing. In C111, Roberts painted over graffiti on the recreation center wall with five neighbors they barely knew. In C113, Roberts caught the recap of the previous night's game at a counter and stayed for the second retelling. Also in C113, the log says Jude Hess "became a real friend, not just a colleague."

The Tribune has named Roberts three times: in the apprenticeship pipeline pieces of C98 and C101, and in the West Oakland new-business-money piece of C109.

**Mac Hillie** moved into a place of their own in Uptown this cycle, renting. Hillie is a bakery worker, self-employed.

The same cycle's log says Hillie reconnected with an old friend. Hillie wanted the two kept apart. "To be honest, reconnecting with my old friend hasn't had much to do with my new living situation in Uptown," Hillie said. "I mean, I've been living here for a bit now, and it's been great. The bars and galleries are always lively. But catching up with my friend was more about us finally having the time to reconnect. We hadn't spoken in ages, and it was nice to grab a coffee and reminisce. My new place has been a big change, though. It's my own space, and I'm really enjoying the freedom that comes with it."

Earlier, in C112, Hillie recognized the Oakland Alternative Response Initiative van idling outside the corner store and knew better than to ask who it was waiting for.

**Ingmar Jordan** lost on the UNDOCKED book this cycle. Jordan, a spoken-word poet in Uptown, staked 219, and the book kept it. This is the second empty slip on Jordan's record; the first was in the cycles before.

Jordan does not tie the loss to the rest of the week. "I've lost on the UNDOCKED book, yeah, but to be honest, it's not really on my mind when I'm thinking about the neighborhood changes," Jordan said. "I've been noticing the rent signs popping up and then disappearing just as fast, and the bars and galleries staying packed. It's like the neighborhood is always in flux."

The log for the week gives some of the same ground. In C112, Jordan heard the rent talk on the block turn sharp. In C113, Jordan grumbled about the neighborhood over coffee and walked the perimeter of the Coliseum District Development, trying to picture it in five years. The log also says Jordan watched someone running for the bus make it and felt "weirdly invested."

**Fang Crawford** lost on the UNDOCKED book this cycle. Crawford, an elevator mechanic in Laurel, staked 199, and the book kept it. The log has the evening after it: Crawford "spent the walk home insisting Iris Pham should have sold instead of holding." Crawford had no say in what Pham did.

Asked about Roberts, Crawford said: "I haven't really seen or heard much about Elijah Roberts, but I did hear about someone having a significant life event recently. It's been on my mind because I overheard a goodbye at the corner that sounded pretty final the other day."

That goodbye is on the C112 record, and it does not say whose it was. Crawford carried it a block. In C113, Crawford dodged the running groups circling the lake and found a quiet bench near the pergola.
