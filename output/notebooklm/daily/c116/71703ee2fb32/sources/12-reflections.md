# Jack London, in their own words

## Xing Patel — Ship Repair Foreman

> I've been enjoying my retirement. The A's games have been exciting lately, and I was able to trade an extra ticket for a promise from someone to bring their kid next time. It's been nice to have more free time and be able to watch the games with my family.

> Hey, Celo. Good to see you out here. I was just thinking about that water main break last week—they fixed it up quicker than I expected. Yeah, that's the truth. Back in my shipyard days, you'd be waiting weeks for a simple repair like that. But I guess the city's got a little more life in it these days, especially with all the excitement around the A's. I hear you. It's tough to think about losing a guy like Aitken. He's more than just a player to this city—he's the one who gets the kids out to the ballpark and shows up at all those community meetings. Can't imagine someone else filling those shoes. *(talking with Celo Patel)*

## Celo Patel — Container Yard Supervisor

> Yeah, they got on it fast. That crew worked through the night, had it patched before the morning commute even started. Reminds me of how the city used to drag its feet on stuff like that. Funny you mention that. I was just talking to a buddy at the yard—everyone's got the same question on their minds. Even with the championship fresh, you can feel the nerves about whether Mark sticks around or heads south. That's the thing, isn't it? We've seen players come and go, but Mark's different. He's got roots here deeper than the BART tracks. When he walked into the Little Leaguers' practice last month, just to watch, you'd think he was their uncle. That kind of connection doesn't just transfer to another uniform. *(talking with Xing Patel)*
