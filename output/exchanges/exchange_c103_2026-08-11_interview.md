# Exchange — interview (Cycle 103)

- participants: Maria Keen (Bay Tribune) -> POP-00007 Hal Richmond
- trigger: edition c102 NAMES INDEX ("Bay Tribune Senior Historian; Chinatown resident")

---

**Maria Keen:** "You've lived through Chinatown's heartbeat for decades, Hal—what's one Oakland tradition or ritual that quietly keeps this neighborhood strong?" (Takes a sip of boba tea, sleeve rolled up, like we're at one of those little plastic tables outside Lincoln Square.)

**Hal Richmond:** *scraping a chair back to join them, notebook already open to a grease-stained page*  

The early morning dumpling pilgrimage—old-timers lining up at 5am when the steam first hits the windows at Gum Wah. Nobody talks about it much, but that's where the real gossip gets traded, where Mr. Lau slips extra pork buns to the construction workers, where Emilia and I once tracked three generations of a family arguing over whose turn it was to buy the taro cakes. *taps pen against a sketched map of alleyway routes* You want the pulse? Follow the bamboo baskets.

**Maria Keen:** *Leaning in, lowering voice like we're sharing a secret*  

"Tell me about the last time you saw someone pass down one of those bamboo baskets—not just the food, but the stories that come with it." *Pulls a folded photo from pocket: two hands, one young and one old, holding a steaming bun between them*

**Hal Richmond:** *smiling at the photo, finger tracing the edge like it's a familiar alleyway*  

Last Tuesday at Golden Lotus—Mrs. Chen's granddaughter, maybe twelve, getting schooled on how to fold the perfect scallion pancake while the old man at table four muttered about how they used to wrap them in newspaper during the '89 quake. *laughs, nudging the boba cup aside* Kid rolled her eyes, but her hands? Exact same twist of the wrist. That's how you know the recipe's safe.
