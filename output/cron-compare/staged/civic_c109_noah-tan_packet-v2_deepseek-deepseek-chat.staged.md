**The Weight of Neutral**  
By Noah Tan  

Colin Soria blew on his hands outside the Temescal laundromat, watching his breath hang in the air just long enough to be seen before vanishing. The security guard’s usual post-shift ritual—picking up takeout, maybe stopping by the bus shelter to chat with neighbors—had been pared down to just this: a quick errand, then home.  

"It’s not the kind of weather that stops you in your tracks," he said, nodding at the overcast sky. "Just that dull, gray blanket that makes everything feel a little slower." His rounds this week had been quieter, his head cold lingering like the fog from yesterday. Even the laundromat’s usual murmur of conversation had dimmed to the occasional rustle of turning pages.  

Three blocks away at a dim sum cart, Melton Neilon patted a batch of dough waiting under a cloth. "You know, when it’s this kind of flat gray for days," he said, "even the dough feels slower to rise—not bad, just... deliberate." His regulars had been coming in with fewer stories, their usual banth abbreviated by the need to get back indoors.  

Elio Perez, passing through the plaza with a paper bag tucked under his arm, paused mid-stride as if considering whether the detour to the post office was worth it. "The overcast days make everything feel slower, like the world is holding its breath," he said finally, adjusting his grip on the bag. "It’s not gloomy, just... neutral." He turned toward home instead.
