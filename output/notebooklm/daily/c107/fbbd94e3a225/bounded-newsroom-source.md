# 12th Street Station Feels the Rhythm of Downtown

At 7:40 AM, Coen Cartier pulls the AC Transit bus up to the curb outside 12th Street Oakland City Center Station. The fog is still thick enough to blur the station’s scaffolding, and Cartier can already see how this morning will go: fewer riders stepping on board, more people lingering at the curb like they’re waiting for the fog to lift before moving on.

“Lately at 12th Street, I’ve noticed the crowds come and go with what’s happening around town,” Cartier says later, between shifts. “When there’s a game or some big vote at City Hall, we get that rush before and after. Other days, especially with the fog rolling in, folks seem to stay put more. It’s not just one thing — it’s how all these pieces fit together that changes who’s waiting at the curb when I pull up.”

Inside the station, Eleanor Rivera watches it all unfold from her post near the fare gates. “At 12th Street, we see how everything outside the station affects what happens inside,” Rivera says. “Fog, holidays, even a big game downtown — it all changes the flow. People adjust their routines, and we adjust right along with them. It’s not just about numbers going up or down — it’s about reading the neighborhood’s rhythm and keeping things moving.”

Jerald Jensen, the station janitor, sees the shifts in the patterns of trash left behind. “When the fog rolls in thick like it did last week, or when there’s some big game downtown, I see it in the trash cans,” Jensen says. “Overflowing. More coffee cups, more wrappers, more people rushing through like the place owes them money. And when the Baylight vote happened? Whole different kind of crowd — folks in suits leaving flyers everywhere, talking loud like they own the benches I just cleaned.”

Joel Wren, a mechanic with AC Transit, sees the crew keeping the buses running. “Those mechanics and drivers? They’re the ones putting in the long hours to make sure everyone gets where they need to go, rain or shine, game day or not,” Wren says.

The question now is how the system adapts to the fog, the holidays, the games, and the votes — all the pieces that shape who’s waiting at the curb.
