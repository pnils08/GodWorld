# The Fund Has Moved $7 Million Out of $28 Million. The Record Names Two Households.

By Carmen Delaine, The Bay Tribune

The renewal draft for the West Oakland Stabilization Fund sits in the clerk's hands, and the council takes it up at Cycle 116. By Marcus Webb's account, the paperwork is done. "The closeout is on the record, the renewal draft is in the clerk's hands, and the 23-day standard holds — but renewal belongs to the council, and I will not call their vote before they do," Webb said in the office's Cycle 115 statement.

Brenda Okoro, the deputy mayor whose authority the tracker names for the early disbursements, used almost the same sentence. "The closeout is published, the renewal ordinance is transmitted, and the standard holds at 23 days. Renewal is council's call — I won't call it before they do."

Two offices gave the same answer, and neither one said who got the money. That is the gap this ledger can measure.

**What the tracker holds**

The Initiative_Tracker row for INIT-001 puts the Fund at $28,000,000 in total budget, with $21,000,000 remaining and a last disbursement at Cycle 115. The difference is $7,000,000 out the door. The council passed the Fund 6-3 at Cycle 78. The tracker names Ramon Vega yes, Leonard Tran no, and Elliott Crane yes by remote vote. It does not name the other six seats. Mayor Avery Santana proposed the Fund and signed it the same cycle.

The tracker's milestone notes then trace the money:

- **C84–C90:** 47 checks, about $4.2 million, under Okoro's authority.
- **C109:** a $400,000 tranche, described as "2 household grants, $81,188."
- **C110 through C114:** $400,000 each cycle, with the 23-day processing standard noted along the way. The C112 note adds "cumulative $5M+ disbursed."
- **C114:** $400,000 executed, closeout publication ordered and charter renewal drafting directed.
- **C115:** the closeout ordered, the renewal ordinance transmitted, and the business-and-resident scope and the 23-day standard "preserved pending council action."

The C109 line is the only one that counts households, and it counts two. Taken alone, $81,188 across two grants is a little over $40,000 each. That is arithmetic, not a record of who received what. The same tranche was $400,000, which leaves $318,812 the note does not account for.

The C109 scope correction says the Fund serves "West Oakland businesses and residents." The unexplained balance may therefore have gone to businesses. The tracker does not say. I read the row for a recipient list, an amount per household, a business count and a street, and found none. A search of the paper's prior coverage returned the same gap in last cycle's ledger piece. It also returned a Cycle 104 mayoral statement saying 12 more households had cleared verification and were waiting on disbursement. That statement describes a queue. It is not a payee list.

**The clock against the queue**

The 23-day standard is a speed figure. It measures how fast a file moves from application to check. It does not say whose file moved or whether the checks reached the long-tenure residents the C108 scope note says the charter was written for.

Webb's second statement leans on the queue. "The mood in West Oakland is slipping and the housing pressure is building — which is exactly why the queue does not stop while the paperwork goes to the record." This is the same argument for keeping the checks moving. The problem is that "the record" he points to is a closeout, and the closeout is a document about the process.

Picture the clerk's counter the day the ordinance lands. A drafted renewal sits in a folder with a published C114 closeout clipped behind it. Someone in West Oakland waiting on a file would have to read the closeout to learn whether anyone on their block was paid. By the tracker's own notes, it names two households and one tranche.

**The exposure on the council floor**

Cycle 116 is crowded. Council also has the recorded decision on Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake ($2.5 million, proposed by Tran). The tracker also schedules a decision on the West Oakland Tenant Defense Fund ($4.2 million). The Tenant Defense row reads "eviction filings outpacing Stabilization Fund relief." That is the proposal's own stated reason, and it implies the Fund's reach is already being questioned from inside the chamber.

The Fund has $21 million remaining, and the renewal ordinance asks the council to keep it alive. Votes at 6-3 made it, and the tracker has not recorded a renewal vote yet. Its renewal fields (vote cycle, amount, outcome) read 108, a blank and "Standing." That is not a finding, only what the row holds today.

**The question the record can't answer yet**

The clerk can hand over the draft. Webb and Okoro have both said they will not predict the vote. The council will be asked to extend a fund with a 23-day clock, a $21 million balance and one recipient line in the public record, covering two households in a single tranche.

When the ordinance reaches the floor at Cycle 116, will any seat ask for the payee ledger first: how many households and how many businesses were paid out of the roughly $7 million, and where? Or will the council renew a fund that is good at publishing closeouts, and not one that can show it is reaching West Oakland?
