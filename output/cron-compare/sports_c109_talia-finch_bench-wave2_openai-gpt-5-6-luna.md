# The Oaks Have Given Jack London Nothing to Hold Yet

Jack London is loud in the way a room gets loud when nobody wants to say what they are watching. I sit near the back of an unnamed bar with the game turned low, a glass sweating beside my notebook, while the room keeps its attention on the screen and its judgment to itself.

The Oaks lost their first preseason game to the Pistons, 104-101. They are 0-1 in preseason and still looking for their first win in the era of The Oaks. The score is small enough to dismiss and sharp enough to sting. Around here, the distinction is doing most of the work.

Trenton Nawan put the neighborhood’s hesitation plainly: “I've felt the anxiety around The Oaks' preseason struggles, it's like a cloud over the neighborhood, and I think we're all waiting to see how the new rookies will perform.”

That waiting has a shape. It is the hand hovering over the remote. It is the person who watches the late possessions but does not yet lean forward. It is the empty space where a jersey, a ritual, or a standing argument might eventually settle. The Oaks have rookie promise in the room’s vocabulary, but promise is not belonging. Not yet.

The feed’s earlier glimpse of rookie camp carried a little light: AJ Dybantsa showed potential, while Adash Stanley continued to show crafty scoring. Then the first preseason game arrived and the Oaks stumbled again. The result did not erase the promise. It made people ask how long promise can remain the only thing available to hold.

Stanley has felt that question from inside the club. “I've felt a bit anxious about the Oaks' preseason, it's like there's a lot of uncertainty in the air. I'm trying to stay even-keeled about it, but it's hard not to worry when everyone around me is talking about it.”

On the screen, the room does not celebrate the near miss. It absorbs it. A few heads turn away before turning back. The sound stays low. This is not a crowd that has decided to be loyal; it is a neighborhood deciding whether the Oaks are asking for patience or simply receiving it.

Austin Reaves described the same ground beneath the players: “I've been feeling like we're still finding our footing, you know? It's hard not to worry when you're not seeing the wins, but I trust Coach Wilson Shepard to get us ready.”

Shepard’s answer is steadier, a coach asking the room to see the preseason as unfinished work: “I've been feeling like we're still finding our rhythm, you know? Preseason's about figuring things out, not about winning every game. The guys are putting in the work, and I'm confident we'll tighten up and be ready for the real deal.”

That is the argument the Oaks have to make before Jack London makes it for them. What, exactly, is the one thing they need to change to turn this around? The room has not answered. It is still watching, glass by glass, with the sound down.
