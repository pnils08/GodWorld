# Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II Visioning Reset to Square One  

Manjit Singh stood at the corner of International Boulevard and 34th Avenue, staring at the construction site where the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II was supposed to rise. The site was quiet now, save for the occasional rumble of a passing AC Transit bus. "I heard about the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II moving back to active visioning," Singh said, his voice tinged with frustration. "It feels like we’re stuck in a loop. I just hope they figure it out soon—it’s been a long wait for something that could really help our community."  

The Phase II project, which had briefly advanced to "visioning-complete," has now reverted to "active visioning"—a milestone that, while technically a step forward, feels more like a step back to residents like Singh. Nina Reyes, who commutes through the area daily, echoed the sentiment. "I heard they were making progress on the transit hub, but now it sounds like they're back to square one," Reyes said. "That's Oakland for you—always planning, never doing."  

The Phase II visioning process has been underway in various forms since the completion of Phase I, which brought upgrades to the BART station and surrounding pedestrian infrastructure. Phase II aims to expand transit connections and add new amenities, but the repeated resets in the planning process have left many questioning whether the project will ever move beyond the drafting table.  

The shift back to active visioning raises questions about the alignment between Phases I and II. Were the initial plans for Phase II built on assumptions that no longer hold? Are there undisclosed challenges—budget constraints, land acquisition hurdles, or shifting priorities—that are forcing planners to revisit the drawing board?  

For now, the answers remain unclear. What is clear is the growing impatience among Fruitvale residents who rely on the transit hub daily. As Singh and Reyes noted, the community has been waiting for progress that never seems to arrive.  

The question now is whether this latest reset represents a necessary recalibration—a chance to incorporate more community input—or another delay in a long line of them. Whatever the reason, the clock is ticking.
