# What Stays When Programs Don't  

Melton Neilon leans against the chainlink fence outside East Oakland Learning Center, watching kids dribble a half-deflated basketball across cracked pavement. "I’ve heard East Oakland’s been struggling to keep programs steady," he says, rubbing his chin as the ball bounces unevenly. The civic initiative meant to support education here hasn't moved forward in several cycles, leaving families to patch together what stability they can.

Inside, the after-school tutoring tables stay full even as uncertainty lingers like the scent of old textbooks. Rick Walker, whose nephew attends twice a week, notes how constant shuffle affects kids: "It’s like they’re always starting over, and that’s tough on the kids."

Jerome Pittman stands near the unused projector in the community room. "I’ve heard the civic initiative for East Oakland hasn’t moved forward in a while," he says, the screen still rolled up behind him. The center's doors open at 3:15 every weekday, same as last year, same as the year before. The real question isn't about closures, but about what these students might access if initiatives ever kept pace with need.
