# West Oakland, in their own words

## Elias Varek — Founder of Civis Systems

> Let me give it to you straight, because you're asking the right questions and you deserve a real answer, not a press release. Civis Systems Field opens on Opening Day. That is not a hope, that is a schedule. I've got crews on double shifts and the steel is up, the foundation is poured, and the pillars Mike saw in C97 are carrying real weight now. The playing surface goes in next month. We will be ready for first pitch. But here's where I'm going to be honest with you, because I don't do spin: "fully finished" and "ready for Opening Day" are two different things. The stadium itself — the bowl, the clubhouse, the concourses, the lights, the scoreboard — that's done. You walk in, you'll feel it. It's going to be the best baseball experience in the league, and I don't say that as the guy whose name is on the building. I say that as a fan who's been sitting in the Coliseum bleachers since I was a kid. The neighborhood around it? That's a longer game. The housing, the retail, the corporate headquarters — those are real, and they're rising, but they're not all going to be ribbon-cutting ready on day one. Some of it will be. The main retail spine will be open. But if you're asking whether every single unit and storefront is going to be finished, the answer is no. ## Mike Paulson — General Manager, Oakland A's & The Oaks

> Elias. Yeah, it's been a long couple of days, I won't lie. Losing Mark stings more than any box score can show, but we've got a decision to make at third and two kids who deserve a real shot. That's the thing, isn't it? We've been waiting for a problem like this for years. Kevin's got the stick and Giancarlo's got the leather, and somewhere in between we've got to figure out what wins us ballgames. Spring training's only a luxury if we use it right. I've seen too many clubs let those six weeks decide a guy's whole future, when the real answers come in July and August. We'll give both of them a fair look, and the one who forces the issue keeps the job. *(talking with Elias Varek)*

## Philly Rodriguez — Casino Manager

> Yeah, we lost three. It wasn't layoffs — it was attrition, people moving on. Two of them were dealers who got offers down in San Jose, and one was a security guy who went back to school. I can't give you their names, that's not my place. But I'll tell you this: nobody who stayed got a raise, and nobody got a cut either. Hours are the same, pay's the same. The floor's just a little thinner now, so we're all covering a bit more. The casino's not hurting — it's just not growing. And that's the thing about this town right now: the places around us in Baylight and Jack London are adding a job here and there, and we're the ones giving one up. I don't know what that says about who gains from the casino, but I know what it feels like to walk the floor with three fewer familiar faces.

## Eleanor Rivera — BART Station Manager

> Game days at 12th are a different animal — you can feel it before you even see the platform. The crowd comes in waves, not a steady stream, and that's what stretches the dwell time. Trains are there on time, but everyone's squeezing on at once, so the doors hold longer and the schedule slips a little. My crew just adjusts — no big announcements, everyone knows their spot. We read the rhythm of the station, and we move with it. It's not glamorous, but when the platforms fill up like that, you see the team do what they do best: keep it moving, keep it safe, keep it human.
