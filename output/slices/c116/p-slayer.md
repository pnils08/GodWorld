Carmen Mesa was the A's best trade chip, and now he is gone, sent to the Phillies, the team the A's beat in five games for their seventh World Series title. Mesa, 23, 81 overall with B potential, goes to Philadelphia with relief prospect Porter Broadway, 20, 70 overall with C potential, and second baseman Jose Fuentes, 23, 63 overall with B potential. Coming back to Oakland are third baseman Giancarlo Hernandez and left fielder Antone Baez. Mesa never got a fair shot at a rotation spot here. He was called up in C91 when Henry Rivas dislocated his shoulder, threw a complete game shutout against the Cubs in his first start, 9 innings, 5 hits and 12 strikeouts, and struck out 10 more against the Rays his next time out. When Rivas came back, Mesa was optioned to AAA, and Pablo Almanzar got the late-season call-up and then the fifth rotation spot behind Coles, Ramos, Rivas and Ellis. Mesa, born in Cuba and living in Lake Merritt, was stuck at $780K with nowhere to start. Hernandez is the prize. He is 23, a 2036 first round pick born in the Dominican Republic, wears #15, bats and throws right, and has almost no major league service time. His glove is already elite: 96 fielding, 80 reaction and a 70 arm. The bat is behind the glove, 65 contact against righties and 68 against lefties with modest power, but he hits to all fields and improved in nearly every attribute this cycle, including +9 contact against lefties. He grades A potential at 78 overall and climbing. That gives the A's three young third basemen with A potential. Ernesto Quintero took the job from Martin Richards, and Kevin Clark, 22, was supposed to be next, with Quintero sliding to first base if Mark Aitken leaves in free agency. Baez, 24, 70 overall with C potential, starts in the minors, organizational outfield depth behind Desmond Morton, Frank Reyna and JR Rosado. The Phillies players carry no listed salary, and the A's budget room rises from $129.42M to $130.35M.
