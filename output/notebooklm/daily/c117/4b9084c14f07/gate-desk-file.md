# Three Neighborhoods Are Sliding. Two Officials Say It's Theirs. Neither Has a Fix.

I asked who owns the fix. I got two answers and no repair.

Civis Systems flagged three districts this cycle. Fruitvale, Grand Lake and East Oakland all decayed, and the readings say none of them has a matching active initiative.

The numbers:

- **Fruitvale.** Sentiment fell 0.17. Housing pressure rose 0.5 to 10, which ranks 20th of 22 districts. The city median is 0.
- **Grand Lake.** Retail vitality fell 1.23, to 13.65. Housing pressure rose a full point to 6, 17th of 22. Civis rates this one high.
- **East Oakland.** Sentiment fell 0.14. Retail vitality fell 1.15, to 8.78, 14th of 22.

Elias Varek, who founded Civis, put it in his own journal this week: "A reading with no program behind it is a smoke alarm in an empty building." He asked the city to name one owner per district. So I asked the two people most likely to claim them.

## Delgado owns it. The thing she owns is a station.

Council member Rose Delgado, District 3, didn't dodge. "You want to know who owns the fix? That's me," she told me. She described the shopkeeper on International "holding on by her fingernails" and the family "sleeping on a cousin's couch in San Antonio."

Then she told me what she actually holds: "the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II. That's the lever I've been pulling." She put it at $230 million of design and site work. The business ledger lists the Fruitvale Transit Hub Authority at $232,340,226.

Here is the problem. The hub just moved from visioning to design-phase. It's a real project, and it's also a design phase. It is not a rent program, and it isn't aimed at a shopkeeper's lease. Civis looked at the same hub and still found Fruitvale's housing pressure with nothing matching it.

Delgado also said it plainly: "Housing pressure — nobody in this building owns that alone." That is the honest part. It is also the gap. Fruitvale's worst number has no owner by her own account.

She has set the community review for the first Thursday of next month. I'll hold her to being on International taking questions. A review that talks about design isn't a response to rent.

## The Mayor concedes the pattern

Mayor Avery Santana was blunter about the system: "I'm not going to sit here and tell you that every street in this city gets the same attention at the same time."

He said the system "is built to respond to noise," and that he is trying to rewire it "so that we respond to *need*." He called Grand Lake "not sliding, that's creeping, and it's just as unacceptable."

For East Oakland he gave me potholes on MacArthur. He gave me no program, no owner and no date.

A mayor with a 77 approval rating, the highest in the ledger, can call this a design flaw. He is also the person who can fix it. Naming it is not fixing it.

## What is actually on the board

- **Grand Lake.** Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake is vote-scheduled. A scheduled vote is not an active program. Meanwhile retail is down 1.23 and housing pressure is up a full point.
- **East Oakland.** The only initiative that touches it is the Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline, which just went from implementation-active to stalled. Nobody has said who is waiting on whom.
- **Everywhere else.** Downtown Economic Revitalization has sat at vote-scheduled for three cycles. The Dimond Revitalization Initiative has sat at announced for four. Baylight's final vote, by contrast, is already in construction.

The city moves when it decides to. It decides unevenly.

## The ask

Three districts, three readings, and a promise from Civis to publish a stall clock. A clock only measures the wait. It doesn't end it.

I'm asking for three names. Who answers when Fruitvale's housing pressure climbs again? Who answers for Grand Lake's retail? Who answers for East Oakland's anything?

If the answer is "the Mayor's rewiring," that is a mission statement, not a name. I'll check next cycle whether anyone filled in the blanks.
