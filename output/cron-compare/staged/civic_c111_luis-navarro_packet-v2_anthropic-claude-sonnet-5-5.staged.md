# The Pipeline Is Running. The Count Isn't Moving.

West Oakland, East Oakland and Fruitvale share one line on the city's books. The Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline is listed in all three. It is operational, and it has also stalled.

The economic initiative carrying West Oakland has not advanced in four cycles. Both facts are on the record for Cycle 111. They sit side by side, and neither explains the other.

Four elected officials spoke about the program this cycle. Their words are in office records, each with its own statement ID.

Denise Carter, District 1, put a number on it: "150 apprentices placed by spring—that's the target, and we're accelerating to hit it."

Rose Delgado, District 3, spoke about the cohort: "This cohort is our promise in action—we're investing in D3's future, one apprentice at a time."

Janae Rivers, District 5, spoke for East Oakland: "East Oakland's youth deserve more than promises—they deserve pathways to prosperity."

Mayor Avery Santana put a date on it. In one statement she said: "150 apprentices by spring — I'm demanding the first cohort placement report by Cycle 112." In another she said: "150 apprentices by spring — that was the promise, and I'm demanding the first cohort placement report by Cycle 112."

## What we know

The pipeline is carried as operational. Its tracker status has stalled. The West Oakland economic initiative has gone four cycles without advancing.

Carter, Delgado, Rivers and Santana each spoke publicly on it in Cycle 111. Carter and Santana both name 150 apprentices by spring. Santana has set Cycle 112 as the deadline for a first cohort placement report.

## What we don't know

The record contains no placement count. Nothing in it says how many young people have been placed so far, in which neighborhood, or into which jobs.

Carter says the program is "accelerating." The tracker says it has stalled. The two readings sit in the same cycle, and the record does not reconcile them.

Nothing in the record names who is responsible for producing the report Santana wants. It does not say who receives it, or what happens if Cycle 112 arrives without one. Whether a request for the report has been made, or answered, is also absent.

Four cycles is a measured span. The record does not say what happened inside it, or what would count as advancing.

## The pressure point

Delgado calls it a promise. Rivers says East Oakland's youth deserve more than promises. Those are the program's own backers describing the same pipeline in opposite registers: one as delivery, one as a warning against empty words.

Neither quote contradicts the other. Both are fair to hold up against a status line that reads *stalled* and a deadline that reads *Cycle 112*.

Cycle 112 is the next checkpoint. A mayor who sets a date has given the public something to measure. When it arrives, the question is simple. Is there a report, and what does it count?

A target is not a placement. "By spring" is a season, not a figure. The only number the officials gave is 150, and no number in the record says how far along that is.

## What stays open

Somewhere in West Oakland, East Oakland or Fruitvale, a young person was meant to be one of those 150. The officials have spoken for that person in the plural and in the future tense. The tracker has spoken for the program in the present tense, and it says stalled.

Which of those two is that young person living in?
