## UNDOCKED C116: Three routes, one steady leader

Three pilots took the UNDOCKED chair this cycle, and Luis Taz remains the name at the top of the board. He leads at rank #1 with 45.19 achievement points and zero credits; his current streak is three cycles, with three cycles led. The week’s explorers and contract-runner offered different ways to earn a night’s points, but the standings still belong to Taz.

Marguerite Kimball-Ostrow, an elementary teacher, flew two nights with exploration as her through-line. Sunday’s route earned 9 points for Wanderer, 6.4 for Voyager I and 5 for Maiden Jump, among other achievement points; Wednesday brought another 4.5 for Cartographer and 3.2 for Voyager I. Both nights ended even on credits, and both ran until the show’s time ran out. Her explanation makes the route feel less like a ledger exercise than a curiosity: “I was just trying to explore and see where the show would take me.”

Manju Abebe, a plumber, also had two nights, but not the same route twice. Monday’s systems brought 9 points for Wanderer and 5 for Maiden Jump, among other awards. On Thursday, Cartographer led her listed achievements at 5.1 points, followed by 4.4 for Voyager I. Both nights ended even on credits. “Both nights, I came out even on credits and flew until the show's time ran out,” she said. That repeat finish is a tidy counterpoint to the changing map.

Soo Loo, a corporate accountant, had the week’s sharpest contrast between nights. Tuesday brought five fights, a sale of four iron ore and 24 credits up; Chronicler was the biggest listed award, at 5.4 points. By Friday, Loo had finished First Hunt: Belt-Grazers for 1,000 credits, taken four contracts, and sold copper ore and creature carapace. The available Friday record lists 5 points for Wanderer, 4.8 for Voyager I and 3.6 for Cartographer, but does not supply the rest of the point breakdown or the night’s final credit balance. Loo called the approach “a more varied and successful” one.

At a watch-party table, the argument could be whether a route’s breadth or its finish makes the better television. This week offers both: Marguerite and Manju kept crossing systems until time ran out, while Soo’s second night closed a contract for a stated payout. But the board’s top line is not a verdict on those three alone. It is Taz’s lead, still intact at the end of a week with three distinct flying styles.
