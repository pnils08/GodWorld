# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Jocelyn Santo — Yoga Instructor

> Oh, that's a good question. Let me think. One thing that's already different—really different—is the coffee situation. Travis is a pour-over guy, like, he has the little scale and the gooseneck kettle and he weighs the beans. I used to just slam a cup from a drip machine and run out the door to teach a 6 a.m. class. Now I actually sit down with him for ten minutes before I go, and it's this slow little ritual I never knew I needed. It's small, but it makes the mornings feel like ours, you know? And something we haven't worked out yet... honestly, it's the laundry. Or more specifically, the folding. I fold everything a certain way—tight, military-ish, because I grew up that way—and Travis just... balls things up and shoves them in drawers. We've had this gentle, silly standoff about it for weeks. We haven't figured it out yet. It's not a big deal, but it's the kind of thing you realize you have to actually negotiate when you merge two lives. We'll get there. Probably after I teach him my folding method one drawer at a time.

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> The breeze came up off the lake around dusk, and the terrace lights swayed a little on their wire. Robert pulled his sweater tighter and said it felt like the first night of spring that meant it. I still haven't heard from Serg Cerrano. I'm not troubled. A father of a few days sleeps in pieces and answers in pieces. But I thought about him on the walk home, because the same week he was heard, a flyer for the apprenticeship program went up on a pole outside a market in his neighborhood. The promise is twelve and a half million dollars. The people it was written for are still reading it from the sidewalk. Uptown is quieter in a different way. Five households there are planning to go, and not out of any dislike for the place. The arithmetic of staying has stopped working for them. Nobody throws a farewell party for that kind of leaving.
