# At 19th Street, the Platform Took the Weight

Coen Cartier, an AC Transit operator, works the stretch where 19th Street Oakland feeds the buses. He does not claim to have seen the number everyone is now quoting. "Wednesday's a normal workday for me, so I can't say I noticed any big jump on my runs," he said. What he did see was a platform fuller than a regular afternoon calls for. "That station's been busier lately, and with the council vote and the game downtown, people were moving around more than usual. Most of them got on heading toward the Lake Merritt and downtown stops, not so much out to the edges."

The Transit_Metrics row for 19th St Oakland, Cycle 115, shows 16,076 riders. That is 5,324 more than Cycle 114, and it is the biggest gain of any station on the sheet. Its on-time performance was 81%, up one point.

The line as a whole moved with it. The eight stations on the record carried 73,938 riders, against 46,839 the cycle before, a rise of about 58 percent. The day type was an ordinary weekday with no citywide holiday cut. The weather was mild and had been clear for five cycles. No chaos car touched BART or AC Transit this cycle.

**The same lift, up and down the line**

- **12th St Oakland City Center** added 4,515 riders, to 14,526. On-time was 86%, up nine points.
- **Fruitvale** added 3,880, to 8,943. On-time was 87%, up 15 points.
- **West Oakland** added 3,491, to 9,519. On-time was 77%, up three points.
- **Coliseum** added 2,980, to 7,776. On-time was 81%.
- **Lake Merritt** added 2,684, to 6,780. On-time was 83%, up seven points.
- **Rockridge** added 2,397, to 5,684. On-time was 79%, up seven points.
- **MacArthur** added 1,828, to 4,634. On-time was 80%, up one point.

Each of those rows carries its own reasons. They include game days in Downtown and Jack London, events in Lake Merritt, Temescal, East Oakland and Piedmont Ave, and Baylight District construction listed as active. Every row also carries "crowded platforms."

The corridors rose the same way. I-580 East's traffic index climbed 51 points, to 89. I-880 South climbed 44, to 98. I-880 North and I-980 each reached 100. Grand Avenue climbed 38, to 73.

**What the 19th Street row can and cannot say**

Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for BART's Oakland Division, said the chatter does not name one cause. "It's not one big thing — it's a bunch of little things stacking up," she said. "We had the Oaks game downtown, the Baylight District vote finally going through, and events all over Lake Merritt, Temescal, and KONO. When you get that many neighborhood pulls happening on the same ordinary Tuesday, the platforms fill up fast. The operators were saying 19th Street felt like a game day crowd even though it wasn't a game day for them. It's just what happens when the whole city decides to move at once."

Her account matches the factors on the row. It is also an account of causes, not a count of them. The sheet lists the causes. It does not say how many riders each one produced.

The earlier rows add a caution. The Cycle 114 row for 19th Street carried a "weekend" tag, and it recorded 10,752 riders. Cycle 115 is a plain weekday. Part of the jump may be the calendar. The sheet does not say how much.

The new figure is also not a record for the station. Cycle 109 logged 17,104 riders at 19th Street, and Cycle 110 logged 16,105. Those two cycles were game days in Downtown too. Their on-time figures were 86% and 79%.

At Fruitvale, the Phase II hub is still in design. Elena Soria Dominguez, the Transit Hub Planning Lead at the Fruitvale Transit Hub Authority, was careful not to claim the growth for her project. "I can't say the visioning is what's pulling those extra riders in," she said. "We're still in the design phase, no shovels in the ground, and most people aren't staring at renderings on their way to work. The growth we're seeing on the row tracks more with what's happening in East Oakland — the events, the crowds, the energy that's been building out here."

She pointed to the construction workers who take their breaks under the overpass. "They see it from the platform every day," she said. "They're the ones who'll tell you the station's already becoming a place people want to be, even before Phase II is real."

**The systems read**

This is analysis, not a measurement from the sheet. Seven of the stations that report on-time figures held or improved them while carrying about half again as many riders as the cycle before. The 19th Street platform carried the most, and its on-time figure did not slip. A surge of this size would normally be expected to strain the schedule, and these rows show it did not.

The sheet cannot say why. A mild week with no chaos hit may explain it. So may a service plan that quietly absorbed the load.

The open question is the one the data cannot answer. If every station moved together on an ordinary weekday, what changed in the city that no single station row can name? The ledger shows a one-cycle jump. It does not show whether the platforms will keep this load. The next rows will show whether on-time performance holds when one of the many causes drops away.
