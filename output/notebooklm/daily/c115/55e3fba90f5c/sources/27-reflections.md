# West Oakland, in their own words

## Clarissa Dane — DISTRICT ATTORNEY

> Vinnie, look around Grand Lake. The storefronts that survived the last five years are the ones with landlords who held the line on rent. This stabilization fund keeps those busines

> East Oakland's nineteen percent clearance on seven incidents sits with me heavier than it should, given my own district's barely better twenty on five. I keep comparing them anyway, looking for some pattern that isn't there, some lever I haven't pulled yet. The Fruitvale number—eighteen—makes me think of my neighbor again, though I don't need the reminder; I live it. OARI protocols or not, I'm filing charges on cases I know won't close, and I'm doing it because the alternative is worse, even when the math says it's futile. Sixty-five percent standing with the city means something to the budget people; it means nothing to the ten in my district that won't clear.

## Elias Varek — Founder of Civis Systems

> Civis is flat. $60.3 against $60.2 — that’s not a plateau, that’s a wall. I built the instrument that this city reads itself with, and it’s stopped telling me anything new. Then there’s the signal in West Oakland that still hasn’t answered downstream, and it’s moved. That’s the part that bothers me more than the revenue line: when my own product shows me a gap and the system I built to catch it doesn’t close, I’m not building infrastructure anymore, I’m just maintaining a status quo with a nicer dashboard. The Oaks and Varek are humming — predictable, steady, almost boring. But boring is fine. Boring means the machine works. The flat line at Civis and the moving signal are the same problem wearing different coats, and I’ve been staring at both long enough to know I’m not going to fix either one by staying at this desk tonight.

## Mike Paulson — General Manager, Oakland A's & The Oaks

> The catcher situation keeps circling back on me. Jose’s had a rough year, and Calzado outhit him in fewer games—that’s hard to ignore, but Calzado is 30 and out of options, so there’s no easy path there. The real weight is the timing. We’re winning, the city’s buzzing, and I’m supposed to be enjoying this, but instead I’m staring at a position where the backup did more with less, and I don’t know if that’s a problem to fix now or a decision to make in November. It’s not about loyalty—Jose has the tools and the years, but this is a business and these are the calls that keep me up. And underneath all of it, I keep coming back to the families who’ve been here since the beginning, wondering if they see a general manager who’s steady or just one who’s tired.

## Wilson Shepard — Head Coach, Oakland Oaks

> Eight and one, and I still catch myself waiting for the other shoe to drop. That Hayward kid—nobody talked about him on draft night, and now he's the reason we're not looking at seven and two. He's not flashy, just keeps showing up, keeps making the right play, and that's the kind of trust you can't coach into a guy. The room feels hungry, not satisfied, and that's the part I like most. We're not gonna sneak up on anybody anymore, but maybe that's fine. Maybe we're just getting started.

## Austin Reaves — SG / The Oaks

> The 15 points all from three feels right for how I’m fitting in — I’m not forcing anything, just letting the game come to me. Seven assists with zero turnovers next to Stanley’s eight, that’s the kind of backcourt night you can build on. But I keep circling back to the Pacers loss, even with three straight wins since. A 34-point quarter on the road tells me we’re still learning how to handle a punch in a loud building, and 7-1 doesn’t change that. The crowd was 94% full for Orlando, and they got a show — but I want that same energy when things get tight, not just when we’re up 20.

## Dillon Brooks — SF / The Oaks

> Back-to-back 20-piece games. That’s the first time in a minute I’ve felt that rhythm early, where the rim just feels like a bucket and the defense is reacting a step late. But I’m not fooling myself — the Magic were done by halftime, and it’s easy to look good when the game’s already broken open. The real test is next week when we’re not playing with a 20-point cushion. Still, 10-for-14 feels clean, and the guys are locked in. We’re 7-1 and nobody in that locker room is pretending it means anything yet, but it’s a hell of a lot better than the alternative.
