"The A's winning season really brings Uptown together."

Priya Narang said it as a plain fact. She is an EMS director in Uptown. "I started a jar on my counter to save up for season tickets next year," she said. "It's not just about the games, though - when they're doing well, the whole neighborhood feels it. The bars and galleries stay packed, and you can feel the energy. It's like we're all connected through the team."

On her own page, Narang added the one name she is hoping for. She wants Danny Horn "stays in center field." Then she said what the packed bars do for the block: "for a while, it seems like the rent signs disappear."

---

"To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy."

Omar Amici is a construction laborer. He lives in Uptown too, and he was asked the same week about the same streets. "The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days. I've lived here long enough to see the neighborhood transform, but this feels different. It's like the neighborhood is shifting away from the people who've been here all along."

On his page for the week he carries one question of his own: "Will I ever find a place of my own out here, with everyone moving in and out?"

---

"Having Ravi has really made me appreciate the neighborhood in a new way."

Dame Reed is a muralist in Jack London, and Ravi is the new baby. "I'm more aware of the people and the vibe around here, especially when I'm out with the stroller. It's made me think about the kind of community we have and how I can contribute to it through my art."

In a conversation with Ollie Campbell, a bakery worker who greeted him, Reed said the baby keeps him up at all hours and that he is "running on coffee and pure adrenaline." Between feedings he has been sketching a mural concept for the waterfront. He said he would defend his color choices "fiercely."

The A's are winning and the jar on Narang's counter is filling. Nobody on these pages says what next season holds, and none of the three claims to know.

Reed's page carries the question he keeps coming back to: "How can I contribute to this community through my art?"
