# Light Platforms at 12th Street: A Weekend Count and What It Doesn't Explain

At 12th St Oakland City Center the announcements carry differently when the platform runs light. Eleanor Rivera, the BART station manager, has learned to hear that first.

"Honestly, the first thing I notice on a light weekend morning is the sound," she said. "The platform echoes more. You hear the announcements bounce off the tile instead of getting swallowed up by the crowd. And you see the regulars more clearly — the folks who are always there, not the game-day rush. It's a different rhythm. We just adapt, keep things moving, make sure the trains run on time. That's the job, whether it's five thousand people or fifty."

The metrics sheet puts the quiet at 9,341 riders for Cycle 113. The previous cycle, C112, the same station logged 14,162, a drop of 4,821. That is the largest drop of any station on the record, and about a third of the station's own C112 volume.

On-time performance fell with it. The station ran 84 percent on time, down 5 points from 89 percent in C112.

## The Row's Own Causes

The row for 12th Street lists the weekend first. The sheet's weekend rule cuts riders citywide, so a lighter platform was expected. Beyond the weekend, the row lists game day in Downtown and Jack London, events in Downtown twice, Lake Merritt and Jack London, and the Baylight District final council vote at the construction-active stage.

Game day appears on this row in both cycles, C112 and C113. The sheet's notes column still reads "game day crowds" for C113, but the crowd was 4,821 smaller than the week before. The rows do not say why. Game day was on the row and the platform still fell. Whether the game-day lift shrank, or the weekend cut swallowed it, is not recoverable from the record.

Evan Lewis, an AC Transit bus driver, reads the same pattern from his seat. "When the system runs light on a weekend, you feel it in your bones before you ever see the numbers," he said. "12th Street is the heart of downtown — that's where people transfer, where they catch the game or head to an event. When it's quiet there, it's not just empty platforms. It's the whole city catching its breath. I see it on my route too — fewer folks waiting, more empty seats, and a kind of stillness that tells you everyone's home or somewhere else entirely. The construction around the Baylight District doesn't help either; when streets get torn up, people just find another way, even if the bus still runs on time."

Lewis's point about construction is his own observation. The sheet carries Baylight District only as a listed factor with the construction-active label. It does not say the work turned any rider away.

## Eight Stations, One Direction

Every station on the sheet fell. Total ridership across the eight was 46,520, against 72,291 in C112. 19th St Oakland lost 4,777 riders and landed at 10,012. Coliseum fell 3,467 to 5,595. West Oakland fell 3,413 to 6,038. Fruitvale fell 3,405 to 4,553. Lake Merritt fell 2,630 to 4,825, MacArthur 1,708 to 2,650, and Rockridge 1,550 to 3,506.

The ten corridors fell the same way. Broadway's traffic index dropped 43 points to 47. I-880 North and I-580 East each dropped 32. Every corridor row carries the weekend factor.

On-time performance did not follow ridership. 19th Street gained 7 points to 90 percent while losing nearly 4,800 riders. MacArthur gained 5 and Lake Merritt gained 1. Coliseum lost 8, to 82 percent, and Fruitvale lost 7, to 81 percent. West Oakland held at 87 and Rockridge slipped 1 to 82.

Fewer riders did not lift every platform's punctuality. Coliseum's row lists events in the Baylight District twice. Fruitvale's row lists events in Fruitvale and the Transit Hub Phase II visioning at design-phase. The rows name no cause for the on-time losses, and none should be assumed.

## Who Is Left on the Platform

Coen Cartier, an AC Transit operator, described what the thinner count looks like from the cab. "When the system runs light on a weekend, the bus feels different — you notice the regulars more," he said. "The folks who ride every day, they're still there, but there's no rush. You get a chance to actually say hello, maybe ask how someone's day is going. Riders don't see that part. They just see the bus showing up. But for us, it's the difference between moving a crowd and moving people. And on a quiet day, you see the city itself more — the light on the buildings, the empty sidewalks. It's a slower rhythm, but it's still a rhythm."

That is the useful part of a light count. The people left on the platform are the ones the schedule is actually built for. A one-third drop does not remove them.

## What the Next Rows Will Show

Three BART train incidents were logged this cycle: a signal failure, a medical hold on board, and a stalled train in Rockridge where riders walked the tunnel. Train hits reach station rows a cycle later, labeled "last week." None of the three appears on this cycle's rows, including Rockridge's, which lists only events in Montclair.

The open question is for C114. If those three incidents reach the platforms, will the rows show it at the stations they touched? The sheet does not yet say which stations that is. If the weekend rule lifts and 12th Street's count recovers, will its 84 percent on-time figure recover with it? Nothing on the record says it will.
