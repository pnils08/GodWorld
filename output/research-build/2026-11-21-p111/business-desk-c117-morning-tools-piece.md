# Ivy Hill's dental office closes, and the casino floor is thinner too

**By Jordan Velez, Economics & Labor**

Ivy Hill Family Dental closed this cycle. The city's economic-event record logs the closure as a magnitude -25 hit to the neighborhood, with zero jobs left on the books. The same ledger shows every other small business in Ivy Hill at zero employees.

Here are the five Ivy Hill entries on the Business Ledger, with their yearly growth rates:

| Business | Employees | Annual revenue | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ivy Hill Market (retail) | 0 | $513k | -4.7% |
| Hillcrest Kitchen (restaurant) | 0 | $572k | -3.3% |
| Ivy Hill Cafe | 0 | $311k | -2.4% |
| Ivy Cleaners | 0 | $258k | -1.9% |
| Fold Laundry | 0 | $258k | -1.8% |

Hillcrest Kitchen lost its last employee this cycle, and its revenue is still $572k. The ledger doesn't say who is doing the work with no one on payroll, so I won't guess. The ledger lists average salaries of $39,000 to $43,000 at these shops. Ivy Hill's median income is $77k, so the jobs that have gone paid roughly half of what a typical household in the neighborhood earns.

## A slow squeeze

This has been building for several cycles. In C113 this column noted Ivy Hill Market had shed two workers and was growing at -3.9%. In C114 it had shed three more, at -4.5%. The market is now at -4.7%. The city's ripple record has it in the third cycle of a four-cycle carryover, at magnitude -8, so the engine expects further drag.

Rent doesn't explain it. Ivy Hill's median rent is about $2k and its housing pressure score is 0, against 10 in Uptown, Downtown, Jack London and Fruitvale. The neighborhood's trajectory is listed as "decay." Nobody is being priced out. The block is emptying of paid work.

## It isn't only Ivy Hill

Two other dental practices lost three employees each this cycle:

- **Hillside Family Dental** (Glenview) is now at 0 employees, with revenue of $936k.
- **Northshore Dental** (Adams Point) is down to 1 employee, with revenue of $1.3M.

The ledger shows no closure event for either. I'm not calling them closed, but a practice reporting revenue with no staff deserves a phone call.

Glenview's other small businesses each lost one worker this cycle:

- Glenview Cafe: 5 employees
- Parkridge Cleaners: 3 employees
- Glenview Kitchen: 9 employees

Glenview's median income is $81k and its trajectory is also "decay."

## The casino thread

Oakland Casino Town, in the Baylight District, is down three employees to 94. Prior Tribune coverage had it at 99 in C115, so it has shed five jobs in two cycles. The ledger puts its growth at -1.5%.

Casino Manager Philly Rodriguez said the cuts came from the floor. "We're down three. It's the floor staff — two of the dealers and one of the cashiers," he said. "The dealers, they were good kids, but they found steadier hours elsewhere. The cashier, she'd been with us since before the pandemic, and she just said she needed something with benefits that didn't swing so late."

That is a different story from a layoff. By Rodriguez's account, the workers left for steadier hours and benefits. Nobody was cut. That is a labor-market signal: late-night casino hours are losing people to other employers. "The floor feels thinner, especially on weekend nights," he said. "The pit boss has to cover two sections instead of one."

Manfred Owens, who keeps the book near the back tables, linked the casino to the neighborhoods around it. "You see a dental office close in Ivy Hill, you see a kitchen go down to zero, and you start counting your own people a little more carefully," he said. Of the people who remain, he said: "They're not going anywhere yet."

The casino's house float stands at $246k. The city treasury balance is $136.9M. Both are small next to the $522.4M in revenue at DigitalOcean, which is also in the Baylight District and added one employee this cycle, to 801.

## Where the jobs are

Not every business is shrinking. The Bay Tribune, Ridgeline Studio, Atlas Bay Architects and Bayline 6 each added one employee, and all four report double-digit yearly growth. The growth is in architecture, media, venture capital and cloud infrastructure, in neighborhoods where median income runs from $99k to $186k. The contraction is in dental offices, cafes, cleaners and corner markets, in neighborhoods the map tags as decay.

## What to watch

- Whether Hillside Family Dental and Northshore Dental are closing, or whether the ledger is mislabeling them.
- Whether Ivy Hill Market's carryover ends after cycle four or keeps going.
- Whether the casino backfills its dealer and cashier slots, and at what hours and benefits.

If Ivy Hill's shops keep their revenue with no payroll, I'll need to find out who's behind the counter.
