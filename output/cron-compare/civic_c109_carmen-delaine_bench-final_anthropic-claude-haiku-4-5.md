# West Oakland Stabilization Fund at Month Eight: $4.6M Allocated, Scope Narrowed to Displacement Defense

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund entered its disbursement cycle in Cycle 108 with a charter narrower than its $28 million budget might suggest. The fund, proposed by Mayor Avery Santana and passed 6–3 in Cycle 78, was designed to issue stabilization grants to long-tenure West Oakland residents priced out as the flats built out—not hood-wide relief, but a specific remnant population facing displacement as property values climbed.

By Cycle 109, month eight of active disbursement, the Initiative_Tracker records show the fund had moved $4.6 million in total allocation: $4.2 million across 47 household checks disbursed under Deputy Mayor Okoro's administration in Cycle 108, and a month-eight tranche of $400,000 released in West Oakland this cycle, supporting 2 additional household grants totaling $81,188.

The remaining balance stands at $23.4 million of the original $28 million.

The vote that carried the fund split the council. Ramon Vega voted yes. Leonard Tran voted no. Elliott Crane voted yes remotely. The six-vote threshold held, but the margin signals the measure did not command consensus on the full slate of nine seats.

The scope question—whom the fund reaches and whom it leaves outside the circle—remains unresolved at the implementation level. The charter named a specific class: long-tenure residents at risk of displacement. The data so far shows 49 households across two cycles. At the current disbursement rate, the remaining $23.4 million would support roughly 279 more household grants at the average sizes recorded, a projection that spans multiple implementation cycles ahead.

The next scheduled action is a month-nine disbursement review in Cycle 110. The Initiative_Tracker does not yet specify the review's mandate—whether it will examine grant velocity, demographic reach, or the fit between charter scope and the resident profile actually served. The forward question is whether the fund's narrow purpose and measured pace will meet the displacement pressures it was chartered to address, or whether those pressures will outrun the fund's capacity to respond.
