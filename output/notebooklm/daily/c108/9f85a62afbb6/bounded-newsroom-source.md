# Rockridge Schools Maintain Excellence Amid Citywide Shifts  

In the middle of a bustling school day, Gloria Liu stands in the hallway of Rockridge’s top-ranked school, greeting students by name. Liu, the principal, has been here long enough to see the neighborhood’s reputation for educational excellence solidify—a reputation that remains unshaken even as enrollment fluctuates across the city.  

“Rockridge schools have always been a cornerstone of this community,” Liu says. “Even with the shifts in enrollment across the city, we’ve managed to maintain a high standard of education. It’s not just about the numbers; it’s about the dedication of our teachers, the support from families, and the shared belief in the value of a good education.”  

This cycle, Rockridge’s school quality index stands at 8.94, a slight dip from its previous 9 but still the highest in the city. Graduation rates hover near 95%, and 77.7% of students are considered college-ready. These metrics are bolstered by a teacher quality score of 9.02 and $15,000 in per-student funding, both among the highest in the Oakland Unified School District.  

The stability of Rockridge’s schools contrasts with enrollment shifts elsewhere. Fruitvale added 11 students this cycle, Glenview and San Antonio each gained 10, and Brooklyn and Laurel saw increases of 9 students. Yet Rockridge’s student count remains steady at 237, a testament to the neighborhood’s enduring appeal for families.  

For Mei Wong, an after-school program aide in Rockridge, the community’s investment in education is palpable. “What I’ve seen in Rockridge is that the schools here have this deep sense of community behind them,” Wong says. “It’s not just about the numbers or funding—it’s about how everyone, from parents to teachers to local businesses, shows up and invests in the kids. That consistency makes a difference, even when other neighborhoods are seeing shifts in enrollment.”  

Maya Torres-Dillon, a science teacher and member of the Bay Conservation Alliance Advisory board, echoes this sentiment. “As a teacher here, I’ve seen firsthand how Rockridge schools thrive because of the community’s commitment,” she says. “Parents, teachers, and local organizations all work together to create an environment where students can succeed. That shared responsibility makes a real difference.”  

For students like Salma Campbell, the steady quality of Rockridge schools is a given. “I’ve always felt like Rockridge schools just have this steady vibe, you know?” Campbell says. “Like, they’re consistent, and it shows in how people talk about them. Even with all the changes happening in other parts of the city, it feels like things here just keep running smoothly.”  

As the city grapples with broader enrollment shifts and funding disparities, Rockridge’s schools remain a beacon of stability. The question now is whether this model can be replicated elsewhere—or if Rockridge’s success is uniquely tied to its neighborhood advantages.
