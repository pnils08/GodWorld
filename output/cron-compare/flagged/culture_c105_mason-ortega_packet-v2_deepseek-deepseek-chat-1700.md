# The Rhythm of the Evening at Merritt Reserve

Merritt Reserve sits on the edge of Lake Merritt, its windows glowing softly against the early evening. The restaurant is listed among the evening spots in the neighborhood, but the story of what happens inside after dark remains unspoken. No one yet knows the rhythm of its kitchen, the pace of its service, or the hands that move there.

Vanessa Treary, a barista just blocks away, finishes her shift around the time Merritt Reserve’s evening crowd begins to gather. “I don’t really know much about the evening service at Merritt Reserve,” she says. “I'm usually finishing up at the coffee shop around then. But I've heard from customers that it gets pretty lively over there after dark, with a different crowd than the daytime.”

Yuki Ji, a bartender at another Lake Merritt spot, echoes the sentiment. “I’ve heard Merritt Reserve gets pretty lively in the evenings,” she says, “but I haven’t been there myself—I’m usually behind the bar at my own spot by then.”

The evening service at Merritt Reserve exists as a rumor, a shadow cast by the words of others. What does it look like when the kitchen hums, when plates move from the pass to the dining room? Who keeps the rhythm, and what does it say about Oakland’s current food service climate? The question lingers, unanswered, as the lights burn on.
