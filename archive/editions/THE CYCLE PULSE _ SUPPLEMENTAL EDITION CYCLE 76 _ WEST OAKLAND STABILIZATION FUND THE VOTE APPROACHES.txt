COUNCIL POSITIONS: WHERE THEY STAND BEFORE THE VOTE

Committee hearing Cycle 77, floor vote Cycle 78 — the math remains tight

By Carmen Delaine | Civic Ledger

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund needs six votes to pass. Four are locked. Two are needed. One remains unknown.

With the committee hearing set for Cycle 77 and the floor vote scheduled for Cycle 78, the Bay Tribune contacted all nine council offices to establish their positions on the $28 million reserve fund draw.

**THE YES VOTES — 4 CONFIRMED**

Councilwoman Denise Carter, District 1, has championed the fund since her appointment. West Oakland is her district. The displacement is her constituency.

“This isn’t charity,” Carter said. “This is the city protecting the people who built it. Tenant legal services. Down payment assistance. Storefront retention grants. These programs work. We have the money. The question is whether we have the will.”

Rose Delgado (D3), Janae Rivers (D5), and Terrence Mobley (D9) confirmed yes votes through their offices. The OPP bloc holds.

Rivers, the Progressive Caucus Leader, issued a statement: “Four votes from our caucus. We need two more. The path is clear.”

**THE LEAN YES — 1**

Council President Ramon Vega has signaled support in principle but stopped short of a firm commitment.

“I believe in the fund’s goals,” Vega said. “I want to see the committee hearing. I want to hear the testimony. Then I’ll make my position official.”

Vega’s lean brings the likely count to five. One short.

**THE UNDECIDED — 1**

Leonard Tran, District 2, remains genuinely undecided.

“I’ve met with advocates on both sides,” Tran said. “I’ve reviewed the fiscal projections. I understand what the fund is trying to accomplish. I also understand the concerns about reserve fund precedent. I’m still weighing it.”

Tran represents the swing vote. If Vega holds and Tran commits, the fund passes. If Tran votes no, supporters need a CRC flip — a steeper climb.

**THE SKEPTICS — 2 LEAN NO**

Warren Ashford, District 7, has been openly skeptical.

“Twenty-eight million from the reserve fund,” Ashford said. “For programs that haven’t been piloted. Based on projections that assume best-case implementation. I have questions. That’s my job.”

Nina Chen, District 8, wants more data.

“Show me the fiscal impact analysis,” Chen said. “Show me what happens to the reserve fund if we have an emergency next cycle. I’m not opposed to helping West Oakland. I’m opposed to spending without adequate review.”

**THE UNKNOWN — 1**

Councilmember Elliott Crane’s office did not respond to requests for comment. Crane continues to participate remotely due to ongoing medical issues. As CRC Caucus Leader, his position could influence Chen and Ashford. His silence leaves a gap neither side can calculate around.

OPINION
############################################################

THE STABILIZATION FUND ISN’T ABOUT MONEY. IT’S ABOUT WHETHER OAKLAND KEEPS ITS WORD.

By Farrah Del Rio | Civic & Cultural Opinion

Let me make this simple.

West Oakland residents have been packing trucks for three years. The city watched. The city studied. The city held meetings. Now there’s $28 million in a reserve fund and a council that could actually pass something — and we’re still talking about “fiscal projections” and “adequate review.”

Warren Ashford wants to know what happens if there’s an emergency next cycle. I’ll tell you what’s happening right now: families who’ve lived in West Oakland for generations are leaving because they can’t afford to stay. That’s the emergency. It’s not next cycle. It’s this one.

Nina Chen wants data. Here’s data: negative migration in West Oakland for multiple cycles running. Here’s more data: the people leaving aren’t coming back. You can’t pilot-program your way out of displacement once the displacement has already happened.

Leonard Tran says he’s weighing both sides. I respect that. Weighing is part of the job. But at some point you have to put the scale down and decide what kind of city you want to live in.

The Stabilization Fund isn’t perfect. No policy is. But tenant legal services keep people in their homes. Down payment assistance turns renters into owners. Storefront retention grants keep the businesses that make a neighborhood a neighborhood.

These aren’t theories. These are programs that work in cities across the country. Oakland has the money. Oakland has the need. The only question is whether Oakland has the will.

Councilmember Crane hasn’t said a word. His office cites his recovery. Fine. But he’s voting remotely on other matters. He can make his position known if he wants to.

Here’s what I know: when this vote happens, every council member will have to answer for it. Not to me. Not to the Bay Tribune. To the people who trusted them to represent their interests.

Denise Carter is fighting for her district. Ramon Vega is leaning the right direction. Leonard Tran is the swing vote, and he knows it.

Four yes. One lean yes. One undecided. Two lean no. One silent.

The math is the math. But math doesn’t pack trucks. People do. And they’re watching.

Names Index: Farrah Del Rio (Opinion), Warren Ashford (D7), Nina Chen (D8), Leonard Tran (D2), Elliott Crane (D6), Denise Carter (D1), Ramon Vega (D4)

“I’LL BELIEVE IT WHEN I SEE IT”: WEST OAKLAND RESIDENTS ON THE STABILIZATION FUND

By Luis Navarro | Managing Editor

WEST OAKLAND — The council will vote. The advocates will testify. But the people who actually live in West Oakland have heard promises before.

The Bay Tribune spoke with residents about what the Stabilization Fund means to them — and whether they trust the process to deliver.

-----

**Javier Harris, 57, Electrician — 30 years in West Oakland**

Harris plans to testify at the Cycle 77 committee hearing. He’s been following the fund since it was first proposed, and he’s cautiously hopeful — but he’s heard the landlord bailout criticism, and it bothers him.

“People say the retention grants are just giving money to landlords,” Harris said. “That’s not how it works. The grants are tied to keeping rents stable. If a landlord takes the money and raises rent anyway, they pay it back. There’s enforcement built in.”

He paused.

“But I get why people are skeptical. We’ve seen programs that sound good on paper and disappear when it’s time to write checks. I want to believe this is different. I’m testifying because I want to make sure it is.”

-----

**Marcus Wright, 29, Server — Lifelong West Oakland resident**

Wright was skeptical when the Tribune spoke to him in Cycle 75. He’s still skeptical now.

“I’ll believe it when I see it,” Wright said. “They’re talking about twenty-eight million dollars. You know how many times I’ve heard big numbers from City Hall? The money gets announced, everybody claps, and then it disappears into some department where nobody can tell you where it went.”

He shook his head.

“I’m not saying don’t pass it. Pass it. But don’t expect me to throw a parade until somebody actually gets helped. Show me a family that stayed because of this fund. Then I’ll believe it.”

-----

**Howard Perez, 59, Server — 25 years in West Oakland**

Perez is focused on one specific program: tenant legal services.

“My neighbor got an eviction notice last year,” Perez said. “Bogus. Completely bogus. But she didn’t know that. She doesn’t speak English well, she didn’t know her rights, and she almost left because she thought she had to.”

A legal aid attorney got involved. The eviction was thrown out. The neighbor stayed.

“That’s what the fund pays for,” Perez said. “Not charity. Not handouts. Just making sure people know their rights and have someone to fight for them when landlords try to push them out.”

-----

**Joel Roberts, 22, Plumber — Born and raised in West Oakland**

Roberts is the youngest voice the Tribune spoke with, and maybe the most direct.

“I want to stay here,” Roberts said. “This is where I grew up. My mom’s here, my cousins are here, my whole life is here. But I’m twenty-two and I’m already doing the math on whether I can afford to stay.”

He looked down the block.

“If this fund passes and it actually works? Maybe I get help with a down payment in a few years. Maybe I buy something small and stay in the neighborhood. If it doesn’t pass, or it passes and nothing happens, I’m probably gone by thirty.”

He shrugged.

“That’s the stakes. That’s what they’re voting on.”

**COMMITTEE HEARING PREVIEW**

The Cycle 77 hearing will feature testimony from multiple groups. The witness list is still being finalized, but expected voices include:

**For the fund:**

- West Oakland Neighborhood Association
- Bay Area Tenants Union
- Small landlord coalition (retention grant beneficiaries)

**Against the fund:**

- Oakland Taxpayers Coalition
- CRC-aligned business representatives (reserve fund precedent concerns)

**Wildcard:**
One longtime resident who already packed a truck — someone the fund would have helped but arrived too late for.

“That testimony is going to be the hardest to hear,” one advocate said. “Because it’s not hypothetical. It already happened.”
