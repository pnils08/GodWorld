## Two Counters, One Complaint

Elio Perez has a rag in one hand and a coffee pot in the other when I catch him between tables. West Oakland, mid-shift, the kind of double that doesn't leave room to sit down. He doesn't stop moving while he talks.

"I hear people at the diner talking about how the council keeps promising to bridge the gap between neighborhoods, but all we see is more meetings and no real change," he says. "It's frustrating when you're working doubles just to keep up and nothing gets better where you live."

Across town in Rockridge, Depak Ying is running the same kind of shift, the same kind of counter, and landing on almost the same words. I didn't ask him a leading question. He got there on his own.

"I hear people at the diner talking about how nobody's stepping up to fix what's broken — just a lot of finger-pointing at council meetings while the neighborhood keeps waiting," Ying says.

Two servers, two neighborhoods, no connection between them that I can establish — and they're describing the identical stall. That's not proof of anything by itself. But it's a pattern worth sitting with: when people who don't know each other name the same gap, in the same institution, using the same word — *waiting* — it's usually not noise.

Here's what the record actually shows this cycle: a civic gap between neighborhoods, and no initiative on the books addressing it. Not a promise walked back. Not a vote that failed. Just an absence — nothing filed, nothing built, nothing that answers what Perez and Ying are describing from their own counters.

I want to be precise about what that absence means and what it doesn't. It doesn't mean the council refused to act. It doesn't mean anyone was asked and said no. I haven't seen anyone ask for one, and I haven't seen anyone in an office decline to answer — that's simply not something I have in front of me. What is in front of me: a documented condition, the gap, and a documented non-response to it, in the sense that nothing in the civic record this cycle addresses it. That's a different animal than a scandal. It's a vacancy.

Which raises the plainer question, the one that doesn't need a subpoena to ask. If two servers on opposite ends of the city can name the same failure without coordinating, who's supposed to be the one connecting those dots for a living? That's not an accusation. It's a job description nobody's filling right now, as far as the record shows.

Perez goes back to his tables. Ying goes back to his. Neither one is waiting for me to tell them something's broken — they already know. What's still open is who picks it up, and when.

What is still unfinished for the people living this?
