# Rockridge, in their own words

## Benji Dillon — Pitcher, Oakland A's Legend

> The door's been open for a while now. That's not me being coy—it's me being honest with myself. Vinnie made his call. It was the right one for him. For me, the question isn't whether I can still pitch. It's whether I can pitch in a way that means something. The bullpen stint in C104 was good for me in a way I didn't expect. There's a different kind of clarity that comes when you're not pacing a five-day rhythm. You just... react. You find out what your arm has left when it doesn't have to save anything for the seventh. The numbers aren't what they were. I know that. The velocity is what it is. But I sat with Deacon after the season, and we talked about roles, not results. There's a version of this where I'm a piece instead of the picture. Where I teach Taveras something about reading swings that he can't get from film. Where I'm there on the road when Quintero needs a steady voice. And there's a version where I walk away and the door clicks shut, and that's okay too. Oakland's the only place I'd even have this conversation. That's not sentiment—that's just the truth of it. Some teams want a name on the back of a jersey. The A's want to know what's underneath it. I'm still figuring out what that is.

## Mei Wong — After-school program aide

> In the classroom, a kid can sit still and look like they're following along. In the after-school hours, you see the real picture — who's got energy left, who's running on fumes, who's carrying something from home that has nothing to do with math or reading. When a new kid shows up tired, they don't always say it. They find a corner, they put their head down, or they get a little short with the kids next to them. You learn to read it. You give them a snack and a quiet spot, and you don't push. The classroom has to move forward; we get to wait.

## Wanjiru Liu — Electrician

> To be honest, I'm still getting used to my new place in Rockridge. As an electrician, it's not directly related to where I live, but having a stable home life helps me focus on my work. I've been keeping busy with jobs around the neighborhood and helping out when I can. The Oakland Alternative Response Initiative making waves locally is a good sign too; it feels like the community is coming together. I'm just taking things one day at a time and looking forward to continuing my work as an electrician here.

## Yu Zen — Commercial Insurance Broker

I've been working non-stop, trying to get back on my feet after my accident, and a loss like that doesn't help. It's just another thing to deal with on top of everything else.

## Gloria Liu — School Principal

> When a new student comes in mid-cycle, the first thing I notice is the quiet. Not the building being quiet, but the way the hallways get a little more careful. Teachers glance up a beat longer when they hear footsteps they don't recognize. The office staff moves a little faster. And the child themselves — they're usually trying to take up as little space as possible, which is the opposite of what we want. Getting them caught up isn't about a test or a worksheet. It's about finding one adult in the building who they trust by the end of the first week. That's the whole job. The academics follow once that trust is there. We're lucky in Rockridge because we have the resources to do that well — small classes, aides who know the kids by name, a PTA that shows up. But I've seen it work in schools with a fraction of our funding, and it always comes down to the same thing: one person who says, 'I've got you.' That's what I look for first, and that's what I make sure happens before we even talk about grade-level standards.
