### A Farewell Gone Well

The first thing that jumps out when you look at Benji Dillon’s line from last night’s game is the eight strikeouts in five and a third innings. Two hits allowed, no earned runs. For a pitcher in the twilight of his career, it’s a performance that feels both improbable and inevitable—a veteran who has learned how to outthink hitters even as the velocity on his fastball ticks downward.

Across the diamond, Vinnie Keane went four-for-five with a home run, a double, and three RBIs. At thirty-seven, Keane is not the same player who once stole thirty bases in a season, but last night’s production was a reminder that he can still punish mistakes. His home run in the sixth inning wasn’t a towering shot—it was a line drive that barely cleared the right-field wall—but it came at a moment when the game still felt close.

"I’ve seen veterans like Dillon and Keane pour everything they’ve got into this farewell season, and it’s inspiring," said Isley Kelley, the team's shortstop and a fixture in the Oakland clubhouse. "Their individual brilliance isn’t just about stats—it’s about lifting the team when it matters most."

Mark Aitken, the team’s first baseman, added two home runs and six RBIs of his own, but this game belonged to Dillon and Keane. The A’s won their second consecutive game, improving their record to one hundred twenty-seven wins and thirty-five losses. At this point in the season, they are playing for history, and for each other.

The question hanging over this team is whether this is the best version of the A’s anyone has ever seen, or simply the most timely. Dillon’s eight strikeouts came against a lineup that has struggled against breaking balls all season, and Keane’s home run was his thirteenth of the year—solid but not extraordinary. Still, there’s something about the way these veterans are performing at the end that feels right, as though their career arcs were always meant to converge here.

This is not a team built on youthful exuberance or raw talent. It’s a roster of players who have learned how to win, and who are determined to leave everything on the field. Last night was just another chapter in a season that has already secured its place in the record books, but it was also a reminder of why this team has been so special. Dillon and Keane may not be here next year, but for now, they are exactly where they need to be.
