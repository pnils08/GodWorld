# Oakland Casino Town is down three jobs. The people inside can't agree on who they were.

**Baylight District** — Oakland Casino Town now has 94 employees, three fewer than last cycle. The Business Ledger puts its growth rate at -1.5% a year and its average salary at $70,000. When I asked who the three people were, the casino manager and the bookmaker gave me two different lists.

**Two accounts**

Manager Philly Rodriguez said two of the three worked the graveyard shift, a dealer and a cage cashier. He said the third was a floor supervisor "who'd been here longer than me." In his words, the dealer and the cashier "found other work, better hours." The supervisor "retired early, said he'd had enough of the night life."

Bookmaker Manfred Owens, who keeps the book, described them differently. He said one was a dealer "who'd been here since before I started keeping the book." Another was "a pit boss who could read a table better than anyone I've seen." The third was "a newer hire I only knew by face."

The two accounts agree on a dealer. They don't agree on the rest. Rodriguez names a cashier and a supervisor, and Owens names a pit boss and a new hire. The ledger records only the headcount change. It doesn't say who left or why. I can't tell you from the record whether these are two views of the same three departures or whether the house and the floor are counting differently. I put the question to the casino's ownership and will report what comes back.

**Backfill or contraction**

Rodriguez said the house is "backfilling the dealer and the cashier spots, but the supervisor slot is staying open for now." He said he is covering more of the floor himself and that shifts are "running a little tighter."

Owens said the house "hasn't said much." In his telling, management "moved the schedules around, doubled up some shifts, and told us to keep the ledger tight."

Deputy Mayor for Economic Development Marcus Osei read the ledger differently. "The casino is contracting, not backfilling," he said. "A shrinking operation doesn't refill posts it just cut." He said a real backfill would show up as a headcount that moves and a growth rate that turns, and "right now neither has happened." Osei said his office will check whether the three posts are seasonal or permanent. He would not put a number on it for the council today.

**The trend**

This isn't a one-cycle dip. In Cycle 115 the casino had 99 names on its roster, and now it has 94. That is five fewer in two cycles. A $70,000 average salary across three posts is about $210,000 in annual payroll. That is an average, not what these three actually earned. For scale, the casino's house float stands at $246,000.

The casino is not just a small shop. Osei called its 94 jobs at that average "one of the biggest private payrolls in the Baylight District." The district's median income is $139,000 and its neighborhood trajectory is listed as steady. A district can look steady on income while its largest nightlife employer shrinks.

**The books**

The Casino Ledger for this cycle shows 17 undocked wagers and 14 sports wagers placed. It lists 2 settled and 15 voided. The record doesn't say how those two figures map onto the two wager types, and I won't guess. Owens said he hasn't seen voided wagers "myself" and that "nobody's tearing anything up in front of me." Of the settled bets, Maria Keen staked $385 on an undocked bet and was paid $705. Jax Caldera staked $428 and lost it to the house.

A fifteen-in-seventeen void rate is unusual on its face. Whether it is a bookkeeping artifact or a sign of a thinning operation is a question for the casino. I have asked and will follow up.

**The same week, elsewhere**

Rent math is a short walk from the casino floor. Patrick When has swept Downtown halls for twenty years. He has three kids, and the landlord slipped a rent notice under his door last week. "My check covers the basics," he told me, "but when I wrote out what's left after rent and the light bill and groceries... there wasn't much." He has no tie to the casino. His situation sits next to it because Downtown's median rent is listed at $3,000 against a median income of $123,000, with housing pressure at 10, the top of the scale. A median describes the middle of a neighborhood, not a worker who earns less than the middle.

Osei said the "real damage this week" is Ivy Hill. Ivy Hill Family Dental closed there, and Ivy Hill Market, Ivy Hill Cafe, Ivy Cleaners, Fold Laundry and Hillcrest Kitchen are all at zero staff in the ledger.

**What I'm watching**

1. Whether the headcount at 94 moves next cycle. Rodriguez's backfill claim can be tested against that number.
2. Who the three were. The two accounts need to be reconciled with names and roles.
3. Whether the voided wagers have an explanation on file.

Rodriguez said he doesn't like talking about the cuts, "but it's real." Owens said the room "doesn't feel different" in any way he can chart, and then told me it does.
