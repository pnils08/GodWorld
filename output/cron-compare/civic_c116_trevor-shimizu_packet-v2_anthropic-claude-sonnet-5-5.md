# 12th Street Lost 2,313 Riders. The Roads Didn't Pick Them Up.

Eleanor Rivera, the BART station manager at 12th St Oakland City Center, works from the booth beside the fare gates. She hears a bad cycle before she can count it, because the platform stops filling at an even pace. The station's newest row shows ridership of 12,213 in Cycle 116, down 2,313 from 14,526 in Cycle 115. On-time performance fell from 86 percent to 81. The week was mild, the Spring Equinox carried no ridership cut, and two game days and events in Downtown and Lake Merritt were on the calendar. The row also lists crowded platforms among its factors.

Rivera read the five-point slip as a timing problem, not an exodus.

"A five-point drop on a crowded day doesn't read as people leaving the station," she said. "It reads as people arriving later, all at once. You see the platforms fill up in waves instead of a steady stream, and that's when the dwell time stretches — the trains are there, but everyone's squeezing on at the same time, doors cycle slower, and the whole rhythm backs up." In plain terms, a train that sits longer with its doors open runs late, and a late train strands the next crowd. "The ledger shows the number that got on," she said. "What it doesn't show is the crowd that's still on the platform when the doors close, waiting for the next one. That's not a loss — that's a delay that shows up in the next train's count."

That is a testable claim, and the rows only partly support it. The station's own history shows how wide its swings run. Cycle 111 logged 8,293 riders and Cycle 112 logged 14,162. Cycle 113 fell to 9,341, Cycle 114 came in at 10,011, and Cycle 115 reached 14,526. A station that swings that hard between cycles is hard to read from a single week. The paper's Cycle 111 piece noted that 12th Street ran smoother on 86 percent on-time while the people were elsewhere. This cycle runs the other way. Both numbers fell together.

## The neighbor lost the same amount

19th St Oakland shed 2,251 riders, to 13,825, and its on-time rate slipped three points to 78 percent. Together the two downtown stations lost 4,564. Rockridge lost 1,127, West Oakland 746, MacArthur 462 and Lake Merritt 66. Lake Merritt's on-time rate fell six points to 77 percent, the steepest drop on the board.

Only two stations gained. Coliseum added 1,132, to 8,908, and its on-time rate rose two points to 83 percent. Fruitvale added 400, to 9,343, though its on-time rate fell four points to 83 percent. Those two gains total 1,532. The six losing stations lost 6,965 between them. Across all eight stations on the record, ridership went from 73,938 to 68,505, a net loss of 5,433.

Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for BART's Oakland Division, reads the Coliseum and Fruitvale numbers as geography. "I don't read it as people not wanting to ride," she said of 12th Street. "I read it as people finding another way to get where they're going." She called downtown "a destination, but it's not the only one anymore," and said riders are "moving to the stations that connect to their actual lives."

The arithmetic complicates that. If riders were only relocating, the two gaining stations would have absorbed far more than a fifth of what the others shed. The transfer pattern is not on the record, so no one can say where the rest went.

## Not on the roads, either

Mehta's other point was that congestion is low enough that driving "doesn't feel like a punishment yet." The corridor rows give her no help. All ten corridors fell this cycle: I-880 North by 11, I-980 by 14, Broadway by 13, International Blvd by 11 and Telegraph Ave by 8. Fewer people on the train and fewer on the road is a different pattern from a mode shift. The rows do not say which explanation holds. They do not say whether trips were skipped, moved to other hours or taken some other way.

At Fruitvale, Rosario Medina, the CBA compliance monitor for the Fruitvale Transit Hub, said the visioning phase closing out changed little on her platform. "The riders I see every day are the ones who live here," she said. She described them as people who need the hub "to get to work, to get their kids to school, to get to the doctor." Of the loss downtown, she said: "That's a different population with different options." On her platform, she said, "the woman with three bags of groceries who just made the transfer" and a kid heading to job training at St. Esperanza are who the ledger cannot show.

Her point and Rivera's meet on one side of the system. Fruitvale gained riders and still lost four on-time points, and a station can be growing and straining at once. No chaos car touched the trains this cycle, so the on-time slide at Fruitvale, 12th Street and Lake Merritt has no outside shock behind it.

## What to watch

Rivera's theory has a clear signature. If the missing 12th Street riders are queued behind slow doors, the next cycle's counts should show a rebound and a recovery in on-time performance. If the next row stays low on both, the platform is pushing riders away or the riders have changed their routine. Someone at BART should be able to say which. The record this cycle can't.
