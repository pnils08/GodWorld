**Grand Lake’s Empty Windows**  
The stretch of Grand Avenue near Lake Park has started to look like a mouth missing teeth—dark storefronts where businesses used to be, some with paper still taped over the windows from whatever last left them. No "For Lease" signs, just blank glass.  

"I walk through Grand Lake sometimes to clear my head between bakes," says Melton Neilon, a sourdough baker based in Temescal. "It's hard not to notice how many storefronts just stay empty now—not even 'for lease' signs, just dark windows. Feels like the whole block's holding its breath waiting for something to change, but nobody's stepping up to make it happen."  

Rick Walker, a line cook from Fruitvale, echoes the unease: "I’ve heard folks talking about Grand Lake losing its spark, and it’s sad to think about those empty storefronts just sitting there, waiting for something to happen. It feels like no one’s stepping up to fix it, and that’s hard to watch."  

The question isn’t just why the slide started, but what comes next. Without an anchor business or development initiative to reverse the trend, does Grand Lake’s retail strip quietly hollow out—or is this the lull before an unannounced pivot?
