**West Oakland's Stabilization Fund: Money Moving, Questions Lingering**  

Elio Perez wiped down the counter at Jimmy’s Diner for the third time in ten minutes, the rag moving in tight circles over laminated wood. At the booth nearest the register, two construction workers debated the West Oakland Stabilization Fund—who got in early, who was still waiting. Perez caught phrases between coffee refills: *Saw the checks go out last week* and *Didn’t even know you could apply*.  

The fund, now in active disbursement phase per the city tracker, exists to shore up small businesses and housing cooperatives in West Oakland. Approved last cycle through a council vote (9-0, no abstentions), its dollars are meant to flow quickly—but speed doesn’t guarantee visibility.  

“I hear people at the diner talking about that fund all the time—who’s getting help, who’s not,” Perez said. “Feels like we should know where every dollar’s going, you know? Money’s tight for everyone around here.”  

Down Seventh Street, Daniel Cloak layered dough at Adeline Bakery. His hands worked without pause. “I’ve heard folks at the bakery talking about that fund, but honestly, I just keep my head down and focus on my work,” he said. “If the money’s helping the neighborhood, that’s good enough for me.” The distinction between *helping* and *being seen to help* went unspoken.  

At Perch Coffee, Irene Fay steamed milk for a latte. The espresso machine’s hiss cut through her reply: “I’ve heard people talking about the Stabilization Fund, but honestly, I don’t know much about where the money’s going or how it’s being used. It’d be good to see more clarity on that.”  

Clarity—not just disbursement—is the unmet metric. The fund’s administration falls under the Office of Economic Development, which has not released a public ledger of recipients or amounts. No deadline exists for such disclosure.  

The question isn’t whether the money is moving. It’s whether anyone can follow its trail.
