# Dimond's Cooling Corridor: Where Did the Foot Traffic Go?

The stools at the lunch counter near Dimond's Fruitvale Avenue intersection stayed empty through the noon rush last Tuesday—a sight Melton Neilon, a sourdough baker from Temescal who occasionally supplies bread to the area, called uncharacteristic. "I hate to hear that about Dimond—it's always had such a good energy," Neilon said, kneading dough in his bakery kitchen. "The Tribune should ask what's drawing people away, whether it's rents or just shifting tastes."

Walk down MacArthur Boulevard this week and you'll notice it too—fewer shoppers ducking into storefronts, more cafes running at half-capacity even during prime hours. While neighborhood economic trends are rarely uniform, the slowdown appears concentrated along Dimond's eastern commercial strips rather than the Laurel district corridors to the north.

"I hear folks talking about Dimond slowing down, but I'm over in Fruitvale most days—can't say I've seen it myself," said Rick Walker, a line cook whose work keeps him anchored further south. His question lingers: "If the Tribune's digging into neighborhoods cooling off, they oughta ask where everybody's going instead."

The shift leaves two unresolved questions hanging over Dimond's storefronts: Is this a temporary dip or the leading edge of a more sustained departure? And if people are spending less time and money here—where, exactly, are they spending it instead?
