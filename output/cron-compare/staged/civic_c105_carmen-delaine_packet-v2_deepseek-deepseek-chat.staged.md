**The Shadows of What’s Promised: West Oakland Stabilization Fund Enters Disbursement Phase**  
By Carmen Delaine  

Elio Perez wiped down the last table at the end of his shift, his movements slow, deliberate. It was late, and the diner on Seventh Street was almost empty. Outside, the streetlights cast long shadows over cracked sidewalks. Perez had heard the talk — the Stabilization Fund was supposed to help, was supposed to mean something for families like his. But here, in this quiet hour, the diner felt the same as it always did.  

“I’ve heard folks talking about the Stabilization Fund,” Perez said, folding the rag in his hand. “And honestly, I’m just hoping it reaches the people who really need it — families like mine who’ve been holding on tight.”  

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund, passed earlier this cycle, has now entered its disbursement-active phase. The Initiative Tracker confirms the shift, marking the fund’s transition from planning to action. Yet, on the ground, the promise of stabilization feels distant.  

At the bakery on Peralta Street, Daniel Cloak slid loaves of bread into the oven, his hands dusted with flour. “I’ve heard folks at the bakery talking about the Stabilization Fund like it’s supposed to help,” he said. “But honestly, I don’t see much changing on my block yet. People still struggling same as before.”  

The fund, designed to inject resources into West Oakland’s most vulnerable neighborhoods, was heralded as a turning point. But the gap between policy and practice remains stark. Irene Fay, a barista at a coffee shop on Mandela Parkway, echoed the sentiment. “I’ve heard people talking about the Stabilization Fund at the coffee shop,” she said, steaming milk for a latte. “Mostly wondering how it’s going to help the neighborhood directly.”  

At another bakery on Adeline Street, Darius Clark wiped down the counter, his apron streaked with dough. “I’ve heard folks talking about the Stabilization Fund at the bakery,” he said. “But I haven’t seen much change around here yet.”  

The fund’s disbursement phase is active, but the question lingers: Where is the money going, and when will it reach the streets? The Initiative Tracker provides no specifics on disbursement timelines or recipients, leaving residents like Perez, Cloak, Fay, and Clark to wait and wonder.  

For now, West Oakland remains a place of shadows and quiet persistence. The Stabilization Fund’s promise hangs in the air, tangible yet elusive. The clock is ticking, but the streets haven’t changed.  

When will they?
