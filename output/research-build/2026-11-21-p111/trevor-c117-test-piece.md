# 12th Street ran the system's worst on-time record on its biggest crowd. The crowd doesn't explain all of it.

**By Trevor Shimizu, Infrastructure & Transit**

12th St Oakland City Center carried 14,175 riders this cycle, up 1,962 (about 16%) from 12,213. Its on-time running fell four points to 77%, the lowest of the eight stations on the record. 19th St Oakland carried a similar crowd, 13,856 riders, and ran 81%. That is three points better than last cycle.

BART's people say crowding stacked up at 12th Street. The sheet supports part of that. It does not support all of it.

### What the record says

12th Street's row lists these causes: breeze, a game day at Downtown and Jack London, events in Lake Merritt and West Oakland, the Baylight District final council vote (now construction-active), and crowded platforms at a citywide transit congestion of 0.14.

The Downtown Transit Snarl ended this week after four weeks. This is the first reading since it cleared, and 12th Street still came in last.

Elena Soria Dominguez, the Transit Hub Planning Lead, read me the row and was careful about it. Crowding, she said, is "the most defensible cause on the sheet." But the 0.14 congestion figure "appears on every station's row — so crowding is a citywide condition this cycle, not a 12th Street special." She also said she'd want "more than one cycle before I put a number on how much is crowding versus how much is the 12th Street event load itself."

### The comparison that doesn't close

Eleanor Rivera, the BART station manager at 12th, put it this way: "it's not the doors, it's the platform." She said game-day mornings bring the Downtown and Jack London crowds in at once, with Baylight rerouting on top. "19th Street has a similar crowd, sure, but they don't have the same mix of events landing on them at once."

The rows show a narrower difference than that. 19th Street also carries the Downtown game day, the Baylight construction flag, and events in Lake Merritt and Temescal. What 12th Street has that 19th doesn't is the Jack London game-day tag and the West Oakland events.

West Oakland has both of those tags plus Baylight. It ran 86%, up seven points, on 9,100 riders. That is the best on-time figure in the system. So "game day plus Baylight" doesn't by itself produce a bad platform. Soria Dominguez pointed at the same gap: "Those stations didn't carry the same crowd. That gap is the part I'd watch."

Ximena Mehta, BART's transit PR contact, offered another explanation. 12th is "the transfer hub — everyone's changing trains there, not just getting on and off." That is plausible, and it fits how dwell time works. A train held at a platform for transferring passengers loses seconds at every stop, and those seconds carry down the line. But it is a characterization, not a logged cause. Nothing on the row says "transfer volume." I'd want BART's dwell-time data before I'd print it as the reason.

The station-level arithmetic shows how lopsided the week was. Systemwide ridership rose 2,585 to 71,090 (from 68,505). 12th Street supplied 1,962 of that gain, about three-quarters of it. 19th Street added 31.

AC Transit operator Coen Cartier described the pattern from the bus bays. Downtown crowds at 12th "come in waves — you get that big push before and after, then it clears out. 19th Street feels different. It's steadier." That is one driver's observation, not a measurement. It fits a station that is doing fine most of the day and failing in the surge. Daily averages won't show that. Hourly platform data would.

- **Rockridge** gained about 30%, the biggest percentage jump on the board. Its row cites events in Rockridge and on Piedmont Ave. On-time didn't move. Soria Dominguez called it "an event-driven spike at a station with a small base," and said she has no structural story for it.
- **Fruitvale** lost 1,336 riders, about 14%, while on-time improved two points to 85%. The Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II visioning stage closed this week and moved to design-phase. The planning lead said the dip proves nothing: "One cycle is one cycle." She also said that last cycle's ridership gain at Fruitvale, which she had read as life, not a drawing, now cuts the other way.
- **Hub status:** Further design advancement is held. Preliminary schematics and a documented Fruitvale public comment-and-response record are not in the record. There were no payments on INIT-003 this cycle. Both deliverables are scheduled for Cycle 118. I'll check then.
- **The BART train on the line** this cycle was a clean run at Lake Merritt, with zero logged delay. It should not show up on next cycle's rows.

### Corridors: a flag I can't close

I-880 North and I-980 both hit an index of 100, up 11 and 14. Both rows cite game-day crowds and Baylight construction, so those moves have a stated cause.

International Blvd rose 11 points to 84 with no cause on its row beyond the breeze and the ledger base. MacArthur Blvd rose 7 to 77 and has the same blank. The ordinary-weekday frame says a mover past the citywide rule names its own reason. These two don't name one. The record doesn't say whether that means no event, a missed event, or a gap in what gets logged. I'm asking.

### What to watch

1. **12th Street's next on-time reading.** If it stays below 81% with no game on the calendar, the explanation moves from event load to something structural about the platform. Soria Dominguez said the same.
2. **Hourly data.** The daily average hides the surge pattern that Rivera and Cartier both describe. BART has the dwell and headway numbers. We should ask for them.
3. **Cycle 118** for the Fruitvale schematics and comment record.

Joel Wren, an AC Transit mechanic, said it plainly from the maintenance side: "you don't wait for a problem to show up before you fix it." A station that posts the lowest on-time on the sheet is a problem showing up.
