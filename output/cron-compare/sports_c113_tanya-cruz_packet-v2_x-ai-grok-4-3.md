In the clubhouse the conversation circles back to the same arithmetic: five outfielders for three spots next season. Vladimir Gonzalez stands at his locker, towel over one shoulder, and keeps his voice low enough that only the guys nearby hear him. "Honestly, the vibe is a lot less tense than you'd think. We all know the business side of it — somebody's gonna get moved or sent down, that's just how it goes. What I tell the young guys is you can't control what Paulson decides. You just control how you show up. And truth be told, it's a good problem for the A's to have. Means we got depth, and that's how you win. I earned my spot, sure, but I remember what it was like trying to crack a crowded room. You just hope the decision gets made quick so whoever it is lands somewhere they can play."

Desmond Morton and Frank Reyna are both still in the room, stretching, neither one pushing the point. The whiteboard at the far end has the names listed but nothing circled. What I still need is a minute with Paulson after a workout — just to see which name he glances at when he thinks nobody's watching.
