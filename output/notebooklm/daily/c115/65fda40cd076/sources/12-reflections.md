# Fruitvale, in their own words

## Sonia Parikh — Podcast Host / Software Engineer

> Beatrix and I—we still text about nothing, meme links and shitty weather, but it’s been months since we actually sat down. She’s got her own life now, her own orbit, and I keep telling myself that’s fine, that’s adult friendship. But there’s this quiet thing where I don’t know if we’d know what to do with each other if we were in the same room, and that scares me a little. Jessica’s different—she’s the one I call when I’m arguing with a Tribune column in my kitchen, because she gets that I need to yell it out. But even she’s been distant lately, like we’re both waiting for the other to say something real first. I think we’re okay, but I’m not sure “okay” is the same as “close” anymore.

> Jess! Hey, it’s been too long. I swear I was just thinking about you the other day—I saw that meme you sent me and laughed way harder than I should have. How’ve you been? That does sound peaceful, honestly. Sometimes the slow stuff is the stuff you remember. I’ve been painting over graffiti with a few neighbors—barely knew them before, but now we’re on first-name basis. It’s a weird way to make friends, but it works. Oh, tell Camila I miss her too—and I've got a new one about the clothing drive. I recognized a coat I donated two years ago, and it just made me laugh, like the neighborhood keeps things moving in circles. You should bring her by the Hub sometime; I’ll buy her a horchata and we’ll catch up properly. *(talking with Jessica Brooks)*

## Brooklyn Nguyen — Community Programs Coordinator

> It's the little things, honestly. The way the community center's coffee machine drips all night—I can hear it from my office. I know it's just a coffee machine. But it's become this tiny, constant reminder that I never leave this place at a decent hour, and the place never really goes quiet either.

## Rico Valez — Musician

> As a musician living in Fruitvale, I've noticed the energy around Oakland's cultural scene is buzzing. It's cool to see people excited about what's happening here. I'm not sure how it's directly affecting my work, but it feels like there's a good vibe. I've been playing gigs when I can, and it's great to see the community coming out to support local artists. The Fruitvale Community Bloom Festival is a great example of this - it draws crowds to the neighborhood and shows the beauty of what we're doing here.
