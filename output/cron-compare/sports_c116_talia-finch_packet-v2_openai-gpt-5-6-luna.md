## SPORTS

Mike Paulson is in West Oakland, where the Oaks have made a habit of turning doubt into a conversation that keeps going after the final horn. He describes the change plainly: “As a resident of West Oakland, I've seen the mood around the Oaks shift dramatically. The team was 0-9 to start, and now they're 8-1.”

That was before San Antonio came to Oakland and handed the Oaks their first home loss, 126-117. The disappointment has not erased the earlier rush. It has made the new fans more watchful.

I can feel that distinction in the way people talk about this team. They are no longer asking whether an expansion club belongs in the room. They are asking whether the first nine losses were a plan, whether the defense is real, and whether Wilson Shepard’s offense has another gear when the game stops being easy.

Shepard told me the team had found its rhythm. “The team's performance so far has been great, we're 8-1 and the guys are playing with confidence. I think we've found our rhythm, and it's a testament to the hard work they've been putting in. The offense is clicking, and we're getting good contributions from everyone. Of course, we had a hiccup against San Antonio, but that's to be expected. We're still a young team, and we're learning as we go. Overall, I'm proud of how we're performing, and I'm excited to see how we'll do as the season progresses.”

The word that keeps coming back is confidence. Not certainty. Confidence is what you have when you are still willing to explain the loss.

Elliot Abraham said the same thing from the other side of the new attention: “I've seen the fans are excited, and it's infectious. The team's turnaround has really brought them out, and you can feel the energy. We're 8-1 now after a tough start, and it's like a different team. The confidence in the room is palpable, and it's translating to the stands.”

The Oaks later rose to No. 1 in the NBA.com power rankings, then fell to 9-2 after the San Antonio game. In between, they went to Denver and won 147-114, shooting 80 percent and setting a franchise scoring record. Those are facts people can repeat. The harder thing to name is what happens when the first home loss arrives and the room does not empty of belief.

Melton Neilon, a sourdough baker, gave me the less tidy version of that feeling: “To be honest, Mark Aitken leaving still has me a bit down. I keep thinking about what it means for the A's and the community. It's not just about the player; it's about what he represents. I try not to overthink it, but it's hard not to feel a little empty knowing he's gone.”

That sadness sits beside the Oaks’ excitement, not beneath it. A new team can be winning and still be asking for a place in people’s loyalties. In West Oakland, the answer is not finished. It is being made in the pause after a loss, when somebody says they will come back next time.
