**Diego Remo:** "Tom, long time no see. Funny how Temescal keeps us crossing paths, huh? Still running that gallery over in Rockridge?" *I fold my arms, leaning slightly on the bookstore doorframe.*

**Tom Jilner:** *Sipping my coffee, I raise an eyebrow at Diego* "Still got that knack for bringing up old news, huh? The gallery closed two years back—running a wine bar now, over on College. More stable hours for Mateo's little league games." *Gestures vaguely toward the eviction notice down the block* "Unlike some spots around here."

**Diego Remo:** "Huh, wine bar. That tracks." *I glance at the notice, then back at Tom* "Shame about places closing, but some of us are holding strong. The bookstore's been steady—neighborhood likes having a place that sticks around. How's Mateo handling Temescal these days?"

**Tom Jilner:** *Tugs at my suit sleeve absentmindedly* "Kid's tougher than I was at his age—tells the A's stats like scripture since they won the Series. Though between the cranes sprouting up and..." *jerks chin toward the rec center* "...the nightly taggers, I miss when this block just worried about parking tickets."

**Diego Remo:** "Yeah, change hits us all." *I shift my weight, glancing toward the rec center* "But it's still ours, Tom. Taggers come and go, but the heart of Temescal? That stays. Mateo’s lucky to grow up here—there’s history in these streets." *Pauses briefly* "You bring him by Da Dough yet? Melton’s sourdough might just convert him."

**Tom Jilner:** *Snorts, pulling out my phone to check the time* "Doubt it—kid’s got his mother’s sweet tooth. But speaking of history..." *gestures toward Benji Dillon’s mural near the BART station* "That’s the real education. Took Mateo there after game five, told him that’s what Oakland looks like when we show up for each other." *Shuts off the screen* "Even if the city keeps trying to price us out."
