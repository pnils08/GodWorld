#### 2.1 Anthony Raines Feature Analysis — Eric Taveras Undocked Pledge

**Player Contract & Bio**
Eric Taveras, 25, is entering the first season of a $32.1M contract with the Oakland A's. Coming off a career-defining campaign, Taveras hit 54 home runs and swept individual honors as both Postseason MVP and World Series MVP.

**Undocked Competition Data**
Beside his MLB record, Taveras's *Undocked* competition line currently stands at: 3 episodes, 20 points (5th place), and 1,170 credits (1st place).
* **Standings Discrepancy:** The official *Undocked* leaderboards sort strictly by total points, placing Taveras fifth behind overall leader Ronald Silk (38.15 points over 2 episodes). 
* **Credit Accumulation:** Conversely, Taveras holds the top spot in credits (1,170 credits over 3 episodes), accumulating nearly four times the volume of second-place Clarissa Dane, who sits at 10 points and 317 credits over 9 episodes. Dane represents the sole contestant on the board with high episode volume.
* **Historical Tracking:** Prior *Tribune* logging confirms Taveras secured 1,170 credits across two flights in Cycle 111 and held the #1 overall point ranking during Cycles 112 and 113. He dropped to fifth place in Cycles 114 and 115 due to small-sample point volatility.

**Pledge Details**
During Cycle 112, Taveras posted on Moltbook that all financial winnings from his *Undocked* appearances would be donated to the Vinnie Keane Firehouse 29 West Oakland Baseball Academy—a free program on Mandela Parkway operating out of a rehabbed firehouse under Keane's foundation. With Keane's formal retirement, Taveras's pledge symbolically links the franchise's incoming star to its outgoing icon. However, as of Cycle 115, zero prize funds have been disbursed to the academy.

**Analytical Evaluation & Gate Soundness**
Desk review confirms the analytical integrity of Anthony Raines's piece. Raines demonstrates that Taveras's pledge is conditional on unstated show mechanics. Producer SpaceMolt has not announced whether prize payouts are tied to point rankings or credit totals, nor has it released an official payout schedule. Because Taveras leads in credits but ranks fifth in points, the true financial yield of his pledge cannot be calculated. Raines's conclusion—that the record does not support calling the pledge broken because no money has yet been awarded or withheld—is verified as factually and contextually sound.
