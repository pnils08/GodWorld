### I. The Street Below the Sill

At dawn in Chinatown, the morning does not arrive with the rush of commuter traffic or the distant horn of a container crane down at the Port; it begins with the metallic rattle of scissor-gates rolling upward along 8th and Webster. Steam from morning bao trays bleeds into the cool coastal air, mingling with the bitter, herbal perfume of dried roots airing on wooden trays outside the corner apothecary. 

Hal Richmond (POP-00007) knows the sequence by heart. At sixty-two years old, with thirty-one years on the masthead at the *Bay Tribune* (BIZ-00018) and forty-four years chronicling Oakland baseball in the city’s collective ledger, Hal’s internal clock has been calibrated by deadlines and box scores. Yet his mornings belong entirely to Chinatown. 

While the new money of the prosperity boom engineered glass towers in West Oakland around Civis Systems and broke ground on the sprawling $2.1 billion Baylight District build at the sixty-five-acre former Coliseum site, Chinatown refused to be redesigned. It remained dense, multigenerational, and fiercely rooted. Hal’s household (HH-0104-F001) sits right in the heart of this enduring enclave. He lives well—his senior historian role earns a steady $300,000 annual income, underpinning a net worth of $7,001,534 built over decades of disciplined equity and master’s-level craft—yet his life is anchored to the neighborhood’s steady, unhurried cadence.

Later that afternoon, walking back from the grocer, he caught himself thinking of an old friend who had covered the city hall beat in the late nineties, wondering where the decades had slipped.
