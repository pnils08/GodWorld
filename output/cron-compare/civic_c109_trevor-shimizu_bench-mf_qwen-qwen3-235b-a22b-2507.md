At 12th St Oakland City Center, the morning rush didn’t end at 9:03. It bled into noon, then pooled again by 4:17. The fare gates clicked in near-constant rhythm—*chick, chick, chick*—a metronome set to double time. Eleanor Rivera stood at the mezzanine corner where the BART stairs meet the AC Transit bus loop, arms folded, watching the flow. Her station had seen a surge that wasn’t just data but footsteps, breath fog on glass, backpacks brushing railings worn thin.

At 12th Street, we've definitely felt the uptick in ridership. It's not just numbers - it's people adjusting their routines, and us adjusting right along with them. The team just... adapts. No big announcements, just everyone doing their part to keep things moving smoothly.

The pattern held across multiple stations and corridors. At Coliseum, the concourse hummed under stadium lights even on non-game days. Fruitvale and MacArthur both registered sustained increases in foot traffic. Even Rockridge, without a game-day spike, showed heavier use—though its on-time performance dipped slightly.

Jerald Jensen, janitor for AC Transit, mopped the same stretch outside 12th Street’s outbound doors three times by 11:15. You wanna know about 12th Street? I see it in the wear and tear—more coffee spills to mop up, more trash cans overflowing by noon. Used to be you could catch your breath between rushes, but now? It’s constant. Folks pouring in for work, for games, for whatever's happening downtown.

The buses themselves showed strain and resilience. Joel Wren, a mechanic based at the East Oakland yard, ran diagnostics on three out of six inbound 57-series coaches during his shift. You know, I've been seeing more folks hopping on the buses lately—especially down at 12th Street. It's good to see people out and about, trusting the system again. But what really gets me is the crew keeping those buses running. Those mechanics and drivers? They're the ones putting in the long hours to make sure everyone gets where they need to go, especially with all these extra riders. Makes you appreciate the work that goes on behind the scenes.

Coen Cartier, pulling an afternoon loop from 12th to MacArthur, confirmed it from the driver’s seat. I’ve seen more folks hopping on the buses lately, especially around 12th Street. It’s been busy, but it feels good to know we’re helping people get where they need to go. The team’s been working hard to keep things running smoothly, and it’s paying off.

On-time performance improved at every major station except Rockridge. 12th Street climbed. 19th Street reached 86%. Coliseum hit 83%. Yet the traffic index told a different story: I-880 South rose, Telegraph Avenue up sharply, I-980 marginally. Downtown corridors absorbed pressure while surface routes stayed congested.

No new service was added. No fleet expansion logged. The increase came from existing infrastructure, staffed by the same crews, operating within the same thresholds. The Baylight District’s construction phase continues. Events in Downtown, Lake Merritt, Temescal, and East Oakland drew crowds. Riders responded. Systems held—for now.
