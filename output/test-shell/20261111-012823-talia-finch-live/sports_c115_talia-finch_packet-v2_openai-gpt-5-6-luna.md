# The Oaks Have KONO Listening for the Next Sound

Trenton Nawan is wiping a glass at the counter in KONO when the room breaks into the same argument it has been having since the Oaks stopped making losing look inevitable: Is this the beginning, or merely the part before everybody decides?

The screen above him is carrying the replay without sound. Dillon Brooks rises into another three from the Oaks’ 101-95 NBA Cup win over the Oklahoma City Thunder, and the people nearest the counter lean toward it as if the replay might answer the question. The Oaks led after every quarter, but never by enough to let anyone relax. Brooks’ six threes kept the game at arm’s length. The home crowd had been sold out. The room here feels like it is still inside that building.

Nawan checks his phone, then sets it face down.

“I’m still feeling pretty good about the Oaks,” he says. “That 4-0 start has really got me hopeful. I checked the scores twice a day all week and was happy every time they won. Maybe they’re finally getting their act together.”

The record has moved since that earlier hope. The Oaks are now 6-1, with two victories in a row, and their first Cup game is in the room as more than a result. It is a test people watched together, close enough that every Brooks jumper felt necessary and every late possession made the glasses stop moving.

A stranger at the end of the counter points at the screen when Adash Stanley appears in the replay. Someone says Stanley’s six turnovers do not matter tonight. Someone else says they matter exactly because this one stayed close. Nobody says the Oaks have arrived. That would be too clean. But nobody is looking away, either.

Stanley has a name for the feeling that has followed the team into KONO.

“The mood among fans is electric,” he says. “I’ve felt the excitement building around the Oaks, it’s like the whole city is waking up with the team. There’s a buzz in the air, and I think people are really looking forward to seeing what they can do this season.”

At the counter, that sentence lands less like a slogan than a dare. The people here are not behaving like a settled audience. They are checking the next possession, the next game, the next reason to come back.

Trevor Henderson described the Cup opener from the other side of the noise.

“I’ve seen the energy around the Oaks lately, and it’s electric,” he says. “The crowd was locked-in during the NBA Cup opener against OKC, and you could feel the excitement. As a player, it’s motivating to see the fans so engaged. We’re on a two-game win streak, and the vibe is great.”

The screen goes black between replay segments. For a moment, the counter is only glass, elbows and the low mechanical hum from behind the bar. Then somebody asks when the next game starts.

No one answers immediately. They are still watching the dark screen.
