# The Oaks’ 5-1 start now has two very different readings

**Selena Grant | Bay Tribune Sports**

The Oaks’ 104-100 loss to the Pacers ended a perfect start, even after Oakland erased a 20-point deficit. One game later, the Oaks answered with a 135-75 rout of the Raptors. The record is 5-1, with a one-game winning streak, but the back-to-back results expose the central question of an expansion build: how much of the early success is repeatable, and how much depends on the game breaking their way?

The Indiana loss was not an empty performance. Adash Stanley scored 19 points, collected five rebounds and four assists, and made five of 11 three-point attempts. Austin Reaves also scored 19, adding six rebounds and seven assists without a turnover. AJ Dybantsa had 15 points and five rebounds, while Wendell Carter Jr. went 5-for-6 from the field for 10 points and seven rebounds. The Oaks came back from 20 down, but the recovery stopped short of a win.

That distinction matters. A comeback can show that a roster has enough creation to repair a bad start. It does not establish that the same roster can avoid the bad start. The record supplies evidence of resilience in Indiana, not an explanation for the 20-point hole.

Toronto supplied the opposite kind of evidence. Reaves scored 20 points on 8-for-16 shooting, including four of six from three-point range. T. Jerome posted 19 points, seven rebounds and seven assists. Stanley added 16 points, seven rebounds and six assists while making all four of his three-point attempts. Carter finished with 14 points and 15 rebounds, including five offensive rebounds, and Dybantsa scored 12 points on 5-for-8 shooting.

The difference between the games is too large to treat as a simple continuation of form. The Oaks’ top contributors were productive in both results, but Toronto brought a broader and more efficient statistical profile. The first game required recovery; the second never became competitive. Neither result, by itself, identifies which version is the foundation.

The clearest development line belongs to Harry Hayward. The second-round rookie scored eight points, grabbed four rebounds and made three of four shots, including both of his three-point attempts, in 13 minutes. That is a small workload, but it is a concrete contribution in a team whose franchise stability is listed as growing. For an expansion club, a useful rotation piece is not decoration; it is part of the answer to how the roster survives uneven nights.

The Oaks now have a 5-1 record and the league’s most recent evidence is a 60-point win. The front office still owes the harder answer: what factors produced the swing from a 20-point deficit and a four-point loss to a 60-point rout, and which number should define the build—the recovery, the margin, or the rotation that made both possible?
