# The Small Adjustments

Drew Lemon paused by the hydration station outside the downtown co-op, refilling his water bottle for the third time that afternoon. The gesture had become automatic this season—a minor ritual against the dry air that left his throat scratchy and his skin tight. He capped the bottle and pocketed it, then checked the time on his wrist display. Still early enough to make rounds.

The hydration stations themselves were unremarkable—standard issue filters bolted to brick walls—but their placement told a story. One by the pharmacy. Another near the community bulletin board where job listings and event notices overlapped in layers. A third tucked between the ramp access and the bike racks. Practical nodes in daily paths, not waiting for crisis to make them necessary.

Jessie Hess, watching the flow of people through the co-op’s courtyard, nodded toward Lemon’s bottle. "I’ve noticed how people like Drew Lemon adapt to seasonal health concerns—it’s all about small, practical changes, like staying hydrated and keeping an eye on each other."

Lemon tapped his bottle against the station’s sensor, logging the refill out of habit more than requirement. The system didn’t mandate it, but he’d gotten into the rhythm of tracking.

How long before these small adaptations became as unremarkable as the stations themselves?
