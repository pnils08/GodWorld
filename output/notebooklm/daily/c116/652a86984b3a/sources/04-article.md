# The Lives Page, Cycle 116

By Tamsin Pruitt, The Bay Tribune

**Edmund Alegero** moved into a place of his own in Lake Merritt this cycle. It is a rental, the first address in the record with only his name on it. Alegero is a construction laborer, self-employed. Around the same time, the record shows, he bought a new A's jersey. He also heard a bassline pass in a car and let it go by.

He said the move matters. "Getting my own place in Lake Merritt is a big deal for me. It's a step up, you know? I've been working hard, and it's nice to have a spot that's just mine."

The record bears that out. In C111 the ledger shows the last of his debts cleared, and it reads clean. In C115 he walked past the window of the UNDOCKED parlor twice before going in, then acted like it was nothing. This cycle he read the Tribune's front page twice, once for the news and once to see who was in it. He also disagreed with a Tribune column out loud, to an empty kitchen. Before the move, that kitchen was someone else's.

He noticed younger people asking him for advice now. He did not say what he tells them. He did name what's weighing on him in the A's news: "it's tough to see Mark Aitken leave. He's from around here, and he's always been involved in the community. But I'm trying to focus on my own game, you know? I've got my own things to work on."

**Wanjiru Liu** moved into a rental of their own in Rockridge. Liu is an electrician, entry-level. The record has the last two cycles in order. In C115 Liu walked across the stage at a commencement ceremony. The same cycle's record has Liu pulling copper wire through a Baylight sub-basement until their shoulders burned. This page named Liu in C115, and the household entry is the next line the record kept.

Liu was plain about it. "To be honest, I'm still getting used to my new place in Rockridge. As an electrician, it's not directly related to where I live, but having a stable home life helps me focus on my work."

This cycle Liu also helped a neighbor through the West Oakland Stabilization Fund portal, because the interface was confusing. Another line has Liu not having really talked to Rick Dillon in a while. The record gives no reason, and this page won't supply one. Liu said they are taking it "one day at a time."

**Drago Helio**, a home health aide in Brooklyn, won on the UNDOCKED book this cycle. He staked 460 and was paid 842.

The record says the week changed color. The ledger has him pricing out a bigger apartment in C109 and deciding to wait another year. It does not say what the money is for, and he did not say.

What he did say is where it went first. "The win on the UNDOCKED book put a little extra cash in my pocket, and it was nice to share the story with my family over dinner. We were all talking about our weeks, and it was a good night. It didn't change my outlook, but it was a welcome surprise."

That long dinner is in the record, with everyone's week laid on the table. So is a quieter line from the same cycle. Near a quiet side street, he caught himself lowering his voice when he talked about the block's future. The two lines sit side by side and the record does not reconcile them. His outlook is unchanged, he says, and the block's future still gets the lower voice.

**Yu Zen**, a commercial insurance broker in Rockridge, lost on the UNDOCKED book. Zen staked 372 and the book kept it.

The paper's C112 coverage had Zen moving carefully after an accident, and the C107 coverage had Zen in a Rockridge hospital bed. Zen's thread this cycle is work. The record says Zen worked through another weekend and called it fine. That same line is in the ledger from C109. In C111 there was a third late night in a week, and the ceiling again at four.

"To be honest, losing on the UNDOCKED book stung," Zen said. "It's not just about the money; it's the frustration. I've been working non-stop, trying to get back on my feet after my accident, and a loss like that doesn't help. It's just another thing to deal with on top of everything else."

"Called it fine" and "stung" are two entries for the same weekend. The record keeps both.

**Shawn Nguyen** set up the chairs in the fellowship hall in Fruitvale, because if he didn't do it, no one would. The record counts him among the people the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II work reached this cycle. In C115 he drove the whole way home without remembering a single turn. This cycle he remembered a teacher who had said one thing that stuck. He also watched an Oakland Alternative Response Initiative team de-escalate a situation quietly on the corner. The paper named him in C84 and C87, in pieces on Baylight construction and hiring. The record does not say what the teacher said.

**Davey Martinez** in Temescal answered "how's it going" the exact same way for the fifth time this week. The record counts him among the people the Temescal Community Health Center's opening reached. In C115 he noticed a familiar face missing from his morning routine. This cycle he built a new casual acquaintance. He also let the phone ring twice before answering, so it wouldn't seem like he'd been waiting. The record does not say whose call it was.

**Tanisha Freeman**, in Grand Lake, attended a community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II and actually felt heard. In C115 she helped a neighbor through the West Oakland Stabilization Fund portal. A seniors lunch program flyer was on a bulletin board. The paper named her on the C114 Lives page.

**Mesi Westin** moved from Jack London to KONO this cycle, for a better fit between household income and housing costs. In the same cycle the record has Westin reading the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline and wondering who was actually going to get those spots. Westin also grabbed coffee from the cart outside the hospital before the morning rush, and found a favorite song on a jukebox at a ferry-adjacent corner.

**Adash Stanley** moved up from Downtown to Piedmont Ave. In C115 Stanley pulled an all-nighter for a midterm and swore it was the last one. This cycle came a win. Stanley drove home with the radio off and then paced the kitchen. Stanley told the story of the moment three times at dinner, and it got better each time. Stanley also stayed up rewatching two plays that will not matter to anyone else. The paper's C115 Sports pages named Stanley beside Harry Hayward's 22 minutes. The record does not say which game it was.

**Grace Oyelaran** in West Oakland got a street-sweeper parking ticket. She woke at 5 AM for thirty minutes of total silence before the house woke up. The record also has her realizing the week had passed without a single thing worth retelling. In C115 an old photo album surfaced and the evening went where it wanted. She also learned a useless, wonderful fact and told two people near a student cafe.

**Joaquín Nakamura** in Uptown got a ticket from a cop car. This cycle he compared rent notes with a neighbor over the fence. He memorized the Oaks' starting rotation and tested himself on the train ride home, near a gallery opening. He bought a new A's jersey near a street mural. In C115 he felt a lift at Pulse District after the win. The paper named him in C101, in its Business coverage of the retail slide.

The record is silent on what Edmund Alegero will put on the walls of his first place, and on whether Liu has made the move permanent. It does not say whether Helio's winnings went toward the bigger apartment, or whether Zen got a night's sleep.
