# The Book Keeps What the Rumor Cannot

Two men on the Athletics' ledger list Jack London as home. One is a first baseman, born 2009, whom the ledger carries as an A's legend, Player Rep and Community Liaison. The other is a third baseman, born 2021, whom the ledger carries as early-career. This cycle the A's line in the off-season feed reads one word: rumor. It names no man, no deal and no date. Mark Aitken and Kevin Clark are the two names the desk set beside it, and the feed does not say the rumor concerns either of them.

So the record stays plain. A rumor is weather, and the season lines are the ground.

## The Elder

The ledger gives Aitken's origin city as California and nothing finer. A search of the Archive returned his ledger line and his seasons, not the pages of his boyhood, and I will not furnish a town or a field the record has not handed me. The seasons are all there.

His dossier opens in 2031 with 23 games, 4 home runs and a .221 average. That is a young man who has been let through the door and not yet shown the room. Then comes 2032, and the career line reads Rookie of the Year and a Gold Glove in the same year. That season he played 156 games and collected 167 hits, 21 home runs and 80 RBI, with a .278 average. Few men get both honors in one summer. He got them before the city had learned how to pronounce his name properly.

The road after that was not straight, and the dossier prints the bend as it happened. In 2033 he played 45 games and hit .191. A reader who stopped there would have written him off. The reader who turned the page would have found 2034: 160 games, 39 home runs, 100 RBI and a .289 average. In 2036 he drove in 124 runs and hit .296 with 35 home runs. The Gold Gloves came again in 2038 and 2039, so the glove outlasted the slump and the bat found its way back.

Then the quiet years, as the dossier prints them. In 2039 he hit .285 with 20 home runs. In 2040 he hit .260 with 30 home runs and struck out 110 times. Then 2041, the line the desk keeps returning to: 138 games, 540 at-bats, 153 hits, 33 home runs, 87 RBI, a .283 average, a .317 on-base mark and a .509 slugging percentage. His WAR on the card is 3.2.

The year before, he walked 64 times and struck out 110. The year after, he walked 25 times and struck out 65. That is a different hitter, swinging earlier and missing less, and the record does not say why. It says only that he did it at an age when most first basemen are being told what they used to be.

The city never pinned a nickname on him that I can find in the record, and I will not coin one. What the ledger gives him instead is a title that is not a stat: Community Liaison. A man who has been a first baseman for eleven seasons on the dossier is now also the person a neighborhood calls.

## The Kid

Kevin Clark's book is one page long. His dossier holds a single season, 2041: 18 games and 64 at-bats. He had 19 hits, 5 doubles, a triple and 3 home runs, with 12 RBI and 6 stolen bases. He hit .297 and slugged .547 with a .313 on-base mark. His WAR on the card is 0.9. He struck out 3 times in 64 at-bats and walked once.

That is eighteen games, which is barely enough to learn where the dugout steps are. The Archive's coverage of him is a call-up and an infield crunched by a trade fallout, and no more. The ledger lists him as a Jack London man, same as Aitken, with the same employer on the card. He is, as far as the written record goes, the beginning of a chapter, and the book is still mostly white space.

I have covered enough spring camps to know what eighteen games prove. They prove nothing. They are only a signature at the bottom of a first page. But a signature is how every long book in this archive started.

## The Door

Here is what the desk can say and no more. There is a rumor in the A's off-season line. There is a first baseman with eleven seasons on his dossier and a 33-homer year freshly in the book. There is a third baseman with eighteen games and a slugging mark above .500. Jack London holds them both.

Whether the rumor touches either man is the question the record leaves open, and the person who should answer it is whoever the A's name when they are ready to. The feed does not say that anyone has. Until someone does, a rumor is a sound heard through a wall.

The era door in a ballclub is rarely a single day. It is a long hallway where one man's last good seasons overlap with another man's first few weeks, and for a while both are on the field and the city cannot say which chapter it is reading. The record is clear on one thing. Aitken did not drift into 2041. He hit 33 home runs in it, in his eleventh year on the dossier. Clark did not wait for permission to arrive. He stole 6 bases in 18 games.

Neither line comes with a prediction. Both come with a date and a number, and in the Archive that has always been the better place to start.

A rumor asks what comes next. The book only ever answers what was done, and it answers the same way every time it is opened.
