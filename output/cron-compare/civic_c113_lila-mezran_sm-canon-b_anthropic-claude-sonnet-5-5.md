# Chinatown's 140 sick: 6 in beds, the rest at home

Marisio Curazo works Chinatown from a list, and the list is not the one the hospital keeps. Curazo is an elder care coordinator in the neighborhood. The hospital's row for Chinatown shows 6 residents in care at the close of Cycle 113. Curazo's list is the other households.

"When I see a number like 140 sick in Chinatown, I don't see a number — I see the families I work with every day," Curazo said. "Most of them aren't going to the hospital. They're staying home because that's what we do. The daughter takes the bus from Fruitvale to cook. The nephew calls in late to pick up prescriptions. I'm the one who helps figure out who needs a home health aide, who needs their meds delivered, who just needs someone to check in. The hospital has six of our folks right now — the ones who really couldn't stay home. The rest of us are doing what we've always done: taking care of each other, block by block, door by door."

**What the rows hold**

The neighborhood demographics sheet for Cycle 113 puts Chinatown at 140 sick residents out of 2,201. That is 6.4 percent, at or over the 6 percent watch line. It is still the highest count of any neighborhood on the sheet. East Oakland is next at 121, then Ivy Hill at 120. The sheet counts 1,845 sick residents across 22 neighborhoods.

The paper's Cycle 112 coverage printed Chinatown at 142 sick, 6.5 percent of 2,193 residents. This cycle's 140 is two fewer, and the population figure has moved slightly. The direction is down. The neighborhood is still over the line it was over last cycle.

The care trail for Chinatown reads as follows, from the Care and Justice census hospital rows:

- 140 sick residents.
- 3 admissions this cycle.
- 6 in care at the close, and 6 beds occupied.
- None of the 6 are tracked residents, so the record names none of them.

The world summary also lists a recorded low-severity health spike in Chinatown this cycle. A low-severity spike does not mean the people living inside it had a low-severity week. The record does not say what is behind the spike. This column will not guess.

Citywide the hospital took in 35 residents this cycle, 71 were in care at the close, and 37 left. Adams Point shows 86 sick and 4 in care. Dimond shows 93 sick and 4 in care. Downtown shows 77 sick and 4 in care. East Oakland, at 121, also has 4 in care. By that measure Chinatown's 6 is the largest of those neighborhood counts, but the gap between 140 and 6 is the story.

**Who is not in a bed**

Omarion Farah drives a cab in Chinatown and hears what the neighborhood talks about between stops. It is not the hospital.

"People talk in the cab, but not about hospitals much," Farah said. "They're more worried about who's going to cover a shift or pick up the kids. I see folks staying home, taking care of each other. The ones I hear about going to the hospital are the ones who really can't avoid it. It's a lot of home care, a lot of family stepping in."

Yu Zen is a Rockridge insurance broker. He was admitted in Cycle 105 after a workplace accident and is still listed in hospital care as active. The paper first heard from him from that bed in Cycle 107. He is not one of Chinatown's six, whose rows carry no names. But he lives inside the arithmetic of a hospital stay, and he has watched this count from the other side of the paperwork.

"The names on the forms aren't the same as the names on the beds," Yu Zen said. "Chinatown's been hit so hard, and I know it's not because those folks are any less careful or any less strong. It's that when illness clusters, it lands on households where someone's already stretched thin — caring for a parent, watching the grandkids, working two jobs. The six of us in hospital care, we've got nurses and monitors and someone bringing us meals. The other 134 — they've got a cousin or an auntie or a neighbor who's doing all that and still trying to keep the shop open. That's who's caring for them. And that's the part that keeps me up at night, because I know that kind of care doesn't show up on any ledger."

The 134 is Yu Zen's subtraction: 140 sick, minus 6 in beds. It is sound arithmetic. It also carries an assumption the ledger does not test. Not every sick resident is necessarily in need of a bed or a caregiver. The sheet counts the sick. It does not say how sick, or who is nursing whom.

**What the record cannot say yet**

The record holds counts. It does not hold a single named Chinatown resident on a hospital row, a discharge date, or a follow-up appointment. It holds no home health aide hours or delivered prescriptions, which is the work Curazo describes. Nothing in the sheets measures a cousin's or auntie's shift. Yu Zen is right that it does not show up on a ledger.

Other names in this cycle's health summary are ordinary and small. Victor King nursed a sore back in Rockridge after overdoing it around the house. Cee Lee dealt with a seasonal health concern in Lake Merritt. Elle Lilo in Jack London finally shook a cold that had hung around. They sit alongside the hospital's named cases, Deacon Seymour of Lake Merritt and Maurice Franklin of Rockridge, who remain in care. These are the quiet end of the record. The Chinatown number is the loud end, and it sits mostly behind doors.

The next record needed is discharge data for the 3 Chinatown admissions this cycle. Beyond that is the question the sheets cannot yet answer: what follow-up care the 6 in beds receive once they leave, and who in the neighborhood is waiting at home to provide it.
