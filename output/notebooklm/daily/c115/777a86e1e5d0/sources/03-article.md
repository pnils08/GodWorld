# Fifth on Points, First in Credits: Taveras's Undocked Pledge Has Nothing to Attach To Yet

By Anthony Raines, The Bay Tribune

Eric Taveras is 25, hit 54 home runs, was Postseason MVP and World Series MVP, and did it in the first year of a $32.1M deal, all as the feed prints it. The season line is the part of his résumé that needs no interpretation. The Undocked line beside it, from the same cycle-115 feed row, does.

That line reads 3 episodes, 20 points, 1,170 credits, fifth place. The standings sort on points. Taveras is fifth on that column and first on credits. The feed puts his 1,170 credits "nearly four times" second-place Clarissa Dane's 317. Ronald Silk leads on points with 38.15 over 2 episodes. Dane sits at 10 points over 9 episodes.

The feed also records a pledge. In C112, Taveras posted on Moltbook that anything he won on Undocked would go to the Vinnie Keane Firehouse 29 West Oakland Baseball Academy. The paper's C86 and C89 coverage described that academy as a free program in a rehabbed firehouse on Mandela Parkway, funded through Keane's foundation. With Keane's retirement now official, the feed reads the pledge as the next face of the franchise promising money to the program carrying the last one's name. As of C115, no winnings have gone to anyone.

## What the two columns can and cannot carry

The Box-Card habit is to ask which cell carries the claim, and here neither cell can. The pledge is conditional on winning something. SpaceMolt, the show's creator, has not said what the rankings pay out. The feed says so directly: the pledge is real, but the amount isn't.

Taveras's best number, credits, is therefore the one with no stated payoff. His worst number, fifth on points, is the one the standings sort on. Both are unattached to any prize. The pledge is therefore a promise against an undefined payout rule, and the standings cannot settle it either way.

## Is it real, or is it sample?

The sample is thin. Applying the is-it-real test, volume separates signal from noise, and almost nothing here has volume.

- **Silk, 38.15 points over 2 episodes.** The top of the board rests on two episodes. A two-episode leader is a variance fact, not an identity.
- **Taveras, 20 points over 3 episodes.** The feed has him leading the standings for two cycles after his C112 episode, then passed in C114 and C115 by Silk and three others. A drop from first to fifth on one more episode's worth of competition says the points column moves fast on small counts.
- **Dane, 10 points over 9 episodes.** She is the only contestant in this feed row with real volume, and the volume buys her 317 credits and 10 points. That frames how unusual Taveras's 1,170 credits over 3 episodes is. The feed does not say whether he can repeat it.
- **Earlier Tribune coverage.** The paper's C111 piece logged 1,170 credits from two flights, and the C112 and C113 pieces had him at number one. The credits figure has held across those cycles. It is the stable number on his card. The rank is the noisy one.

So the reading that holds up is narrow. If the show pays on credits, Taveras's pledge has real weight behind it. If it pays on points, he is fifth with a sample too small to project from. Because SpaceMolt has not said which, any forecast of what Firehouse 29 receives is a guess.

## The fit question

Taveras's playing-side contract is on the feed and the Undocked prize is not. That asymmetry matters for how the pledge should be read. The $32.1M, the 54 homers and the two MVPs show what he is worth to the A's. They do not show what he is obligated to give, and this feed row prints no obligation. The record does not support calling the pledge broken, because nothing has been won to give. Its last line puts the open question plainly: does he give to Firehouse 29 anyway if the money never comes? It is a fair question, and nothing in the record answers it.

The pending items are small and checkable:

1. SpaceMolt's payout rule: points, credits, or something else.
2. The payout date.
3. Whether Taveras goes back on the show to reclaim the top spot, as the feed asks.

The standings can be rechecked each cycle, and one more episode will move his 20 points more than any other number on his line. SpaceMolt can answer the first two items whenever it chooses, and until it does, the pledge has nothing to attach to.
