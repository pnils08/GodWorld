# Three Cycles Without a Move: The Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline

West Oakland, East Oakland and Fruitvale are the three neighborhoods the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline names as its own. A young person in any of them who wants a trade can look up the program. It promises three tracks, eight trades and more than 400 Baylight placements by Year 3.

At Cycle 115 the program has not advanced in three cycles. That is the city's own audit reading. Here is what the record shows and what it doesn't.

**What the record establishes**

I read the Initiative_Tracker row for INIT-007. It lists the Pipeline as a program with a $12.5 million budget and the status "passed." Its implementation phase is "implementation-active." It was announced in Cycle 73 as part of the Baylight community benefits package, not as a standalone council vote. Mayor Avery Santana's office, listed as MAYOR-01, is the proposing office.

The milestone notes run in order:

- **C107:** a public town hall enrollment event, with applicant intake operating and cohort placement targets set.
- **C111:** a first cohort placement report mandated for Cycle 112.
- **C113:** the first cohort placement report published, with an accountability review directed.
- **C114:** a placement-gap analysis and adjustment plan directed for publication, with explicit West Oakland and Fruitvale review.
- **C115:** the same requirement restated. The row says implementation continues.

The next scheduled action is to publish the placement-gap analysis and the program adjustment plan. The tracker puts that in Cycle 116.

**What the tracker row leaves open**

The budget fields read $12,500,000 total and $12,500,000 remaining. The last-disburse field is blank. I am reading fields on a sheet. I am not reporting that no money has moved. The row doesn't say why the fields read that way, and I can't tell you.

The row also doesn't contain the placement-gap analysis, so I can't say what the gap is. The C113 report that prompted the review isn't in the tracker either, only a note that it was published. The row says West Oakland and Fruitvale get explicit review. It doesn't say what showed up in those two neighborhoods to earn it.

The tracker's own notes also flag a gap in the paper's coverage. They say there was no coverage after Cycle 73, a 12-cycle gap. They list four milestones as unverified: the Oakland City Schools agreement (C76), the Oakland Community College District agreement (C78), the budget allocation (C80) and the A's commitment (C83). I wrote the Cycle 73 feature, and this paper has the same gap to answer for.

I searched the paper's archive for the program's name. It returned recent staged material and citizen pages from C114 and C115, none of which reports on the program itself. I'm not treating that search as the last word, and the only thing I'm claiming is what it returned.

**Who could answer**

The tracker names the proposing office, Mayor Santana's. It doesn't name anyone responsible for writing or publishing the gap analysis. It doesn't say who owns the adjustment plan or who signs off on the cohort placement numbers. I have no record of a request put to any office on this, and I have no response to report. The elapsed silence is unestablished, and I won't put a number on it.

That leaves questions the record can't close for now:

- Who writes the placement-gap analysis?
- What does the C113 placement report say about West Oakland and Fruitvale?
- Will the Cycle 116 publication date hold?

It matters because the program's pitch is concrete: trades and placements, aimed at young people in three named neighborhoods. A town hall enrollment event drew applicants in C107. Applicants carry that enrollment forward as time they've already spent.

So when the adjustment plan comes out, the question is whether it will say who in West Oakland, East Oakland and Fruitvale got placed. Or will a young person who signed up at that C107 town hall still be waiting to find out whether a spot was ever theirs?
