# The Page of Lives, C113: A Place of Their Own

**Orion Kerkering** moved into a rented place of his own in Lake Merritt this cycle. The ledger carries him as a retired former A's, and the Tribune once named him in "Unsung Architects of a Dynasty." Christopher Johnson, a Lake Merritt janitor, has seen the new place. "I've seen Orion Kerkering's new place, they're renting in Lake Merritt," he said. "I noticed it because I've been keeping an eye on the neighborhood. It's nice to see young folks setting up on their own. I've been around here long enough to know it's not always easy."

The record shows Kerkering was already on those blocks. In Y3C5 he compared rent notes with a neighbor over a fence on a quiet side street in Lake Merritt. Since the move, the log has him doing four things. He dodged the running groups circling the lake and found a quiet bench near the pergola. He closed his eyes during a choir solo and felt the tension leave his shoulders. He woke at 5 AM for thirty minutes of total silence before the house woke up. And he sorted clothing-drive donations and recognized a coat he had given two years ago.

Two lines of the thread don't sit flat together. The place is his own, but the 5 AM entry speaks of a house that wakes after him. The record doesn't say who is in it, and this page won't guess. It does say he is up before them and keeps the first half hour to himself.

**Isla Sike** moved into a rented place of her own in West Oakland. She directs a domestic violence shelter there. She is widowed, and she says so herself.

"Moving into a place of my own feels like a big step, especially after being widowed," she said. "It's not just about having a new space, it's about starting a new chapter. I've been focused on my work at the shelter and this feels like a personal milestone. I'm still getting used to it, but it's a relief to have some stability."

The thread behind her is mostly work. In Y3C8 she logged a routine period with no major changes, and a stack of stabilization fund applications waiting on the community board. The same cycle she couldn't shake a strange dream through the morning coffee. This cycle the log has her hearing Jack London was lively last night. It also has her grinning at a kid who explained something to a parent with total authority. She had logged the same grin before, at some other kid. The record doesn't say when her husband died or how long she looked for the place.

The Tribune has followed him from "Six Straight and a Debut: The A's Pull Away as Morton Arrives" to "The Ledger Closes Before the Ink Is Dry," and this cycle he saw his name in the sports pages again. The log also has him trading produce over a fence and coming out ahead. It has him watching the fog roll over the port cranes from his front window. Two others, Isla Sike and Clarence Moody, heard Jack London was lively, then packed, the night before. The record doesn't say whether the neighborhood he left was the reason for the move.

**Omar Amici** lost on the same book. He staked 329 and the book kept it. A construction laborer in Uptown, he logged the slip as coming back empty. Earlier that cycle he had argued with a stranger about the Oaks bullpen depth and ended up buying the stranger a beer. He also felt a lift at Art Walk Cafe after the win, which was the team's and not the book's. He found a quiet minute at a venue doorway and felt the season starting to turn. The log says he relied on familiar social circles during a cold period.

His words on the housing news: "I've seen more for-rent signs popping up around Uptown, it's like the neighborhood's changing fast. People moving in, people moving out. I don't know Orion Kerkering or Isla Sike personally, but I can relate to the struggle of finding a place to call your own. It's tough out here." The for-rent signs were already in his log in Y3C8, when he noted they outnumbered the welcome mats.
