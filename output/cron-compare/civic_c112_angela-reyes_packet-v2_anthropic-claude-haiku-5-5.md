# The Top Mark, From the Pickup Line

At a few minutes past three, the after-school room in Rockridge starts to fill one backpack at a time. Mei Wong, the program aide, stands near the door and watches who comes in and who gets collected. She has a habit of learning the kids by how they walk through the frame.

Asked what parents want from her, she doesn't reach for the table at all.

"Parents mostly ask about homework and whether their kid ate a real snack, but underneath it's about feeling safe and known. They want to know their child is with adults who see them, not just supervising. What keeps people here is that sense of community—the same faces at pickup, teachers who remember your kid's name, neighbors who look out. It's not flashy, but it's steady. That's why families stay even when other places might be growing faster."

The steadiness she describes is what the numbers are trying to measure. Rockridge holds the top school quality index on the table this cycle, at 8.63, ahead of Baylight District and Piedmont Ave among the 22 neighborhoods ranked. Last cycle the same index read 8.7. The dip is small enough that it reads as a rounding question on a page. Inside the building, it is harder to find.

Down the hall, Simone Adesanya, the school principal, has her own version of that point. She is not interested in defending a score, and she says so plainly.

"Honestly, that dip is a blip on a page to most of us. Teacher quality at 9.08 — that's the number my staff feels every single day, in the hallways, in the way a kid who was struggling suddenly gets it. The index is a snapshot; the teachers are the whole album. We don't walk around quoting our own score. We walk around making sure every kid in this building knows someone's got their back. That's the real index, and it doesn't move a tenth of a point because of a cycle."

The table gives Rockridge a graduation rate of 94.53% and a college-ready rate of 76.03%, with 247 students and funding of 15,000 on the books. Enrollment moved by one student this cycle, from the count last cycle. That is the whole change in the number that moves. Everything else on the table held.

Maya Torres-Dillon teaches high school science here, and she has been on the other side of that 76% for long enough to distrust it a little. She talks about it the way teachers talk about a report card they know is true and also incomplete.

"Honestly, that 76% number — I recognize it in the hallways more than in any single test score. It's the kid who stays after class to ask one more question about the lab, or the group that argues about the data until the bell rings. I see it in the parents who show up to every back-to-school night, and in the way my colleagues share lesson plans without being asked. But I also teach long enough to know that number doesn't capture the kid who's brilliant but has to work nights, or the one who freezes on test day. It's a real signal, but it's not the whole story. What I recognize is the effort behind it — and that's something you can't put in a percentage."

Citywide, the district's Oakland City Schools carries 5,201 employees. Across the neighborhoods on this table, the per-neighborhood funding totals 286,000. Rockridge's share is 15,000 of that. The table does not say what the rest of the district will do with the gap between its strongest and weakest rooms, and it does not say whether a stable top score keeps a school in that place.

What the record does show is movement at the edges. West Oakland gained three students this cycle, East Oakland two, and Piedmont Ave, Laurel, and Adams Point one each. Rockridge gained one. Temescal sits at the bottom of the table at 6.51, down from 6.6, and its own enrollment is on the same page. Nothing on the table explains why any one family moved in or out.

So the question at the end of the day is the one Angela keeps coming back to. The table says one more student came to Rockridge this cycle. It does not say who that student is, what drew the family, or whether they arrived this week or last. And it cannot tell anyone what it feels like to sit at the top of a list and know that a new family was among the ones who chose it.
