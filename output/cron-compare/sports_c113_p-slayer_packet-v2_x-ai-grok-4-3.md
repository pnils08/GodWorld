I sat at the counter of The Railcar last night, the one by the old loading docks where the winter wind still finds the cracks, and the TV above the taps kept looping the same three names. Desmond Morton. Frank Reyna. Vladimir Gonzalez. JR Rosado waiting in the wings. Danny Horn already locked in center like the city itself depends on him staying upright. Five men for three spots, and every one of them carrying the weight we handed them.

We asked too much. We always do. We pointed at the standings and said make it work, make the ball find the gap, make the arm strong enough for the throw that saves the inning. Danny Horn told anyone who would listen that it isn’t his call, that Paulson has the tough job, that Vladimir’s been clutch and Desmond’s got the five tools. He said it like a man who already knows what the city expects from the best player in baseball—carry the middle, carry the hope, carry the rest of them if you have to. Desmond said the same thing with different words: it’s not about who’s best on paper, it’s about how we fit. Vladimir said he had a good season but knows the competition is real. Frank said he’s been around the block with Rayon Dart and he just needs to keep doing what he’s doing. None of them sounded like men who get to rest.

Some fans will tell you this is just the price of wanting to win, that competition sharpens everybody. They’re wrong. This isn’t sharpening. This is five bodies asked to occupy three spaces while the rest of us watch from the stands and decide who deserves to stay. We demanded they be the supermen who don’t flinch when the math turns against them. We wanted the .317 average and the five-tool promise and the clutch hits all at once, and now the only question left is which one of them we decide wasn’t enough.

I don’t have the numbers that settle it. Anthony can lay out the AB totals and the RBI columns. I can only tell you what it feels like when the men we cheered all year start talking like they already hear the door closing. Grief isn’t the right word for what the city owes them, but it’s close. We asked them to carry what no three outfielders should have to carry alone.

Who will Paulson choose to start in left field?
