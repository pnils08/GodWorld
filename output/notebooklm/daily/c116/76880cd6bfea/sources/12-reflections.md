# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> I walked Downtown this afternoon, as I said I would, and left the notebook in my coat. Eight incidents this cycle, the most of any neighborhood, and three more than before. On the sidewalk it doesn't look like eight. It looks like a man sweeping in front of a shuttered storefront, two women comparing bus times, and a door propped open that nobody walked through. The number is true and so is the sweeping. I'd like us to keep both in the same sentence. Down at the lake, the lanterns went around after dusk, a slow string of paper light, and Robert and I watched from the terrace rail. A child's lantern kept drifting out of line, and nobody pulled her back. I'm still thinking about that. Paul's trade news landed hard around the lake today. Carmen Mesa, twenty-three, is a Phillie now, sent off by the club that beat his new team for the seventh title. P Slayer wrote about the crowd moving past that station as if nothing had happened. That's how a city takes a trade. The board changes first and people catch up afterward. Fruitvale keeps adding riders while the trains keep slipping. I keep coming back to that, a place people choose to arrive at even when it lets them down a little. Robert says the lanterns were prettier than the news. I told him they were the news. — Mags

## Daniel Han — Senior Pastor / Faith Leader

> I've seen firsthand the impact of job training programs in our community. While Adams Point United Methodist isn't directly running such a program, our involvement in the Interfaith Council has shown me the value of faith organizations coming together to support local initiatives. I recall the interfaith prayer breakfast we had last cycle, where leaders from various faith backgrounds gathered to discuss ways to serve the community. It's this kind of collaboration that can lead to meaningful support for job training and other vital programs.

## Ramon Vega — City Council District 4

> Let's get right to it. You've laid out the record cleanly. Here's where I land, and I'll be direct with you because that's how I operate. **On the process point — you're right.** A review starts with a filing the Clerk can verify. Not a rumor, not a meeting summary, not a promise. If we're going to ask the city to spend staff hours and public attention on something, it has to be real on paper. That's not bureaucracy for its own sake; that's how we keep trust from bleeding out. **On the meeting-not-being-a-record point — also right.** I've sat through a hundred meetings where everyone felt good and nothing changed. Good rapport doesn't pave a street or fund a nurse. Glenview wants to know what happens next, not who showed up. **On Ivy Hill — I'm holding my position.** Mixed data means we watch, we verify, we don't overreact in either direction. Sentiment up, retail up, crime up a hair. That's not a settled picture, and I'm not adding another proposal this week. I also owe Glenview and Dimond a clear record, and I won't let one neighborhood's story crowd out the others. **What I'll do with my procedural authority:** I'll make sure whatever comes to the floor for D4 has a verifiable filing, a clear work plan, and a timeline for follow-through. If we can get Ivy Hill to a place where the text is clean and the numbers tell a consistent story — then we move. Not before. That's my word. Anything else you need from me on this?

## Nina Chen — City Council District 8

> Council Member Chen's office acknowledges the record as stated. District 8 remains committed to the principles outlined: procedural integrity, verifiable docketing, and the Chen-Vega audit controls as the non-negotiable framework for Grand Lake stabilization funds. To clarify the third point: the "transparent departmental coordination" language refers specifically to the unresolved discrepancy between Public Works' maintenance log timestamps and the health inspection readings from the same week. My office has requested the cross-departmental reconciliation report three times. We have not received it. That report is not optional — it is the documentation baseline for the next phase of commercial corridor assessments. On the broader point: my affirmative vote was cast with the understanding that roll call certification would follow. I expect that certification to be published in the next docket cycle. Until then, District 8's position remains conditional, not final.

## Matilda Diop — Paramedic

> The one thing I'm quietly hoping for is that today is a slow day. Not for me — I can handle the pace, always have — but for the rig, for whoever's out there. I keep counting the gauze twice and checking the address list, and I know that's not about the inventory. It's about the last one. The blood. The way I washed it off and still felt it on my hands at dinner. And I'm dreading the quiet, too. Because when it's quiet, my head gets loud — and it starts asking me if I made the right call, years back, the one that led me here. Inez tells me I save lives for a living, that the doubt is just proof I care. But there's a bench near the water where I sat last week and argued with a column out loud to an empty kitchen, and I still don't know who I was arguing with — the writer or myself.
