# Uptown, in their own words

## Evan Morello — Actor

> The corner store on 23rd got its newspaper delivery early, and the guy stacking them left the rubber bands off this time. That's it. That's the whole thing. I noticed because the papers fanned out on the curb like they were waiting for someone to read them, and no one did. Felt like a small mercy, a quiet morning where nothing needed to be performed. I stood there a second longer than I should have, just watching the ink dry in the sun.

## Elliott Crane — City Council Member

> The closeout language held, and that’s what matters. I keep circling the same truth: we can’t spend on promise when the ledger is still open, and I won’t pretend otherwise just to move a charter faster. Piedmont Avenue doesn’t need me to invent problems—it needs the trash picked up and the permits moving, and that’s the work I’m actually paid to protect. I’m restless tonight because the quiet feels like a trap; someone’s going to float a shiny project tomorrow and I’ll have to say no again. Fine. Discipline isn’t flashy, but it’s the only thing that keeps the corridor from becoming a photo op.

## Priya Narang — EMS DIRECTOR

> Ariana’s chart is still on my desk. I don’t know why I keep coming back to it—the staging, the timeline, the fact that we got her in as fast as we did. It still reads like a countdown, and I can’t shake the feeling that somewhere upstream, someone should have caught it sooner. Then there’s Deacon’s hand and Maurice’s lungs, both of them sitting right on top of the board like proof that the systems we’re supposed to run on have cracks in them. I’m tired of answering for cracks I didn’t make, but I’m the one who has to make sure the next crew doesn’t walk into the same hole blind.

## Terrence Mobley — City Council Member - D9

> The Grand Lake yes is on the record, and that's done. I said the conditions are real and the public record backs it up—that's the honest part. The quiet after a vote is when I actually feel the weight of it, not during the debate. Laurel's stability, Uptown's pace—I keep telling people I won't blink, but privately I have to check my own pulse too. And that bit about reports not being finish lines—I wrote that because I've seen too many good intentions die in a PDF nobody reads. The agenda comes next week, and I'll be the one asking for the receipts again. That's the job, and I don't need the noise to know I've done it.
