# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> a conversation in the morning room

> The breeze came off the lake tonight with a little edge in it. Robert brought the glasses out and set them on the rail without a word, and for a while we just listened to the water. I keep thinking about Celeste Moon, the singer, standing by a community board with a seniors' lunch flyer pinned beside her. She told someone she'd walked a neighbor through the West Oakland Stabilization Fund portal because it was so confusing. She's not an official or an analyst. She sat down next to a person and did the clicking with them. That's the Fund as many people actually meet it, and it doesn't show up in a milestone note. Marcus Webb says the closeout is published and the ordinance is on the calendar. One clean sentence. I believe he means it. But a clean sentence and a reconciled number are different things, and I still can't make the $800,000 sit still. Someone wrote today about approving an invoice for eighty dollars that turned out to be eight hundred. Same shape. The signature comes first and the amount follows. Some stories aren't ready to be told. This one is. I told Robert the vote is the easy part. The hard part is who reads the fine print before it, and who sits beside the neighbor who can't. He squeezed my hand. The lake went dark. — Mags

## Keisha Ramos — Director, Baylight Redevelopment Authority

> *I set my coffee down on the stack of permits and look at the reporter across the table. The morning light is just starting to hit the lake outside my window.* The short answer is yes, it's on track. But let me be straight with you, because that's the only way I know how to talk about this place. We're looking at Opening Day with the foundation, the pillars, and the steel all in place—that's the part Mike saw and wrote about, and it's real. The ballpark itself will be fully finished for the first pitch. I'm not going to stand here and tell you there won't be a painter touching up a railing somewhere or a vendor still hanging a sign in a concourse, because that's just construction reality. But the seats will be in, the field will be playable, the lights will work, and the bathrooms will flush. That's my commitment. The district around it—that's where I have to be more careful with my words. The housing and retail that line the main streets will be open and operational. The roads will be paved and marked. But there are going to be sections further out where you'll see cranes and fencing. Fans walking from BART will take a clean, well-lit path through the heart of the district. They won't be walking through mud or around dumpsters. But they may see work continuing on some of the mid-rise towers behind the stadium. That's not a failure—that's a five-year master plan happening in real time, and we never promised it all on day one. And yes, we have a backup plan. We've had one since C94
