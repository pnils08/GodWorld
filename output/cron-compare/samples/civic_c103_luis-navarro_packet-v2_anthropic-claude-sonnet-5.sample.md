# Nine Cycles In One Phase: What The Record Shows On Fruitvale Transit

The tracker says nine cycles. Not nine days, nine cycles — the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II visioning work has sat in "construction-planning" that long, according to the Initiative Tracker entry flagged in this cycle's audit. That's the fact. What's missing is everything that would explain it.

**What we know:** The initiative is real and it's stuck. The audit names the phase — construction-planning — and gives the duration — nine cycles — and that's the full extent of what the record hands me. No vote count. No budget line. No named office holding the file. No document trail showing a request went out and got answered, or went out and got ignored.

**What we don't know:** Who owns this initiative right now. Whether anyone has asked for an update and been told something, or asked and been told nothing. Whether the holdup is money, design, permitting, or a decision nobody's made yet. I'm not going to guess at which, because guessing is what got two residents in this neighborhood watching a construction-planning phase that hasn't moved in nine cycles.

Calvin Turner has been tracking it. "What the record shows does not line up with what I expected," he told me. "Who is responsible for answering this? I am going to keep watching this."

Tomas Renteria is asking a version of the same question from a different angle. "What the record shows does not line up with what I expected," he said. "What explains the gap in the record? I am going to keep watching this."

Two residents, two separate conversations, same complaint: the paper trail doesn't match the neighborhood's expectation of where this project should be by now. Neither one told me who's supposed to be answering for it. Neither could — that information isn't in what either of them has access to, and it isn't in what I have access to either.

That's the honest state of this story. I don't have a silence clock to run, because I don't have a request-and-response pair to time. I don't have a name to put in a "declined to comment" line, because no office has been asked on the record — at least not in anything I've been handed. What I have is a tracker entry that says nine cycles in one phase, and two residents on record saying the record doesn't add up to what they were told to expect.

The open question is the one the initiative's own timeline poses: what specific obstacle is keeping this out of the next phase? Design revision. Funding gap. A sign-off sitting on someone's desk. The tracker doesn't say, and I'm not going to fill that blank in for it.

What happens next is straightforward. Somebody owns this initiative's file. Until that name surfaces — in the tracker, in a public update, in a response to a resident who asks directly — nine cycles stays the number, and it keeps ticking. I'll be watching the tracker for cycle ten same as Turner and Renteria are watching the ground.

— Luis Navarro
