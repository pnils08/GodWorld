# Who Owns KONO's Rent Pressure When Everyone Says Business Is Good?

Michael Corliss had his camera up at Midnight Norae Karaoke, late, shooting somebody else's bad chorus. The block outside still smelled like smoke. I asked him what he sees through the lens.

"I've seen KONO's energy firsthand," he said. "We're always out and about, whether it's a game or a night out at Midnight Norae Karaoke. But with the housing pressure, it's like there's this undercurrent that's hard to ignore." He's painted over graffiti with neighbors he barely knew. He called it a small win. Then he said it made him wonder "what's being lost as the neighborhood changes."

That's the photographer. Here's the audit.

The Neighborhood_Map has KONO's RetailVitality at 8.68. That's 12th of 22, under the city median of 9.21. It's down 1.06. HousingPressure is 1.5. That's 15th of 22, against a city median of zero, and it's up a full 1.000. Sentiment is 0.05, 15th of 22, under the median of 0.06. The audit flags all of it as decay. It also says no active initiative, and no mitigator, owns the break.

The same map row still labels KONO's trajectory "growth." It lists the median rent at 2066. Decay on one line, growth on the next. Which one is the paper supposed to print?

I went looking for who's feeling it. Dmitri Soto, who supervises a container yard, wasn't.

"To be honest, I haven't really seen the housing pressure affecting local workers around here," he told me. "KONO's been bustling, retail's busy, and we're getting new households moving in." He moved here recently from Jack London and calls it "a positive change so far." I believe him. A man with a supervisor's paycheck and a fresh address would say that. But the map has retail slipping, and "busy" doesn't mean healthy. Foot traffic isn't a ledger.

Jasmine Torres runs Tenth Street Digital out of KONO. She moved here from Chinatown with her wife. She wasn't buying the bustle.

"KONO's got a bit of a mixed bag going on right now," she said. "I've noticed that the neighborhood's vibe feels a bit off." Then she got to the city. "As for the city's support, I'm not really sure what to say." That's a small-business founder who can't name one thing the city is doing for her block. She added: "It's clear there's some tension around housing and retail vitality. I hope the city can figure out a way to balance things out."

Hope is not an initiative.

Celeste Moon, a singer, stood by a community board with a seniors lunch flyer pinned on it. She told me she'd helped a neighbor through the West Oakland Stabilization Fund portal "because it was really confusing for them." That's a KONO woman doing unpaid navigation for a program that lives in a different neighborhood. "Housing feels like a big issue," she said, and she pointed out the newcomers, like the Jack London person "who just settled in down the street." That could be Soto. I didn't ask. It could be somebody else. Either way, KONO is absorbing people from Jack London, and nobody at City Hall has put a name on what that does to rent.

Here's the count. Four residents, four different readings. One says energy. One says undercurrent. One says off. One says busy. And a map that says retail down, housing pressure up, and no program attached.

I raised this in my C115 column. I asked whether Councilmember Warren Ashford (D7, CRC), whose district holds KONO on the map, knew about the flag. I have no response from his office on file. I'm not claiming he ignored it. I'm saying I have nothing to print.

So I'll ask again, plainly. Councilmember Ashford, Mayor Santana: KONO's retail is down 1.06 and its housing pressure is up 1.000, and a rent that's already 2066 is the thing people are whispering about over karaoke. Which office owns that, and what's the first thing it does?
