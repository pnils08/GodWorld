## UNDOCKED: Three pilots finish even; Luis Taz keeps the lead

Three pilots made this week’s UNDOCKED slate, but the board’s top line belongs to someone who wasn’t in the chair: Luis Taz remains #1, with 45.19 achievement points, zero credits, two cycles led and a current streak of two. The week’s three runs offer different kinds of drama; none changes who is holding the lead.

The hallway argument is an old one: does a long route make the night, or does the finish? This time all three pilots came out even on credits. So the spotlight falls on how they spent their time before the clock ran out.

Elijah Brown, the electrician, flew Monday and Thursday. His Monday route ran through Sol, Alpha Centauri, Tau Ceti and a string of further systems; Thursday took him through a different chain, from Last Light and Fang to Betelgeuse and Gsc 0037. Both nights ended even. “I didn't really have a strategy,” he said of his two nights, describing a run at getting through as many systems as possible before time ran out. For the week, his strength is consistency across two very different routes—not a credit swing.

Brooklyn Nguyen, a community programs coordinator, also had two nights, Tuesday and Friday. Tuesday brought mining: three copper ore, three iron ore and one sol alloy ore. Friday took her through a separate route, including Sirius, Atlas and Gliese 876. Both nights, again, ended even. Her summary is plain: the mining “didn't translate into any profit.” That makes her week less a mining payoff story than a contrast between what she gathered and where she went.

P Slayer, journalist and fan columnist, had Wednesday’s single run. The route passed through Sol, Electra, Mimosa, Ross 154 and other systems; the record includes one iron ore and two fights before the even finish. Slayer has said the route was spontaneous, with risks taken in pursuit of a climb. This week’s episode supplies the risk and the fights, but not a credit reward or a new board position to claim.

So the week’s ranking story has a clear boundary: Luis Taz owns the lead, while the three pilots in this slate each closed their recorded run even. The distinction is worth keeping. A route can be busy, a haul can be real, and a night can have fights—without any of those becoming a change at the top. That is the board’s quiet tension this week: three different ways to fill the chair, and the same name still at #1.
