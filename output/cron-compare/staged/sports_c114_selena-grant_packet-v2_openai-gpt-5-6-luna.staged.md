# Sports

The Oaks’ record moved from 4-1 with a one-game losing streak to 5-1 with a one-game winning streak in the same cycle. That is not a contradiction; it is the clearest available description of an expansion team whose floor and ceiling are still being established.

In Indiana, the Oaks lost 104-100 after falling 20 points behind and recovering enough to make the game competitive. Adash Stanley scored 19 points with five rebounds and four assists, making five of 11 three-point attempts. Austin Reaves added 19 points, six rebounds and seven assists without a turnover. Wendell Carter Jr. scored 10 points on five-of-six shooting and had seven rebounds. The production was broad, but the result remained a loss.

The next result was the opposite in scale. The Oaks beat the Raptors 135-75 in Toronto, a 60-point rout that restored the winning record. Reaves scored 20 points while shooting eight of 16 from the field and four of six from three-point range. T. Jerome had 19 points, seven rebounds and seven assists. Stanley finished with 16 points, seven rebounds and six assists while making all four of his three-point attempts. Carter had 14 points and 15 rebounds, including five offensive rebounds.

The contrast matters more than either result by itself. In Indiana, the Oaks needed a recovery from a 20-point deficit and still came up short. In Toronto, the offense and rebounding totals turned the game into a runaway. The record says 5-1. The performance lines say the expansion build remains capable of producing radically different versions of itself from one game to the next.

The most useful development may have come in the rotation rather than the margin. Harry Hayward, a second-round rookie, scored eight points, collected four rebounds and went three for four from the field, including two for two from three-point range, in 13 minutes. That is a compact line, but it gives the franchise something an expansion team needs: a young player whose usefulness can be measured without requiring the entire roster to be remade around him. Hayward is shaping up as a solid rotation piece, and the 13-minute sample is enough to identify the possibility, not enough to settle it.

The available record labels franchise stability as growing. That assessment is supported by the Oaks’ response to the Indiana loss, but not proved by one rout. A first-year team can look organized in one game and unsettled in the next. The developing rotation, particularly Hayward’s place within it, will matter more than the winning streak’s length.

The front office still needs an answer for what produced such a wide swing between the 104-100 loss and the 135-75 win: was it the Oaks’ rotation, their shot-making, their ability to control the glass, or the level of opposition? The next number that matters is the one that shows whether this inconsistency is a feature of the build or an early flaw to correct.
