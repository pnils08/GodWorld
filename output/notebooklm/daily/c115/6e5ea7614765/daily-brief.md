#### **The Sea-Level Desk Is Down to Three**
*By Jordan Velez, Business Desk*

At **Marine Systems** on the edge of the Baylight District, sea level monitoring technician **Mina Linas** is now one of only three workers left on staff after the firm shed two positions this cycle—a 40 percent payroll cut representing roughly \$360,000 in lost annual compensation. 

While Marine Systems contracts without a recorded top line, neighboring corporate tech giants in the district continue rapid financial growth:
*   **Anthropic** carries 86 employees at an average salary of \$185,000, growing 2.7 percent to reach **\$649.8 million in revenue** (adding \$335,000 this cycle), with AI safety researchers **Kelvon Ochoa**, **Iggy Davis**, and **Linda Chow**, and security guard **Broderick Mitchell**.
*   **DigitalOcean** expanded to 800 employees and **\$521.4 million in revenue**, growing 5.2 percent.
*   **Baylight Construction Authority** employs 451 workers under Director **Keisha Ramos**, generating \$31.3 million in revenue with trade leads like historic preservation carpenter **Brianna Lee**, repair technician **Tariq Jello**, and solar installation lead **Talmon Lint**.
*   **Oakland Casino Town** shed one worker down to 99 employees (-2.3% growth) under manager **Philly Rodriguez**, bookmaker **Manfred Owens**, and **Gregory Mims**.

Reporter Jordan Velez noted that the businesses disappearing first are rarely the weakest, but rather those whose public value—like shoreline flood warnings—does not appear as direct revenue on a corporate balance sheet.

#### **Chinatown's Sick Are Counted by the Hundred and Named by No One**
*By Dr. Lila Mezran, Health & Civic Desk*

Chinatown holds **135 sick residents** out of 2,205—the highest count in Oakland at 6.1 percent of the neighborhood, remaining above the city's 6.0 percent watch bar—followed by East Oakland (117), Ivy Hill (115), and Fruitvale (109). Yet the neighborhood hospital census recorded only three admissions and six residents occupying hospital beds.

*   **Akua Adeyemi**, a Chinatown plumber, described the quiet adaptations families make behind closed doors:
    > *"I go into a lot of homes, and you can tell pretty quick when someone's down... It's not the mess or the dishes — it's the quiet. The TV off in the middle of the day. A chair pulled up by the window where somebody's been sitting a long time. Sometimes the medicine gets moved to the kitchen counter so it's right there where someone'll remember it... It's not always the hospital that carries the load. It's the family that rearranges the whole house around one sick person."*
*   **Deacon Seymour** (Manager, Oakland A's), who spent time hospitalized following a workplace accident alongside **Yu Zen** and **Maurice Franklin**, reflected on the uncounted care:
    > *"Chinatown's carrying more sickness than anywhere else in this city, and if the ledger doesn't show them, that doesn't mean they're not there. It means nobody's counting. And when nobody's counting, people get missed. That's the part that keeps me up at night."*

#### **The Mechanics of Release: John Ellis and the Arithmetic of Laurel**
*By Anthony Raines, Special Feature*

On the rear patio of a rented craftsman bungalow on 39th Avenue in Laurel, Athletics starting pitcher **John Ellis** (25) sits rolling a baseball. Despite delivering a 20-win season (20–3, 3.42 ERA) and pitching six crucial innings to win World Series Game Five, Ellis earns \$780,000 on a pre-arbitration deal and pays \$2,150 a month to a landlord who hasn't fixed the sticky back door:
> *"People read the sports pages and assume every guy who threw six innings in the World Series drives a six-figure coupe. Then I walk down MacArthur to buy milk and the guy behind the counter asks me if I’ve got enough quarters for the coin laundry. I tell him I’m good. It keeps your feet on the concrete."*

Ellis’s deep connection to Laurel is rooted in the pavement:
*   Frustrated by rotted dugouts and broken pavement at the **Laurel Recreation Center**, Ellis confronted Superior Court Liaison **Gordon Tate** on a Downtown sidewalk over capital funding disparities between waterfront venues and neighborhood parks.
*   Ellis channeled that frustration into direct community work, teaming up with smoke shop owner **Rosalind Young** on First Friday with rollers and gray primer to paint over cinderblock graffiti facing the basketball courts while AC Transit driver **Evan Lewis** steered his bus past in quiet recognition.
