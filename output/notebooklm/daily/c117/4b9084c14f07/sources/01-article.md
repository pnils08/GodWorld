# Voices: Two People, One Week Before Everything Is Different

By Ines Sorensen, The Bay Tribune

*Ines Sorensen*

Years on the overnight call-in line taught me that people give you the planned answer first. The true thing comes at the end of the sentence. This week I asked two people at the edge of a big change what the first day would look like. I wrote down where each of them ended up.

---

## Joseph Cramp, Downtown

After 28 years as a janitor for the City of Oakland, Joseph Cramp has been promoted. He told me it's "still sinking in." I asked him what his first fire would be. That's the phrase he'd used about the new job: more fires to put out before noon. I expected a story about a crisis. He gave me a supply closet.

> "First fire? Honestly, it's probably not the dramatic one folks expect. It's the supply closet inventory on the third floor of the Civic Center. That's the one that's been a slow bleed for years—nobody's kept a proper log since before I got my first set of keys. Come Monday morning, I know I'm gonna open that door and it'll look like a tornado hit a Staples. Paper towels everywhere, the good floor cleaner mixed in with the cheap stuff, and a mop bucket that's got a squeaky wheel that'll wake the dead."

He went on without hurrying. This is the part I'd tell you to read twice:

> "Twenty-eight years taught me that the big fires are just a bunch of little sparks that got ignored. You don't put out a fire by yelling at it. You show up early, you check the corners, you learn which door sticks in the humidity and which tenant always leaves the back exit propped open. You take care of the small stuff so the building can breathe. That's the whole job. The title changed, but the principle didn't. You keep the place solid, and the people in it can do their thing."

Then he got to the end of the sentence:

> "So yeah, I'll be the guy with the clipboard and a fresh roll of trash bags on Monday. It ain't glamorous, but it's mine. And I'm ready for it."

Earlier in the week he told another reporter, more plainly: "I'm not sure what comes next." That was true when he said it. What he told me on Monday is what he has in hand while he waits to find out.

---

## Jocelyn Santo, Lake Merritt

Jocelyn Santo teaches yoga, and her classes start early. She and Travis Paiz are newly married and are setting up a household in Lake Merritt. She had told the Tribune it's been "a whirlwind." I asked what was already different and what they hadn't worked out. She said "Oh, that's a good question. Let me think," and then she talked for a while.

> "One thing that's already different—really different—is the coffee situation. Travis is a pour-over guy, like, he has the little scale and the gooseneck kettle and he weighs the beans. I used to just slam a cup from a drip machine and run out the door to teach a 6 a.m. class. Now I actually sit down with him for ten minutes before I go, and it's this slow little ritual I never knew I needed. It's small, but it makes the mornings feel like ours, you know?"

Then she moved to the second half of my question:

> "And something we haven't worked out yet... honestly, it's the laundry. Or more specifically, the folding. I fold everything a certain way—tight, military-ish, because I grew up that way—and Travis just... balls things up and shoves them in drawers. We've had this gentle, silly standoff about it for weeks. We haven't figured it out yet. It's not a big deal, but it's the kind of thing you realize you have to actually negotiate when you merge two lives."

And the end of the sentence:

> "We'll get there. Probably after I teach him my folding method one drawer at a time."

Her own line for the record this week is a question she's carrying: *Still figuring out how Travis and I make this household work while everything is so new.* Her answer to me was a kettle and a drawer.

---

Joseph is thinking about a closet door and Jocelyn about a drawer. Neither of them said anything about the larger change. They were both talking about the small, specific places where it will actually happen.
