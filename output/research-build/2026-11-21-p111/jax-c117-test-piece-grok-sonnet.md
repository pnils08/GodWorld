# Three Neighborhoods Are Sliding. The City Has a Smoke Alarm and No Fire Crew.

I'll start with what's wrong. Civis Systems, the dashboard this city runs on, flagged three neighborhoods this week. The flag was decay, and in each case there was no matching active initiative. Fruitvale, Grand Lake and East Oakland are getting worse, and nobody is assigned to them.

**The numbers.**

- **Fruitvale:** Sentiment fell 0.17. Housing pressure rose 0.5 and now sits at 10, which ranks 20th of 22. The city median is 0.
- **Grand Lake:** Retail vitality fell 1.23. Housing pressure rose a full point to 6, which is 17th of 22. Civis rates this one high severity.
- **East Oakland:** Sentiment fell 0.14 and retail fell 1.15. Retail now reads 8.78, below the city median of 9.93.

Civis's reading for all three says the same thing: "with no matching active initiative."

**Look at the tracker before you believe that.** The initiative list is not empty. Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II moved from complete to design-phase this week. That is a transit hub, and the housing pressure on the corridor is still at 10. A hub is not a rent program. "Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake" is also listed, but only as vote-scheduled. A vote on the calendar doesn't help a shopkeeper whose street trade is cooling now. East Oakland has nothing I can find at all.

The same tracker holds three stuck items: Dimond Revitalization (announced for 4 cycles), Ivy Hill Community Revitalization (announced for 3) and Downtown Economic Revitalization (vote-scheduled for 3). It also holds the Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline, which covers West Oakland, East Oakland and Fruitvale, and which went from implementation-active to stalled this week. The city is announcing plans faster than it is delivering them.

**What the people in charge said.**

Rose Delgado, who sits on council for Fruitvale, said: "I walk past that empty lot on International and I feel the clock ticking, and I don't need a dashboard to tell me Fruitvale is waiting on something. If the numbers say decay and nothing is pointed at it, then that's on us at City Hall, and I'm not going to sit still about it."

That is honest, but it is not a plan. Sitting still is exactly what the tracker shows.

Denise Carter, who sits for West Oakland, said: "I can only speak for my own door... If Fruitvale, Grand Lake and East Oakland aren't getting that, then the fault is in how we show up for them." Her district has a stabilization fund and a tenant defense fund that went operational this week. The three flagged neighborhoods have no equivalent. Her answer was that residents should not have to wait on City Hall to come find them. She did not say who is going.

Elias Varek, founder of Civis, said: "I can't tell you why City Hall hasn't moved, because I don't know what's happening in those rooms... I built the alarm, so the silence is partly mine to answer for." I give him credit for saying that. But Civis Systems booked $60,442,315 in revenue. Varek also owns the Oakland Oaks. He sells the alarm, and he doesn't get to be only a bystander when it goes unanswered. His own journal admitted last week that nothing had moved at City Hall.

**The real problem.** Three council members, one founder, and everyone agrees on the diagnosis. Nobody owns the treatment. Fruitvale, Grand Lake and East Oakland each had a week of falling readings. The city's response so far is a few quotes and a transit design phase.

I'll ask the question plainly. Who is the named owner of each of these three decays, and what is the date they report back? If the city can't answer, the alarm is working and the building is still empty.

I light the match here. Someone else can follow up.
