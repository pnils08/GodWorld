### **Shift Rhythms Behind the Counter: Downtown Kitchens**
*By Mason Ortega, Food & Culture Desk*

Beyond the numbers on corporate ledgers, Tribune reporter **Mason Ortega** spent the shift observing back-of-house line cooks and servers who keep Downtown Oakland fed [1].

*   **Devon Willis**, server at **SpeedyBurger**, managed the counter through steady afternoon lunch waves [2, 3]. Willis reflected on the unwritten rhythms of service: *"The part that doesn't show up on a receipt is the rhythm of it — knowing when to step in, when to give someone a minute, when to just let the quiet settle. It's honest work, and lately I've been thinking about how much of what I do is just being there, steady, for people who need that"* [2, 3].
*   **Andre Turner**, line cook at **Miso Metro**, ran the full station on a two-person crew as tickets stacked up [2, 4]. Turner shared the reality of the line: *"Two-person kitchen means when the tickets stack up, there's no hiding. You're the whole line — grill, sauté, plating, expo... What I wish people understood is that every plate that goes out is a little piece of us"* [2, 4].
*   **Drew Lemon**, passing through during morning inspections, noted the quiet effort of both workers: *"Devon's always moving fast behind that counter... Andre, he's back in the kitchen at Miso Metro, and I can hear the sizzle from the sidewalk. Their days are long and hot, and they don't get much credit, but they keep this neighborhood fed"* [2, 5].
