# From the Tribune archive: Voices: What the A's Left Them

What The Bay Tribune published before today about the people and places in Ines Sorensen's story "Voices: What the A's Left Them". Earlier coverage, not today's news.

In Cycle 89, veteran designated hitter Vinnie Keane established a free youth baseball academy at a decommissioned West Oakland firehouse, seeking to anchor his legacy in the community even as working-class neighbors waiting on stalled municipal stabilization funds observed that athletic wealth rarely resolved flatland economic struggles. That same season, first baseman Mark Aitken attended City Council hearings regarding local-hire loopholes on waterfront projects and ran free youth clinics across municipal parks, acknowledging the persistent disconnect between major development promises and neighborhood reality (*POP-00003-Lore-mark-aitken-anchor-at-first (Y3C1)*).

Throughout the team's march to 127 regular-season wins and their seventh World Series title, general manager Mike Paulson managed an unyielding roster transition, trading tenured third baseman Martin Richards to clear room for younger bats. Following the downtown championship parade, Paulson admitted that the cold arithmetic of determining who stays and who goes began the moment the double-decker bus engine stopped, leaving everyday fans and workers to shoulder their own unresolved rent, employment, and kitchen-table costs (*The Cycle Pulse — Y3C7*; *Lore: martin-richards-deep-dive (Y3C10)*).

In subsequent cycles, left fielder Desmond Morton navigated an intense five-man outfield roster squeeze before quietly moving his residence from Jack London to Chinatown for what team logs described as a better fit. In Chinatown and adjacent flatlands, elder care coordinators and multigenerational households absorbed chronic health and economic pressures on their own, while public planning workshops and civic task forces repeatedly convened without delivering tangible changes to neighborhood stability.
