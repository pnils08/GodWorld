**Fruitvale Transit Hub: A Step Backward as Visioning Restarts**

The sun slants through the chain-link fencing surrounding the Fruitvale Transit Hub construction site at midday, casting geometric shadows on the bare earth where Phase II was supposed to begin taking shape by now. Nearby, Manjit Singh leans against a bus shelter, his breath visible in the crisp air as he checks his watch—again. "I heard about the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II moving back to active visioning," he says, shaking his head. "And it feels like we're stuck in a loop."

The reversal—from "visioning-complete" back to active "visioning" status—marks at least the third major rework of the project's planning phase since the first community workshops in 2024. No timeline adjustments or specific technical reasons for the regression have been released, but public records confirm the demotion in project maturity. Transit advocates had hoped the hub's second phase would begin aligning contractor bids this winter.

Nina Reyes, waiting for the 1R bus near the stalled site, voices a common frustration: "That's Oakland for you—always planning, never doing." Her remark cuts to the core of a broader skepticism about large-scale infrastructure delivery in the city, where multi-phase projects frequently see planning milestones slip backward even as budgets burn forward.

The critical unknown remains whether this reset reflects deeper systemic issues—like unaddressed feedback from Phase I's rollout—or merely another iteration of a famously iterative public process. Absent clarity on what specifically triggered the backslide, the community faces another indeterminate wait. Phase I's completed platforms and canopies stand ready, awaiting connections that now seem perpetually over the horizon.
