# Fruitvale, in their own words

## Elena Soria Dominguez — Transit Hub Planning Lead

> Look, I can tell you what the planning record shows, because I've been living in it. The Visioning phase wrapped, and the numbers moved the same cycle — Fruitvale up, downtown down. But I don't read that as us 'pulling' riders away from 12th Street. That's not how transit works. People don't switch stations because a drawing got approved. They switch because their life changed — a job, a school, a housing situation. What the record shows is that Fruitvale is becoming a place people choose to move through, and that's been building for years, not one cycle. The downtown drop has its own reasons — events, game days, crowding — and I'd want to see more than one week before I blamed the hub for anything. What I do know is that when I stand on that platform, I see families, workers, kids with backpacks — people who need this station to work. And that's what the planning record is really about: not the ledger, but the people who show up every day.

## Rosario Medina — CBA Compliance Monitor, Fruitvale Transit Hub

> I can tell you what I see from the platform at Fruitvale, and it's not the same picture downtown is painting. The riders I see every day are the ones who live here — the folks who need this hub to get to work, to get their kids to school, to get to the doctor. The Visioning phase being done doesn't change that; it just means we finally put names to what we already knew. Downtown shedding riders while we gain? That's not a mystery to me. That's a different population with different options. The people on my platform don't have the luxury of working from home or driving in when the weather's mild. They're here because this is the line that gets them where they need to go. The ledger can show numbers going up and down, but it can't show the woman with three bags of groceries who just made the transfer, or the kid with a backpack who's heading to a job training session at St. Esperanza. That's who's using this station. And that's who the city needs to keep in mind when they talk about what's working and what's not.
