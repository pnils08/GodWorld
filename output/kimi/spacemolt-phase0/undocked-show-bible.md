## 1. Premise & Format

*UNDOCKED* is not a game broadcast. In civic terms, it is a serialized workplace drama: a small, persistent cast of real Oakland citizens pilot ships inside SpaceMolt, and their game-telemetry turns into weekly episodes the city watches, argues about, and eventually wagers on.

- The cast is the story. Their ships, reputations, alliances, firings, and auditions are covered events.
- The rest of Oakland is the audience. Watch parties, bar arguments, and aspiration lines are part of the coverage.
- Nothing is canon for the paper until the Saturday edition. The review gate exists precisely because raw telemetry is not yet civic news.
- Episodes are serialized. Each week advances pilot arcs, succession pressure, and audience reaction.

## 3. Coverage Rules for the Media Terminal

- **Desk:** CULTURE / serialized-drama beat. Sports-desk framing is forbidden by the owning plan.
- **Canon door:** Saturday edition only. Until then, episode material is watch-party chatter, not established fact.
- **Translate, don't expose:** Never use engine words like "Cycle," "engine," or "simulation" in in-world copy. Cast members are pilots, employees, or contestants; events are in-universe actions.
- **Story over stats:** Lead with what a pilot did and why it matters to Oakland, not with leaderboards or ship builds.
- **Fourth wall is absolute.** The paper does not acknowledge that its citizens are simulated. The drama is real to the city.

## 4. Audience Texture

Jack London is the show's ground zero. Coverage should carry the neighborhood's voice:

- **Dockhouse BBQ** keeps its TV tuned to Marcus's episodes; regulars cheer, heckle, and place narrative-only bets among themselves.
- **Watch parties** at Jack London bars produce ECL lines: aspiration ("That could be me"), argument ("P Slayer threw that run"), and loyalty ("Marcus is our guy").
- **Aspiration lines:** citizens measuring their own lives against the cast's on-screen choices.
- **Argument lines:** recurring factions forming around pilots, not playstyles.
- **Wager talk:** narrative-only until a separate casino-ledger design is signed off. No balances move. No odds are canon.

## 5. Tone Guidance

The show's drama comes from what cast members *do*, not what they say.

Proof of concept: a SpaceMolt ship called *AetherWraith* flew for five months in total silence, built a -93 reputation blood feud with a pirate clan, sacked their home station twice, leveled a rival faction's station in a forty-minute solo assault (eleven kills), and then quietly mailed eighty-eight gifts of top-tier gear to strangers. It ended the season fourth-richest out of eight thousand ships.

*UNDOCKED* should chase that kind of paradox: ruthless generosity, patient grudges, actions that only make sense in hindsight. The recap voice should let behavior accumulate meaning over episodes.
