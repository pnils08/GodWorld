# The Oaks Have a Four-Game Story, and Uptown Is Still Deciding

Trenton Nawan is carrying two glasses through an Uptown bar when the Oaks finish dismantling Washington. The television is mounted above the bottles, soundless, its blue light sliding over the counter and the wet rings beneath the drinks. Nawan pauses long enough to look up, then keeps moving.

The score is 105-62. The Oaks have won their first four games, and the latest one is the loudest statement yet even with the volume off: Adash Stanley and Austin Reaves each scored 23, while the defense held the Wizards to 62.

Nobody in the bar needs the whole explanation. The room has been waiting for the next score, the next reason to believe this club is more than a new name on a screen. The mood is hungry, but not settled. Four wins can make a person lean forward. It cannot make them forget they are still deciding.

Nawan has been checking the results twice a day all week. His earlier worry has not disappeared; it has been pressed flat by the wins. ��I'm still a bit worried about the Oaks, but their 4-0 start is really giving me hope,” he says. “I checked the scores twice a day all week and was happy every time they won. Maybe they're finally getting their act together.”

He says it while setting the glasses down, as if hope is another order to deliver carefully.

The first two wins came on the road, including a 91-78 victory in Detroit in which Stanley scored 29 and the defense carried the room. The third came in New York, 115-93, with Reaves and AJ Dybansta scoring 23 apiece and Harry Hayward recording another double double. Now the fourth win has arrived from Washington, a 43-point margin that leaves the bar with less to argue over and more to stare at.

Reaves has seen that argument move beyond the game itself. “To be honest, the neighborhood's been buzzing about the Oaks' 4-0 start,” he said. “I've seen fans around here getting really excited, and it's infectious. People are talking about it while they're out and about, like when they're unloading groceries or just hanging out on their stoops. The mood feels hungry for more, like they're really craving the next win. I've even noticed some folks around here discussing the team's defense and how well they're working together. It's great to see the community coming together like this behind the team.”

Stanley calls the feeling electric. “I've felt the excitement building around the Oaks,” he said. “There's a buzz in the air, and I think people are really looking forward to seeing what they can do this season.”

At the bar, the screen moves on. Nawan returns to the counter. The glasses leave two clean crescents in the condensation, and the Oaks remain above the bottles, waiting for the next room to make up its mind.
