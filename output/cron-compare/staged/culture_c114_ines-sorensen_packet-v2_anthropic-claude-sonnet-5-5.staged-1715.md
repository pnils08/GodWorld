"The A's success is all anyone can talk about around here."

That is Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for BART and lives in Lake Merritt. She told a reporter the rest in the same breath: "I'm a huge fan, and my husband Deacon is too. We plan our whole schedule around the games."

"Will the A's trust their farm system and build something lasting, or chase flashy names with the money?"

Belle Lopez wrote that on her night page. She is a home health aide in Jack London, and the question is one she carries into the dark with her. She did not write an answer under it.

"Baseball talk always gets loud around here, but I see it like I see my jade—you don't rush a good piece just because folks are impatient."

Tio Rio is a jade jewelry artisan in Chinatown. This was his midday page, written while the talk was loud. He stopped the line there and left it standing on the page.

Three people, three ends of a city that is winning. One of them plans her week around it. One of them asks what it will turn into. One of them says he is not in a hurry.

Then there is the fourth. Omar Amici is a construction laborer in Uptown. He told a reporter this week what he sees when he walks out his door:

"To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy. The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days. I've lived here long enough to see the neighborhood transform, but this feels different. It's like the neighborhood is shifting away from the people who've been here all along."

His page also carries a question he is still holding at the end of the week:

"Will the people who've been here all along still be able to stay in Uptown as the for-rent signs keep multiplying?"
