Chinatown recorded eight incidents this cycle, the highest count among the 22 neighborhoods on the table. Ronald Williams kept to his shift at the restaurant as the numbers circulated.

"I hear Chinatown's got more incidents than other spots lately, but honestly, at the restaurant we just keep our heads down and do our work. The numbers don't really change how we move through the day."

Jonas Patel, working nearby, described the same stretch of blocks.

"Working in Chinatown, I've noticed the streets feel busier lately with more incidents, but it's hard to say if that's just my perception or if it's really worse than other neighborhoods. The clearance rates seem low, which makes people nervous."

Jacquez Tearn finished his own shift with the same reports in mind.

"Man, I hear Chinatown's got the most trouble right now, but I keep my head down, work my shift. Folks say the cops take their time clearing things up, but what can you do? Just keep moving, stay sharp."

The neighborhood's figures showed an increase of one incident from the prior cycle, a 10.5-minute average response, 23 percent clearance, a property index of 37 and a violent index of 26.
