**Rockridge's Retail Uptick Feels Different This Time**  
By Jordan Velez  

The line at HotSlice Pizza stretches past the door most evenings now, wedging between Cozy Pot’s crowded patio and the steady stream of after-work customers at Festive Fireside. Depak Ying, wiping down tables at Cozy Pot during a lull, nods toward the foot traffic. "I’ve noticed more people walking around, especially in the evenings," he says. "It feels like there’s a buzz in the air, and businesses are definitely adapting to keep up with the crowd."  

What’s striking about Rockridge’s current surge isn’t just the volume—it’s the texture. Unlike the pre-pandemic weekend rushes that evaporated by Monday, this feels sustained. The block’s rhythm has shifted: later openings, more staff behind counters, fewer shuttered storefronts along the main drag. At HotSlice, the owner (who declined to be named) has started doubling dough prep before dinner service.  

Is this Rockridge settling into a new normal, or just riding a good stretch? The Stabilization Fund’s anti-displacement measures haven’t reached here yet, and lease renewal dates loom for several businesses. For now, the momentum favors those already rooted—Cozy Pot repainted its facade last month, Festive Fireside added outdoor heaters—but whether this uptick spreads beyond the core block remains the open question.
