# Faith in the Week: Congregations Respond

Anand Krishnamurthy sits in the sanctuary of Bharat Mandir of the East Bay on a Tuesday evening, the space quiet around him. The temple in Montclair—a South Indian congregation founded in 1990 with some four hundred members—holds its rhythms in what Krishnamurthy, the pandit who leads it, carries forward: the chant, the offering, the careful tending of a tradition that does not shout.

This cycle brings the faith communities of Oakland into sharp relief. The data shows a pattern. Across the city, eight congregations have opened their doors or activated their resources since cycle 102—some in response to crisis, some in observance of holy days. At Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship in Downtown, the congregation gathered seventy-two people in C107 to open doors for community support. Temple Beth Or in Rockridge drew one hundred and four. New Covenant Pentecostal Assembly in West Oakland brought one hundred and fifty-seven. The Temescal Islamic Center expanded pastoral counseling. At Lakeshore Unitarian Universalist Fellowship, an emergency assistance fund activated with fifty-three in attendance. 

Meanwhile, the great cathedrals held their masses. Cathedral of the Living Word in Lake Merritt observed traditional Epiphany rituals with one thousand seventy-seven in attendance in C108. St. Esperanza Parish in Fruitvale gathered three hundred and seventy-four for the same holy day.

What does it mean that these congregations move at once—some toward crisis, some toward ritual—without coordination, without a single message, but with what appears to be a shared understanding that the sanctuary is a place where people come?

Eunice Marston, a faith leader in Downtown, has watched the various initiatives across neighborhoods. "I haven't had any direct involvement with the Bharat Mandir of the East Bay," she said, "but as a faith leader in Downtown, I appreciate the various initiatives and organizations that bring people together and foster a sense of community."

The question that remains unaddressed: What distinguishes the congregations that open their doors in crisis from those that observe the calendar—and what can the city learn from the different approaches to community support that these eight institutions have chosen?
