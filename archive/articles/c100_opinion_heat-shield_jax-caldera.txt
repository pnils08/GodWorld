# The Heat Shield
**By Jax Caldera | Bay Tribune Opinion**

I was sitting at Kingfish Pub on Telegraph on Tuesday night when the ticker ran across the bottom of the screen. Cy Newell to New York. Herbert Jones and an unprotected future first gone. The Oaks move up to number one. 

The guy next to me slammed his pint glass on the wood hard enough to spill half his IPA. He spent the next twenty minutes cursing Mike Paulson. He called him arrogant. He called him a tourist. He said Paulson didn't understand Oakland loyalty. 

Everyone in the bar agreed. Everyone is yelling at Mike Paulson. 

You're all yelling at the wrong guy. 

Paulson is the general manager of the Oakland Oaks. He makes the basketball trades. He's the guy who stands at the microphone and calls a rookie GM his "young Jedi." But Mike Paulson does not own the Oakland Oaks. 

Elias Varek owns the Oakland Oaks. 

Varek is thirty-eight years old. He founded Civis Systems over in West Oakland. They do sixty million in revenue a year telling the city how to model its transit and its emergency response. They hold the data. 

Then Baylight Phase II gets announced. Two billion dollars of infrastructure. Varek takes an equity stake. Then the new Athletics stadium needs a name. Civis Systems Field. Then an expansion NBA franchise lands in our laps. Varek buys it. 

Four massive pillars of Oakland's future—its emergency models, its biggest real estate development, its legacy baseball stadium, and its new basketball team. One guy. 

Keisha Ramos put out a four-part recusal protocol this cycle to address the "overlap." She says the process is clean because Civis Systems stepped back from the planning. 

Translation: Ramos is giving Varek a hall pass to leave the room while the city hands him the keys to Jack London Square, based on urban models his own firm helped build. 

And right when people might start asking questions about how much of the waterfront one thirty-eight-year-old tech founder is allowed to own, Paulson trades away a fan favorite. Suddenly, the whole city is burning up talk-radio minutes demanding Paulson's head. 

It is the oldest trick in the book. You buy a franchise, you hire a loud, aggressive GM, and you let him take all the shrapnel while you quietly buy the block. Paulson is a heat shield. He's doing exactly what he's paid to do. He distracts you with the draft board while Varek runs the zoning board. 

Look at the real estate map around the new stadium. Look at the data contracts his firm holds with the city. Do the math and tell me this is just about basketball. 

When the expansion honeymoon wears off and the city realizes it handed its waterfront to a tech CEO who already owns its emergency response data, who do you think pays the tab?
