# Henry Rivas's 1.6 Walks per Nine Is the Lowest on His Dossier. It Rests on 136.1 Innings.

Henry Rivas's 2041 career line shows 24 walks in 136.1 innings. That works out to 1.6 walks per nine and 5.54 strikeouts per walk, both worked from the season line on his dossier. Both are the best marks among his eight seasons. This memo makes one claim about them. The 2041 walk rate is a partial-season reading and does not show a command breakthrough. The strikeout rate has barely moved since 2034, and the dossier's other short season fits the same caution.

## What the table shows

**The sample.** Six of the eight seasons run between 170.0 and 187.2 innings, with 32 or 33 starts. Only 2041 (22 starts, 136.1 innings) and 2034 (23 starts, 130.1 innings) fall short of that. The 2041 walk rate is therefore compared against full seasons, and the only other short season on file is seven years back.

**The strikeouts did not change.** His 2041 mark of 8.8 per nine equals his 2039 mark and his 2034 mark. Nothing in the strikeout column marks 2041 as different. Any change is in the walk column.

**The walk column is split by team.** In his four PHI seasons, from 2034 to 2037, his walk rate ran 2.5 to 2.8 per nine. With the Athletics it has been 1.9, 1.8, 2.9 and 1.6, in order from 2038 to 2041. That is a wide spread for four seasons. The 2040 figure of 2.9 (59 walks in 184.2 innings) is the highest on the dossier. It also sits directly before the 2041 low. A one-season swing that large in either direction is variance until a second full season repeats it. The nearest full-season rate to 2041's is 1.8, in 2039 over 187.2 innings. That makes 2041 a continuation of a 2038–2039 level that 2040 interrupted. It does not stand alone.

**The two lowest ERAs are the two shortest seasons.** His 2.21 ERA in 2034 came in 130.1 innings and his 2.64 in 2041 came in 136.1. Every season of 170 innings or more on his dossier carries an ERA from 3.13 to 4.65. That pattern is consistent with short seasons running below his full-season norm. The dossier does not prove it, because two seasons are too few to make a rule.

## The peer band

Two Athletics starters from the same 2041 season give the comparison. Neither is a full-season peer for Rivas on innings alone.

- **Arturo Ramos** threw 212.2 innings with 41 walks. That is 1.7 walks per nine and 4.44 strikeouts per walk, at an ERA of 2.41 and a record of 23-1. His walk rate matches Rivas's 1.6 over a sample about 76 innings larger. That is the difference between "possible" and "carried a full workload." His 7.7 strikeouts per nine are also lower than Rivas's 8.8.
- **Benji Dillon** threw 150.1 innings with 65 walks. That is 3.9 walks per nine and 2.03 strikeouts per walk, at an ERA of 4.43. His 2041 walk rate is the weakest of the three. It is also higher than any Rivas season on file.

Rivas's 2041 walk rate is therefore ordinary for a front-end Athletics starter this season. Ramos's full-season 1.7 shows that the level is reachable at a full workload. Rivas's 1.6 shows only that it was reached in 136.1 innings.

## What is blank

The dossier holds no starts-by-month splits, no pitch counts, and no per-start walk log. Those blanks are why this memo cannot say whether the 24 walks came evenly across 22 starts or in a stretch. The dossier also carries no reason for the 22-start total, and the memo does not supply one.

## The claim and what would end it

**Claim:** Henry Rivas's 2041 walk rate of 1.6 per nine is a short-sample reading within the 1.8–1.9 range he posted in 2038 and 2039, interrupted by 2.9 in 2040. It is not a new command level.

**What would kill it:** a 2042 season of 170 or more innings with a walk rate of 1.8 or lower per nine. That would give him a second full-workload season at the 2039 level and make 2041 the confirmation of a run rather than an outlier. A 2042 line near 2.9, the 2040 mark, would settle the opposite way. In either case, a season well under 136.1 innings cannot decide it.
