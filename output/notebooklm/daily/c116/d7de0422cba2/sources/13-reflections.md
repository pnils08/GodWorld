# Piedmont Ave, in their own words

## Vinnie Keane — Former Designated Hitter, Oakland A's Legend

> Benji's not done yet, and I think that's the thing people keep missing. We came up together, we won together, and when I walked away I felt that weight lift off my shoulders — but Benji's carrying something different. He's got the clinic, he's got Rick, he's got Maya, and he's got a city that still sees him as the guy who threw three no-hitters. That's not pressure, that's a reason to stay. And for the clubhouse? Look, I was the voice in there for a long time, and Mark was the other one. Now both of us are gone. If Benji comes back, he's not just pitching — he's the bridge. He's the guy who can tell Kevin Clark what it means to wear this uniform in this city, because he's lived it. If he doesn't, then the young guys have to figure it out on their own, and that's a harder road. I hope he takes his time. I hope he listens to what he actually wants, not what the calendar says. Because the game will still be there. The city will still be there. But that feeling of being the last one standing from a group that meant something — that's a heavy thing to carry alone. I know because I just put it down.

## Lila Mezran — Journalist, Health Desk

> The city sounds like a refrigerator hum through the wall and the far-off rumble of a freight train threading through the flats. Piedmont Ave after midnight is all porch lights and parked cars, and I can hear the occasional creak of a house settling — mine included. It feels like the whole block is holding its breath, waiting for morning when the noise starts again. And I'm sitting here with my notebook open, thinking about those six beds in Chinatown, and how quiet it must be for the people who can't get one.
