**Marcus Whitfield's Court**  

The hoops at Eastside Hoopers Coalition still smell like fresh paint, though the cracks in the asphalt tell the truth—three years of sneakers pivoting against time. Marcus Whitfield stands at the free-throw line, a basketball balanced on his fingertips. "I've seen how community programs can really bring people together and make a difference in their lives," he says, watching a group of kids chase down a loose ball. The Coalition's banner flutters above them, its corners frayed from hanging in the same East Oakland wind that carries the scent of fried plantains from somebody's kitchen down the block.  

Maria Conteras leans against the chain-link fence, nodding as Whitfield talks. "I've seen that community programs in East Oakland are really making a difference," she says, folding her arms against the chill. Around them, the rhythm of dribbles and laughter fills the gaps between words.  

Down the street, the Allen Temple Food Pantry's line stretches past the bus stop. Jerome Pittman shuffles his feet near the front, rubbing his hands together. "I feel like community programs and their founders in East Oakland are really making a difference," he says.  

The neighborhood holds them close. Always has.  

What happens when the people who build these programs get tired?
