# What Happens to the 105: Oakland's C117 Incidents Without an Arrest

**By Rachel Torres, Public Safety**

Oakland logged 133 incidents across 22 neighborhoods in Cycle 117. The custody census shows 28 arrests. That leaves 105 incidents with no arrest attached. Neighborhood clearance rates for the cycle run from 19% in Fruitvale to 26% in Grand Lake, San Antonio and West Oakland.

When I was a department spokesperson, "no arrest" was never one outcome. It covered a report taken and assigned, a report taken and parked, a call logged and cancelled, and an incident entered and never written up. The public sheets don't separate these. I asked Chief Rafael Montez to do it.

## What the department counts as cleared

Montez gave me a definition with four parts. In his words, a case is cleared by:

1. An arrest with a charge filed.
2. An exception: the person is identified, deceased, or already in custody for the same offense.
3. A referral to the District Attorney on a case the department considers solved, even if the DA declines.
4. A case the complainant formally withdrew or declined to prosecute, documented.

"I don't count a case as cleared because it went cold, and I don't count it because a supervisor closed it to clean up a stack," the chief said. "If it goes cold, it's open. That's my order, and it's in the command staff review now."

That is a stricter definition than some departments use, and it is the right one to hold him to. It also means the 105 are not hidden clearances. By his account they are open cases.

## Where the chief's number doesn't hold

Montez opened his answer with a figure: "The number: 22." He described it as roughly 22 incidents "entered, triaged and dropped inside the cycle without a report chain that survives audit."

I can't reproduce that figure from the record. His own arithmetic is 133 incidents minus 28 cleared charges, which leaves 105 open. Twenty-two is also the number of neighborhoods in the city. Spreading 105 open incidents across 22 neighborhoods gives about five per neighborhood, not 22 dropped incidents in total. Either the figure is a separate count of incidents with no surviving report chain, or it is a slip between two numbers. His answer doesn't say which, and I won't print 22 as a finding until the department shows its work.

There is a second discrepancy. He said 28 incidents produced an arrest intake and that "27 of those cleared on a charge that was fined at the hood rate." A few lines later he cited a revenue entry of $125,905 "for 28 cleared charges." The census counts 28 arrests. I have asked for the 27/28 difference to be reconciled.

## What the sheets show

The census records charges equal to incidents in every neighborhood. Downtown shows 7 charges and 7 incidents. Fruitvale shows 8 and 8. So every incident carries a charge line, but only 28 of 133 reached an arrest.

Six neighborhoods had two arrests each: Downtown, Grand Lake, East Oakland, Fruitvale, Temescal and West Oakland. The other 16 had one each. Custody at the close was 52 citywide, with 24 left. The census lists none of those held as tracked residents. The 28 arrests are all other residents.

The published clearance percentages also don't track the arrest counts. Fruitvale had 2 arrests on 8 incidents and a 19% clearance rate. Uptown had 1 arrest on 6 incidents and a 21% rate. The rates may include earlier cases cleared this cycle, or an exception clearance. The sheet doesn't say. Montez's definition allows for those categories, but the public data can't show them.

The hot spots follow the same pattern. Temescal was the only neighborhood over the city's bar, at 1.15 times the median. It had 8 incidents, a property index of 50 and 2 arrests. Fruitvale was the one neighborhood the metrics flagged as easing. It had 8 incidents, up two, and the lowest clearance in the city. Montez singled it out: "Fruitvale at 19 is the floor and it's the one keeping me up at night."

## The cases that fit none of the four

The world record for the cycle includes an OARI van response in West Oakland, listed as de-escalated: "a crisis ended without cuffs." West Oakland logged 7 incidents, 2 arrests and a 26% clearance rate. I can't tell from the sheets whether that call is one of the seven, or how it was classified.

Under the chief's four categories, a resolved crisis call is not an arrest, an exception, a DA referral or a withdrawn complaint. If it enters the incident count, it ends up among the open cases by default, even though nothing is left to investigate. If it doesn't enter the count, then the incident count understates what responders handled. Either way, the gap between what happened and how it gets categorized is where these numbers get hard to read.

## What I'm asking for

- The breakdown of the 105 non-arrest incidents: report taken and assigned, report taken and inactive, no report written, and resolved on scene.
- The basis for the figure of 22, and the reconciliation of 27 versus 28 cleared charges.
- How OARI diversions are classified in the incident and clearance counts.
- A neighborhood-by-neighborhood account of which clearances this cycle came from arrests and which from the other three routes.

Montez said a cleared case is "a family that gets an answer, or it doesn't count." The 105 are the cases still waiting on an answer. The department can say how many it has, but not yet how many are still being worked.
