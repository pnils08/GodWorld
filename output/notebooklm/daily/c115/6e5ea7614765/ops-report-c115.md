### 2. Comprehensive Gate Decision & Source Verification Audit

#### 2.1. Draft 1: "The Fund Has Moved $7 Million Out of $28 Million. The Record Names Two Households." (Carmen Delaine — Investigations / Civic Desk)

*   **Draft Reporting Summary:** Carmen Delaine filed an investigative report examining the West Oakland Stabilization Fund (INIT-001). The piece notes that out of a total budget of $28,000,000, $7,000,000 has been disbursed ($21,000,000 remaining) following its 6–3 passage in Cycle 78. Delaine details matching statements from Economic and Workforce Development Director Marcus Webb and Deputy Mayor Brenda Okoro affirming that the closeout is published, the renewal draft sits with the city clerk for Cycle 116 consideration, and the 23-day processing standard holds. The piece highlights that while $7 million has moved, the official record names only two households across all historical milestone entries.
*   **Gate Findings & Discrepancies:** Copy desk audit flagged an explicit arithmetic anomaly within the official tracker: the Cycle 109 milestone row logs a $400,000 tranche described as "2 household grants, $81,188." Dividends across two grants average ~$40,594 each, leaving $318,812 within that single $400,000 tranche completely unaccounted for in the narrative row. Furthermore, desk audit noted that while the text notes the fund serves "West Oakland businesses and residents," no business count or payee ledger exists. The copy desk also noted legislative details regarding the initial vote: Council Member Ramon Vega voted yes, Leonard Tran voted no, and Elliott Crane voted yes by remote vote. Finally, the initiative tracker's renewal fields list vote cycle 108 as "Standing" with blank dollar amounts, indicating unrecorded legislative mechanics.
*   **Cross-Source Verification & Hold-Up Evaluation:** Ledger cross-examination confirms the gate's findings hold up entirely upon verification with raw municipal sources:
    *   *Initiative Tracker Metrics:* Confirms $28M total budget, $21M remaining, passed C78 under Santana's signature (Webb/Okoro authority). Milestone notes confirm C84–C90 moved $4.2M across 47 checks; C109 moved $400k (2 households, $81,188); C110–C114 executed $400k per cycle; C112 noted cumulative $5M+ disbursed; C114/C115 ordered closeout and preserved 23-day standard.
    *   *Archive & Mayoral Statements:* In C104, Mayor Avery Santana issued a statement that 12 households cleared verification and were waiting on disbursement. In C110, Webb testified before the City Council Finance Committee that 79 households were stabilized over nine months under the 23-day processing standard.
    *   *Verification Conclusion:* Supported by sources. Municipal tracking accurately records aggregate outlays ($7M total) and operational processing speed (23 days), but completely lacks an itemized, named payee ledger accounting for business allocations versus resident grants.

#### 2.2. Draft 2: "Nineteenth Street Filled Up, and the Rows Do Not Say Why" (Trevor Shimizu — Transit Desk)

*   **Draft Reporting Summary:** Trevor Shimizu reported on a systemwide transit ridership surge during Cycle 115, driven by 19th St Oakland Station boardings rising to 16,076 (an increase of 5,324 riders, the largest in the network), with on-time performance reaching 81%. Across eight monitored stations (12th St, West Oakland, Fruitvale, Coliseum, Lake Merritt, Rockridge, MacArthur, 19th St), aggregate ridership jumped 58% from 46,839 to 73,938. Parallel freeway traffic indices spiked dramatically (I-580 E at 89; I-880 S at 98; I-880 N and I-980 at 100).
*   **Gate Findings & Discrepancies:** Copy desk audit flagged a direct, unresolvable source contradiction regarding the day of the week:
    1. BART PR representative Ximena Mehta explicitly attributed the platform crowds to multiple overlapping events occurring on an "ordinary Tuesday."
    2. AC Transit operator Coen Cartier stated on his citizen page: "Wednesday's a normal workday for me, so I can't say I noticed any big jump on my runs."
    3. The underlying metric logs assign no day-of-week tag to the row.
    Additionally, the gate flagged a systemic data paradox: despite total ridership jumping by 27,099 passengers, the platform congestion metric dropped to 0.12 across all eight station rows (down from 0.30 at 19th St in C114), showing a calmer system score during peak volume. Desk audit contextualized this volume by referencing Shimizu's historical analysis, which shows 19th Street boardings swinging between 5,000 and 17,000 in past cycles, raising the question of whether 16,076 represents a temporary spike or a new operational baseline.
*   **Cross-Source Verification & Hold-Up Evaluation:** Ledger cross-examination confirms the gate's flag on the day-of-week contradiction is valid and unsupported by raw metrics alone:
    *   *Station & Traffic Verification:* Boardings verified (12th St: 14,526, +4,515; West Oakland: 9,519, +3,491; Fruitvale: 8,943, +3,880; Coliseum: 7,776, +2,980; Lake Merritt: 6,780, +2,684; Rockridge: 5,684, +2,397; MacArthur: 4,634, +1,828). Freeway indices (I-580 E 89, I-880 S 98, I-880 N 100, I-980 100) are accurate to transit logs.
    *   *Event Pulls:* Oaks game attendance confirmed (125–109 victory over Orlando Magic at KONO, 94% capacity crowd).
    *   *Verification Conclusion:* Supported by sources. Unresolved source tension requires a mandatory editor's footnote highlighting the logging ambiguity between Tuesday and Wednesday.

#### 2.3. Draft 3: "Chinatown's Sick Are Counted by the Hundred and Named by No One" (Dr. Lila Mezran — Health Desk)

*   **Draft Reporting Summary:** Dr. Lila Mezran reported on Chinatown's persistent respiratory illness cluster. Chinatown logged 135 sick residents out of a population of 2,205 (a 6.1% rate, exceeding the city's 6.0% watch threshold), leading all city neighborhoods ahead of East Oakland (117), Ivy Hill (115), and Fruitvale (109). The story contrasts this neighborhood burden against official municipal hospital logs, which show only 3 admissions, 6 occupied hospital beds, and zero patient names attached to Chinatown records. The narrative features accounts from plumber Akua Adeyemi and Oakland A's manager Deacon Seymour.
*   **Gate Findings & Discrepancies:** Copy desk audit highlighted a structural data visibility gap: out of 135 sick residents in Chinatown, municipal acute-care records account for only 6 hospital beds (and 3 admissions), leaving 129 sick individuals managed entirely within informal home care without municipal tracking, discharge dates, or patient names.
*   **Cross-Source Verification & Hold-Up Evaluation:** Historical ledger verification confirms the gate's findings hold up precisely:
    *   *Historical Trajectory Audit:* Archive records show Chinatown's sick counts moving from 142 in C112, to 140 in C113, 137 in C114, and down to 135 in C115. This confirms Mezran's thesis that Chinatown has persistently led the city in illness burden across four consecutive readings despite minimal municipal hospital tracking.
    *   *City State Hospital Census:* Confirms citywide totals of 34 admissions, 69 residents in care, and 69 beds occupied (0 tracked by name in the municipal census). Chinatown totals (6 beds, 3 admissions) match City State metrics.
    *   *Archive Verification:* Archive records from C105 confirm named hospitalizations for Deacon Seymour (Lake Merritt) and Yu Zen (Rockridge) following workplace accidents. C113 archives confirm elder care coordinator Marisio Curazo managed Chinatown's homebound illness cluster directly in residences (134 homebound in C113), supported by family caregivers and prescription deliveries.
    *   *Verification Conclusion:* Supported by sources. Municipal dashboards capture acute inpatient occupancy but remain structurally blind to home care burdens carried by families and neighborhood networks.

### 3. Newsroom Synthesis & Final Desk Action Matrix

A holistic review of Cycle 115 filings reveals four systemic tracking gaps across municipal records that directly affect newsroom operations:
1.  **Municipal Ledger Omissions:** Municipal databases consistently record aggregate dollar amounts and speed figures while failing to publish itemized payee ledgers. This is evident in the West Oakland Stabilization Fund, where $7 million has been spent without identifying business versus resident recipients.
2.  **Unbilled Utility vs. Corporate Revenue:** City ledgers track top-line revenues for large corporate entities but register zero revenue for essential specialized service firms (e.g., Marine Systems' sea-level monitoring), making small firms appear economically non-viable when shedding critical staff.
3.  **Transit Log Categorization Gaps:** Public transit logs collect volume data without standardizing day-of-week tags or accounting for how platform congestion drops during major systemwide spikes, creating conflicts between public relations statements and operator logs.
4.  **Informal Home Care Blind Spots:** Public health dashboards track acute hospital bed occupancy (6 beds in Chinatown) but remain blind to broader community illness clusters (129 residents managed in home care).

#### Actionable Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for Future Cycles

To prevent recurring verification holds and protect reporting integrity, the newsroom is instituting the following mandatory editorial SOPs effective immediately:
*   **Civic & Ledger Reporting SOP:** All investigative pieces analyzing municipal funds (e.g., INIT tracker outlays) must explicitly state in the body text whether an itemized payee ledger exists. If municipal records omit recipient breakdowns, reporters are required to note the structural omission rather than treating aggregate outlays as complete payee data.
*   **Business Desk SOP:** When reporting on service-sector or environmental firms showing zero top-line revenue on city ledgers, reporters must conduct secondary cross-referencing against municipal tax filings or public utility contract registries to evaluate unbilled civic value before citing ledger figures.
*   **Transit Desk SOP:** The transit desk will not publish day-of-week assertions based solely on source quotes when primary telemetry logs lack explicit day-stamping tags. Reporters must cross-verify platform volume against official city day tags or include standard logging ambiguity caveats.
*   **Health Desk SOP:** Health desk filings evaluating neighborhood illness burdens must include standard operational caveats distinguishing acute hospital census figures from non-hospitalized home care estimates, ensuring health dashboards are not mischaracterized as exhaustive patient registries.
