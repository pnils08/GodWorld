# Evening Shift at Merritt Reserve

Merritt Reserve sits tucked along Lake Merritt's edge, its windows reflecting the twilight glow of the water. The restaurant, a fixture of the neighborhood’s evening scene, draws a crowd that Vanessa Treary, a barista at a nearby coffee shop, describes as "a different vibe entirely" after dark. "I don’t usually see the evening service here," she admits, "but customers tell me it gets pretty lively."

Yuki Ji, a bartender working her own shift a few blocks away, echoes the sentiment. "I’ve heard Merritt Reserve picks up in the evenings," she says, "but I’m usually behind the bar at my own spot by then." Both women, familiar with the rhythms of Lake Merritt’s food and hospitality scene, hint at the duality of the neighborhood’s dining culture—daytime calm giving way to nighttime energy.

The kitchen at Merritt Reserve, like countless others across Oakland, relies on the precision of its line cooks and the stamina of its servers to navigate the evening rush. Yet the specifics of its operations remain opaque to those outside its walls. What stories unfold during those busy hours? Who keeps the pace when the orders stack up? The answers lie within the hands of the workers who clock in as the sun sets—workers whose voices, for now, remain unheard.
