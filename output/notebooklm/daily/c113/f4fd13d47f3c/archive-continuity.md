### **1. Six in Beds, One Hundred Thirty-Four at Home: Chinatown's Sick Residents and the People Who Carry Them**

#### **Direct Published Facts**
* **Chinatown Emergency Response Delays:** In Cycle 112 (Y3C8), OARI Program Director **Vanessa Tran-Muñoz** (Rockridge) documented that an emergency response call in Chinatown sat heavy because *"someone eventually came but the wait was too long,"* noting West Oakland response crews would have been faster [1, 2].
* **District Data Transparency Demands:** Tran-Muñoz is actively pushing for district-level data transparency to force City Hall to view response gaps as real streets rather than lines on a spreadsheet [2].
* **Uptown EMS Queue & Patient Files:** EMS Director **Priya Narang** (Uptown) holds 29-year-old Rockridge resident **Yu Zen’s** open file on her desk as he faces months of rehabilitation for a severe hand injury, questioning whether emergency crews arrived quickly enough [3-5].
* **East Oakland Healthcare Census:** In Cycle 112 (Y3C8), East Oakland’s hospital census logged **123 sick residents, 2 admissions, and 4 in care (4 beds occupied)**, with healthcare records counting residents without tracking or naming individuals [6].
* **Data Discrepancies:** District Attorney **Clarissa Dane** (West Oakland) reported an unresolved data discrepancy for Fruitvale, where internal sheets read **18%** while public files read **23%** [1, 7].

#### **Analytical Inferences & Unresolved Storylines**
* **Spreadsheet Metrics vs. Lived Emergency:** Program directors (Tran-Muñoz, Narang), District Attorney Dane, and Editor-in-Chief **Mags Corliss** infer that averaged municipal data and compliance claims obscure life-altering response delays and healthcare gaps on the pavement [1, 2, 4, 5].
* **Unresolved Storylines for Cycle 113:** Whether municipal leaders resolve the 18% vs. 23% Fruitvale data discrepancy [1, 8], address emergency wait times in Chinatown and Uptown [1, 8], and provide case-level transparency for homebound sick residents [6].
