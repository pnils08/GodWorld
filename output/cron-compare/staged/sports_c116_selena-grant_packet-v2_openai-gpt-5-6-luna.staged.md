The Oaks’ 80-percent shooting in Denver produced a franchise record and a 147-114 win. One game later, they lost their first home game, 126-117 to the Spurs. Those results belong to the same early-season read: Oakland’s expansion team has moved from an 8-1 record and four-game winning streak to 9-2, with the loss interrupting the run but not yet changing the larger evaluation.

The larger evaluation is unusually high. The Oaks were No. 1 in the NBA.com power rankings, one of 31 teams, before the Spurs arrived. They were 9-1 at the time of the ranking, 3-0 over the previous week, 6-1 over the previous two weeks and 9-1 over the previous month. The ranking did not change after the loss. The Oaks also owned wins over Detroit, Oklahoma City, New York and Philadelphia, each listed among the league’s ranked teams.

That is the evidence for the milestone. The evidence against premature certainty is the home loss itself.

The Spurs exposed a less forgiving version of the roster. The Oaks shot 41-for-95, or 43 percent, and went 12-for-36 from three-point range. They committed 14 turnovers. Dillon Brooks finished with 13 points but shot 5-for-19 and missed all six of his three-point attempts. Harry Hayward had 5 points, 10 rebounds and 3 assists on 2-for-9 shooting. Austin Reaves supplied 13 points, 6 rebounds and 6 assists, with four turnovers. Wendell Carter Jr. had 14 points, 12 rebounds and four offensive rebounds, but went 6-for-11 at the line.

The bench still produced 44 points, led by T. Jerome’s 23 points in 21 minutes. Jerome made 6 of 8 shots and all six of his three-point attempts, adding two steals. That contribution matters because the Denver result also came from the reserves: Jerome scored 28 with 10 assists, while Hayward added 14 points in 19 minutes and made all five of his shots.

The contrast is not simply good offense versus bad offense. Denver’s line was 63-for-79 from the field, 19-for-26 from three and 40 assists against five turnovers. The Spurs game had 14 turnovers and a much narrower margin for error. A first-year team can look structurally advanced when its passing and shooting reach the Denver level; it can look unfinished when those same advantages disappear.

The defensive question remains open rather than answered. The 8-1 season-state carried a 109.9 points-per-game offense, an 89.6 opponent average and a plus-20.3 margin. Four opponents had been held under 85 points: Charlotte, Detroit, Toronto and Washington. But the Spurs scored 126. The record now says 9-2, and the ranking still says No. 1. The next useful test is not whether the Oaks can recover from one loss. It is whether the Denver version and the Spurs version can both belong to the same team.
