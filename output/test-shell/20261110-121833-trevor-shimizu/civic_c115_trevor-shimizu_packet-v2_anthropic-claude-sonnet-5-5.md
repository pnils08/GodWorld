# Eight stations rose together on an ordinary weekday, and no single row explains it

Coen Cartier drives for AC Transit. He told the Tribune that Wednesday was a normal workday for him, and that he "can't say I noticed any big jump on my runs." What he did notice was the stop at 19th. "I did see more folks waiting at 19th than I'd expect on a regular afternoon," he said. "Most of them got on heading toward the Lake Merritt and downtown stops, not so much out to the edges."

The Cycle 115 Transit_Metrics row for 19th St Oakland supports him. Ridership was 16,076, up 5,324 from Cycle 114's 10,752. The row's notes read "game day crowds" and "above-average ridership." On-time performance was 81%, up a point. It is the largest gain among the eight stations on the record.

**What moved**

All eight stations rose. Total ridership across them was 73,938, against 46,839 the cycle before, a gain of about 58 percent.

The on-time column matters as much as the ridership column. More riders did not cost the line its punctuality at any station on the record. Fruitvale gained 15 points while adding 3,880 riders. West Oakland, at 77%, is the weakest on-time figure on the sheet, and it still improved by 3 points.

**Reading the prior row**

The Cycle 114 rows carry factors that this cycle's do not. The 19th St row for that cycle lists "weekend" and "First Friday." This cycle's frame is an ordinary weekday with no citywide cut. That helps explain why Cycle 114 was the low base. It does not explain the size of the jump. By the row's own rule, a station that moves more than the citywide rule names its own reason, and each of the eight does.

The reasons are different at each station. 19th St lists a downtown game day and events in Downtown, Lake Merritt, Temescal and KONO. It also lists Baylight District construction-active and crowded platforms. 12th St adds Jack London to the game-day list. Fruitvale and Coliseum name events in East Oakland, twice over. Fruitvale also carries the Phase II visioning, which is in design. Rockridge cites events in Piedmont Ave and Montclair, MacArthur events in Temescal, and Lake Merritt events in Lake Merritt and Chinatown.

The streets rose with the platforms. I-880 North and I-980 each read 100 on the traffic index, up 37 and 38. I-580 East gained 51, the largest corridor jump. Grand Ave gained 38 and International Blvd 37. No chaos car touched the trains or the agencies this cycle, so no disruption explains the crowds. Weather was mild at 55°F, the fifth clear cycle running.

**What the people near the platforms say**

Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for the BART Oakland Division, called it an accumulation: "it's not one big thing — it's a bunch of little things stacking up." She named the Oaks game downtown, Baylight, and events in Lake Merritt, Temescal and KONO. "The operators were saying 19th Street felt like a game day crowd even though it wasn't a game day for them," she said. "It's just what happens when the whole city decides to move at once."

Elena Soria Dominguez, the Fruitvale Transit Hub Planning Lead, did not take credit for the hub. "I can't say the visioning is what's pulling those extra riders in — we're still in the design phase, no shovels in the ground," she said. She pointed to East Oakland's events instead. She added that the construction workers on their breaks under the overpass "see it from the platform every day."

**Analysis**

Each cause is real on its own row. The difference is that eight rows now carry different causes and moved the same way at once. Mehta's stacking explanation fits that. The sheet shows the stack but does not weigh it, so it cannot say whether any one cause carried more than the rest. If every station on the line moved together on an ordinary weekday, what changed in the city that no single station row can name?

Cartier's afternoon run points one way: toward Lake Merritt and downtown, which is where the rows say the events were. At 19th, the people waiting boarded the train and left.
