# Temescal's Eight: Why an Arrest Is Not a Clearance

*Rachel Torres, Public Safety*

Three numbers sit side by side on the city's Cycle 117 sheet for Temescal: 8 incidents, 2 arrests, 3 people in custody at the close. A reader could easily turn them into a rate, such as two arrests on eight incidents, or a quarter solved. That reading is wrong. Police Chief Rafael Montez said so when I asked him about it.

"An arrest is an intake, not a closure, and clearance is worked after the fact, not on the same clock," he said. "I'm not going to let those two get conflated in print."

I'll lay out how each number gets counted, and which question the city couldn't answer.

## The incident

Temescal logged 8 incidents this cycle, up 2 from Cycle 116. It is the only neighborhood the sheet flags as a hotspot, at 1.15 times the city median. Incident count doesn't explain the flag. East Oakland and Fruitvale also logged 8. What sets Temescal apart is its property index of 50. The next-highest on the sheet is 42, in Adams Point, Eastlake, Glenview and San Antonio. Temescal's violent index is 25, which is mid-pack.

Incident categories matter for what follows. The OARI director, Vanessa Tran-Muñoz, described how a wellness response is logged. "It counts in our incident total just like any other," she said. "But the outcome we record isn't 'no arrest,' it's 'resolved without enforcement.'" So a call can be counted as an incident and still never produce a charge, an arrest or a custody entry. OARI vans are dispatched in West Oakland, Fruitvale and East Oakland, not Temescal. The same principle applies anywhere, though. The incident total counts calls the department classified. It does not count offenses that ended in an arrest.

## The arrest

The Care and Justice census tracks the same neighborhood differently. Temescal shows 8 charges, then 2 arrests, then 3 in custody at the close. None of the three are tracked residents. There are more people in custody than arrests this cycle, so some of the three were not booked in this window. Citywide, the Chief gave the flow: 28 intakes, 24 released, 52 in custody at the close. If those figures are complete, about 48 people were in custody at the start of the cycle. That is my arithmetic, not a figure the department gave me.

An arrest records that someone was taken into custody. It says nothing about which of the 8 incidents the person is connected to, or whether any incident is closed.

## The clearance

Temescal's clearance is listed at 22%. I can't reconstruct that from the cycle's numbers. Two arrests against eight incidents is 25%. Eight incidents can't produce 22% as a whole-case ratio. The sheet doesn't say whether the figure is a rolling rate, a cycle ratio or something else, and the Chief didn't define it. I am treating it as an unexplained figure.

The Chief did describe what clearance depends on. "The cases that get an arrest are the easy ones — the caught-in-the-act, the named suspect, the domestic where the parties know each other," he said. "The rest — property crime, no witness, no camera, a description that won't survive a lineup — those don't close in one cycle, and when follow-through thins, they don't close at all."

Temescal's profile is mostly property. If the Chief's account holds there, the neighborhood's arrests are probably the least informative number about its hotspot. The cases pushing the index to 50 are the kind that arrest counts miss.

## What the department couldn't say

I asked how many of Temescal's 8 incidents were closed with no report filed or reclassified. Montez declined to estimate. "That's a records question and the honest answer is: I'd have to pull the case files to tell you, and I'm not going to improvise a figure for the Tribune on something that specific. If the number is zero, I'll say zero."

I think that is the correct answer. It also means the city has not told the public whether any of Temescal's 8 incidents were closed administratively, reclassified or left without a report. He invited a written request for the dispositions with case numbers, and I am filing one. Until the file comes back, that gap stays open.

Samantha Viteri, who leads Internal Affairs, spoke about classification generally and not about any file. "When a classification gets challenged, it lands on my desk because somebody on the street or in the ranks felt the paperwork didn't match what actually happened," she said. "We don't start with the file, we start with the doubt." Her point is that a classification is a judgment someone made and can challenge. It is not a measurement.

## The citywide range

Clearance across the 22 neighborhoods runs from 19% to 26%. Fruitvale is the low at 19%. Grand Lake, San Antonio and West Oakland are at 26%. Temescal's 22% sits in the middle. Montez called the range "not good enough" and said he had said so internally. He gave three reasons:

- Open cases compete for the same investigators.
- The investigative bench hasn't grown to match the work.
- Residents in some neighborhoods don't talk to police. "That's not an excuse, it's a diagnosis," he said of Fruitvale.

He said command-staff reviews are pressing case backlog and follow-through by district. He kept the patrol strategy balanced, saying no district has worsened sharply enough to justify moving resources.

One more oddity on the sheet. It lists Fruitvale as the only neighborhood "easing" this cycle, even though its incidents rose by 2 and it has the lowest clearance. The sheet doesn't give a basis for that label, and I haven't been able to find one.

## How to read the numbers

- **Incidents** are classified calls. They include calls that end without enforcement, such as OARI wellness responses.
- **Arrests** are intakes at the jail. They are not linked on the sheet to specific incidents.
- **Custody** counts everyone held at the close, including people booked in earlier cycles.
- **Clearance** is a separate figure. The sheet doesn't say what it is calculated from.

Until the Temescal dispositions arrive, the honest summary is that 8 incidents, 2 arrests and a 22% clearance rate describe three different things. The department has said it will give a case-by-case count if asked in writing.
