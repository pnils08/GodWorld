**The Adjustable Keystone**  
_by Simon Leary_

Melton Neilon folds his arms against the dugout railing at Laney Field, watching the grounds crew drag the infield between games of a routine doubleheader. A baker by trade—Neilon owns the sourdough stall at the Old Oakland farmers market—he sees baseball’s adjustments through the lens of his own craft. "It’s like adjusting the hydration in a dough," he says, squinting at the bullpen where Benji Dillon has just begun his warmup throws in relief. "Sometimes you just need to tweak things to keep everything rising right."

The A’s don’t rebuild, and they don’t pivot. At 126-35, with two consecutive wins humming beneath them like subway tunnels under a standing city, their blueprint tolerates fine-tuning without requiring drama. Dillon’s move to the bullpen isn’t an earthquake—it’s a joist shimmed by a carpenter who knows the house will settle. 

This is what dominance looks like when it’s not performing dominance: a roster so structurally sound that role changes read as maintenance, not reinvention. The wins accrete like brickwork. Even the questions—*Does this feel like being stored in the toolbox until the precise moment you're needed?*—quiet against the steady pour of victories. 

A pitcher adjusts his grip. A baker adjusts his water ratio. A team, already more load-bearing than any narrative could animate, adjusts its parts in daylight.
