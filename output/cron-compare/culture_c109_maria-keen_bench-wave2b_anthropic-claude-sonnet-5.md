## Culture — Fruitvale

Lucia Polito keeps two sets of hours in the same afternoon — the practitioner's and the founder's — and on the Fruitvale roll this cycle they land on the same line. She is listed as an Aura Wellness Practitioner. She is also, since C101, the founder of the Postpartum Oakland Network, one of three active programs the record still carries for this stretch of the flats: hers for new mothers, a youth-sports circuit called the Fruitvale Summer Speed Series that has run the same length of time, and a mutual-aid line that follows the transit corridor through the neighborhood.

"I've seen the impact that community programs can have on people's lives, and I feel grateful to be a part of it," Polito says. "As someone who's founded a program myself, I understand the passion and dedication that goes into creating something that truly helps others. It's amazing to see the difference these programs make in Fruitvale, and I'm always looking for ways to support and connect with the people who make them happen."

The neighborhood she's talking about added people this cycle — eleven more students, thirty-nine more adults, eight more seniors than the count before it, 408 and 1,497 and 339 now on the books. Numbers don't found a program. Somebody has to want the thing enough to start it, and then enough other people have to notice.

Rick Walker noticed. "I've seen the community programs in Fruitvale really making a difference, and I think it's great that people like Lucia Polito are founding initiatives that help our neighborhood," he says. Rafael Pilgrim says much the same thing in his own words — "I appreciate the work of founders like Lucia Polito who are dedicated to helping our neighborhood thrive" — and Tomas Renteria has heard about the postpartum work specifically, even if he hasn't needed it himself: "I've heard Lucia Polito's work with the Postpartum Oakland Network is really helping new moms in the area."

Three men, three separate sentences, the same name recurring in all of them. That's what a founder looks like from the outside — not a ribbon-cutting, just a name that keeps coming up when neighbors talk about what's working.

What none of the four of them say — what the roll doesn't say either — is whether three programs are enough for a neighborhood that just picked up fifty-eight more people in one cycle. A youth league, a mutual-aid line, a postpartum network: those are the three things Fruitvale decided were worth keeping running since C101. Whether they're the three things this fuller Fruitvale needs now is the question nobody on this record has answered yet.
