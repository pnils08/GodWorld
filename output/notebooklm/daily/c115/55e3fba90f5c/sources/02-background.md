# Earlier in the Tribune: Temescal's 314 Students Stayed Put, and the Table Ranks Them Last

Seven Cycles ago, in Cycle 108, civic reporter Luis Navarro documented Rick Walker, a former restaurant line cook teaching K-8, grading spelling tests before the first bell and criticizing the lack of official follow-through for working-class school communities.

Six Cycles ago, in Cycle 109, reporting examined Temescal's public school, where the school quality index registered at 6.8—the lowest across Oakland's neighborhoods—and per-student funding stood at \$11,500. While 85 percent of Temescal students graduated, only 57 percent tested college-ready. Patricia Nolan, who taught in Temescal for decades before retiring, warned that celebratory graduation ceremonies masked students leaving school without being equipped to write a proper paragraph or think critically. Student Jun Leu reflected from a bench near the gym that students did not track district ratings or graduation tables, focusing instead on finishing daily assignments and getting through classes where some teachers pushed while others let things slide. That same cycle, Walker waited near a vacant lot two blocks from his school as crossing guards guided children across the street, noting that municipal promises for school surroundings repeatedly stalled without explanation.

Two Cycles ago, in Cycle 113, Walker observed that classroom conditions grew increasingly difficult as rising rents squeezed local families, leaving school staff to navigate neighborhood strain with limited institutional support.
