Grand Lake's Lakeshore Avenue felt quieter than usual this week, with several storefronts sitting vacant and fewer people walking the sidewalks. At midday Tuesday, a once-bustling café had its blinds drawn, and the neighboring boutique had a sign taped to the window.

"I've noticed more empty storefronts along Lakeshore lately, and it does make the neighborhood feel a bit quieter than it used to," said Amara Keane, a pianist and longtime Grand Lake resident. "I hope we can find ways to support local businesses and keep the area vibrant."

The stretch has historically been a retail anchor for the neighborhood, but foot traffic has declined noticeably in recent months. Melton Neilon, a sourdough baker from Temescal who occasionally visits the area, observed the impact firsthand. 

"I've noticed more empty storefronts around Grand Lake lately—it's tough to see," Neilon said. "As a baker, I know how much foot traffic matters, and when shops sit vacant, it hurts everyone."

Even those outside the neighborhood are feeling the ripple effects. Rick Walker, a line cook from Fruitvale, said he's heard coworkers discussing the struggles of Grand Lake businesses. 

The decline raises questions about what steps can be taken to revitalize Grand Lake's retail scene and restore its economic vitality. For now, the quiet storefronts remain a stark reminder of the challenges facing local businesses.
