# Fruitvale Hit the Ceiling on Housing Pressure. The Hub Gets a Design Phase. Rent Gets a Press Statement.

Fruitvale's housing pressure is 10. That is the top of the scale. Median rent is $2,504 against a median income of $100,178. Civis Systems says no active initiative matches it.

The Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II moved from visioning to design phase this week. The housing pressure got an interview.

I'm not blaming the hub. I'm asking why a neighborhood gets a $232 million authority for the building and nobody for the people living next to it.

**The dashboard flagged it. Nobody picked it up.**

The Civis reading is plain. Fruitvale's sentiment fell 0.17 and its housing pressure rose 0.5. Pressure sits 20th of 22 neighborhoods, against a city median of 0. Grand Lake is flagged high: pressure 6, up a full point, retail vitality down 1.23. Both are tagged "with no matching active initiative."

I asked the man who built the dashboard. Elias Varek, founder of Civis Systems, told us: "I've seen the dashboard flags for Fruitvale and Grand Lake, but I'm not aware of a specific city official assigned to act on it; I need to follow up with some contacts."

So the machine raises the alarm, and its own founder doesn't know who answers it. Fine. That's honest. It's still the problem.

**Council saw the gap too.**

District 6's Elliott Crane said it outright: "The lack of initiative behind addressing housing pressure on Fruitvale and Grand Lake, while the Transit Hub advances, creates a gap in accountability for me as it suggests we're prioritizing infrastructure over resident needs."

Crane represents Uptown. Fruitvale's own council member is Rose Delgado, District 3. She said she wants "real community engagement, not just a checkbox" on the hub. I want that too. But a documented community review of schematics is not a rent plan.

**I took it to the Deputy Mayor.**

Brenda Okoro, Deputy Mayor for Community, answered. Two things stand out.

First, she disputes me on Grand Lake. She says it is not unmatched. She says Grand Lake Commercial Stabilization is certified at $2.5 million, with a full-council roll call on the Clerk's record. The initiative tracker on my desk still lists it as vote-scheduled. One of those is stale. I'm flagging it and not picking a side. Even if she's right, it's a retail fund. Grand Lake's pressure is a housing number.

Second, on Fruitvale she gave a straight answer. "Who owns it: I do." She said I should print her name "rather than someone's who can be reassigned."

I'm printing it.

Her plan has three parts:

- **The West Oakland Stabilization Fund won't stretch to Fruitvale.** She called its charter West Oakland only. If Fruitvale wants a fund, she says, that's "a proposal to Council."
- **Existing institutions get pointed at the pressure.** She named St. Esperanza's job-training session, Gurdwara Singh Sabha, and the Fruitvale Transit Corridor Mutual Aid. She also named the hub's compliance monitor and planning lead. She admitted this is not a substitute for a fund.
- **Numbers come before the exodus.** Fruitvale's migration flow reads 0, so no one has left in the ledger yet. She called that "the window."

Credit where due. She didn't dodge. She also said a press line doesn't fix a $2,504 rent.

**Here's what it adds up to.**

Fruitvale has a pressure of 10 and a sentiment of 0.19. Neither number has an initiative behind it. It has a named owner as of this week, and a promise to argue for a fund someday. Nothing is filed, scheduled or funded.

Compare the neighborhoods that already have instruments. The Tenant Defense Fund went operational in West Oakland. The Stabilization Fund is paying out there. Those are real, and they're what a plan looks like. Fruitvale has a window and a phone number.

One caution on the data. The Civis reading calls Fruitvale "decay." The map row shows its trajectory as growth, with momentum at 10. Both can be true. A neighborhood can be booming while the people in it get squeezed. That is the whole story.

**What I'd want answered.**

1. When does a Fruitvale proposal go to Council, and who writes it?
2. Who tracks displacement before it shows in migration flow? Okoro's "window" is the one thing the city can't get back.
3. Does the hub's community benefits monitor, Rosario Medina, have any housing mandate? She asks whether the city will count the riders who actually live here. Count the renters too.

The hub is moving. Let it. But a city that can fund a $232 million station and can't name a rent plan is choosing, and Fruitvale will notice.

I'm not doing a follow-up. Somebody should.
