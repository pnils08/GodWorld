# The Fund Has Moved $6.6 Million in West Oakland. The Public Record Names Two Households.

On the blocks of West Oakland where the flats have built out around the long-tenure homes, a stabilization check is a paper envelope that either arrives or doesn't. The Initiative_Tracker's Cycle 114 row for the West Oakland Stabilization Fund says the Month-12 envelope was sent. The tranche was $400,000, "executed under the 23-day standard." The tracker does not say who opened it.

That is the gap this ledger entry is about. It is not the gap between $28 million and zero. It is the gap between a running total and a list of recipients.

**The money, reconciled.** The Fund's budget total is $28,000,000. Remaining is $21,400,000, last disbursement Cycle 114. By subtraction, $6.6 million has left the Fund. That figure is derived from those two tracker fields. It is not a number the tracker prints. The milestone notes reach the same place by another route. At C108 they recorded 47 checks, about $4.2 million, disbursed under Deputy Mayor Brenda Okoro's authority across C84 to C90, with roughly $23.8 million remaining. Six $400,000 tranches then follow in the notes, C109 through C114. That is $2.4 million, which takes $23.8 million to $21.4 million. The arithmetic closes. The C112 note's "cumulative $5M+" is consistent with it, though it understates the later total.

**The names.** Across that whole run, the tracker's milestone notes break out exactly one tranche by recipient. The C109 month-eight release, $400,000 "in West Oakland," lists "2 household grants, $81,188." That leaves about $319,000 of that tranche in the notes unitemized. No business is named anywhere in the row. The C84–C90 checks are counted, not named. A search of the paper's own prior coverage, including editions 79, 83, 84 and 85, turned up the authorization and the "where's the check?" questions. It turned up no list of recipients.

**The scope question.** The scope has been rewritten on the page more than once, and the tracker shows how. At C108 the notes recorded the Fund as chartered for long-tenure residents priced out as the flats built out. They called it "not hood-wide relief." At C109 a correction followed: "the Fund serves West Oakland businesses and residents," and the "residents only" reading was superseded. The same entry says Downtown is outside the Fund's area. That belongs to INIT-008, which is proposed and not voted.

So the Fund has two constituencies on paper. The C113 note attached a "charter scope preservation directive" to the Month-12 approval. The C114 note directs closeout publication and charter renewal drafting "with West Oakland business-and-resident scope intact."

Okoro put the same commitment in her own words in her Cycle 114 office statement. "The Fund renew's with its scope intact and its Month-12 records public, because trust requires both delivered support and documentation."

Her sentence sets two tests. One is delivered support, and the tracker's tranche lines speak to it. The other is documentation, and the published record to date shows only partial documentation. Both tests apply to a $28 million fund that Mayor Avery Santana proposed and signed at Cycle 78. The Council passed it 6-3 that cycle. Ramon Vega voted yes. Elliott Crane voted yes remotely. Leonard Tran voted no. The tracker lists Vega as the swing voter.

**The clock.** The next scheduled action is "Publish Month-12 closeout and complete charter renewal draft," Cycle 115. The tracker's renewal fields (RenewalVoteCycle, RenewalAmount, RenewalOutcome) are blank. So the renewal has a draft date and no vote date. Nothing in the row says when the Council would see it, or at what amount.

The city is not short of cash. The treasury took in $5.2 million this cycle, and its balance closed at $121.5 million, up from $116.3 million. That does not answer the Fund's question. It only rules out one excuse. The Fund's remaining $21.4 million is a ledger line, and the tracker says nothing about whether it is constrained by anything other than the pace of the tranches. The paper had no basis to call that pace slow, and it does not.

**What the record shows and doesn't.** It shows a processing standard, 23 days, held across several cycles. It shows a tranche size, $400,000, that has not changed across six tranches. It shows a scope corrected once and promised intact twice. It does not show a single business receiving a grant. It names two households' worth of money, $81,188, and no household.

A renewal drafted on totals can preserve scope in the charter text while the public cannot check what the scope bought. A renewal drafted on a roster can be checked.

So the question for the Month-12 closeout, due next cycle: will it name the businesses and residents who actually received stabilization money, or just report a total?
