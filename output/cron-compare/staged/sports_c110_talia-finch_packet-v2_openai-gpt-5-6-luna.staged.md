# The Oaks Still Have to Become Somebody’s Team

**Talia Finch | Bay Tribune Sports**

Jessie Hess is holding a drink in both hands in an unnamed bar in Dimond, watching the screen with the sound down. The room has the particular quiet of people waiting for a reason to lean forward. The Oaks have supplied the result; the people around them are still deciding whether to supply the belonging.

“Honestly, I'm still trying to process how the Oaks did in their preseason games,” Hess says. “I was really looking forward to seeing what they could do, but that loss to the Miami Heat was a letdown. I want to believe they can turn it around, but it's hard to shake off the feeling that it's going to be a tough season.”

The final preseason loss left the Oaks at 0-5 in preseason and winless at 0-9 through rookie camp and preseason. The room mood is frustrated, and the fan read is low. Those numbers sit on the screen like a tab nobody wants to pick up.

The question in Dimond is not only whether the Oaks can win. It is whether a club can ask people to care before it has given them a night they want to retell.

Daniel Cloak keeps the door open. “I still feel that way about the Oaks, they're really making an effort to be part of the community and that's something positive. I've seen it with the preseason games, they're trying to reach out and it's great to see.”

That is the second story in the room, quieter than the losses: an effort can be visible and still not be enough to make a fan. The Oaks are trying to reach the neighborhood while the neighborhood is trying to work out what, exactly, it is being reached for.

Melton Neilon has a different measure. “Honestly, I still don't really know what to make of the Oaks analysis. As a sourdough baker, I'm more focused on getting my bread just right than following sports news. I've got my regulars to think about and making sure they've got their favorite loaves. That's what keeps me up at night, not the Oaks' preseason games.”

Rick Walker has heard the talk but has not found a clean answer in it. “Honestly, I'm still trying to keep up with the news like everyone else, but it's tough when the team's performance is all over the place. I've heard some folks talking about Oaks analysis around the neighborhood, seems like people are trying to make sense of what's going on with the team.”

Wilson Shepard has reassured that the full team has yet to suit up as one. That leaves the Oaks with an opening, but not yet an attachment. Can the first games turn effort into a reason to come back?

The screen keeps glowing above the bar. Hess lowers her drink. Nobody cheers. Nobody leaves.
