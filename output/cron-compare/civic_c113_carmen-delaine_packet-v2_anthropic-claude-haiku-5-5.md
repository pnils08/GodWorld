# West Oakland's Twelve Checks

Brenda Okoro stood at the front of the tenant hall on Tallow Street with a folder open on the lectern and did not look at it. The room held about forty folding chairs, and most were full. A radiator under the window knocked twice and then went quiet. Okoro read the figure from memory, the way someone does after saying it at many kitchen tables.

"Twelve months, twelve checks, $5.6 million," she said. "And West Oakland's oldest residents are still in their homes. That's the race against displacement, and we're still ahead."

The line is hers, and it is on the record. The number in it is hers too, and the ledger does not carry it.

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund was proposed by Avery Santana and carried a $28 million budget. It passed in Cycle 78 by a 6-3 vote, with Ramon Vega voting yes, Leonard Tran voting no, and Elliott Crane voting yes remotely. The record for that vote does not itemize the other six seats, so this account does not place them. Santana signed the measure the same cycle. The Fund is now in its disbursement phase, and the tracker shows $21.8 million remaining. Subtracting that balance from the $28 million budget leaves $6.2 million drawn. The Cycle 112 milestone describes "cumulative $5M+ disbursed." Okoro says $5.6 million. Three figures, all inside the same Fund, and none of them is reconciled in the record this cycle.

That gap is the first thing the ledger has to say. It is not an accusation. It is a question about which number the Fund's own books will stand behind.

Cycle 113 closed the eleventh month. The tracker logs the Month-11 audit as published and the Month-12 disbursement of $400K as approved, with a directive to preserve the charter's scope. The last disbursement of the cycle has therefore been made, and the Fund's next scheduled action is the Month-12 execution and the drafting of the charter renewal, in Cycle 114.

At the lectern, Okoro moved from the numbers to the people. "The Mayor names our housing unit for West Oakland's improvement," she said. "I'll take the recognition, but the credit belongs to the block associations and tenant leaders who did the daily work." Several people in the chairs nodded. Nobody spoke. The hall has no public-comment line in the record this cycle, and none was called.

Across town the same month's accounting was being spoken differently. Avery Santana, in her own words on the record, put it this way: "Five million dollars in households' hands — and the books are open for anyone to read." She repeated the figure in a second statement, adding the 47 households. Denise Carter, speaking for Council District 1, used the same count: "Five million dollars kept 47 families in their homes — that's Oakland keeping its word." Three officials, one figure, and a 47 that appears in two of the three statements and in none of the tracker's entries.

The City Treasury shows the wider money flow for the cycle: $5.1 million taken in, including the weekly $5 million budget allocation, and a $116.3 million balance at close, up from $115.4 million. It also shows $4.2 million paid to the West Oakland Tenant Defense Fund. That is a separate account under a separate vote, and this piece does not merge it with the Stabilization Fund. The two funds share a neighborhood and a set of tenant organizations. They do not share a ledger line.

Okoro's third quote in the record is the one that will matter in Cycle 114. "Charter renewal isn't a blank check," she said. "It's a recommitment to the exact cohort the Fund was created to serve." The tracker records a scope directive attached to the Month-12 approval. It does not publish the renewal text, and it does not define the cohort in the language a renewal would use.

So the record can show what the Fund has disbursed, what is left, what was approved, and who has claimed it. It cannot yet show whether the charter language keeps the Fund's original long-tenure scope or narrows it. It cannot show whether the 47 households in the mayor's and council member's statements are the same households the Month-12 check reaches.

Okoro closed her folder at the end of the meeting. The radiator knocked again. Outside on Tallow Street the porch lights were coming on, one house at a time.

When the charter renewal is filed and the Month-12 money clears, who will be asked to show which households it reached, and will their count match the one already on the record?
