# Forty-One in the Room at Bay Pure Land, and a Pantry That Predates the Count

Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple, in Chinatown, opened its doors for community support in Cycle 109. The Faith Ledger's event row for that cycle is short. It lists the temple, the tradition, the event type "crisis_response" and the description "congregation opens doors for community support." It gives an attendance of 41 and a status of "occurred." The row doesn't say what the crisis was. It doesn't say who came through the door, what they were given, or who stayed after the 41 had gone.

Two cycles later the same ledger has the temple hosting a community health fair, with attendance of 38. One row shows doors opened in an emergency. The other shows doors opened on a planned day. Neither row says what the room held.

Kenji Tanaka is listed in the city ledger as a senior pastor and faith leader in Chinatown. He was careful about what he knew when asked about a congregation across town. "To be honest, I don't know much about Foothill Baptist Tabernacle," he said. "I've seen that faith communities in Oakland, like the Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple in my own neighborhood, can be a source of comfort and support for people. I appreciate the role they play, even if I'm not directly familiar with their specific activities or events."

That is an honest answer, and it describes the city's faith life fairly well. Congregations do their work in their own neighborhoods, and the people next door often see it only as a lit window or a line at a door.

## The big building in East Oakland

Foothill Baptist Tabernacle is the other end of the scale. The registry row lists a Baptist congregation of 5,000 in East Oakland, founded in 1919 and led by Dr. Ophelia Brenner. Its character is recorded as "anchor institution, community programs," and its status is active.

The event rows show how the building's year runs. Thanksgiving in Cycle 97 drew 2,659. The Christmas service in Cycle 101 drew 3,179, and the next three Christmas cycles each held above 2,700. Those are holiday crowds, and the sanctuary fills for them.

From Cycle 106 the ledger's rows for Foothill change. They record work, not holy days. A community health fair drew 608 in Cycle 106. A food pantry serving neighbors in need drew 503 in Cycle 107. A youth program graduation drew 519 in Cycle 108. A second health fair drew 572 in Cycle 109. Each of these is a fraction of the holiday crowd, and each is a working-week number: people who came because they needed something or were handing something on. The Community Programs sheet lists the Foothill Baptist Tabernacle Food Pantry as a mutual-aid program in East Oakland, running since Cycle 78. That is well before the program sheet's other East Oakland entries, which begin at Cycle 101. These include the Mark Aitken Youth Baseball League, the Eastside Hoopers Coalition, the Coliseum District Vendor Incubator and the East Bay Harm Reduction Alliance.

Brenner describes the work in terms of growth. "I've seen our community engagement grow significantly. We have various programs that cater to different age groups and needs," she said. "For instance, our expanded tutoring program, which is now visible with posters outside St. Esperanza Parish, has been a huge success. It's wonderful to see the mercado buzzing with activity as people grab lunch between train transfers. Our congregation is actively involved in supporting local initiatives, and we're committed to being a positive presence in Fruitvale."

Marcus Whitfield, a youth basketball coach who founded the Eastside Hoopers Coalition, saw the same posters. "I'm not really sure how Eastside Hoopers Coalition interacts with faith organizations in East Oakland," he said. "I founded the Coalition, but I haven't really been involved with any faith organizations directly. I've seen some posters for tutoring programs outside St. Esperanza Parish, but that's about it."

So the tutoring program is known to its pastor as a success, and to a neighbor who runs a youth program as paper on a wall. Both are true. The distance between them is the useful finding.

## What the other rooms did this week

The week's other rows are holy days. Cathedral of the Living Word, at Lake Merritt, recorded Epiphany attendance of 1,185 in Cycle 107, 1,077 in Cycle 108 and 1,265 in Cycle 109. St. Esperanza Parish in Fruitvale recorded 427 and then 374. Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship Downtown recorded 237 at Epiphany in Cycle 109. In Cycle 107 it also recorded a crisis response of its own, with 72 attending and the same description Bay Pure Land would later carry.

Two congregations, two cycles apart, each opened its doors for a crisis. The Packet names neither the crisis nor the cause. It does not say whether the two events are connected, and I won't suggest they are.

## What isn't established

The ledger records that people came. It does not record what was done for them. It does not say whether the 41 in the Chinatown temple were neighbors in trouble, volunteers or both. It does not say who the pantry's regulars at Foothill are, or whether the tutoring posters near St. Esperanza have put a single child in a chair. The question for Foothill is plain: what difference do its programs make to the people who live near them? The row can say 503 came to the pantry in Cycle 107. It cannot say what each of them carried out.
