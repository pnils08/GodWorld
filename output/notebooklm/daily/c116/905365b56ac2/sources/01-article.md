# Uptown, the Second Half of the Sentence

By Ines Sorensen, The Bay Tribune

"It's a double-edged sword - more energy, but also more work."

Priya Narang said that to a reporter this cycle. She directs EMS and lives in Uptown. The paper's C113 and C114 pieces carried the front of her answer, the packed bars and galleries and the jar on her counter for season tickets. This is the part she added after it: "As EMS Director, I see the impact firsthand; the increased activity and gatherings mean more calls and more people to respond to."

She didn't leave it there. "Still, it's hard not to get caught up in the excitement."

She was answering what the A's winning season does to her neighborhood. On her own page, after work, the same week, she was somewhere else. "Ariana's chart is still on my desk. I don't know why I keep coming back to it—the staging, the timeline, the fact that we got her in as fast as we did." She wrote about the board too, and the two men on it: "Deacon's hand and Maurice's lungs, both of them sitting right on top of the board like proof that the systems we're supposed to run on have cracks in them." Then she wrote: "I'm tired of answering for cracks I didn't make, but I'm the one who has to make sure the next crew doesn't walk into the same hole blind."

The page doesn't say how the excitement and the board sit in one head. It leaves them next to each other.

"The corner store on 23rd got its newspaper delivery early, and the guy stacking them left the rubber bands off this time. That's it. That's the whole…"

That is Evan Morello, an actor who lives in Uptown. It's his morning page, C115, and the page cuts off there. He gives you a corner store, an early delivery and a missing rubber band, and then he stops. Nothing in the page says what the whole of it was.

"To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy."

Omar Amici, a construction laborer in Uptown, said that to a reporter again this cycle. The paper has printed that opening more than once, in C113 and C114. This time the answer ran a sentence longer than it did before: "The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days. I've lived here long enough to see the neighborhood transform, but this feels different. It's like the neighborhood is shifting away from the people who've been here all along."

Then he added one more line.

"It's like the neighborhood I knew is being priced out."
