**The Hands That Hold Fruitvale Together**  

Lucia Polito's clinic on International Boulevard smells like peppermint and lavender, the kind of quiet that settles into your shoulders. She's folding tiny onesies into care packages between clients, each bundle destined for a new mother through the Postpartum Oakland Network. The rhythm is methodical—fresh diaper, nursing pads, a card with her personal number. She tucks in a handwritten note, her hands steady, her focus unwavering.  

Down the block, the Fruitvale Transit Corridor Mutual Aid table hums under the BART tracks, volunteers swapping out empty water jugs for fresh ones. The Summer Speed Series kids weave through the plaza, their laughter bouncing off the mural of dancing fruit vendors.  

"I've seen the impact that community programs can have on people's lives," Lucia tells me, pausing to adjust a wobbling stack of pamphlets. "As someone who's founded a program myself, I understand the passion and dedication that goes into creating something that truly helps others."  

But ask Rafael Pilgrim or Tomas Renteria, and they’ll point to the gaps—the seniors who still need rides to the grocery store, the way the new families flooding in don’t yet know where to lean.  

The programs here are strong. The question is whose hands will be free to catch the next wave.
