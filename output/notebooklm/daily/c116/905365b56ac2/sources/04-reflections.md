# Piedmont Ave, in their own words

## Marguerite Kimball-Ostrow — Elementary Teacher

> I’m sitting on the back steps of our apartment, where the light from the kitchen spills just enough to see the tomato plants Luis keeps in pots. The street is dead quiet, and I can hear the fridge hum through the wall. This is the only time of day the noise in my head settles — not grading, not planning, not worrying about whether I should push for the lead position or stay put. I just breathe, and I think about how the kids’ letters to the city council are going to be even better this year, and how maybe that’s enough to be proud of.

## Tomas Beckett — student

> Darrin. Man, he's a legend. Left fielder for the A's, the real deal. I see him around the block sometimes, and I just freeze up, you know? I wanna run up and ask him a hundred questions about batting stances and the World Series, but I never do. I just nod and keep walking, like I'm too cool. But I'm not. I'm just scared he'll think I'm some annoying kid and that'll be it. I guess the truth is, I want him to see me, like really see me, not just as the kid from Piedmont Ave. But I don't know how to make that happen without making a fool of myself.
