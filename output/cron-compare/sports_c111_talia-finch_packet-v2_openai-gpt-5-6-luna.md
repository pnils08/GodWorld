# The Oaks Have Not Won the Room Yet

**Talia Finch | Bay Tribune Sports**

Melton Neilon is talking over the clink of a glass in an unnamed Jack London bar, the kind of place where the game can be on without owning the room. He is a fan, he says, but not the sort who wants to turn following the Oaks into a second job.

“Look, I'm a fan—I plan my whole life around the games—but the analysis side of things? That's a different beast. Between keeping my starter alive and running the bakery, I just don't have the bandwidth to get bogged down in the stats. I'd rather just watch the game and hope for the best than try to decode some column in the Tribune.”

That is the Oaks’ early-season problem in its most ordinary form: not hatred, not devotion, but the work of deciding whether this team belongs in the part of a life that already has other claims. The ground read around the Oaks is uncertain. The room mood is frustrated. The club’s losing has become something GM Elliot Abraham has had to address with media before the season is set to start next cycle, while questions hang around the team, including whether Draymond is coming back to the Oaks. Abraham acknowledged the losing and offered reassurance.

No one in this bar looks like they are panicking. They look busy.

Rick Walker, who follows the talk around the neighborhood between grading papers and everything else, gives the other half of the room’s answer. His confidence is not certainty. It is motion.

“Yeah, that's still where I'm at. Look, I'm a fan, I love the game, but between grading papers and everything else, I don't have the time to be a full-time analyst. I hear the talk around the neighborhood and I follow the news, but honestly, it's just a lot to keep up with. I'm just riding the wave like everyone else.”

That wave has not carried the Oaks into unquestioned belonging. It has carried them into conversation, which is a smaller thing and, for a new club, maybe the first thing that matters. Melton wants the game without the decoding. Rick is willing to keep moving with it, even while admitting he cannot keep up with every turn.

Abraham’s reassurance may be a strategy for holding the room steady. It may also be the shield a struggling early start requires. From where I sit, the answer is not in the media room. It is in the pause before somebody chooses the Oaks game, the glance back at the screen, the decision to care when nobody has demanded it yet.

The television keeps playing without sound. A glass settles on the bar. Nobody leaves.
