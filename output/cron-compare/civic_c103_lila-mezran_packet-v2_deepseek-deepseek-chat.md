# Temescal Health Center Construction Prompts Neighborhood Questions  

Construction is underway at the Temescal Community Health Center, approved earlier this cycle, but residents are already raising questions about its accessibility and neighborhood fit.  

Melton Neilon, a sourdough baker whose shop sits blocks from the site, worries about parking. “Where are all those extra cars gonna go when my customers are already circling the block for a spot?” he asks. “Ask them how they’re gonna keep Temescal from turning into a traffic nightmare.”  

For Kevin Nelson, a line cook, the concern is scheduling. “Who’s it really built for—people with flexible hours or the ones burning the candle at both ends?” he says. “Nobody’s talking about how working folks like me are supposed to get there during lunch rush or after a double shift.”  

Kaila Raimon, another sourdough baker, highlights language access. “Half my customers at the bakery are immigrants who need doctors who speak Spanish and Vietnamese,” she says. “Nobody’s talking about whether it’ll actually have enough bilingual providers.”  

The center’s approval marks progress, but its construction phase raises practical questions about how it will serve Temescal’s diverse needs. Residents wait for answers on access, logistics, and community fit.
