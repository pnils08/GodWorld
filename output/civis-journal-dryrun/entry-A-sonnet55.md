I own the instrument, so I read its misses before its hits. This week the clearest miss is in how it reports mood.

Three districts, Fruitvale, Downtown and Grand Lake, show the same fault. The burden on each is climbing, and the city's stated mood has not moved with it. In Fruitvale, students, adults and seniors are all showing up in greater numbers, and so are sick residents, yet recorded incidents fell. Downtown and Grand Lake went the other way on incidents, which rose in both. The mood reading sat flat in all three. When two readings of one neighborhood disagree, one of them is lying by omission. Until Civis can say which, I won't tell anyone the mood is steady. A city that reads itself calm while the load builds underneath will miss the point where the load tips.

The second thing is quieter and bothers me more. The same signal keeps returning with no answer downstream. Nobody at the other end is picking it up, or the instrument cannot see them doing so. Those two cases look identical on my desk, and they mean very different things for Oakland. A signal that fires again and again with nothing answering it has stopped being information. It is just noise we are paying to hear.

Third, Temescal Community Health Center has advanced. That is good news, and I am wary of how fast I want to count it. Sick resident presence in Temescal fell in the last reading, and I will not credit the center for that. An advance on paper is a promise. The instrument has to show whether the service reaches the people it was built for, and so far it can't.

The last item is a caution aimed at us. A remedy applied earlier moved past the mark we expected. Overshoot is not success, and I will not report it as one. It tells me the calibration was off, and a result we can't explain is one we can't repeat.

The move for the coming weeks: Civis will take the mood reading apart in Fruitvale, Downtown and Grand Lake and test why it stays flat while the underlying burden shifts. We will also build a proper way to follow Temescal Community Health Center from approval to people actually being served. Until then I'm publishing the gap in the record. The city should ask the instrument harder questions, and we owe it answers it can trust.

Elias Varek
Civis Systems Journal
