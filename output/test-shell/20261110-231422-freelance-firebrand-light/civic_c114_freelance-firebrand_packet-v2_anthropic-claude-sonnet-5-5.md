# Rockridge Wine Bar Says "Steady." The Map Says Decay. Who Answers?

Tom Jilner was wiping down the counter at his wine bar in Rockridge, with the door propped open and the retail strip going past. A woman stopped to look at his chalkboard. Two guys in good jackets walked by with the look of people still learning the block.

"I've noticed new faces around my wine bar, people from Downtown exploring Rockridge," Jilner told me. "The retail strip is still lively, although BART delays can be a bit of a hassle for some customers. Overall, business feels steady."

Steady. I wrote it down and then I went and read the map.

Neighborhood_Map has Rockridge at a Sentiment of 0.03. The city median is 0.085. That ranks Rockridge 17th of 22. RetailVitality reads 10.98, which is 8th of 22 and above the city median of 8.265. That part matches Jilner's strip. But the audit flags that retail number as down 2.16 from where it stood. A full strip that is shrinking is not the same thing as a full strip. The audit also has HousingPressure at 5, up 1.000. The city median is 0. Rockridge ranks 17th of 22 on that one too. The map's own trajectory column calls this "growth," and the same row lists median rent at 4048.

So one row says growth, and the audit says decay. The audit also says no active initiative, and no mitigator, owns the break. I checked the roster. Grand Lake has a Commercial Stabilization plan. Ivy Hill has two initiatives. Dimond has one. Rockridge has nothing. I searched my own columns and the paper's prior coverage for Rockridge and found nothing. This is the first time I'm writing it, and that's the point.

Down the strip I asked Yu Zen what the street feels like. Yu is a commercial insurance broker, so he reads risk for a living.

"To be honest, I've seen some mixed things in Rockridge lately. On one hand, our retail strip is doing well, and it's nice to see new folks from Downtown checking out our neighborhood. But at the same time, I've noticed that with BART delays, commuters are getting a bit frustrated. As someone who's been around for a while and has a family to think about, it's made me wonder if we're doing enough to keep our neighborhood livable for everyone. I've been thinking a lot about community and what's important since my accident, and Rockridge feels like home, but I want to make sure it stays that way for everyone."

"Doing enough" is the whole question. Yu asked it out loud, and nobody on the record is assigned to answer it.

Naomi Sterling, the senior pastor, wasn't buying any gloom. "From what I see, Rockridge is still vibrant. The retail strip is bustling, and while BART delays can be frustrating, it doesn't seem to be dampening the atmosphere much," she said. "We've also had new residents moving in from Downtown, which is nice to see." She's right about the strip. The retail number backs her up. It doesn't speak to sentiment, though, or to the housing pressure.

Maya Torres-Dillon teaches high school science and sits on the Bay Conservation Alliance Advisory board. She came closest to the number. "To be honest, I'm a bit torn about the changes in Rockridge. On one hand, it's great to see the retail strip bustling and new residents exploring the neighborhood. It's a sign of life and energy. On the other hand, I've heard from friends who bought homes here last year that the first year was really tough, and that's concerning."

Translate that. A neighborhood can look alive from the sidewalk while the people who bought into it are struggling, and the only record of the struggle is a housing-pressure reading with no program attached. Foot traffic is a mood. A pressure reading of 5 against a city median of 0 is a bill that comes due.

I don't have a prior comparison to prove how fast any of this is moving. I don't have an office on record saying a word about the mismatch. Those are the two things I couldn't get, and I looked.

The map files Rockridge under District 7. Council member Warren Ashford, CRC, holds that seat. Nobody I can find has said whether Rockridge's revitalization is truly underway or just a numbers game.

So I'll ask the office the map points to. Councilmember Ashford, the map says decay and the program roster says nothing. Who owns Rockridge?
