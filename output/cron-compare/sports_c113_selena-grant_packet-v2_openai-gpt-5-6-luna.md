# Oaks’ 4-0 Start Has Become a Rotation Test

**Selena Grant | Bay Tribune Sports**

Adash Stanley’s 23 points, six rebounds and eight assists in Washington completed the Oaks’ move to 4-0, but the record is more revealing than the latest scoring line. The expansion franchise has won four straight after a winless preseason, and each result has supplied a different answer about how this group can function.

The first answer was Stanley as the primary engine. In the 91-78 win over the Pistons in Detroit, he scored 29 points while Ausin Reaves added 12 points, seven rebounds and 10 assists. AJ Dybantsa contributed 14 points and nine rebounds, and Harry Hayward scored 12. The Oaks won with Stanley carrying the largest offensive load and Reaves organizing the rest of the attack.

The next result widened the picture. In a 115-93 win over the Knicks in New York, Reaves scored 23 points and collected nine rebounds. Dybantsa also scored 23, while Hayward produced 10 points and 10 rebounds. Stanley finished with 16 points, 11 rebounds, nine assists, two steals and two blocks. That line matters because it describes a player affecting the game across the floor without requiring the same scoring total as the opener.

Washington then supplied the sharpest evidence of the early build. The Oaks defeated the Wizards 105-62. Stanley scored 23 points with six rebounds and eight assists. Reaves scored 23 points and added five rebounds, while Dybantsa had eight points and seven rebounds. The margin and the distribution show a team that did not need one fixed scoring script to produce its strongest result of the start.

That is the useful distinction between a hot streak and a developing structure. The record is now 4-0, and the franchise’s stability is marked as growing. Those facts establish the result, not its permanence. They do show that Stanley has already been able to lead through scoring and all-around production, that Reaves has supplied both creation and rebounding, and that Dybantsa and Hayward have provided different forms of support across the opening wins.

For an expansion team, the early test is not whether one good night can be repeated. It is whether the next night can be won when the leading contribution changes. The Oaks have moved from Stanley’s 29-point opening performance to balanced production against the Knicks and then to a 43-point victory over Washington. The numbers support a team with more than one workable route.

The front office still owes the harder answer: what, exactly, is the key to the Oaks’ sudden success? Is it Stanley’s control of the offense, Reaves’ two-way distribution, the supporting production from Dybantsa and Hayward, or a rotation that can preserve all three? A 4-0 record establishes that the build has started well. It does not yet identify which part of the build can survive when the first streak ends.
