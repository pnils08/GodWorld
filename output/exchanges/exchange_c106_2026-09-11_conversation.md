**Vinnie Keane:** "Hey, you walking home from the community center?" I ask, falling into step beside Amara. "Saw the parade last night—those kids with the light-up costumes reminded me of our first summer in this neighborhood." My hand finds its way to the small of her back, automatic as breathing.

**Amara Keane:** "Yeah, just finished up a session with the youth orchestra," I say, leaning into your touch. "Those costumes brought back memories, huh? Remember how we'd sit on the stoop and watch the neighborhood come to life in the evenings? Seems like forever ago." I glance up at you, catching the familiar crinkle at the corner of your eyes. "Feels like the neighborhood's changed so much since then."

**Vinnie Keane:** "Changed, sure, but still ours," I murmur, my thumb tracing circles on her back. "Just like that beat-up piano of yours—still plays the same songs, just got a few more scratches." I nod towards Port Grind where the regulars sit. "Still got our spots. Still got our rhythm." There's a pause as I watch the lake shimmer through the trees. "You free for breakfast tomorrow? Could grab coffee at the Kaiser cart before your morning rush."

**Amara Keane:** "Always time for you," I smile, squeezing your hand. "Though I swear that Kaiser cart guy still thinks I'm going to finally order something other than black coffee after five years." I pause as we pass the empty storefront next to the theater, my brow furrowing slightly. "You know what I miss? That little florist that used to be here - the one that always had peonies in spring."

**Vinnie Keane:** "Yeah, Miss Eunice's place," I say, slowing our pace. "Used to buy you those peonies every damn birthday until she retired. Now I gotta trek to Temescal like some kind of pilgrim." My laugh comes out rougher than I meant it to. "You remember that time Rico bet me I couldn't name all the flower types? Lost twenty bucks because I called a ranunculus a 'fancy buttercup.'"

**Amara Keane:** "God, I'd forgotten about that!" I laugh, the sound bouncing off the brick storefronts. "Rico never let you live it down either - left a packet of ranunculus seeds in your locker every opening day for three seasons." My fingers tighten around yours. "Think he's still doing that up in Seattle?" We cross the street as the new streetlights flicker on, their glow painting the sidewalk in warm circles.
