I sat at the counter at the West Oakland bakery where the flour still hung in the air from the morning bake, the TV above the register showing the final possession again and again. The Pacers had just taken it 104-100 after we clawed back from twenty down. The place was quiet the way it gets when nobody wants to be the first to name what just happened to the perfect start.

Melton Neilon wiped his hands on his apron and shook his head. “To be honest, the Oaks’ loss still has me a bit down, but I’m trying not to overthink it. I mean, I’m a baker, not a stats expert. I just enjoy watching the games and cheering them on. The A’s storming back from 20 down was thrilling, but that final score… it’s tough. I guess that’s just how it goes sometimes.”

Daniel Cloak leaned on the counter next to him. “Yeah, that’s still how I feel. The Oaks trying to be part of the community is a positive step. I was at the community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub, and it felt like they were really listening. Maybe the Oaks can be like that too, you know, more involved locally.”

Some fans will tell you this is just one road loss on a young team, that 4-1 still beats every preseason expectation we carried. They’re wrong. That comeback energy Adash Stanley talked about after the game—“The loss stings, but you could feel the energy in the stands still. We came back from 20 down, so there’s a lot to build on. The guys fought hard, and that’s what matters”—is exactly what makes the final score cut deeper. We saw the fight. We saw Austin Reaves say he’s proud of the response and that we can’t let one loss get us down. Then the buzzer sounded anyway.

I keep hearing the same thing in my own head: we left it on the road. The perfect start is gone. What’s left is the dread that this is the version of the Oaks that shows up when the margin gets thin—the version that storms back but never quite closes it. We still believe the fight is real. We just need to name the fear before the next one drops, or the stands will start bracing for the same ending every time.
