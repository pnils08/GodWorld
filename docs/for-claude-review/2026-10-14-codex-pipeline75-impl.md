## 1. `proseUnion` — HOLD (wrong citizen can join)

`extractPlayerNames` accepts two or three capitalized words, so a Notes fragment such as `Jake London Square` can enter the pool (`scripts/sportsSubstrate.js:182-201`, `:498-513`). `resolvePlayer` then falls back to the **first** same-surname ledger row whose role *or name* matches a broad sports expression (`scripts/sportsSubstrate.js:204-229`). The `proseUnion` gate tests only whether that resolution produced a POPID (`scripts/sportsSubstrate.js:518-529`); it cannot distinguish the mentioned person from an unrelated sports citizen with the same surname. In a read-only, in-memory synthetic probe, Notes `Jake London Square was busy` resolved to a different `Test Square` pitcher with `POP-99999`. That is a wrong-person association despite the current test's unresolved-fragment assertion (`scripts/buildTanyaSlice.test.js:116-126`). If `NamesUsed` is empty, the `explicitNames.length` condition also admits **unresolved** prose fragments as players (`scripts/sportsSubstrate.js:520`). Require an exact identity match (or explicitly verified alias) for prose-only names, and enforce that rule even with empty `NamesUsed`; reject ambiguous surnames. `buildTanyaSlice` is the only caller passing `{ proseUnion: true }` (`scripts/buildTanyaSlice.js:92`); Simon, Anthony, Hal, and the Oaks callers omit it (`scripts/buildSimonSlice.js:71,76`, `scripts/buildAnthonySlice.js:366`, `scripts/buildHalSlice.js:440`, `scripts/buildOaksBeatSlice.js:67`, `scripts/buildOaksGroundSlice.js:66`).

## 2. Tanya Notes facts and speech — FIX (provenance/visibility)

StoryAngle and up to three Notes chunks become `prewrite.anchorFacts`; a sentence over 480 characters is silently skipped and the four-fact limit silently stops further chunks (`scripts/buildTanyaSlice.js:55-76`, `:92-94`, `:118-125`). W1 imports surviving chunks as `FACT`, but silently drops a chunk beginning `CASINO:`, `CITY MONEY:`, or `DEBT:`, or containing `do not/never` followed by one of the listed verbs (`scripts/livedExperiencePacket.js:351`, `:376-387`). W3 carries W1 FACTs forward (`scripts/livedExperiencePacket.js:860-861`, `:925-929`); these appear in `manifest.approvedFacts`, **not** `manifest.approvedQuotes` (`scripts/livedExperiencePacketV2.js:296-317`, `:336-355`). Rhea's exact approved-quote list comes only from that quote manifest (`scripts/cron-rhea-gate.js:424-430`). Thus `Stanley shared that …` in Notes can be used as a factual paraphrase but is not approved speech. With no approved quote, W3 instructs the writer to attribute no person's statement (`scripts/livedExperiencePacketV2.js:395-396`), and its deterministic audit rejects quoted runs of at least five words even if they occur in an approved fact (`scripts/livedExperiencePacketV2.js:603-625`). Short record-derived scare quotes may pass that audit; this does not make them approved speaker quotes. Preserve Notes as feed claims while marking attributed speech as paraphrase-only, and surface skipped/truncated chunks so the writer's evidence loss is visible.

## 4. `proseOnly` word count — FIX (false thin flag)

`draftWordCount`, engine-term scan, and verbatim scan all use `articleContamination.proseOnly` (`scripts/cron-desk-run.js:1401-1412`, `:1425-1429`). That helper truncates at the first `***` divider or line starting with twelve `#` characters, after its INTAKE split (`scripts/articleContamination.js:85-91`). A normal article with a mid-body Markdown divider can therefore have a full body counted only up to the divider, fall below 150, and become flagged after Rhea PASS (`scripts/cron-desk-run.js:1404-1407`, `:2955`). A later engine term would also be invisible. Use an INTAKE-bounded body extractor for these checks, without the legacy divider stops, or prove those dividers are forbidden in article prose.

## 5. Tamsin verbatim check — SHIP as WARN, with expected false positives

Lives facts explicitly restate `Lived_Events` and `LifeHistory_Log` event text, including `kindLine` and the recorded next event (`scripts/buildLivesSlice.js:161-183`). The check selects precisely those source labels, then reports an exact six-word run or one sentence containing at least 80% of four or more unique content words (`scripts/cron-desk-run.js:1398-1400`, `:1425-1453`). This is qualitative, since the first unattended Tamsin packet has not run. `verbatim` is excluded from `checkFlags` and only logged/recorded (`scripts/cron-desk-run.js:2910-2914`, `:3082-3088`), so it cannot itself flip `pass` or block Tamsin.
