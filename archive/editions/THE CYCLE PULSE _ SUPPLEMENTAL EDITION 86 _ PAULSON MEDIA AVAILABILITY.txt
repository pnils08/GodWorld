PAULSON ENDS GOLDEN STATE SPECULATION: “THERE HAVE BEEN NO CONVERSATIONS”

Bulls GM gives clearest statement yet on Warriors rumors, says focus is championship

By Selena Grant | Skyline Tribune Bulls Beat

Mike Paulson sat down in front of fifteen reporters Wednesday afternoon and did something he hasn’t done in six months: he answered the question.

“I’ve never had a conversation with Golden State about a position,” Paulson said. “I don’t know where that started but I can end it here.”

The speculation has followed him since the All-Star break. Golden State traded for Giannis Antetokounmpo without a permanent general manager. The Warriors’ interest in Paulson — the architect of Oakland’s six-championship dynasty — was treated as fact in national coverage. Chicago watched. Chicago waited. Chicago braced.

On Wednesday, Paulson said there was nothing to brace for.

“There have been no conversations or even an interaction,” he said. “I appreciate the interest but that’s not likely to happen.”

When asked why he let the speculation run for months without addressing it, Paulson was direct.

“I owed it to this organization to not make it about me during a season. We were winning. We’re still winning. If I come out in February and make a statement about my future, that becomes the story instead of 56 wins. These players earned the attention. Not my contract situation.”

The Bulls are currently the one seed in the Eastern Conference. They’ve clinched a playoff spot. Seeding is not yet locked.

When asked what success looks like for this postseason, Paulson offered one word before leaving the podium.

“Championship.”

His contract status remains unresolved. He said he would address it after the season ends.

“I’m not negotiating anything in the middle of a playoff push — that’s not fair to the players or the front office.”

But the larger question — what comes next for a man who spent twenty years building in Oakland and two years building in Chicago — remains open.

“At some point you ask yourself what the next thing is that’s worth building,” Paulson said. “I don’t have an answer for you today. But I’ll know it when I see it.”

TWO CITIES, ONE ANSWER: PAULSON ON OAKLAND, CHICAGO, AND WHAT STAYS

“Oakland is never a closed chapter for me.”

By Selena Grant | Skyline Tribune Bulls Beat

The Golden State question was the headline. But the substance of Wednesday’s media availability was something quieter — a general manager explaining, for the first time publicly, how he holds two cities at once.

Paulson grew up in Chicago. Bridgeport. The Red Line to school. Romano’s at seven in the morning. He left, built a dynasty in Oakland, and came back three years ago to build something new in the city that raised him.

“What I built in Chicago — the roster, the record — that’s professional,” Paulson said. “What Chicago gave me is personal. Those aren’t the same thing, but they’re connected.”

He was asked about Oakland — about his continued presence at spring training, at civic meetings, at Danny Horn’s wiffleball tournament. His answer was unambiguous.

“Oakland is home now. That’s never going to change. My kids will grow up there. My family is there. The A’s are — that’s a lifetime thing, what we built there.”

He attended spring training in Mesa this week at Deacon Seymour’s invitation. He watched Vinnie Keane, Benji Dillon, and Isley Kelley in their final camp. He sat in the dugout tunnel and watched how the players talked to each other.

“I wanted to see if Deacon had the room,” Paulson said. “That’s the thing you can’t measure from a box score — whether the manager has the clubhouse. Deacon has the room.”

When asked to compare what he built in Oakland to what he’s building in Chicago, Paulson drew a distinction.

“Different animals. Oakland was twenty years. I inherited a franchise with history and baggage and a city that had been told it didn’t deserve nice things. We proved that wrong six times. Chicago was a blank page — two years, a new roster philosophy, a city that was ready for something but didn’t know what yet.”

He paused.

“If this team wins a title, it proves the model works fast. Oakland proved it works deep. I’m proud of both. But they’re not the same thing.”

BRIDGEPORT EXHALES

Paulson’s statement reaches the neighborhood that raised him

By Talia Finch | Skyline Tribune Chicago Ground

The news traveled fast on 31st Street.

Paulette Okafor heard about Paulson’s statement from a coworker at lunch. She works school administration in Bridgeport. Last week, she told me she’d been keeping her phone face-down because she didn’t want to see the notification that Paulson had left. This week, phone face-up.

“I texted my sister,” Okafor said Wednesday evening. “Just the quote. ‘No conversations.’ She sent back a bunch of exclamation points. That’s all we needed.”

Raymond Polk, a 58-year-old contractor who grew up three blocks from Paulson’s childhood home, was less surprised.

“I told you,” Polk said, sitting on the same stool at Calhoun’s Tap where he’s watched Bulls games for thirty years. “He’s from here. You don’t leave twice.”

Paulson addressed the neighborhood directly in Wednesday’s presser. He was asked about Okafor — by name, from coverage in this paper — and about Polk’s comment that “he’s a man who leaves.”

“I read it,” Paulson said. “Those aren’t strangers to me. I grew up on those blocks. I should have said something sooner. That’s fair. I’m saying it now.”

He talked about taking the Red Line to school. About Romano’s at seven in the morning. About what it means to build something in the city you came from.

“When Paulette says she had her phone face-down because she didn’t want to see the news — that’s not a fan being anxious. That’s family. I feel that.”

Okafor heard that quote on her commute home.

“I don’t know him,” she said. “He doesn’t know me. But he said my name. He said he felt it. That’s — I don’t know. That’s something.”

The Bulls play their next game Friday. Paulson will be in the building. Bridgeport will be watching.

Phone face-up.

Names Index: Mike Paulson (Bulls GM), Paulette Okafor (Bridgeport resident), Raymond Polk (Bridgeport resident), Talia Finch (Reporter)

FROM MESA: PAULSON SAYS SEYMOUR “HAS THE ROOM”

A’s GM emeritus offers first public assessment of first-year manager

By Anthony | Bay Tribune Sports

Mike Paulson was in Mesa this week.

That’s not unusual — he’s been splitting time between Oakland and Chicago all spring, attending Baylight sessions and civic events in between Bulls games. But this trip was different. He came because Deacon Seymour asked him to.

“Deacon asked me to come watch his guys,” Paulson said Wednesday from Chicago. “I wanted to see Kelley and Keane and Benji in their last camp. That’s personal, not professional.”

Paulson sat in the dugout tunnel for two innings Tuesday. He watched how the players talked to each other. He watched Seymour navigate a clubhouse with three farewell seasons, four rookies pushing for roster spots, and a franchise in transition.

His assessment was unequivocal.

“I wanted to see if Deacon had the room. That’s the thing you can’t measure from a box score — whether the manager has the clubhouse. Deacon has the room.”

It’s the first public statement Paulson has made about Seymour’s tenure. The A’s are 10-3 in spring training, their best preseason record in four years. The regular season opens in three weeks.

When asked whether Oakland remains part of his future, Paulson didn’t hesitate.

“Oakland is never a closed chapter for me. I don’t know what that looks like going forward. But if you’re asking whether I’m done with that city — no. I’ll never be done with Oakland.”

The dynasty’s final season begins soon. Keane, Dillon, and Davis in green and gold for the last time. Seymour holding it together. And somewhere between Chicago and Mesa, Paulson watching from the tunnel.

LETTERS TO THE EDITOR
############################################################

HE SAID MY NAME

To the Editor:

I don’t know Mike Paulson. He doesn’t know me. We’ve never met. I’m just someone who works at a school and watches basketball and worries about things I can’t control.

But he said my name. In a press conference. He said he read what I told your reporter. He said he felt it.

I’ve been a Bulls fan my whole life. I’ve watched teams come and go. I’ve watched people leave. That’s just how it works. You get used to it.

This felt different. This felt like someone was listening.

I don’t know what happens next. Maybe he stays, maybe he goes after the season. I can’t control that. But today, my phone is face-up. And that’s enough.

— Paulette Okafor, 47, Bridgeport, School Office Administrator

-----

THE MODEL WORKS FAST

To the Editor:

Paulson said something in that presser that I can’t stop thinking about. He said Oakland proved the model works deep. Chicago proves it works fast.

Twenty years in Oakland. Six championships. A dynasty.

Two years in Chicago. Best record in the East. Playoff bound.

If he wins a title here, that’s not just a ring. That’s proof you can build something real without waiting a generation. That changes how every franchise in the league thinks about rebuilding.

I don’t care if he leaves after. I care that he showed us what’s possible.

— Damien Roberts, 31, Bronzeville, Line Cook

-----

WHAT ROMANO’S SMELLS LIKE

To the Editor:

He remembers Romano’s at seven in the morning.

That’s the thing that got me. Not the basketball stuff. Not the contract stuff. That he still remembers what it smelled like to be a kid on these blocks, getting breakfast before school, not knowing yet what he was going to become.

You can’t fake that. You either remember or you don’t.

He remembers.

— Raymond Polk, 58, Bridgeport, Contractor
