# Chinatown's Sick Are Counted by the Hundred and Named by No One

Akua Adeyemi works quietly in Chinatown kitchens. She keeps the wrench low and the pipes from knocking. Over the years she has learned to read a flat from the doorway, before she has set her bag down.

"I go into a lot of homes, and you can tell pretty quick when someone's down," the plumber told the Tribune. "It's not the mess or the dishes — it's the quiet. The TV off in the middle of the day. A chair pulled up by the window where somebody's been sitting a long time. Sometimes the medicine gets moved to the kitchen counter so it's right there where someone'll remember it. And you learn to work quiet, not to bang pipes, because somebody's sleeping in the next room."

She lives in Chinatown, and she says she sees more of this there than anywhere else she works.

The neighborhood sheet for cycle 115 backs her on scale. Chinatown has 135 sick residents out of 2,205. That is 6.1%, at or over the 6% watch bar, and it is the highest count of any neighborhood. The next highest are East Oakland at 117, Ivy Hill at 115 and Fruitvale at 109. Across all 22 neighborhoods the sheet counts 1,771 sick residents.

The count has been falling slowly. The Tribune's earlier cycle coverage put Chinatown at 142 in cycle 112, 140 in cycle 113 and 137 in cycle 114. The sheet now reads 135, two fewer than last cycle. Fewer is not few. The neighborhood has stayed at the top of the list through every one of those readings.

## Six beds

The hospital trail for the same cycle is short. Chinatown's 135 sick residents produced 3 admissions this cycle. At the close, 6 Chinatown residents were in care, and 6 beds were occupied. The census lists none of the six as tracked, and it carries no names for them.

The neighborhoods around it on the list look much the same. East Oakland has 117 sick residents, 2 admissions and 4 beds occupied. Dimond has 90 sick, 2 admissions and 4 beds. Eastlake has 86 sick, 2 admissions and 4 beds. Adams Point has 82 sick, 2 admissions and 4 beds. Citywide, the hospital census shows 34 admissions this cycle, none of them tracked residents, with 69 in care at the close in 69 beds. 35 residents left care.

The record does not say where the rest of Chinatown's sick are, or how they are being looked after. Adeyemi gave her own account of what she sees. "People here take care of their own — you'll see a neighbor bring soup, or a cousin come by after their shift," she said. "It's not always the hospital that carries the load. It's the family that rearranges the whole house around one sick person."

That is a plumber's observation from inside the homes she works in. It is not a finding about the 129 residents the beds do not account for, and she did not offer it as one.

## Names on the door

Only a few residents are named on the hospital and cause records this cycle, and none lives in Chinatown. Yu Zen, of Rockridge, remains in hospital care after an admission in cycle 105 for a workplace accident. Deacon Seymour, of Lake Merritt, was admitted the same cycle after a workplace accident and is also still in care. Maurice Franklin, also of Rockridge, has been in care since cycle 106 for severe seasonal flu. Howard Travis, on Piedmont Ave, caught up on sleep and woke feeling like himself again.

Seymour manages the Oakland A's. He knows what it is to be a name on a hospital record, and he spoke to what that name does and does not cover.

"I spent my own stretch in a hospital bed not long ago, and I know what it's like to have your name on the door, to have people checking on you," he said. "But I also know that bed I was in—that was one bed, and there are a lot more filled by folks nobody's naming. Chinatown's carrying more sickness than anywhere else in this city, and if the ledger doesn't show them, that doesn't mean they're not there. It means nobody's counting. And when nobody's counting, people get missed. That's the part that keeps me up at night."

The ledger can be read two ways, and the record settles neither. Chinatown's count of 135 may be complete, with the hospital trail simply showing that most of those residents did not need a bed this cycle. Or the neighborhood's sick may be only partly visible to the systems that name people. The census can say that 6 Chinatown residents are in beds. It cannot say who they are, how long they have been there, or what happens to the people at the top of the neighborhood's list once they leave.

The reporting this cycle did not turn up any of the following:
- how long Chinatown's six occupied beds have been filled
- whether any of the six are among the three admitted this cycle
- what care exists for residents who are sick and not admitted
- who in the city is responsible for following a neighborhood that has held the top count for four straight readings

A resident who is sick in Chinatown and not in a bed has no entry on the record yet. The Tribune will ask the city's health office for what it keeps on those residents, and whether any of it can be seen.
