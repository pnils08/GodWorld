# West Oakland, in their own words

## Mike Paulson — General Manager, Oakland A's & The Oaks

> The Aitken signing changes everything about the Dillon question, and not in the way people might think. Benji's not coming back to be the fifth starter or the long man — if he comes back, it's because he wants to be part of something that still feels like home, and right now the home he knew is down two of its loudest voices. The $13M doesn't scare me as much as the roster math does; we've got young arms that need real innings, and I can't stunt Taveras or Quintero's growth to give a 38-year-old legend a farewell tour that already happened. But I also know that Benji in that bullpen, coming in for big spots, teaching the kids how to breathe in October — that has value no spreadsheet captures. I'll make him a real offer, a lower number, a defined role, and I'll let him decide what the city still means to him.

## Eleanor Rivera — BART Station Manager

> Honestly, from the booth, a five-point drop on a crowded day doesn't read as people leaving the station. It reads as people arriving later, all at once. You see the platforms fill up in waves instead of a steady stream, and that's when the dwell time stretches — the trains are there, but everyone's squeezing on at the same time, doors cycle slower, and the whole rhythm backs up. The ledger shows the number that got on. What it doesn't show is the crowd that's still on the platform when the doors close, waiting for the next one. That's not a loss — that's a delay that shows up in the next train's count.
