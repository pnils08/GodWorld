I was still at the counter when the final horn sounded, the TV above the taps going dark on that 104-100. The place smelled like burnt coffee and old vinyl, the kind of spot where the stools creak louder after a loss. Melton Neilon wiped his hands on his apron and shook his head.

"To be honest, the Oaks' loss still has me a bit down, but I'm trying not to overthink it. I mean, I'm a baker, not a stats expert. I just enjoy watching the games and cheering them on. The A's storming back from 20 down was thrilling, but that final score... it's tough. I guess that's just how it goes sometimes."

He said it quiet, like he was already trying to talk himself out of the feeling. Daniel Cloak, two stools down, leaned in. "Yeah, that's still how I feel. The Oaks trying to be part of the community is a positive step. I was at the community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub, and it felt like they were really listening. Maybe the Oaks can be like that too, you know, more involved locally."

Some will tell you this is the kind of loss you shake off. One road game, down twenty, claw back to within four at the end. They’ll point at the fight and call it growth. They’re wrong. That comeback only makes the final margin sit heavier. We were close enough to taste it and still walked away empty. That’s the part the post-game notes won’t name.

Adash Stanley’s words floated through the room from the replay feed. "The loss stings, but you could feel the energy in the stands still. We came back from 20 down, so there's a lot to build on. The guys fought hard, and that's what matters." Austin Reaves followed right after him. "To be honest, I'm still feeling the sting of that loss. We were down 20 and fought our way back, but it feels like we left it on the road. I'm proud of how we responded, though. We can't let one loss get us down."

I heard the pride in both of them. I felt it too, for a minute. Then the dread settled back in. We started 4-1. Now the streak is snapped and the season is already teaching us the same lesson it always does: effort without the finish just leaves you staring at the same scoreboard from a different angle. The perfect start is gone. What stays is the question of whether the fight we showed tonight is enough to carry us past the nights when the ball doesn’t drop.

Oakland, we can cheer the rally all we want. The fear is that we’ve cheered rallies like this before and still ended up exactly here.
