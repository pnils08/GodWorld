# St. Esperanza Counts Its Lunches: What Fruitvale's Parish Has Logged While Other Doors Opened This Week

By Elliot Graye, The Bay Tribune

On International Boulevard in Fruitvale, St. Esperanza Parish has a thirteen-row history in the Faith Ledger, and none of the rows that came back is dated C115. The newest of them is C113: "after-school tutoring program expands," attendance 110. The one before it is C110, "seniors lunch program marks milestone," attendance 80. Before that is a C108 seniors lunch row at 114, filed the same cycle as an Epiphany celebration that drew 374.

The parish is not in this week's rows. The same two programs are, at other addresses. Masjid An-Nur Oakland in East Oakland logged a seniors lunch milestone on C115, attendance 50. Cathedral of the Living Word at Lake Merritt logged an expanded after-school tutoring program on C115, attendance 250. Fruitvale's largest Catholic parish ran both of those programs earlier in the record, and the ledger shows it did them in its own numbers, row by row, with no announcement attached.

Father Ramon Solano was asked what the parish is, since the ledger can only say what it counted.

"As the Senior Pastor of St. Esperanza Parish, I've seen firsthand how our church supports the Latino community in Fruitvale," he said. "We serve as a community center and a place of worship, with a focus on family. Our congregation is about 800 strong, and we've been a part of this neighborhood for 119 years. We provide various services and activities that cater to the needs of our community, and I'm proud to be a part of it."

The registry row for St. Esperanza gives the same shape in fewer words: Catholic, Fruitvale, congregation 800, "Latino community center, family focus," active, led by Fr. Ramon Solano. The ledger holds the lunches and the tutoring under the same heading as the holy days. It does not rank them. An Epiphany service is an event row, and so is a Tuesday lunch for old people.

The holy-day numbers are worth reading slowly, and they do not need to be turned into a story about decline. The rows run 482 at a special Epiphany service on C105, 480 on C106, 427 on C107 and 374 on C108. The ledger does not say why the figures fell. A count is what a door produced on one day, and weather, work shifts and a calendar's placement of a feast can move it. What the rows establish is that the sanctuary was full by that count in the early cycles and smaller by the last one logged. The same parish also kept ledgered programs running that drew 80, 110 and 114 on days that were not holy days. This paper's C97 coverage found Father Solano at the threshold in his alb, greeting people slowly, with marigolds in glass vases on either side of the door. That coverage is the paper's earlier word on how the parish receives people. This piece does not repeat it. It asks what the parish does between feasts.

The ledger does not answer that fully, and neither does the neighborhood. Fruitvale is crowded with programs that have run since C101: the Fruitvale Summer Speed Series, a youth-sports program, and Fruitvale Transit Corridor Mutual Aid. Gurdwara Singh Sabha Fruitvale, a Sikh congregation, is on the registry in the same neighborhood. The record does not say whether any of them work with St. Esperanza. It names them as neighbors, and that is all it names.

Lucia Polito, who founded Postpartum Oakland Network in Fruitvale, said the connection is a possibility, not an arrangement. "I've founded Postpartum Oakland Network, a program that supports new mothers in our community," she said. "While I don't have a direct connection to St. Esperanza Parish, I've seen the impact that faith organizations can have on people's lives. In Fruitvale, we've had some beautiful collaborations between different community groups, and I think it's possible that St. Esperanza Parish could be a valuable partner for us. I'd love to explore that further."

Her answer shows a gap. A parish of 800 that runs a seniors lunch and a tutoring program has an obvious door for new mothers, and a network built for new mothers has an obvious need for rooms. The two have not met on the record. The question of who should reach first is an honest one, and the ledger cannot answer it.

Other clergy are as candid about what they do not know. Eunice Marston, who leads in Downtown, said: "I've heard that St. Esperanza Parish is active in the Fruitvale community, but I don't have direct knowledge of their specific activities or events. As a faith leader in Downtown, I appreciate the work of various faith organizations throughout Oakland, including those in Fruitvale." Her sentence marks the distance between congregations that sit a few miles apart. They know of each other's work without knowing the specifics.

This week's rows are closing some of that distance, in the one place where congregations sit together. The Interfaith Council logged a prayer breakfast in Adams Point on C115 that brought leaders together, attendance 123. A week earlier, on C114, it logged a dialogue series in Chinatown on shared concerns, attendance 53. The ledger does not list who attended either one, or whether anyone from Fruitvale was among them.

In Chinatown, Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple began a new session of its job training workshop on C115, attendance 40. The paper's C113 and C114 coverage has already worked through the temple's C109 crisis-response row, which logged 41 and did not say what the crisis was. Kenji Tanaka, the Chinatown pastor, took it as proof of something larger than one temple. "I've seen that faith communities in Oakland are stepping up to support each other, like the Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple opening their doors for community support," he said. "It's a reminder that we're not alone, and that there's a network of care in the city."

The network he describes is a belief about the city, and so far the rows only partly bear it out. They show doors opening in Chinatown, lunches at a masjid in East Oakland, tutoring at a cathedral and breakfast in Adams Point. St. Esperanza has been doing the same quiet work for 119 years, and this week it is absent from the rows. The record does not say whether the parish ran something this cycle that no one logged, or whether nothing needed logging. Someone who sits at the Interfaith Council's table, or at Fruitvale's, should be asked which.
