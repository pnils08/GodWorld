# West Oakland, in their own words

## P Slayer — Journalist, Fan Columnist - Sports

> To be honest, my route choices on UNDOCKED Wednesday night were pretty spontaneous. I was flying, reacting to what was around me. I went through a bunch of systems, Sol, Alpha Centauri, and others, and got into a couple of fights. I mined some iron ore, but didn't really come out ahead or behind on credits. It was a solid night, just trying to stay in the game as long as I could. I didn't have a specific strategy going in, just playing it by ear.

## Elias Varek — Founder of Civis Systems

> Civis is flat, and flat is the one thing I don't trust. $60.3 against $60.2 — that's not a plateau, that's a wall, and I built the instrument that reads this city. It's stopped telling me anything new, but then there's the signal in West Oakland that still hasn't answered downstream, and it's moved. That's the part that bothers me more than the revenue line. A signal with no downstream is not a city failure — it's a product failure, and mine. I'll pull the raw feed myself tonight before anyone else on the team spins it into a story.

## Austin Reaves — SG / The Oaks

> The fans are excited, and you can feel it. We've been playing well, and the energy's been building. I'm not surprised, though - Oaks fans are loyal and passionate. When we're clicking, the crowd gets behind us, and it's a great feeling.

## Soriya Rodriguez — Ex-Offender Reentry Counselor

> The thing that's been grating on me is how the new neighbors don't wave back. Sounds stupid, I know. But I've lived here twenty years and I've watched three families move in this month alone, and none of them look at you when you pass them on the sidewalk. They're probably just tired, or busy, or scared of the city, but I keep reading into it like it's a sign of something bigger. Maybe I'm just worn down from work, from hearing so many people's hard stories all day and coming home to a street that doesn't feel like mine no more. Honestly, it probably doesn't matter. But it's late, and I'm tired, and that's what's sitting with me.

## Antone Bautista — Relief Pitcher / Oakland A's

> New faces on the block this week, and that's good, but it's got me watching. The Stabilization Fund checks went out and now there's a moving truck on 24th that wasn't there before, and I caught myself counting the kids' bikes on the porch like I'm making a roster. I'm not stupid — I know what it means when a city starts paying people to move somewhere, and I've been in enough clubhouses to recognize a change in the lineup. But I also saw old Mrs. Delgado on the corner with a new neighbor, pointing at the bus stop and laughing about something, and that's the part that makes me feel okay. I just want the block to stay the block, you know, the kind of place where I can still walk to the BART in my flip-flops and nobody makes a big deal out of it.

## Denise Carter — City Council Member

> The dip in Brooklyn—that slide to -0.11—isn't just a number on a screen. It's Kaila and Drago and Sefu and Minh, the folks I pass at the market who don't know I carry their street's pulse with me. I keep saying I won't call it a catastrophe, but I also won't let it be waved off, and that tension sits heavy in my chest right now. The building's gone quiet, and I'm just sitting with the fact that momentum is flat, decay is the trajectory, and my job is to hold both the truth of that and the dignity of the people living inside it. I don't have a solution tonight, just the weight of knowing I'll have to speak for them tomorrow.
