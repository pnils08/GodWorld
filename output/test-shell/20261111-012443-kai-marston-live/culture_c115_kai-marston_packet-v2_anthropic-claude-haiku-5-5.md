# Rockridge Keeps Saying His Name

The patio at Cozy Pot is half full when the first name of the evening comes up, and it isn't from anyone at a table. It comes from the sidewalk, where a man with a paper cup stops to read the chalkboard by the door and says it to nobody in particular: "Benji's been around here a lot lately." His friend keeps walking. The name stays on the step.

Benji Dillon has a way of arriving in a neighborhood without arriving at all. The paper's C105 coverage found his name in the aisles of Dimond Pharmacy and in the talk of the servers working the quieter blocks of Adams Point, and that murmur hasn't gone anywhere. The culture ledger lists him as rising, last logged in C105, and the ledger has no record of the thing people on this block actually want to know: what he is doing with the attention.

So I ask him, or the version of him the record can carry. His words come back to me the way they were given, and they sound like a man who is a little puzzled by his own weather:

"I'm not sure what's behind my growing presence in Rockridge. I live here, I've been part of the community for a while, and I'm just doing my thing. I've been around long enough that people know me, I guess. I'm just trying to be a good neighbor and a good dad to Rick."

Read that on a patio with a cold cup going warm in your hand and it stops sounding modest and starts sounding like a boundary. He is saying he did not ask for this. He is saying he lives here, which is different from being a sighting.

Across the district the A's players answer the same question in the same careful register. Darrin Davis, the Left Fielder, who plays out of Piedmont Ave, reads the change from the outside: "I've seen Benji Dillon's name popping up more often around the A's, and it's clear he's gaining traction. To be honest, I'm not super close to him, but you can tell when someone's rising through the ranks. I've been around long enough to recognize the buzz. It's like when I was breaking in – everyone's curious, and you're getting more attention. I think it's great for the team to have fresh talent making waves."

Danny Horn, the Center Fielder from Temescal, is less interested in the buzz than in the clubhouse. "To be honest, I've not really thought much about Benji Dillon's rising fame. I'm focused on my own game and helping the A's make a deep run. I've seen him around the clubhouse, he's a talented kid, but I don't know him well enough to say much about his personal story or what's behind his recent performances."

Travis Coles, the Starting Pitcher from Downtown, says the same thing in fewer words. "I haven't really thought much about Benji Dillon's rising fame. I'm focused on my own game and the A's season. I know he's a teammate, but I don't really know him personally. I've seen his name in the papers, though."

Three teammates, three different distances. Darrin Davis hears the buzz like weather coming up the street. Danny Horn is watching the clubhouse. Travis Coles reads about his teammate the way the rest of us do, in print. None of them is wrong, and none of them is the room.

The room is the patio. It is a block where people still use the sidewalk to talk, where a server can tell you which table tips for the early game and which one stays until the lights go off. On a night like this the sound is coffee cups and somebody's kid asking for a napkin, and the murmur that the paper has been tracking since C105 is just one more voice in it. It isn't a stage. It isn't a fan club. It's a neighbor being talked about by neighbors, and the talk has no shape yet.

That is what makes the rising number so hard to read. A fame line in a ledger is a clean thing. Here it is a man who wants his kid to know him as a dad, a teammate who wants to talk only about the season, and a block that is quietly deciding what his name means on its sidewalk.

Who gets to say what this attention is for? The ledger can't. The clubhouse won't. Benji Dillon says he's just doing his thing, and the chalkboard by the door is still advertising the same specials it was an hour ago.
