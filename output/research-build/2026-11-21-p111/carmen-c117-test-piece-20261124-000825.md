# City hall is holding records that don't exist yet. Most of the holds have a cycle but no named owner.

**By Carmen Delaine, Civic Affairs**

I set out this cycle to count how many of city hall's "holds" and "requirements" come with a date and an owner, and how many are open-ended. I read all 14 initiative rows in the C117 tracker and the mayor's and council's statements this cycle. I did not make any calls.

**The dates.** Twelve of the 14 rows name Cycle 118 as the next action. Two name Cycle 117, the current cycle, and both are already slipping:
- **Dimond Revitalization (INIT-012):** The tracker said to file legislation in C117. The mayor's own statement records the filing deadline as missed and moves the measure to stalled.
- **Ivy Hill Community Support (INIT-014):** The tracker calls for council review this cycle. Council member Ramon Vega says he will withhold review until the Clerk supplies a filing reference or confirms none exists. The mayor has also withdrawn any implication that a filing was completed.

**The owners.** The tracker's next-action field names no one. An owner can be inferred only by matching each action to a statement. Some are clear. The Health Center's director, Bobby Chen-Ramirez, says his office will file the Q3 intake audit with HCAI and the Clerk, and will document the Telegraph & 47th merchant walkthrough. The OARI director commits to filing written next dates with the Clerk. Others are murky. The Baylight Authority's own entry says it will publish "responsibility" for the remaining determination work, which means the owner of the Baylight review is itself still to be named.

**The truly open-ended holds.** I count three kinds:
1. **Holds waiting on a date that doesn't exist.** OARI expansion is held until a D3 Fruitvale briefing record is published and until written dates exist for the D5 audit and the Baylight feasibility review. The hold depends on dates nobody has set.
2. **Holds that repeat.** The Fruitvale Transit Hub has required preliminary schematics and a documented public comment-and-response record since C115. At C117 the tracker says neither is evidenced. The Temescal Health Center's oversight certification has been conditioned on the same two filings since C116. Baylight's evidence-file review was ordered at C115 and is still unpublished.
3. **Holds on a vote or filing that hasn't happened.** Grand Lake is vote-scheduled, but the packet holds no Clerk-certified roll call. Mayor Santana backs a yes vote, and council members Crane, Ashford and Chen each say they will vote yes only with the Chen-Vega conditions intact. The Stabilization Fund's charter renewal depends on a verified C114 closeout, and no such verification is in the packet.

This cycle's record also corrects itself in one place. The Tenant Defense Fund passed 6-3 at C112 and was signed. The C116 framing implied a committee vote was still pending, and the mayor and Denise Carter now say none is. Publication of the legal-support analysis and the opening of intake remain unverified.

**The money.** The treasury closed at $136.9 million, up from $131.8 million. It took in $5.1 million this cycle: the $5 million weekly budget allocation and $125,905 in court money on 28 cleared charges.

Only four initiatives show money moving, going by "remaining" balances:
- **Stabilization Fund:** $7.8 million of $28 million paid out, with $20.2 million remaining.
- **Health Center:** about $29.77 million of $45 million paid out, with $15.23 million remaining.
- **OARI:** about $1.92 million of $12.5 million paid out, with $10.58 million remaining.
- **Tenant Defense Fund:** about $81,000 of $4.2 million paid out.

That totals roughly $39.6 million. The other ten initiatives show their full budgets untouched, about $2.376 billion in all. Most of that is Baylight's $2.1 billion, which the tracker lists as construction-active with the full $2.1 billion remaining. Fruitvale's $230 million is also untouched. Authorization isn't cash, so I won't compare these to the treasury balance. Even so, the records that would justify spending on the largest projects are the ones city hall is still asking for.

**What I'd watch in C118.** Twelve actions are due. I'll check each against a published record, not an announcement. I'll also ask whether the OARI dates for the D5 audit and the Baylight feasibility review actually get filed, since they are the dates other holds depend on.
