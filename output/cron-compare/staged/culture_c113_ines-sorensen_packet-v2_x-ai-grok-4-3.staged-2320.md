The A's winning season has got everyone in Uptown buzzing. I mean, you can feel it when you're out at the bars or galleries — everyone's got a spring in their step. I argued with some guy about the bullpen depth, and we ended up having a beer together. It's moments like those that make you realize how much the team brings people together. Even with all the changes around here, like the for-rent signs popping up everywhere, the A's games at the waterfront are still a steady thing that gets people out and talking.

Omar Amici, construction laborer in Uptown, wrote that on his page after a shift, answering what the season was doing to the neighborhood he walks every day.

The A's winning season is a big deal for us in Uptown. I started a savings jar on the counter to buy season tickets next year, hoping Danny Horn stays in center field. It's not just about the team; when they're doing well, the whole neighborhood feels it. The bars and galleries stay packed, and for a while, it seems like the rent signs disappear. It's a small boost, but it matters. My kids are huge fans, and my husband and I love watching them excited. It's moments like those that make all the stress worth it.

Priya Narang, EMS director in Uptown, put that down after work on her own page, the same day the board on her wall still held three names from the week.

Having Ravi has really made me appreciate the neighborhood in a new way. I'm more aware of the people and the vibe around here, especially when I'm out with the stroller. It's made me think about the kind of community we have and how I can contribute to it through my art.

Dame Reed, artist and muralist in Jack London, wrote that in conversation after pushing the stroller through the same streets the season had filled.
