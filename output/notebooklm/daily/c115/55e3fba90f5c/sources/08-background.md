# Earlier in the Tribune: The Page: Lives of Cycle 115

Thirty-one Cycles ago, in Cycle 84, City Planning Director Mara Vance transmitted an implementation review to Mayor Avery Santana's office, flagging an unfunded \$60 million municipal infrastructure gap and warning that major capital projects in Baylight and Fruitvale would compound demand on failing pipelines. In that review, Vance formally recommended granting priority municipal project status to the \$45 million Temescal Community Health Center to expedite its design and permitting schedule.

Thirty Cycles ago, in Cycle 85, civic reporter Carmen Delaine reported that Vance sent four formal written requests across three municipal desks—the Mayor’s office, the Office of Economic and Workforce Development, and the Baylight Redevelopment Authority—demanding written accountings on frozen Stabilization Fund payouts, OARI co-responder rollout delays, stalled health center permitting, and missing Baylight mobilization instruments. Delaine noted that Vance created a permanent paper trail after verbal assurances from city agencies had repeatedly failed to deliver actual results.

Two Cycles ago, in Cycle 113, municipal transit logs documented Rockridge barista Howard Yell during a major morning transit disruption when a train died on the tracks between stations, forcing passengers to evacuate into tunnels. The public record noted that the street above in Rockridge remained bustling with shoppers and diners, leaving unresolved whether Yell had been among the riders aboard the stranded train.

One Cycle ago, in Cycle 114, civic coverage documented Vance residing in Rockridge four streets over from Mayor Santana, where she was regularly observed buying morning coffee along College Avenue before walking over to inspect architectural renderings posted along the Temescal border. Prior published coverage contained no reporting on retired sea-level monitoring technician Xavier Robinson, Imani Delacroix, or Jee Lei before Cycle 115.
