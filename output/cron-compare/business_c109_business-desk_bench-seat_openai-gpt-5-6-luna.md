# The money is moving through West Oakland. The question is who gets to keep it.

Enzo Walker has the kind of job that reveals a neighborhood one pipe at a time. As a plumber at Oakmesh Systems, he says he is fixing water lines in buildings that were not there last year, while the companies around him turn technical work and construction into a new West Oakland payroll.

“Honestly? Those tech firms like Oakmesh and Civis—they're changing the block faster than we can keep up,” Walker said. “I work at Oakmesh, and half the time I'm fixing pipes in buildings that didn't exist last year. The money's there, sure, but it's not sticking around here. Guys like Elias at Civis talk about 'systems' and 'efficiency,' but ask the folks at the bus stop if they feel any of that. We're all just trying to keep the water running while the city builds over us.”

Oakmesh is carrying 46 employees this cycle and reported $9.2 million in revenue, up $55,000 from the previous cycle. Its listed growth is 31.4%, and its West Oakland roster includes Walker, machine-learning engineers Dom Will and Jace Robinson, and construction laborer Elijah Roberts.

That is a small workforce beside the larger construction firms named in the same neighborhood ledger. Coastline Construction reports 221 employees and $185.8 million in revenue, up $246,000 from the previous cycle. Mariner Construction has 165 employees, while Northgate Construction has 95 and reports $78.3 million in revenue, up $96,000.

The figures describe expansion. They do not, by themselves, describe who can afford to remain near it, who rides farther to work, or whether the next payroll creates a durable foothold for the people already carrying the block.

Sam Fazio, a digital twin architect at Civis Systems, sees a more cooperative picture. Civis reports 41 employees and $60.1 million in revenue, up $24,000 from the previous cycle. Fazio put the companies in the same frame as a neighborhood being made in public.

“Working at Civis Systems, I’ve seen how West Oakland businesses like ours, Oakmesh Systems, and Coastline Construction are really shaping the neighborhood. It’s not just about the jobs—it’s about the community we’re building together. People here are passionate about what they do, whether it’s urban systems intelligence or construction. There’s a sense of pride in contributing to something bigger.”

Will, a machine-learning engineer at Oakmesh, offered the same optimism with a sharper edge. “Oakmesh feels like we're building something real here - not just another tech bubble. Marcus keeps us focused on civic impact, not just profit. Civis down the street? Elias runs a tight ship, all that urban intelligence work. You see their drone fleet buzzing around sometimes. Coastline's got cranes up everywhere - hard to miss them. But it's the small crews, like our construction guys, that make the neighborhood hum.”

That tension is the business story in West Oakland this cycle: the companies have scale, the construction firms have headcount, and the workers can still feel the distance between growth and belonging. Revenue can rise without answering the simpler question of where the benefit settles.

Oakmesh, Civis Systems and Coastline Construction are shaping the next big move for West Oakland’s businesses. The unresolved question is whether that move gives workers like Walker more than work to maintain—or whether the neighborhood’s most visible growth keeps building over them.
