# Who Owns Fruitvale's Squeeze? The Map Says Decay and the Program List Has No Answer

I stood at the taqueria down the block at lunch with my elbows on the counter. The line ran out the door. Work boots, blazers, nobody talking about anything but whether the al pastor was gone yet. It was busy, as busy as it gets.

Forty feet away, in the window of a storefront office, the plans for the transit hub expansion sit curling at the edges. The sun has been at them a while. Nobody was inside.

That's the picture the city wants for Fruitvale: a full room, a plan in the window, a neighborhood on its way up.

Now the map.

Fruitvale ranks first of twenty-two neighborhoods on mood. Residents feel better here than anywhere else in Oakland. Retail ranks fifth, 10.55 against a city median of 8.275. So the lunch line is real. I'm not going to tell you it isn't.

But the same map files Fruitvale under decay, and the numbers move the wrong way. Mood slipped. Retail slid by more than two points. The squeeze on housing climbed by a full point and now sits at 4.5. The median neighborhood in this city sits at zero. Fruitvale ranks seventeenth of twenty-two on that squeeze. That means the people buying lunch are getting pressed harder on rent and roof than almost everyone else in town.

Read that again. The happiest neighborhood in Oakland is also one of the most squeezed. It's busy at noon and sliding on the map.

I'll translate. A line out the door doesn't prove people can afford to stay. Work boots and blazers can both pay for tacos and still be one rent notice from gone. I can't tell you who's paying what. Nobody supplied me that, and I won't make it up. I can tell you the map doesn't show it getting better, and the retail number is dropping while the housing number climbs.

So who's on it?

The record says nobody. Not a quiet effort somewhere out of sight. Nobody. No active initiative owns this break. No program on the books is tied to what's happening to Fruitvale's housing.

I went through the list of what the city is running. Fruitvale shows up twice. The Oakland Alternative Response Initiative has Fruitvale on its dispatch list, three weeks in. The Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline has it too, three weeks operational. Fine. Neither is a housing program. Neither claims the squeeze. The big money is the West Oakland Stabilization Fund, and it's in West Oakland. The tenant defense fund is in West Oakland. The commercial help is for Grand Lake. The Downtown revitalization just got filed as legislation and has a downtown address.

Fruitvale gets a hub plan fading in a window.

I don't know that a plan is wrong. I know that a drawing of a platform isn't a roof over anybody. A design on paper is not a rent cap, a defense fund, or a check. When the only thing the city has aimed at Fruitvale is a transit expansion, and the neighborhood's pressure is climbing, I get to ask what the expansion is for and who it's for. That's inference. I'm putting it as a question because I can't answer it.

I also can't tell you how long this has been building. The record I was handed doesn't carry the earlier comparison I'd need to say how fast, or since when. No office has answered for the mismatch either. That silence is part of the story too.

Here's what I'll say flat out. The lunch rush is the cover. It looks like health, and it lets a council and a mayor's office point at a full room and call it recovery. The same map says the squeeze is up and the program list is empty.

Council members from the district to the Mayor's office: I'd like one of you to stand in that taqueria line at noon and tell the people around you which program is protecting their housing.

The map says decay; the program roster does not answer it. So who in this city is supposed to?
