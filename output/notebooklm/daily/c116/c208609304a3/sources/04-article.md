By Mason Ortega, The Bay Tribune

At Green & Gold Tavern in Jack London, dishwasher Hector Leech stands at the sink with steam rising around him. The racks clank as he works through another load, hands moving from sprayer to water and back again. "A full shift in the pit is a marathon, not a sprint," he says. "You're in the steam before the first ticket even prints, and the heat just sits on you like a second skin. The sprayer's going, the racks are clanking, and your hands are in and out of the water so much they prune up and crack. But the rhythm is what keeps you going — the plates come in dirty, you send them out clean, and there's a kind of peace in that. The breaks are short, just enough to catch your breath and drink some water, and then you're back at it. What keeps me there? It's the people. The crew at Green & Gold, they look out for each other. And honestly, there's something about being part of the machine that keeps the whole place humming. I've been doing this a long time, and I'm not looking to be anywhere else."

The tavern lists 30 employees on the current roster, with Hector Leech among them alongside Trenton Nawan, Guadalupe Lupe, and Sora Hint. Nearby at Jack London Square Markets, security guard Karim Avery watches the foot traffic that feeds into the kitchens. "I see the pressure before it even gets to the kitchen," he says. "The markets are the first stop for a lot of folks coming off the shuttle or the BART — they're grabbing coffee, grabbing something quick, and they're already running late or already tired. The kitchen workers, they're inside, they're focused on the tickets, the heat, the rush. But I'm out front, watching who's waiting, who's checking their phone for the third time, who's arguing with a vendor over a dollar. That's the neighborhood's pressure — it's not just about the work, it's about the whole day stacking up on you before you even clock in. And when I walk through Jack London after my shift, I see the same faces at the taquerias and the same tired eyes. The kitchen folks see the plates, I see the people carrying the weight that brought them to those plates."

Harborline Grill lists seven employees on its roster, including Tomas Renteria, Quynh Le, Jalen Hill, and Bruce Wright. Blue Lantern Bar lists 31, with Yuki Ji and Guadalupe Lee among them. Dockhouse BBQ lists two, with Mesi Westin on the roster. What holds the rhythm together when the crew changes?
