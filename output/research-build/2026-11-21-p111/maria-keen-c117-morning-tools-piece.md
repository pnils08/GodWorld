# The Strip Before Eight

Isaac Martinez doesn't need a calendar to tell him what's happening to the Ivy Hill strip. He listens to it.

"The strip sounds quieter at 8 a.m. compared to a year ago," he told me. "There's less activity, and it's more likely you'll hear someone walking their dog or a car driving by than the sounds of a business opening up."

Isaac is a plumber. A plumber's day starts before the shop does. He knows when the roll-up gates go up, when the first water runs through the pipes, and when a back door gets propped open for a delivery. When a strip is healthy, that's the first sound of the morning. In Ivy Hill it's getting harder to hear.

## Three windows

By his count, three storefronts have papered over their windows this year. He was careful about what he did and didn't know, and I'd rather give you that than something tidier.

"I haven't worked inside any of them recently, so I don't know what I'd fix if I went in," he said. "The last people I saw inside were probably the owners or staff getting ready to close up."

He doesn't know why they closed. He doesn't know what's behind the paper, and he won't guess. What he knows is the sound, and that the last people he saw going in were closing up.

The city's own texture notes for this cycle say the same thing in plainer language. A "space for lease" sign has gone up at the dental office. People walk their dogs past empty storefronts.

## What the record shows

The numbers say Ivy Hill is a hill that's emptying slowly.

- The neighborhood map lists Ivy Hill as "outflow accelerating," with a migration flow of -5 and a decay trajectory.
- Retail vitality sits at 5.05. Fruitvale, which borders it, is at 15.16.
- Ivy Hill has 1,366 adults, 329 students and 420 seniors. Only Rockridge, with 458, has more seniors on the list. Only Dimond, Glenview and Laurel have fewer than 1,520 adults.
- This cycle the neighborhood gained 1 student, 5 adults and 1 senior. Those are small gains against a long slide.

The business ledger lists five Ivy Hill names, and every one is shrinking:

| Business | Growth rate |
|---|---|
| Ivy Hill Market | -4.69% |
| Hillcrest Kitchen | -3.31% |
| Ivy Hill Cafe | -2.44% |
| Ivy Cleaners | -1.87% |
| Fold Laundry | -1.79% |

The ledger shows a headcount of zero on each of those rows. I can't tell you whether that means people have been let go or whether the books are missing something. I also can't tell you which of these, if any, are behind the paper on those windows. Isaac didn't say, and I won't connect dots he wouldn't connect. The Tribune has been tracking this contraction since at least C113, when Ivy Hill Market shed two workers. It shed three more by C114.

## The ones who keep the block running

I think about who is on a strip like this before the doors open. It's the plumber with a spare trap gasket in every pocket, who, according to the record, used one before lunch one day this spring and snaked a drain on the first try without bragging about it, much. It's whoever sweeps and whoever hauls the grease.

Isaac is part of this block in other ways too. The record has him painting over graffiti on the recreation center wall with five neighbors he barely knew. He drove the church van on the senior trip and got three hours of gossip for it. This cycle he watched a neighbor fight the Stabilization Fund portal on a phone, and he kept what he knew to himself. A few cycles back he caught himself lowering his voice when he talked about the block's future.

I understand that lowered voice. It's what you do when you suspect the thing you're describing may not need your help to happen. Nobody says "the strip is dying" out loud. People say "it's quiet," and then they walk the dog.

## What I'm watching

Ivy Hill is not a loud story. The city's records call it the smallest and quietest of the neighborhoods, and it's one of five that sat out this cycle's engine signals. Quiet is not the same as fine. I'd rather ask what's behind the paper than wait for a sign to tell me.

Next cycle I want to stand on that strip at 7:45 a.m. with Isaac and listen for the gates. I want to count how many go up.
