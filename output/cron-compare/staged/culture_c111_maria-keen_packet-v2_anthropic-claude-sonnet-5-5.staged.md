# The Lab, the Lease Sign, and the Beds Downtown

NeuroCity Youth Lab has been on the Downtown record since Cycle 101, a tech-education program founded by Priya Nair. It is the only active program the block has on file this cycle. I want to be honest about what that means. One program is not a whole block's care. It is one lit window on a street that has a lot of other windows.

I can't tell you what the Lab smells like at closing time or who is still bent over a keyboard. I wasn't there this cycle, and the record doesn't say. What the record gives me is the block itself. Downtown holds 240 students, 2,286 adults and 232 seniors. Since last cycle it has gained one student, five adults and one senior. That is seven more people on a block where the rooms are already full. Seven doesn't sound like a crowd, but each of them is a person who has to find a door key and a landlord.

## What people said about the new paper in the window

Ryan Stark put the change on Broadway. "I walked by SpeedyBurger on Broadway and saw a new lease sign go up above it," he said. "The paper was still crisp in the display case. It's another new spot opening up in Downtown, which is great to see."

I like that detail, the crisp paper. A lease sign is the most hopeful kind of paper there is, because it says somebody believes in the next year.

Carlos Presti saw the same kind of thing and kept his hope looser. "I saw another new spot opening up Downtown. I don't know what it is yet, but I'm always curious to see what actually sticks around here. It's just good to see something new moving in."

*What actually sticks around here.* Anyone who has watched a storefront turn over knows that sentence has a long memory in it.

Gordon Tate turned the same sign over and read the back. "I just noticed another new spot opening up around Downtown. It's always something new popping up, which is fine, but with the rent going up the way it is, you start wondering who these places are actually for."

I think all three of them are right, and I'm not going to pick. A new lease can be a welcome and a warning at once. The hard part is that the new spot and the rising rent are the same fact seen from two sides of the glass.

Lucia Polito said she had heard about Downtown First Friday Live Music the night before, and that it made her think about how vibrant the block can be. "It's great to see people coming together for events like that," she said. I believe her. A block that can gather around music is a block with a pulse.

## The other ledger

The pulse isn't the whole story, and I would be lying if I wrote only the music.

The hospital census for Downtown shows 83 sick residents, 2 admissions, and 4 people in care at the close, filling 4 beds. The justice census shows 4 charges, 1 arrest, and 3 people in custody at the close. None of those people are tracked by name in the record, so I can't tell you who they are. Each of them is still a neighbor of someone's.

Those numbers are small next to 2,286 adults. They're also the numbers a lease sign doesn't reach. A person in a bed or in custody isn't reading the display case on Broadway. Somebody on the block is waiting on a phone call or a visiting hour, and nobody is writing a feature about it.

## The question I can't answer from here

The Lab is steady, and the lease signs keep going up. First Friday gives people a reason to be on the sidewalk together. That is the stable, visible side of Downtown, and I'm glad of it.

What I don't know is whether it is enough. Does the steadiness of a program like NeuroCity Youth Lab reach the residents who are in a hospital bed or in custody, or the ones Gordon Tate wonders about, who may not be able to afford the new spots? I can't answer that from the record. I'd need to sit with someone from the block and ask who is holding the door open for the four in the beds and the three in custody, and whether the ones who put up the lease signs know they're there.
