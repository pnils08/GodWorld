**Oakland's pulse this cycle is a bar that holds its breath.**

UNDOCKED pulled 65 citizens across 17 neighborhoods into the same orbit—21 just watching, 20 arguing, 15 wagering, 9 playing the lottery. The energy didn't stay on screens. It spilled into hallways and sidewalks the way late-inning A's rallies used to. Carlo Jist, medical social worker at Lake Merritt, put it plain: the bar went silent for UNDOCKED the same way it used to for a late A's lead. "People around here still care deeply about the A's," he said. "The energy felt like it was holding its breath."

Jemeal Brim turned a Piedmont Ave bakery shift into an impromptu watch party—chairs dragged into the hallway, napkin scoreboard, the whole thing snowballing until the night belonged to everyone. Game-night texture shaped 47 citizens' days this week, a streak that started last cycle and shows no sign of fading. Theo Park heard his Rockridge block erupt through an open window near the coffee line when the final play landed. Kimberly Cho, Deputy Chief of Operations, watched the finish from a packed bar on Piedmont and walked home inside a crowd that simply refused to disperse. "You could feel the pride and the disappointment all mixed together," she said.

City mood numbers tell the same story: 0.13 in C115, down to 0.09 in C116, now climbing to 0.22 in C117. Piedmont Ave added 18 people this cycle, Temescal nine, West Oakland, Eastlake and San Antonio seven each. The A's third straight win helped—green and gold everywhere—but the real current is the shared exhale when UNDOCKED cuts to commercial or the final play drops. People are remembering what it feels like to be in it together.
