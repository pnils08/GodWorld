# The First Win Has Made the Room Louder, Not Certain

**Talia Finch | Bay Tribune Sports**

Trenton Nawan is carrying a glass from the counter in Jack London when the room jumps again—not for a new play, but for the replay of the old one. The television is still showing the Oaks’ first home game, the sound low beneath the scrape of chairs and the little burst of voices that rises whenever AJ Dybantsa’s name comes around.

The Oaks opened at home against the San Antonio Spurs and got the first win in franchise history. Dybantsa shone in his rookie debut. The number beside the club is 1-0, but the number that matters in this room is the one nobody says aloud: can one win make a team belong here?

Nawan sets down the glass and keeps one hand on the counter. He has been watching the scores closely, but he is not pretending the worry disappeared with the preseason.

“To be honest, I'm still a bit worried about the Oaks. Their preseason didn't exactly fill me with confidence, but the way they're playing now is giving me hope. I checked the scores twice a day all week and was happy every time they won. Maybe they're finally getting their act together.”

That is the Oaks’ first constituency: people leaning forward, but not all the way in. The team went 0-9 through rookie camp and preseason before the home opener, and the relief in the room is real. So is the caution. A first win can open a door. It does not make everybody walk through it.

Adash Stanley describes the change as something physical, a charge that has moved from the court into the room.

“The mood around the Oaks game has been really electric lately. I've felt the excitement building, and it's like the whole city is waking up with the team. After a tough preseason, it was a huge relief to see them win their first game. The buzz in the air is palpable, and I think people are really looking forward to seeing what they can do this season.”

Across the room, the talk keeps splitting in two. One voice wants to know when the next game is. Another wants to know whether this was the beginning of something or simply the first good night. Nobody has turned that question into a vote. They are answering it with their bodies instead: staying for the replay, turning toward the screen, letting the same winning sequence run again.

Austin Reaves has felt that swing from the inside. After the win, he stayed late signing autographs for kids, an act that now hangs in the room alongside the result—not as proof that the Oaks have arrived, but as evidence that somebody is already waiting for them.

“To be honest, the mood around the Oaks game has been really up and down for me. We're still finding our footing, and it's hard not to worry when we're not seeing the wins. But after that first win, the energy was electric. I stayed late signing autographs for the kids, and it felt amazing. I think fans are getting a little frustrated, but I trust that we'll get it together. We just need to keep working and supporting each other.”

The first win has not erased the doubt. It has given the doubt a place to sit, with a drink in front of it and the replay glowing overhead. Around the counter, the room keeps talking over the low television, electric and watchful, waiting to see who comes back next time.
