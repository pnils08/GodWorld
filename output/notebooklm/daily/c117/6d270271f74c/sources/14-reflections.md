# West Oakland, in their own words

## Deon Whitfield — Community Organizer

> You know, I was walking past the corner store on Market this morning, and there was this old man—Mr. Delgado, he's been on this block for thirty years—sitting on the milk crate he keeps out front. He wasn't doing anything special, just watching the street. And there was a kid, maybe twelve, on a scooter who stopped and asked him for the time. Mr. Delgado told him, and then he said, "You know, they used to sell penny candy right there," pointing at the spot where the ATM now is. The kid just nodded and scooted off. Nobody else would've caught that. But that's West Oakland for you—the layers. The new stuff sits right on top of the old, and if you're not paying attention, you miss the way it all stacks. I stood there a second, thinking about how my own kid Jasper used to ride that same sidewalk, and now he's grown. It's just... a moment you don't get back.

> Hey, Jasper. Good to see you out here. Your mom said you've been putting in extra hours at the shop—you taking a break, or just passing through the neighborhood? That's good to hear, son. Busy's better than the alternative. Listen, I was thinking about grabbing a bite at the usual spot—you got time for a quick lunch, or you gotta get back? Alright then, I won't argue with that. You know, I was watching the replay at the bar yesterday—that young kid Clark's got some real promise at the plate. Reminds me a bit of how Aitken started out. *(talking with Jasper Whitfield)*

## Karem Asante — Youth Counselor

> Man, I sit in those meetings and I hear a lot of talk about numbers, but what I see in my one-on-ones is kids carrying stuff they shouldn't have to. The index slipping? That's just a mirror of what they're bringing in—stress from home, from the block, from not knowing if they'll eat tonight. It's not a stat to me; it's their backpacks getting heavier.

## Elias Varek — Founder of Civis Systems

> Numbers don't lie, but they do withhold. Civis moved a tenth of a percent. That's not growth, that's a heartbeat. Flatlining. The Oaks at 11.6% on $62.8M? That's a real business. That's the engine that keeps the lights on in this city. 250 people, steady, honest growth. I don't have to think about the Oaks. Varek Systems — 12.8% on $9.1M. Small, but it moves. That's the seed capital for the next thing. That's fine. But Civis. $60.4M in revenue and 716% growth over the arc, and this week it didn't move. I built that company to be the instrument this city reads itself with. When Civis flatlines, the city is blind for a moment. I don't like blind. I don't like an unanswered signal that moves. What are you showing me? Because these numbers tell me one thing: the Oaks is paying for the city, and Civis is supposed to be telling us where the city is going. If Civis is stalled, I need to know why before the city does.

## Austin Reaves — SG / The Oaks

> Man. Reading all that, I gotta be honest with you — it feels good. It feels real good. But it ain't about the numbers, you know? Thirty-two in 22 minutes, that's a nice night. Ten-for-ten from the line, no turnovers, that's the stuff I care about. That means I was locked in. That means I wasn't forcing anything. But that Golden State game? That one hit different. You know how much that city means to me — to all of us out here in West Oakland. And to go into their building, where they left us, and come back from 15 down? That's the kind of game you remember. That's the kind of game that builds something. I've been saying it since camp: this group's got something. Ty's been playing out of his mind, Wendell's finding his rhythm, AJ's just scratching the surface. And the way we responded after that Spurs loss — first home loss ever — that told me everything. We could've folded, but we didn't. We came out against Denver and set the tone early. That's who we are. Eleven and two. We're not there yet. But we're headed somewhere.

## Dolores Nwachukwu — Police Captain

> Most Temescal property cases start as a walk-in or a phone call — someone's car got broken into, or a garage got hit. If there's no suspect and no usable evidence, it gets a case number but no report, and it stays that way unless something else comes in. It only gets reclassified if a pattern shows up — say, three break-ins on the same block in a week — then we pull the calls together and open a proper report. Otherwise, it sits as a low-priority entry until the file's closed out, usually after 90 days with no new leads.
