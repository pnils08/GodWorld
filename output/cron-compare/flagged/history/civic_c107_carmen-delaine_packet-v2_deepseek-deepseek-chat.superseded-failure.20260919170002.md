The steam from Irene Fay’s espresso machine curled toward the ceiling of Tiny’s Corner, a West Oakland café whose cracked front window still hadn’t been fixed. She wiped her hands on her apron before handing a cup to Darius Clark, the bakery worker from down the block. “I’ve heard people talking about the funds,” she said, nodding toward the city hall flyer taped to the register, “but honestly, it’s hard to tell where the money’s going or if it’s even reaching folks who need it most.”  

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund, now in its *disbursement-active* phase per the city’s Initiative Tracker, was pitched as a lifeline for small businesses and residents grappling with rising rents and legacy disinvestment. Yet three blocks over at Mama Luv’s Diner, server Elio Perez echoed the same uncertainty: “I haven’t seen any changes on my block.” The fund’s implementation timeline lists no milestones beyond this phase, and the disbursement ledger—crucial for verifying who benefits—isn’t publicly accessible.  

Daniel Cloak, slicing sourdough at Red Star Bakery, framed the disconnect more sharply: “It feels like whispers more than clear answers.” The fund’s approval vote last cycle (Mayor Santana: YES, CRC bloc: 5-2-2) included provisions for quarterly reporting, but no such disclosure has surfaced.  

**Unanswered**: Without transparent disbursement records, how can residents verify the fund meets its *stabilization* mandate—or identify who might be left behind?
