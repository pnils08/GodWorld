# Six Neighborhoods Slipped Eight to Ten Hundredths, and the Superintendent Won't Say Why

I kept my gradebook from my last year in the classroom. A student who drops a tenth of a point in one week gets a pencil mark in the margin, not a phone call home. You wait for the next week.

That is how I read this cycle's district table, with one difference. I went looking for what teachers see, and what I found was thin.

**What moved.** Six neighborhoods fell the most against C116:

- Fruitvale, 7.3 to 7.2
- Jack London, 7.39 to 7.3
- Grand Lake, 7.18 to 7.09
- Uptown, 7.12 to 7.03
- Downtown, 7.28 to 7.2
- West Oakland, 8 to 7.92

Most of the other neighborhoods slipped by a hundredth or a few hundredths. Rockridge is still on top at 8.36, down from 8.4. Temescal is at 6.21, down from 6.25. Four neighborhoods rose: Baylight District to 8.33, Lake Merritt to 8.04, Brooklyn to 8.22 and Piedmont Ave to 8.3.

**What didn't move.** Funding and teacher quality sit where they sat. West Oakland shows 14,500 in funding and teacher quality of 8.2. Fruitvale shows 13,000 and 7.9. Enrollment edged up in nearly every neighborhood, mostly by one or two students. Piedmont Ave added three.

So the dip did not come from money or staffing. Superintendent Rosalind Achterberg made that point when I put the numbers to her. "Whatever moved the index moved inside the classroom, not in the budget," she said. She also declined to explain the dip. Her sheet holds only the current cycle, she said, and she won't "narrate a dip I can't see the baseline for." She said the district will publish the prior-cycle comparison next to the current sheet. She added that she has no classroom-level explanation "that meets the standard of the record."

She also asked readers to keep the scale in mind. Uptown has 63 students on the table. A tenth of a point there is a handful of kids. The same applies to Lake Merritt's 59 and KONO's 60.

**What one counselor sees.** Karem Asante is 34 and a youth counselor at Oakland City Schools. He lives in West Oakland, one of the six. His work is the one-on-one conversation, not the table.

"I sit in those meetings and I hear a lot of talk about numbers, but what I see in my one-on-ones is kids carrying stuff they shouldn't have to," Asante said. "The index slipping? That's just a mirror of what they're bringing in—stress from home, from the block, from not knowing if they'll eat tonight. It's not a stat to me; it's their backpacks getting heavier."

That is one counselor's reading and it isn't a finding. I can't tie his observation to this week's index, and the district hasn't either. But it is the kind of thing a teacher notices before any table does. Attendance, tiredness, who stops asking for help. I'll be asking teachers in Fruitvale and Grand Lake whether they see it too.

**The pipeline.** Achterberg said the thing she is more worried about is not the drift. It is the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline, which the city tracker now lists as stalled. She said a placement-gap analysis with West Oakland and Fruitvale review is due at C118. Both neighborhoods are among the six. She didn't claim the stall caused the dip, and I won't either. But a career pathway on hold in the same neighborhoods where the index slipped is worth watching.

**The gap that stays.** The widest difference on the table is not this week's. San Antonio (6.6) and East Oakland (6.57) both get 11,000 per student, and both have teacher quality of 6.68. Rockridge gets 15,000 and has teacher quality of 9.13. Achterberg called that "a structural problem" that a tenth of a point shouldn't distract from.

"Regular" is my favorite word in this job, and a regular week is the story here. Funding is steady, staffing is steady and enrollment is creeping up. The index is a little lower in six places, and the district can't yet say why. I'll check again next cycle.
