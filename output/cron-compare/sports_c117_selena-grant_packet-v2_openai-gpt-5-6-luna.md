# The Oaks Have Made the Comeback Their First Identity

The Oaks went into halftime at Golden State down 59-44 and won the second half 54-37. That is the cleanest description yet of an 11-2 expansion team: not a club that has avoided unstable games, but one that has repeatedly found a way to change them.

The 98-96 win over the Warriors extended the Oaks’ winning streak to two. It also supplied the strongest version of a pattern that has been visible across the early schedule. Against Charlotte, the Oaks trailed 60-49 after three quarters and won the fourth 36-22. At Detroit, they were down 43-37 at halftime and won the second half 54-35. At Philadelphia, they trailed 82-80 after three and won the fourth 40-24. At Indiana, the same formula nearly worked from a larger deficit: down 82-62 after three, the Oaks won the fourth 38-22 before losing 104-100.

Across 13 games, the Oaks’ fourth-quarter line is 10-2-1. That is not proof that the team has solved its offense, defense or late-game execution. It is proof that the team’s best response has arrived after the game has already become difficult. The distinction matters for an expansion franchise. A stable team controls more of the game before the final period. This Oaks team has so far been defined by how much it can recover.

The Golden State numbers show both sides of that record. Austin Reaves produced 23 points, six rebounds, five assists and three steals in 33 minutes, shooting 9-for-17 from the field and 3-for-6 from three-point range. T. Jerome added 17 points, four rebounds and three assists without a turnover in 23 minutes. AJ Dybantsa scored 12 points and went 5-for-7 at the foul line. As a team, the Oaks shot 44 percent, made 9 of 34 threes and committed 23 turnovers.

That is not a clean statistical profile. The Oaks gave away possessions and did not shoot efficiently from outside. The second-half margin, rather than a dominant full-game shooting line, carried the result. The record says the response was enough. It does not say the underlying process will remain enough against every opponent.

Reaves has been the clearest individual stabilizer. He led the Oaks in scoring in the Golden State game and had done the same in the 137-113 home win over Denver, scoring 32 points in 22 minutes while going 8-for-14 from the field, 6-for-10 from three and 10-for-10 at the line without a turnover. He has led or shared the team scoring lead in six of 13 games. The $30 million highest-paid Oak is producing like the central investment, but the two performances also show different versions of his value: volume and efficiency against Denver, broader two-way production on the road.

The Oaks are 11-2, and the franchise’s stability is growing. The unresolved question is whether the comeback is a foundation or a dependency. The early record establishes that this team can win from behind. It has not yet established how often it can prevent the deficit in the first place.
