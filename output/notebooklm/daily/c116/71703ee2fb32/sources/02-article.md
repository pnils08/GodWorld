# The Fund Says Its Closeout Is Published. The Tracker Says It Isn't Verified.

By Carmen Delaine, The Bay Tribune

By Carmen Delaine | Bay Tribune Civic Ledger

Marcus Webb's statement for the West Oakland Stabilization Fund this cycle runs one sentence and settles one question for him. "The closeout is published, the ordinance is on the calendar with the scope that works and the 23-day clock still running — the hearing record carries the proof, and the roll call belongs to the Council."

The Initiative_Tracker's C116 milestone note settles it differently. It says the charter renewal was "kept on Council calendar" and that final action "requires verified C114 closeout, business-and-resident scope and the 23-day standard." The next scheduled action, set for C117, is to "Verify C114 closeout and conduct Council charter-renewal consideration." The Fund's program director calls the closeout published. The tracker still carries verification as work not yet done.

Brenda Okoro, the Deputy Mayor for Community Affairs, put the same three conditions in her own terms this cycle: "Renewal means West Oakland businesses and residents, 23 days on the clock, and a closeout record on the table before council votes." Her sentence puts the record before the vote. Webb's puts the proof in the hearing record and the roll call with the Council. Neither says who checks the closeout against the other two conditions before members vote.

## What $6.6 million shows

The tracker's milestone notes give a way to count who the Fund has reached. They record 47 checks, about $4.2 million, disbursed under Okoro's authority from C84 through C90. The paper's C84 coverage reported 342 applications, more than double what the city had projected. The notes then record $400,000 tranches approved or executed in each of C109 through C114. That is six tranches and $2.4 million. Added to the earlier $4.2 million, it comes to $6.6 million.

That total does not match the tracker's budget fields, which show $28 million authorized and $20.6 million remaining. Those fields imply $7.4 million out, and the tracker lists the last disbursement as C116. The $800,000 gap is the size of two more tranches, but the milestone notes do not itemize any disbursement in C115 or C116. I read the Fund's row and searched the paper's prior coverage for an itemized figure and found none. The two numbers reconcile only if one assumes tranches the notes do not name. The closeout, once verified, has to say which total is right.

The one tranche the notes break down is C109. It released $400,000 in West Oakland, and the note itemizes two household grants totaling $81,188. The note does not say where the other $318,812 went, whether to businesses, to more households, or to costs. Under the C109 scope correction, the Fund serves West Oakland businesses and residents. A tranche in which only two household grants are itemized does not show whether the business half of that scope has been reached at all.

## A scope that changed twice

The scope Council has made a condition of renewal was itself corrected on the record. The C108 note read the charter as stabilization grants for long-tenure West Oakland residents priced out as the flats built out, "not hood-wide relief." The C109 note then superseded it: the Fund "serves West Oakland businesses and residents," and a "Downtown canvass" line and a "residents only" reading were both set aside. The note adds that Downtown is outside the Fund's area. That is INIT-008, proposed and not voted.

So "the scope that works," in Webb's phrase, is the C109 reading. A closeout covering C114 should show grants under both halves of it. The C108 note counted 47 checks to residents. The C110 through C114 notes record only the $400,000 amounts and the 23-day standard, with no split between businesses and residents. The tracker's text does not show a business recipient anywhere.

## The clock and the roll call

The tracker records the 23-day figure as an achieved average in C110 and as a maintained standard in C111 and C112. The notes for C113 through C116 carry it forward as a condition. They do not say what the 23 days are measured from or to, or whether it is a ceiling or an average. The notes also do not say whether the closeout uses the same measure as the Council's C115 order. Webb says the clock is "still running," which fits a standard that has been kept but does not show how it is checked.

The Fund passed 6-3 in C78. The tracker's vote note names three members: Ramon Vega yes, Leonard Tran no, and Elliott Crane yes, remotely. It does not name the other six seats, and I will not fill them in. Whoever reads the closeout in C117 is voting to renew a fund whose original margin was three votes.

The Treasury closed C116 at $131.8 million, up from $126.6 million. The Fund's $20.6 million remaining is the figure that matters here. Whether it keeps moving in $400,000 steps depends on a charter the Council has not yet renewed.

The record cannot yet answer one question: who certifies the closeout, and against which definition of the 23 days? Webb says the proof is in the hearing record. The tracker says verification is still pending in C117. If those two are the same event, no one has said so on the record.
