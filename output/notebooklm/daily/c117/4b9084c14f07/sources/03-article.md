# Thirty-Nine Walks in 215 Innings: What Travis Coles's 2041 Command Line Proves

By Elliot Marbury, The Bay Tribune

**Elliot Marbury, Data Desk**

Travis Coles walked thirty-nine batters over 215 innings in 2041, his first season with Oakland. His career line works that out to 1.6 walks per nine innings and 6.51 strikeouts per walk, on 254 strikeouts. This memo makes one claim from those figures. The 2041 walk rate is the best of Coles's three seasons on file. It is the only one of the three that sits level with the best command on the Athletics staff. The sample is one season of 32 starts, so it counts as evidence of command and does not yet settle his identity.

**The three seasons**

All figures are from his career line, with rates worked from the season lines.

- **2039, Washington:** 197.2 IP, 51 BB, 198 SO, ERA 3.96, record 9-15. Rates: 9.0 K/9, 2.3 BB/9, 3.88 K/BB.
- **2040, Washington:** 219.1 IP, 61 BB, 207 SO, ERA 2.05, record 13-8. Rates: 8.5 K/9, 2.5 BB/9, 3.39 K/BB.
- **2041, Athletics:** 215 IP, 39 BB, 254 SO, ERA 2.72, record 22-4. Rates: 10.6 K/9, 1.6 BB/9, 6.51 K/BB.

Every season is a full-workload sample: 32 or 33 starts and at least 197.2 innings. Thin-sample variance is therefore not the main caveat here. The caveat is that there are only three seasons.

The hinge is 2040. That year carried the best ERA of the three, 2.05, and also the highest walk rate, 2.5 per nine. Run prevention and command moved in opposite directions. The paper's C83 coverage noted the 2.05 and the 207 strikeouts. His 2039 and 2040 walk rates were 2.3 and 2.5 per nine. Neither year prepares a reader for 1.6.

The ERA runs the other way. His 2041 ERA of 2.72 is higher than his 2040 mark, and he allowed 25 home runs against 17 in 2040. The command gain did not produce his best ERA. The claim here is about walks, not run prevention.

**The peer band**

Two Athletics starters supply the 2041 comparison.

- **Henry Rivas:** 136.1 IP, 24 BB, 133 SO, ERA 2.64. Rates: 8.8 K/9, 1.6 BB/9, 5.54 K/BB. His walk rate matches Coles's. His innings are the shorter sample, 22 starts. The paper's C98 coverage recorded a dislocated shoulder that cost him two months.
- **Benji Dillon:** 150.1 IP over 35 games, 28 of them starts, with one save, 65 BB, 132 SO, ERA 4.43. Rates: 7.9 K/9, 3.9 BB/9, 2.03 K/BB. His workload mixes starts and relief, so the comparison is looser.

Two readings follow. First, 1.6 walks per nine is not unique to Coles on this staff, since Rivas matches it. It is exceptional against Dillon's 3.9, and against Coles's own 2039 and 2040 figures. Second, Coles separates from Rivas on volume and strikeouts. He threw 215 innings against 136.1, and his 10.6 strikeouts per nine and 6.51 strikeouts per walk are both higher than Rivas's 8.8 and 5.54. Rivas has the narrower sample, so that gap should not be over-read.

**What the line does not show**

The record holds no pitch-mix, mechanics or velocity fields. It does not show why the walk rate moved between 2040 and 2041, and this memo does not guess. It also does not contain the cause of the 25 home runs. This cycle's feed note records one mark on the club's year: in World Series game 2, Clark Saryan and a second reliever each gave up two runs in relief of Coles. That is a relievers' line, not a Coles line. The memo carries no postseason sample for him and draws nothing from it.

**The claim and what would kill it**

The claim is that Coles's 1.6 walks per nine in 2041 is a command change and not noise, because it came over 215 innings against his own earlier norm of 2.3 to 2.5.

The test comes in his next full season with Athletics. If his walk rate returns to 2.3 per nine or higher, the 2041 figure was a single-year outlier, and the 2039 and 2040 lines describe him better. If it holds at or near 1.6 over a comparable workload, two seasons will carry the claim where one carries it now. A strikeout-to-walk ratio that falls back toward his 3.39 of 2040 would end it faster still. Only a second season can settle that, and the dossier does not hold one yet.
