# West Oakland's Month-Ten Money Clears the Desk. What Happens on the Street Is Not on the Ledger.

In West Oakland, the Stabilization Fund's latest payment is a number on a page before it is anything else: $400,000, approved for month ten. Mayor Avery Santana's office put the families at the center of it in its own record: "The families who qualified for this money six months ago shouldn't wait another day — month-ten disbursement is approved."

Six months is the gap the Mayor named. The ledger shows the money moving in the other direction, through a schedule that is already set. The program carries a $28,000,000 budget. The tracker shows $22,600,000 remaining and records Cycle 111 as the last disbursement. The Cycle 110 payment was the month-nine disbursement of $400K, and the tracker logged a 23-day average processing time that cycle. This cycle the record reports the 23-day processing standard maintained. That is the first clock the fund keeps, and it is the one the office most readily reports.

The fund's origin is also on the record. It was proposed by Avery Santana and voted in Cycle 78, where it passed 6-3 against a six-vote requirement. The tracker names three seats in that vote: Ramon Vega voted yes, Leonard Tran voted no, and Elliott Crane voted yes remotely. The packet does not name the votes of the other six seats, so this account does not place them. Mayor Santana signed the measure that same cycle. The fund is now in its disbursement-active phase, and its neighborhood is West Oakland.

Council District 1 framed the program's purpose in an office statement from Denise Carter: "When storefronts go quiet, families go next—so we're taking the Stabilization Fund to where the need is moving." The statement names a direction, not a location. The tracker lists West Oakland as the fund's neighborhood and nothing narrower. The packet does not show which blocks or households the "need" refers to, or how the money is distributed among them.

Brenda Okoro's office record offers the sharpest frame for the pace: "Stabilization is a race against displacement — month-ten disbursement keeps us ahead." Ahead of what is the open question. The packet records payment timing and processing speed. It does not record whether any household's housing outcome changed after a payment.

Marcus Webb, whose office issued the fund's administrative statement, made the strongest claim about the cases themselves: "Ninety-one households stabilized means ninety-one West Oakland families who won’t face displacement this month—and every case file meets compliance standards that will survive audit." The ninety-one figure is his. The compliance claim is his too. The packet contains no audit result. The claim is a forecast of what an audit will find, and it is not yet a record of one.

The next clock is already running. The tracker schedules the Month-Eleven disbursement review for Cycle 112. Across the same window, the West Oakland Tenant Defense Fund is at pending-vote, phase vote-scheduled, with a $4.2M budget and a vote set for Cycle 112. The fund's next review and the tenant defense vote fall in the same cycle, and both bear on West Oakland housing.

Two facts remain outside the record. The packet names no West Oakland block, household group, or tenant count beyond Webb's ninety-one. It also gives no measure of how long after a disbursement a household's situation changes, if it changes at all. Those are the points where the money's movement would have to meet the street, and the ledger does not reach them.

The fund's own closing question is the one this record cannot answer: is the Stabilization Fund truly stabilizing West Oakland, or is it just moving money? The month-ten payment is approved, the processing standard is held, and the review is scheduled for Cycle 112. Whether the next accounting shows households held in place, or only payments made on time, is the test the office has not yet been asked to pass in public.
