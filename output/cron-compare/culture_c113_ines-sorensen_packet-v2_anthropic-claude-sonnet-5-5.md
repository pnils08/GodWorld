"The A's success is all anyone can talk about around here. I mean, I'm a huge fan, and my husband Deacon is too. We plan our whole schedule around the games."

That is Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for BART and lives in Lake Merritt. She was answering what the A's winning has done to the city around her, and she said the rest almost as an afterthought, the way people do. The schedule comes first. Everything else gets arranged around it.

Uptown is a short ride from her, and two people there answered the same question in nearly opposite directions.

"The A's winning season really brings Uptown together. When they're doing well, the whole neighborhood feels it. The bars and galleries stay packed, and you can feel the energy. It's like we're all connected then."

Priya Narang runs EMS in Uptown. Her own pages this cycle are mostly the board on her wall and the charts on her desk, so it counts for something that when a reporter asked about the team, her voice went somewhere lighter. She said *then*. When they're doing well, it's like we're all connected *then*.

Omar Amici, a construction laborer, lives in the same neighborhood and was asked the same thing. He did not talk about the games.

"To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy. The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days. I've lived here long enough to see the neighborhood transform, but this feels different. It's like the neighborhood is shifting away from the people who've been here all along."

He builds things for a living, and he said the streets are busier than ever. He did not say it as a boast. Lives has carried his rent worries before, and this cycle he said them again to a reporter in his own words. The line stands as he gave it.

Across the hills in Rockridge, Dax Monroe, an artist, took the question to a window. Lives carried the wager this cycle, and Dax was somewhere nearby in that story, but when I asked, the answer wasn't about money or baseball.

"I've been thinking a lot about the cycles of life in Rockridge lately. The other night, I was at the window, just observing the world go by. It made me reflect on how people's lives are intertwined with the events around them. I've seen it in my own life, with my kids growing up and the community changing."
