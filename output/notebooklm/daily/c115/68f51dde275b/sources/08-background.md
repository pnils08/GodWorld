# From the Tribune archive: Nineteenth Street Filled Up, and the Rows Do Not Say Why

What The Bay Tribune published before today about the people and places in Trevor Shimizu's story "Nineteenth Street Filled Up, and the Rows Do Not Say Why". Earlier coverage, not today's news.

In Cycle 107, AC Transit bus operator Coen Cartier pulled up to 12th Street Station in heavy morning fog, observing to reporter Trevor Shimizu that platform crowds routinely rise and fall based on what is happening across Oakland—from major City Hall votes and stadium games to weather and holidays—rather than a single isolated cause. Station worker Eleanor Rivera similarly noted that external disruptions, sports events, and council votes constantly shift commuter routines across downtown platforms, while mechanics and station crews adjusted operations to sustain an 85 percent on-time baseline.

By Cycle 109, Cartier documented ongoing bus corridor congestion across central transit routes as downtown activity expanded. Running parallel to daily operations, Elena Soria Dominguez, planning lead for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Authority, voiced persistent concerns in Cycle 112 regarding rapid transit-oriented development and community accountability, noting the personal pressure of ensuring major capital projects do not displace established residents or make them feel like outsiders in their own neighborhoods.

In Cycle 114, transportation reporting analyzed sharp ridership divergences across the system, contrasting rising downtown stations with Coliseum Station’s unexplained drop of 799 boardings on a weekend with no maintenance or scheduled events on its ledger. That same cycle, Elena Soria Dominguez observed that while Phase II transit hub planning blueprints detail geometry, walkway widths, and sightlines, they consistently overlook the living presence of street vendors and residents who actively claim station plazas. Transit operators concluded that platforms naturally swell whenever neighborhood pulls converge, leaving open the broader question of whether municipal transit design and service schedules can keep pace when citywide events align.
