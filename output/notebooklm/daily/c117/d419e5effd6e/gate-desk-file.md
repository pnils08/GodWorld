# Oakland Casino Town lost three jobs. Nobody will say who they were.

**By Jordan Velez, Economics & Labor**

Oakland Casino Town in the Baylight District shed three jobs this cycle. The ledger now shows 94 employees, down from 97, and the casino's annual growth rate is -1.5%. In the same cycle, DigitalOcean in Baylight added one job. So did Bayline 6 in Jack London, and so did Atlas Bay Architects and the Bay Tribune Downtown. Disclosure: the Tribune is my employer. Its one added job is in the ledger, but I haven't asked the paper about it.

I set out to name the three people, and I can't. The ledger carries a headcount and an average salary, not names. Marcus Osei, the Deputy Mayor for economic development, told me his office has "no names, no roles, no severance record, no exit survey." Firms that shed people quietly don't file paperwork with his office, he said, and he has no authority to demand it.

## What the casino says

Casino Manager Philly Rodriguez, who is on the record, gave me a description but no names. "It wasn't layoffs — it was attrition, people moving on," he said. "Two of them were dealers who got offers down in San Jose, and one was a security guy who went back to school. I can't give you their names, that's not my place."

He added: "Nobody who stayed got a raise, and nobody got a cut either. Hours are the same, pay's the same. The floor's just a little thinner now, so we're all covering a bit more."

Manfred Owens, who keeps the book near the back tables, tells it a little differently. He said he couldn't name the three either, since he doesn't have the list. He described weeknight tables that are thinner and shifts that are stretched. "I've picked up a couple extra hours myself," he said. He also referred to "the folks who got let go here." That is not how Rodriguez describes the departures. I can't reconcile the two accounts, and no record I have settles it. The "attrition" explanation comes from management and is unverified.

## The numbers

- **Scale of the loss.** Three jobs out of 97 is about 3.1% in one cycle. The one-job gains elsewhere are far smaller in percentage terms. DigitalOcean's is about 0.1% of 801 employees, and Bayline 6's is about 0.7% of 143. These are one-person moves, and I wouldn't call them a trend.
- **A slide, not a blip.** The casino had 99 employees in C115, when I last wrote about it, and 94 now. That is a net loss of five over two cycles.
- **Pay.** The ledger's average casino salary is $70,000. By that average, three departures take roughly $210,000 a year in payroll out of a district whose median income is $139,000. That payroll figure is my arithmetic from an average. It is not a record of what those three were paid. Rodriguez says pay for those who stayed is unchanged.
- **Where the other jobs are.** The firms adding people are in cloud infrastructure (801 jobs), media and architecture. The casino is nightlife and entertainment. The jobs growing around it are not casino-type jobs.
- **Where the gains sit.** Osei called the pattern "churn, not growth." As he put it, "One job added at DigitalOcean and three lost at the casino is not a wash."

## Who gains from the casino?

The ledger can't answer that. It shows a casino that is, in Rodriguez's words, "not hurting — just not growing." The only wagering data I have for this cycle is thin: 17 undocked wagers and 14 sports wagers, of which 2 settled and 15 voided. Maria Keen won on an undocked wager, staking $385 and collecting $705. Jax Caldera staked $428 and lost it to the house. The house float stands at $246,000.

What the record does show is that the people who staff the casino are covering more floor for the same pay. Meanwhile the district around them posts record-high incomes, and the gains are going to firms that don't deal cards.

I'll keep pressing for the three names, and for an exit record that would tell us whether they chose to leave or were pushed. The city, as Osei put it, can't compel that, and neither can I.
