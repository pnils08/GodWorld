**Missing verification records leave passed initiatives in limbo**

Cycle 117 opened with a clear pattern across the city's initiative tracker: several measures that carry "passed" status or advanced phases still lack the documented verification steps their own milestones require before further action or certification can proceed.

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund, passed at Cycle 78 and disbursement-active since Cycle 108, last saw work in Cycle 116. Its next required step—verified C114 closeout before any Council charter-renewal consideration—remains uncompleted. Renewal support stays contingent on that verification, the original business-and-resident scope, and the 23-day standard. No closeout record appears in the current packet.

The Oakland Alternative Response Initiative, passed at Cycle 82 and dispatch-live since Cycle 108, was last worked this cycle by the District 5 council office. Expansion remains on hold pending a published D3 Fruitvale briefing record and written next dates for the D5 audit and Baylight feasibility review. Those records have not been executed or filed.

Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II—Visioning, completed at Cycle 86 and now in design-phase since Cycle 108, requires delivery of preliminary schematics and a documented Fruitvale public comment-and-response record before design can advance. The C117 packet shows neither.

The Temescal Community Health Center, passed at Cycle 80 and operational since Cycle 114, needs its Q3 clinical intake audit filed with HCAI and the Council Clerk, plus a documented Telegraph & 47th merchant walkthrough. Oversight certification is withheld until both are verified; operations continue without that step.

Baylight District construction, passed at Cycle 83, requires publication of the construction milestone review against the assembled evidence file. No such pass has been declared, and the review itself has not been completed or released while remediation, hotline, dust-control, and workforce oversight remain active.

The Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline sits stalled. Publication of a placement-gap analysis and adjustment plan with explicit West Oakland and Fruitvale review is the stated prerequisite; that document has not appeared.

Health Center Project Director Bobby Chen-Ramirez put the standard plainly: "Look, I run a health center, not a ledger. But when I see passed initiatives and the verification records don't line up, that's the same feeling I get when the Q3 audit sits on my desk and I haven't walked the building with the shopkeepers yet. The paperwork can say passed all day long, but if nobody can show me the records that prove it, then it's just a monument, not a working thing. What I want is simple: the same standard I hold my own clinic to. Show me the records, walk me through them, and let the people who actually live with the result—the merchants, the neighbors, the folks who need the care—check the work. That's the answer. That's the only answer that holds up when someone actually tries the front door."

Multiple other measures—Downtown Economic Revitalization, Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake, Ivy Hill and Dimond filings, the West Oakland Tenant Defense Fund, and the Laurel Neighborhood Patch and Clean-Up—carry proposed or operational labels but list missing committee referrals, Clerk-certified roll calls, scope documents, or verified filings as the immediate next actions due Cycle 118.

What we know is the list of required verifications and the cycle-by-cycle milestones that remain open. What we do not yet have are the completed records that would close those gaps or allow the next phase to proceed on documented evidence rather than status alone.
