# Harry Hayward’s 22 Minutes Changed the Question

Harry Hayward scored 19 points on 9-of-15 shooting, added seven rebounds and two blocks in 22 minutes, and helped the Oaks turn a two-point deficit after three quarters in Philadelphia into a 120-106 victory. For a second-round rookie whose minutes have moved from 13 to 23 across the recent game lines, the career high is not just a scoring note. It is the clearest argument yet for a larger place in an expansion rotation.

The Oaks are 8-1 and have won four straight. That record gives the organization room to develop a player without forcing the issue, but it also makes Hayward’s latest performance harder to treat as a one-night departure. He scored five points with two steals and a block in 16 minutes against Orlando, then followed that with the 19-point Philadelphia line. Before those games, his listed outputs included four points in 13 minutes against Oklahoma City, eight points, five rebounds and three assists in 23 minutes against Toronto, eight points on 3-of-4 shooting in 13 minutes against Indiana, 10 points and 10 rebounds against New York, and 12 points against Detroit.

The line is uneven. The trend is the opportunity it creates. Hayward has shown scoring efficiency, rebounding, passing and defensive production in different-sized assignments, but the record does not establish that those contributions will hold at a fixed workload. It establishes something narrower and more useful: the Oaks have evidence that his game can scale beyond the limited role reflected in several of his earlier appearances.

That matters because the rest of the rotation is functioning. In Philadelphia, AJ Dybantsa scored 25 points, Adash Stanley had 23 points and seven assists, and Wendell Carter Jr. finished with 12 points and 15 rebounds. T. Jerome added nine assists. The Oaks did not need Hayward to carry the offense; they needed him to convert the minutes he received. He did that while the team scored 40 points in the fourth quarter.

The Orlando result supplies the other half of the read. The Oaks won 125-109 for their third straight victory, with Stanley producing 24 points, eight assists and no turnovers, Dybantsa shooting 11-of-13 for 23 points, and Dillon Brooks scoring 23. Hayward played 16 minutes in that win. A growing franchise can protect a rookie inside a successful rotation. It can also use the success to test whether the rookie belongs in more demanding stretches.

Hayward’s $4.5 million figure is part of the roster record, not proof of a promised role. The Philadelphia performance changes the evidence, not the obligation to evaluate it carefully. The coaching staff still owes the next answer: how will Hayward’s recent production affect his minutes, and will the 22-minute performance become a larger role rather than remain a career night?
