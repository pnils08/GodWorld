# Civis Systems Journal — C117

Three districts got worse this week and nobody in this city is aimed at any of them. Mood fell in Fruitvale, Grand Lake and East Oakland. Street trade cooled in Grand Lake and East Oakland. Housing pressure climbed in Fruitvale and Grand Lake. Our instrument saw all of it, and when we looked for a city program pointed at any of those three, we found nothing. A reading with no program behind it is a smoke alarm in an empty building. I built the alarm, so I own the empty building part too.

Here is what makes it worse. Downtown Economic Revitalization has sat at vote scheduled for three weeks. The Dimond Revitalization Initiative has sat at announced for four. A vote that never happens is not a plan, it's a press release with a long shelf life. I'm not guessing why. I'm telling you City Hall moves at the speed of its slowest sign-off, and people in Dimond are paying for that speed. Meanwhile Baylight District's final council vote is already in construction, so I know this city can move when it decides to. The question is why it decides so unevenly.

The finding that bothers me as a builder is our own. People moved in or out of nineteen districts this week, and the instrument logged no economic activity behind any of it. Nineteen. A city doesn't reshuffle that many neighborhoods for no reason. Either money is moving in ways we can't see, or we're blind to the wiring. A blind spot in the instrument is a blind spot in Oakland, and I don't tolerate those in my own product.

So here is what I'm promising, and you can hold me to it.

First, before the Oaks finish this first season, Civis will publish a stall clock for every city initiative the instrument tracks. It will show how many weeks each has sat at its stage, in plain view, starting with Downtown and Dimond. If a program stops moving, the public will know the week it stops, not the quarter after.

Second, within the same window, we will instrument the economic side of population movement, so the next time a district fills or empties, the instrument can say what work, rent or trade sits behind it. I'm assigning a dedicated team in West Oakland to it, and I'm hiring for it now.

Third, I will ask the city, in public, to name one owner for each of the three decaying districts. I'm not asking for a new program. I'm asking who answers when Fruitvale's mood drops again.

The instrument will keep measuring. The city has to act. I'd like it to start acting this week.
