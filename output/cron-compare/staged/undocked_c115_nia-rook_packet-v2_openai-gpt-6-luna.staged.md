## UNDOCKED: The leader holds; Elijah Brown runs out the clock

Luis Taz keeps the top spot, but Elijah Brown made Monday night a long scenic route to fifth place. The board lists Taz at #1 with 45.19 achievement points, two cycles led and a current streak of two; Brown’s own account puts him fifth, at 28.13 points and zero credits. So the episode’s delicious tension isn’t a crown changing hands. It’s the distance between the leader’s steady perch and a pilot who kept flying until the show’s time ran out.

Brown’s route had the rhythm of a travelogue whose narrator refuses to land: Sol, Alpha Centauri, Tau Ceti, Timberline, Arcturus, Ross 154, Maplevale, Miaplacidus, Cervantes, Ridgecrest, Hatysa, Nembus, Kitalpha, Lacaille 8760, Kepler 442, Theemin, Hollowcrest. Seventeen named stops, then the credits ledger comes back even. That’s a peculiar kind of finale: motion everywhere, no change in credits.

“I ended up 5th on the board after Monday night, with 28.13 points and 0 credits. I flew through a bunch of systems until the show's time ran out, and I came out even on credits,” Brown said. The quote has its own tidy landing: no grand claim of victory, just the mileage, the clock, and the result. In Rockridge, you can imagine the shared-screen room splitting its attention between the route and the standings—one eye on how far Brown went, the other on whether anyone at the top has reason to blink.

The earlier approved record gives Taz a different kind of screen presence. It lists three C114 entries for him, each with zero credits; the recorded combat figures are 12, zero and zero. The routes vary, and the record doesn’t establish an earlier rank, so this isn’t a before-and-after chart. It’s simply a glimpse of a board leader whose recent show history contains more than one shape of night. That contrast makes Brown’s time-out sprint feel less like a direct challenge and more like a reminder that the episode can give you a route to remember without rewriting the standings.

I like that imbalance. UNDOCKED can hand a pilot a full itinerary and still leave the board’s central question untouched. Taz leads; Brown’s Monday is the episode’s vivid counterpoint, not evidence that the lead has moved. The audience can argue over what counts as a satisfying night—distance, points, credits, or the pleasure of hearing the clock catch up—but the next installment gets the cleanest cliffhanger: can Luis Taz maintain his lead in the next cycle?
