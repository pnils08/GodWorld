### **1. Baylight District Phase II Procurement & Local-Hire Mandates (INIT-006)**

#### **Direct Published Facts & Prior Events**
*   **Authorization & Scope:** Passed in Cycle 83 as a **\$2.1 billion public-private financing package** (Ordinance BD-83-TIF) covering 65 acres of waterfront [1-3]. Phase II procurement covers **3.2 million square feet across 18 to 24 acres** with an estimated capital cost of **\$1.1B to \$1.4B**, centered around a **20,000-seat indoor arena** [4, 5].
*   **The Shortlist:** In Cycle 103, the Baylight Redevelopment Authority, led by Director **Keisha Ramos**, officially published the Phase II shortlist, evaluating and narrowing the qualified bidder pool from four down to **three firms** [6].
*   **The Local-Hire Sub-Tier Loophole:** While Phase I reported **43% local hiring** on paper [7-9], the prime agreement contractually defines "local" broadly as *Alameda County* [10]. Subcontractors routinely pull union labor from Livermore, San Leandro, and Hayward [10, 11]. Master electrician **Kevin Kim** (4 licensed West Oakland employees) lost two Baylight electrical subcontracts to Livermore firms despite meeting all bonding and certification requirements [12]. Columnist Jax Caldera conducted a spot-count at the *Anchor & Rail* construction bar, finding that **only 4 out of 30 workers (13%) wearing Baylight badges were actual Oakland residents** [3, 13-15].
*   **Recusal Firewall:** Director Keisha Ramos executed a **four-part recusal protocol** to firewall Civis Systems from Phase II planning and bidding due to billionaire **Elias Varek's** "triple overlap" as Oaks owner, Civis Systems founder, waterfront equity holder, and stadium naming-rights holder (*Civis Systems Field — The Town*) [16-19].

#### **Analytical Inferences & Unresolved Storylines**
*   **Cycle 105 Milestone:** Cycle 105 is the scheduled municipal tracker gate for **final bid evaluation and selection** among the three shortlisted development teams [6]. 
*   **Sub-Tier Enforcement:** Union representatives like Mateo Walker (Port of Oakland) infer that without explicit Oakland-specific language in sub-tier contracts [20], local hiring mandates will remain toothless unless the City Council enforces strict **25% apprentice mandates** tied to the Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline (INIT-007) [21, 22].

*   **Cycle 105 Context:** Following the publication of the **C104 findings report and intake evaluation** [6], the newsroom must track whether the city implements binding recruitment mechanisms in East and West Oakland to prevent general contractors from importing out-of-town union crews for Baylight Phase II [64].
