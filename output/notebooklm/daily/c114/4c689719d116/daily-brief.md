#### **The Pole on Rockridge Corner Is Starting to Say His Name**
*By Kai Marston, Media & Metro Desk*

On a telephone pole outside the Tuesday Room in Rockridge, a torn, rain-stained flyer has outlasted its staples. Two doors down, **Benji Dillon** walks with a paper cup in hand and a hold on his son Rick’s sleeve. The veteran Athletics pitcher has lived in the neighborhood long enough to be part of the sidewalk scenery, but his presence in local conversation has begun to surge.

Dillon himself deflects talk of celebrity back to his family:
> *"I'm not sure what's behind my growing presence in Rockridge. I live here, I've been part of the community for a while, and I'm just doing my thing. I've been around long enough that people know me, I guess. I'm just trying to be a good neighbor and a good dad to Rick."*

Across the clubhouse and the neighborhood, teammates watch Dillon's rising profile from varying distances:
*   **Darrin Davis** (Left Fielder, Piedmont Ave) recognizes the trajectory: *"I've seen Benji Dillon's name popping up more often around the A's, and it's clear he's gaining traction. To be honest, I'm not super close to him, but you can tell when someone's rising through the ranks. I've been around long enough to recognize the buzz... It's great for the team to have fresh talent making waves."*
*   **Danny Horn** (Center Fielder, Temescal) keeps his focus locked on team goals: *"To be honest, I've not really thought much about Benji Dillon's rising fame. I'm focused on my own game and helping the A's make a deep run. I've seen him around the clubhouse, he's a talented kid, but I don't know him well enough to say much about his personal story."*
*   **Travis Coles** (Starting Pitcher, Downtown) offered a brief note: *"I know he's a teammate, but I don't really know him personally. I've seen his name in the papers, though."*

#### **The Pantry Has Been Here Since C83. The Founder Lives Across the Bridge of Himself.**
*By Maria Keen, Culture Desk*

In Temescal, two distinct community programs frame the block's daily support network. The **Temescal Islamic Center Food Pantry** has operated quietly on the block since Cycle 83, serving as a dependable, long-standing neighborhood anchor. By contrast, **The Reaves Line Transit Fund** arrived in Cycle 109, founded by professional basketball player **Austin Reaves**, who lives in West Oakland.

Reaves spoke with frank honesty about the physical distance between his life and the fund:
> *"To be honest, I'm not really sure about The Reaves Line Transit Fund's impact on Temescal. I founded it, but I've been focused on the A's and family stuff lately. I know it's been around since C109, and it's still active, but I haven't been keeping up with its day-to-day. I live in West Oakland, so I don't really see its effects firsthand."*
