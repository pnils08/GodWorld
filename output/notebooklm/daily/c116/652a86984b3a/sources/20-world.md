  - StoryAngle: Paulson cashes in the Carmen Mesa chip with the team the A's just beat. Giancarlo Hernandez makes three A-potential third basemen
  - Notes: Mike Paulson answered the question this page asked in C114. Carmen Mesa was the A's best trade chip, and now he is gone, sent to the Phillies, the team the A's beat in five games for their seventh World Series title. Mesa, 23, 81 overall with B potential, goes to Philadelphia with relief prospect Porter Broadway, 20, 70 overall with C potential, and second baseman Jose Fuentes, 23, 63 overall with B potential. Coming back to Oakland are third baseman Giancarlo Hernandez and left fielder Antone Baez. Mesa never got a fair shot at a rotation spot here. He was called up in C91 when Henry Rivas dislocated his shoulder, threw a complete game shutout against the Cubs in his first start, 9 innings, 5 hits and 12 strikeouts, and struck out 10 more against the Rays his next time out. When Rivas came back, Mesa was optioned to AAA, and Pablo Almanzar got the late-season call-up and then the fifth rotation spot behind Coles, Ramos, Rivas and Ellis. Mesa, born in Cuba and living in Lake Merritt, was stuck at $780K with nowhere to start. Hernandez is the prize. He is 23, a 2036 first round pick born in the Dominican Republic, wears #15, bats and throws right, and has almost no major league service time. His glove is already elite: 96 fielding, 80 reaction and a 70 arm. The bat is behind the glove, 65 contact against righties and 68 against lefties with modest power, but he hits to all fields and improved in nearly every attribute this cycle, including +9 contact against lefties. He grades A potential at 78 overall and climbing. That gives the A's three young third basemen with A potential. Ernesto Quintero took the job from Martin Richards, and Kevin Clark, 22, was supposed to be next, with Quintero sliding to first base if Mark Aitken leaves in free agency. Baez, 24, 70 overall with C potential, starts in the minors, organizational outfield depth behind Desmond Morton, Frank Reyna and JR Rosado. The Phillies players carry no listed salary, and the A's budget room rises from $129.42M to $130.35M.

- **A's — player-feature (off-season):** Kevin Clark (3B), Giancarlo Hernandez (3B), Ernesto Quintero (3B/1B), Deacon Seymour (Manager), Mike Paulson (GM)
  - StoryAngle: The bat or the glove? Kevin Clark's .327 against Giancarlo Hernandez's 96 fielding for third base
  - Notes: Back in C101, when Kevin Clark got the call from Las Vegas after the Martin Richards trade, he said the strange part was getting your shot because somebody else's luck ran out. "I'm not here to replace anyone," he said. "Just here to help." One trade later, he is the one looking over his shoulder. Giancarlo Hernandez arrived from Philadelphia in the Carmen Mesa deal as a third baseman with an A potential glove, and that puts Clark's everyday job in question before he has really had it. Put the two side by side and they are close to mirror images. Clark, 22, from Connecticut, the A's 2037 first round pick, is the hitter: 76 contact against righties and 72 against lefties, 83 discipline, 87 clutch, 57 power against righties, 72 speed and 79 stealing. He is a pull hitter with the Pinch Hitter quirk, and he gained in nearly every attribute this cycle, +6 contact and +6 power against righties, +5 vision and +5 discipline, now 79 overall and climbing. In his 55 at bats this season he hit .327 with 3 homeruns, 11 RBI, 5 steals and 1.0 WAR. Hernandez, 23, is the glove: 96 fielding, 80 reaction and a 70 arm against Clark's 62, 62 and 55, with less bat at 65 and 68 contact. One is the better hitter, the other may be the best defensive third baseman in the organization the day he arrives. Clark also lists 2B and RF as secondary positions, which Hernandez does not, so there may be room for both, though Eric Taveras owns second base and right field already has Vladimir Gonzalez and Frank Reyna. Clark makes $780K in 2042, is under team control through 2047 and has all 3 minor league options left, so the A's can send him to Las Vegas without risk if Hernandez wins the job. Ernesto Quintero is the third piece: he moves to first base if Mark Aitken, the veteran who reached out to Clark the day he was called up, leaves in free agency. Open questions for Deacon Seymour and Mike Paulson: who starts at third on opening day, does Clark's bat play at second or in right, does the club value the 96 glove over the .327 bat, and does Clark, who stepped in when Richards was traded, now see someone step in for him?

- **A's — rumor (off-season):** Mark Aitken (1B), Mike Paulson (GM), Ernesto Quintero (3B/1B)
  - StoryAngle: The Angels show strong interest in A's free agent Mark Aitken, the Oakland native whose whole life is in Jack London
  - Notes: The rumor this page raised in C112 now has a team attached. Word around the league is that the Anaheim Angels have strong interest in Mark Aitken, the A's first baseman and one of the top free agents on the market at 93 overall. Aitken, 33, is coming off a contract year that looked nothing like a decline: .283 with 153 hits, 33 homeruns, 87 RBI and 3.2 WAR in 540 at bats, plus the 3 homerun night against the Phillies and homeruns in the World Series that clinched the seventh title. He made $21.4M in 2041, and any offer that pulls him south will start near that number. The Angels make sense on paper. Aitken was born in California, and a contender in Anaheim could sell him a short drive and a starting job. But Aitken is an Oakland native, and his whole life is here. He owns his place in Jack London, where he lives with his wife and three kids. He is the A's player rep and community liaison, he runs Aitken Little Leaguers, free baseball for East Oakland kids now going into its sixth year, and he shows up at city council and planning hearings without ever testifying, the way he brought Benji Dillon to the DEIR session. He was a major voice in the Baylight negotiations, and he was the veteran who reached out to Kevin Clark the day Clark got the call. P Slayer once wrote that "Aitken is Oakland in a way that can't be replaced." Aitken has not said where he is leaning, but his own words this season point home. On the parade bus after the World Series he said he was watching Vinnie Keane and Benji Dillon take in their last ride, and that he was taking it in too, "for a different reason. I don't know what's next. None of us do." Later he said, "the number on the calendar means more than the number on my back," and that "the contract will sort itself out; the city has a way of holding on to its own, and I've been its own for a long, long time." The A's side of it matters as much. The Mesa trade brought in Giancarlo Hernandez, which gives the club three young third basemen and an obvious answer at first if Aitken goes: Ernesto Quintero slides across the diamond. That makes it easier for Mike Paulson to let him walk, and harder to explain to a city that just watched Keane retire.

- **Oaks — season-state (early-season):** Wilson Shepard (Coach), Mike Paulson (President of Operations), Elliot Abraham (GM)
  - StoryAngle: From 0-9 to 8-1: Wilson Shepard's Oaks caught people off guard, just like he said they would
  - Notes: Ten cycles ago this page was asking Wilson Shepard for answers. The Oaks went 0-4 in rookie camp and 0-5 in the preseason, 0-9 before they had played a game that counted, and in C109 the media wanted to know why the team was losing and why the full roster had never been on the floor together. Shepard, a first-year head coach who spent the previous season as an offensive assistant in Minnesota, kept saying the same thing. In C107 he said the Oaks would "catch people off guard this year." In C109 he said, "We're still finding our rhythm. The guys are putting in the work, and I'm confident we'll tighten up and be ready for the real deal. We're not getting the results we want, but we're not letting it get us down either." In C110, after the loss to Miami, he said the full team had yet to suit up as one, that opening night would show a team nobody had seen, and that he was not worried. The fans were. Nine games into the real deal, the Oaks are 8-1, have won four straight and are 1-0 in the NBA Cup. They beat Charlotte 85-82 in the franchise's first game, coming back from 11 down after three, won in Detroit, New York and Washington, lost by four in Indiana after coming back from 20 down, then routed Toronto 135-75 and beat Oklahoma City, Orlando and Philadelphia. They are scoring 109.9 a game and allowing 89.6, a margin of more than 20 points a night. The surprise is how they are winning. Shepard came in as the offensive specialist, but the early identity is defense: Charlotte scored 82, Detroit 78, Toronto 75 and Washington 62. He has also found rotation pieces nobody expected, like second-round rookie Harry Hayward, and kept the bench fresh, with nobody playing more than 28 minutes in the Toronto rout. After the opener he laughed that his ears were still ringing, then said the next loss could be a game away and the job was to build on the good and eliminate the bad. The other half of this story is the man above him. Mike Paulson is president of operations for an 8-1 expansion team, with Elliot Abraham running the day-to-day as GM, and he is also the GM of the A's in the middle of an offseason that has already seen Vinnie Keane retire, Carmen Mesa traded to the Phillies, Mark Aitken drawing Angels interest in free agency and Benji Dillon undecided. In C110 fans worried that a first-year coach, a first-time GM and a front office chief busy with a World Series run was too much uncertainty. So far the Oaks have answered that. Then there is Elliot Abraham, the first-time GM who has said almost nothing since C111. That cycle, with the team 0-9, he told the media nobody was panicking, that the losing was intentional and that every variation they tried was data for the long term. He also said the Draymond Green trade "wasn't ideal form" for a player the community reveres, and that Jerami Grant's status was a matter the league was looking into. In C112 Elias Varek confirmed Grant had failed his medical and credited Paulson and Abraham with building a quality team in one offseason. Abraham has not spoken since. His path here is not a typical basketball one: born in Port-au-Prince, Haiti, adopted young by an American family, MIT, the man who built the first successful professional pickleball league, then senior operations under Varek at Civis Systems and Paulson's day-to-day operator in his one season with the Chicago Bulls before Oakland hired him in C101. The Bulls beat his Oaks in the preseason in C110. He lives in Jack London, has been seen staying late in the clubhouse after wins, and around the waterfront he is still more of a familiar face than a public one.
