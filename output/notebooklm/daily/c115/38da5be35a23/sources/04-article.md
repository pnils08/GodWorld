# The Page: Eleven Lives, Cycle 115

By Tamsin Pruitt, The Bay Tribune

Maurice Franklin has a place of their own in Rockridge now, and is renting it. The Household Ledger set the move down in C115. It gives no street, no rent and no lease. It does not say where Franklin lived before.

The same cycle's record kept one small line that fits the day. Franklin brought home a new plant and picked its spot carefully.

The thread under it is less settled. The record carried Franklin through a run of money lines. In C111 they borrowed against next month to close this one. In C112 the card statement was the first thing read and the last thing forgotten. In C113 they paid the minimum and looked away. In C114 they borrowed against next month again. In C115 the card statement came around a second time, word for word. The record shows a person with a lease and a plant, and the statement still arriving.

It also kept warmer lines. In C113 Franklin traded produce over a fence and came out ahead. In C114 they drove the church van on the senior trip and listened to three hours of unfiltered neighborhood gossip. That same cycle, Latasha Rivera became a real friend and not just a colleague. In C115 Franklin recognized the OARI van idling outside the corner store and knew better than to ask who it was waiting for.

The paper has named Franklin before, and the lines don't all agree. Letters in C91 carried a Maurice Franklin of Rockridge as a retired transit supervisor writing about the A's streak. The ledger now carries Franklin as a drone fleet coordinator, mid-career, born 1978. The Page in C112 had Franklin home to Rockridge after a hospital stay for severe seasonal flu. The Tribune also named Franklin in two civic pieces on Chinatown's sick, C113 and C114, and in a Business piece on the Board in the Trailer, C114. The record does not reconcile the old trade with the new one. It does not say how the flu stay led to this lease, or whether it did.

**Soledad Coleman** rents a place of her own in Rockridge too, the same cycle. The ledger has her as an AI safety researcher at Anthropic. In C114 she badged into the Baylight office early and tried not to think about the protestors from last month. In C115 she traded an extra ticket for a promise to bring the kid next time. She attended a small local gathering and stayed later than planned. She also realized the same seven meals had rotated for a month, and shrugged.

Asked about the neighbor who moved in near her, she said: "I've seen Maurice Franklin around the neighborhood, but I don't really know them. I moved into my place in Rockridge, and so did they, I think around the same time. We haven't really talked, just nodded at each other when we see each other."

**Tom Jilner**, the Rockridge wine bar owner, lost on the UNDOCKED book in C115. He staked 144, and the book kept it. The ledger's own line for the week reads that the slip came back empty. The week also held a quieter fact. He woke at 5 AM for thirty minutes of total silence before the house woke, and he said no to something and felt the shape of who he is in it. The Tribune's C114 Civic piece had his bar saying "steady" while the map said decay.

On the new neighbor, Jilner said: "I've seen Maurice Franklin around Rockridge; they just moved into a place of their own. I haven't had a chance to talk to them yet, but it's good to see new faces in the neighborhood."

**Marisol Achebe**, who organizes for the Mutual Aid Network in Fruitvale, lost on the UNDOCKED book in C115. She staked 134, and the book kept it. The same week she took a casual walk through her area and caught herself bragging a little about the neighborhood. Her C114 line was a small one: she felt unexpectedly nostalgic at the smell of rain on warm pavement.

On Franklin, she said: "I haven't seen Maurice Franklin around Fruitvale lately, but I did hear they moved to Rockridge. I'm not sure what to make of it, to be honest. I've been busy with the Mutual Aid Network and haven't had a chance to catch up with them." Fruitvale and Rockridge are the two places her words put Franklin between. The record gives no reason for either.

**Mina Kapoor** of Grand Lake noticed neighbors being more watchful this cycle. She was one of four people the city turn reached, with Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake vote-ready. The Page named her in C113, in "A Place of Their Own." Her thread says what she was watching. In C114 the for-rent signs outnumbered the welcome mats. In C115 she carried three boxes of canned goods from the pantry truck and felt it in her lower back, and she compared rent notes with a neighbor over the fence. The week also passed without a single thing worth retelling.

**Everly Rainey** of Downtown wrote half a letter to family, then decided to call instead. The city turn reached them in a cycle when initiative work landed in seven neighborhoods, Downtown among them. In C115 they listened to the ninth inning on the porch radio and let the street hear it too. In C114 they caught their partner humming the song they'd had stuck all day.

**Tomas Aguilar** of Jack London had papers served at the counter, a lawsuit, and lawyers the shop never budgeted for. Afterward he bought a new A's jersey and let the week's noise settle. The record gives no plaintiff and no amount. The week before, one of the kids asked a question at dinner that stopped the room. The Page named him in C113, and the Tribune's Business desk in C91, in a piece on the NBA expansion bid.

**Kelvon Ochoa** moved from Rockridge to Lake Merritt. The ledger's words: sold up and bought. In C115 he cooked the full Sunday dinner out of habit and packed half of it straight into the freezer. The owner at Anthropic was talking about tightening up, and everyone heard it. The record does not tie the two together.

**Jesus Jorden** moved from Temescal to Fruitvale. In C115 he grabbed coffee from the cart outside the hospital before the morning rush hit. He wrote half a letter to family, then decided to call instead. Everly Rainey's line says the same thing, and the two are different people on different pages. In C114 Jorden felt a lift after the win.

**Jasmine Torres** moved from Chinatown to KONO, for a better fit in the ledger's words. In C115 she tried a new recipe that mostly worked, and she heard a kid explain something to a parent with total authority and grinned. She had listened to a style of music she'd always skipped, twice. The Tribune named her in The Oakland Stack, C84, and The Quiet Investment, C88.

**Ruth Anyanwu** moved from East Oakland to Downtown. In C115 she bought a new A's jersey and found a favorite song on the jukebox. She walked a different street home just to see it. She played a relative's voicemail twice before saving it again. She also recognized the OARI van idling outside the corner store and knew better than to ask who it was waiting for. Franklin's page carries the same line, and the two are separate people.

Of the eleven, only Franklin had both a new address and a new plant, and a card statement that came around again. The record does not say what the rent is or what Franklin does for work now. It does not say whether the plant has taken to its spot.
