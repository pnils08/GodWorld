# Doors Opened in Chinatown, a Workshop Begun at Lake Merritt

The Faith Ledger's event row for Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple in Chinatown, cycle 109, reads "crisis response: congregation opens doors for community support." Its attendance column says 41. The row names no cause for the crisis, no one who came through the door, and no room they used. It records that the doors opened and that 41 people were counted. I will not fill in the rest. A count of 41 means 41 people were inside a temple when something was wrong in the neighborhood. The record can't say more than that.

The same ledger has one more row for the temple. In cycle 111 it hosted a community health fair, with 38 counted. Two rows in a few cycles make a thin file, but they point the same way. The place shows up in the ledger when its neighbors need a room.

Chinatown is also where the Interfaith Council's row sits for cycle 114. A dialogue series "addresses shared concerns," and 53 attended. The ledger doesn't say which concerns, who spoke, or whether anyone changed their mind. A series is a small institution that keeps meeting, and meeting counts for something even when the ledger can't tell what was said.

## The big room at Lake Merritt

Across town, the Faith Organizations registry lists the Cathedral of the Living Word at Lake Merritt as Catholic, active, with a congregation of 2,000. Bishop Antoine Vermeer leads it. The registry calls it an "architectural landmark, diverse congregation."

The Faith Ledger counts Epiphany there twice. In cycle 108, "traditional Epiphany rituals observed" drew 1,077. In cycle 109, "congregation celebrates Epiphany" drew 1,265. The same ledger shows the Cathedral also working on weekdays. In cycle 108 a food pantry served neighbors in need, with 196 counted. In cycle 112, 102 people volunteered for a neighborhood cleanup. In cycle 114, the job training workshop began a new session and drew 204.

Ignacio Zavala is a managing partner at Foothill Builders in West Oakland. He has never been inside, and he says so before anything else. "I've driven by the Cathedral of the Living Word a few times, it's a big presence around Lake Merritt," he said. "I haven't been inside, but I've noticed it's always bustling. I guess that's what 1,000+ people on Epiphany looks like. My wife is into that sort of thing, she's always talking about the different faith communities around Oakland."

His remark shows how the building looks to someone outside it. He sees a lot of people and a lot of motion, and he reads the ledger's number off the traffic. A passer-by can only see the crowd. The workshop is the part of that crowd that isn't there for Epiphany. These are people on a weekday, in a program with a start date and a seat count.

Art Scott, a sustainable materials scientist who lives in Lake Merritt, has noticed them. "I've seen Cathedral of the Living Word hosting a job training workshop recently, it's been drawing people from around the neighborhood," he said. "That's something positive for our community." Bishop Vermeer said nearly the same thing from inside: "I've seen the Cathedral of the Living Word's job training workshop attract attendees from around the neighborhood, which is great. It's a valuable resource for our community."

The bishop and the scientist agree on "from around the neighborhood." That's an observation, and a fair one. It's not a finding. Nobody has put a figure on who gets hired, who finishes, or whether the 204 are the same people each week. I'm leaving out the claim that the workshop is moving local employment. The record doesn't support it, and the point of a quiet program is that it doesn't need a claim bigger than itself.

Declan Toto is a gene therapy counselor who lives nearby, and he has joined the congregation. He described a different kind of attendance. "I've joined the congregation at Cathedral of the Living Word, and I have to say, it's been a really uplifting experience," he said. "The scent of incense drifting from the temple doors left ajar is a familiar comfort for me, and it's great to see the community coming together there." The doors left ajar are his detail, and I'm printing it as his. A door left ajar is a small thing with a lot in it. A building that size could close up between services, and by his account it doesn't.

## What the week's rows have in common

Set the cycle's rows side by side. Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship in Downtown counted 237 at Epiphany in cycle 109. St. Esperanza Parish in Fruitvale counted 374 in cycle 108. At B'nai Tikvah Synagogue on Piedmont Avenue, the seniors lunch program marked a milestone in cycle 114 with 25 counted. Lakeshore Unitarian Universalist Fellowship also sits in Lake Merritt, but the Packet gives it no event row.

The numbers run from 25 to 1,265, and the small ones are the ones most likely to be missed. The lunch with 25 people and the open doors with 41 are not pageantry. They are the places a neighborhood goes when it needs a table or a room.

## What the record leaves open

What happened behind the Chinatown doors in cycle 109 isn't established. Neither is who the 41 were or what became of them. The Cathedral's workshop has opened a new session, but no row says what its first sessions did for the people in them. The question the beat keeps coming back to is the long-term one: what do the Cathedral's community programs do for Lake Merritt over years, and who is keeping that count?
