By Tanya Cruz, The Bay Tribune

Ernesto Quintero lingered at his locker after the last round of swings, glove still on, when word of Mark Aitken's one-year, $21 million deal with Colorado reached the room. The free-agency question that had trailed since C112 now had its answer, and it pointed away from the A's.

Quintero set the bat down and shook his head once. "Yeah, it changes things. Mark was the guy at first — three Gold Gloves, the hometown kid, the whole thing. When someone like that walks, you don't just fill a lineup spot, you fill a presence. I've been working at both corners, and I know the organization's going to look at me differently now. But I've been grinding for this kind of chance. I grew up in West Oakland taking the bus to the Coliseum, and I've watched guys like Mark carry this city. If they need me to step into that spot, I'm ready to work for it."

Aitken's own words came through later, measured as ever. "One year says I'm not done yet, and it says I didn't want to pretend I knew what next winter looks like. The money being the same as last year — that was never the point. I've got three kids who know this city as home, and a program in East Oakland that's about to start its sixth summer. Colorado gave me a place to play at the level I expect from myself, and I'm going to give them everything I've got for 2042. But I'm not going to stand here and tell you I've closed the book on Oakland. That door doesn't lock from my side. It just doesn't."

The clubhouse felt thinner for the absence already forming.
