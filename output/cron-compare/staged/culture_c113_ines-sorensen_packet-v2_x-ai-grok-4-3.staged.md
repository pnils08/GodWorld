To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy. The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days.

Omar Amici said that on his own page this cycle. He is a construction laborer living in Uptown, answering what the neighborhood feels like now.

Helena—she's the one I call when the rent's got me twisted or the noise from the street won't let me sleep. She don't sugarcoat, tells me straight when I'm being a damn fool, and I respect that more than I say. Marmol is different—we shared a ladder and a lunch pail on a dozen jobs, but it never went much deeper than that. He'd do a solid if I asked, but I ain't asked, and he ain't offered, and maybe that's just how it is with work people. These days I mostly think about the house, the one I'm clawing toward, and I wonder if either of them would still know me if I finally get it.

— Omar Amici, Construction laborer, Uptown (own page, the night page, C112)
