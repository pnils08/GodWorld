# At 12th Street, the Crowd Left and the Trains Got Later

Eleanor Rivera works a station where the sound changes before the numbers do. On a light weekend morning at 12th St Oakland City Center, the BART station manager hears the platform differently.

"The platform echoes more," she said. "You hear the announcements bounce off the tile instead of getting swallowed up by the crowd. And you see the regulars more clearly — the folks who are always there, not the game-day rush. It's a different rhythm. We just adapt, keep things moving, make sure the trains run on time. That's the job, whether it's five thousand people or fifty."

The Cycle 113 row for 12th St shows what she was hearing. Ridership was 9,341, down 4,821 from Cycle 112's 14,162. That was the largest drop of the eight stations on the record. The row's factors name the weekend first. They also list a game day in Downtown and Jack London, events in Downtown twice, Lake Merritt and Jack London, and the Baylight District final council vote at the construction-active stage.

The game-day tag did not carry the station this time. Cycle 112 also carried game-day factors, and ridership still fell. The paper's C109 coverage tracked 12th St at the head of a surge, and the C111 piece found the station quieter and running better. The station has now gone from a surge to a lull to a middle reading and back down. Cycle 113's ridership is only 1,048 above the 8,293 logged in Cycle 111.

Service did not improve with the empty platform this time. The station's on-time rate was 84%, five points below Cycle 112's 89%. In Cycle 111 the same station got lighter and punctual together, and the paper flagged that pairing as odd. This cycle the two measures moved in the same direction, down.

## The platforms around it

Citywide, the eight stations on the record logged 46,520 riders against 72,291 in Cycle 112. The packet frames the day type as weekend, with riders cut by the weekend rule. A station that moved more than that rule names its own reason on its row.

The on-time column does not follow the ridership column. 19th St Oakland lost 4,777 riders, the second-largest drop, to 10,012. Its on-time rate rose seven points to 90%. MacArthur lost 1,708 riders and gained five points, to 88%. Coliseum fell the other way, down 3,467 riders to 5,595 and eight points to 82%. Fruitvale lost 3,405 riders, to 4,553, and seven points, to 81%. Its row carries events in Fruitvale and the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II visioning stage, listed as design-phase. Rockridge, at 3,506 riders, slipped one point to 82%.

The rows do not carry a cause for the on-time slide at Coliseum or Fruitvale. Neither row lists a game day, and "design-phase" describes a project's stage. The row does not call it a delay. That gap is a question for the operators, and the ledger does not answer it.

## What the drivers see

Out on the street, the AC Transit drivers see the same weekend from the curb. Coen Cartier described the difference as passengers rather than a crowd. "You notice the regulars more," he said. "The folks who ride every day, they're still there, but there's no rush. You get a chance to actually say hello, maybe ask how someone's day is going. Riders don't see that part. They just see the bus showing up. But for us, it's the difference between moving a crowd and moving people."

Evan Lewis, who also drives for AC Transit, put 12th Street at the center of it. "12th Street is the heart of downtown — that's where people transfer, where they catch the game or head to an event," he said. "When it's quiet there, it's not just empty platforms. It's the whole city catching its breath. I see it on my route too — fewer folks waiting, more empty seats." He added a caution of his own about the Baylight District work: "when streets get torn up, people just find another way, even if the bus still runs on time."

Lewis is describing riders choosing another route. The station row lists the construction as a standing factor and does not say it moved any rider. The corridors fell too. Broadway's traffic index dropped 43, to 47, and I-880 North dropped 32, to 68. Both carry game-day factors. The weekend rule applies to the roads as well as the platforms.

## What the next rows will show

Three BART train incidents are on the record this cycle: a signal failure, a medical hold on board, and a stalled train in Rockridge. Under the record's own rule, a train hit carries one cycle, so any effect reaches the platforms on Cycle 114's rows as "BART train: … (last week)."

Two things remain unknown. One is whether those incidents appear on specific platforms next cycle. The other is whether the points lost at 12th St, Coliseum and Fruitvale were a quiet-day wobble or the first sign of a service problem. The next set of rows should show which.
