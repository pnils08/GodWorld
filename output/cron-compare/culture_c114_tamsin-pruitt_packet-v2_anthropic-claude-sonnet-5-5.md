# THE PAGE OF LIVES: C114

**Alexa Ward** spent longer than she meant to in a bookstore aisle she had never stopped at before. That is the C114 entry on her record, and it sits beside a different one. The household ledger for the same cycle says Ward, a licensed plumber who lives in West Oakland and is listed as retired, moved into a place of her own there, renting.

When the Tribune asked her about it, she said: "I haven't moved into a new place recently. I've been living in West Oakland for a while now."

The ledger and the woman do not agree, and this page does not settle it. Both could be true if the ledger counts a separate household of her own and she means the neighborhood. The record doesn't say which. What it does hold is a long run of her standing in the neighborhood. At Y3C4 she painted over graffiti on the recreation center wall with five neighbors she barely knew. At Y3C6 she set up the chairs in the fellowship hall because, as the record puts it, if she didn't, no one would. At Y3C7 she overheard a stranger asking about rents on a block nobody used to ask about. Then came the bookstore aisle. Where she sleeps now is the open question. That she is still in West Oakland is not.

**Antoine Vermeer** moved into a place of his own in Lake Merritt in C114, renting. He is a senior pastor and faith leader, and the Tribune has named him twice in Culture: in "The Season the Congregations Carried — and the Shortstop in a Deacon's Robes" (C97) and in "A Week of Many Altars: Oakland's Thanksgiving Convergence" (C99).

His record has a small disagreement of its own. At Y3C6 he compared rent notes with a neighbor over the fence in Lake Merritt, so the neighborhood was already his before the move. The move is a household of his own inside it. Around it, the record keeps one thread running. In C113 he wrote half a letter to family, then decided to call instead. In C114 he played a relative's voicemail twice before saving it again. The record names no relative and does not say what was on the message. It also has him at a First Friday gallery that cycle. The record holds nothing past that.

**Vinnie Keane**, the Oakland A's designated hitter and a club legend, was recognized twice at the grocery store in C114 after a story ran. He lives in Grand Lake, and the record says he felt quietly lucky to call it home, near a busy shopping block. Keane Mercantile returned $2043 to the line this week; the week before it was $2041. He saw an Oakland Alternative Response Initiative team de-escalate a situation on the corner without a fuss. He noticed that the same seven meals had rotated through his kitchen for a month, and he shrugged. The Tribune's Sports desk named him at C110, C111 and C112, the last in a piece on his 2041 strikeout rate across 127 at-bats.

Earlier, at Y3C8, the record has him wondering whether a decision from years back had been the right one. On his own page this cycle he put the question plainly: "Do I come back for one more run, or step away for good? I haven't decided yet." That is where it stands.

**Bethany Foster**, who lives in Lake Merritt and works at the Port of Oakland, asked about an assignment and did not get it. A coworker with less time in got it. That is the whole of the C114 entry, and the record does not say what the assignment was or whether she has said anything about it. Elsewhere that cycle, she recognized the OARI van idling outside the corner store and knew better than to ask who it was waiting for. She also retold an old family story and caught a detail she had never noticed. In C113 she navigated the stroller traffic on College Avenue and silently judged the parking jobs, and she checked in with a relative.

**Debbie Kaplan**, also of Lake Merritt and also at the Port of Oakland, had the other kind of week. A director asked to meet with her about her future, and the record's word for the meeting is that it was a good one. It does not say what was offered or what comes next. In the same cycle she read the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline and wondered who was actually going to get those spots. She discovered new local artists, picked a favorite Oak before the team had settled its rotation, and checked in with a relative near the lakeside path. The record does not say whether she and Foster crossed paths at work that week. Both entries are on the page.

**Tobias Ramas**, a bank teller in Lake Merritt, kept score by hand at the kitchen table in C114, the way his father taught him. The record doesn't say which game, and it names no one else at the table. Another entry from that cycle sits close beside it. He noticed the listing price on a place like his and read it twice. In C113 he painted over graffiti on the recreation center wall with five neighbors he barely knew, and he watched an OARI team de-escalate a situation near the lakeside path.

**Sidney Tumolo**, the A's second baseman, attended a small local gathering in West Oakland in C114 and stayed later than planned. "The gathering was really nice, I ended up staying longer than I planned," he said. "It was great catching up with folks from around the neighborhood."

Earlier in the cycle he stopped at the chain-link fence to read the architectural renderings for the Temescal Community Health Center. He counted the cranes on the skyline from the bus stop and got a different number than the day before. In C113 he read the Tribune's front page twice, once for the news and once to see who was in it. The Tribune had named him in Sports at C86, in "What Comes Next: Seymour's A's Emerge From Spring With a Record and a Question." Back at Y3C6 the record has him checking the housing market in Rockridge on his lunch break and realizing he could afford it. No later entry says what he did with that.

**Bruce Turner**, a server who lives in Dimond, heard that another neighbor was between jobs. The record names no neighbor and gives no workplace. He told the Tribune: "I heard another neighbor was between jobs and it didn't really surprise me, to be honest. The neighborhood's been a bit subdued lately, with some storefronts looking quieter than usual. It's just one of those things you notice when you're around."

He also walked a different street home that cycle, just to see it. He told the story of his first A's game again, and nobody minded. At Y3C6 the record has him wishing he had called family instead of writing. He wrote half a letter, then decided to call.

**Ivy Tran** finished the week at AC Transit in Fruitvale with the hardest account on the floor squared away. The record names the account only by its difficulty. Over the same stretch she organized the clothing drive donations and recognized a coat she had donated two years ago. She also saw a stranger laugh at a phone and invented the joke all the way home. In C113 she had read the flyer about the Fruitvale Transit Hub delays and shook her head at the timeline. The Tribune first named her at C76, among the pilot-district residents on alternative response. The coat is the last thing the record holds for her this cycle. She knew it when she saw it.
