# Gonzalez's $119M Pays for One Season, Not Three

**Vladimir Gonzalez is a good right fielder. The A's paid him as if his best year were his floor.**

The 2041 line is real. Gonzalez hit .306/.349/.556 for an .905 OPS in 527 at bats. He had 161 hits, 40 doubles, 28 homers and 60 strikeouts, and he was worth 4.7 WAR (card). The Oakland_Sports_Feed puts the new deal at five years and $119M, or $23.8M a year. That is $5.06M per WAR on 2041 alone, my own division of the feed salary by the card WAR.

The trouble is the three seasons behind him:

| Season | Games | OPS | WAR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2039 | 127 | .762 | 0.9 |
| 2040 | 81 | .874 | 3.0 |
| 2041 | 138 | .905 | 4.7 |

That is 8.6 WAR in three years, or 2.87 a year. At $23.8M, the price is about $8.30M per WAR on that baseline, my own arithmetic. Gridiron's Dawson Nguyen reached the same figure and called the deal "priced on the assumption that 2041 is the new level, not the outlier."

**What carried the jump.** The slugging jumped from .471 in 2040 to .556 in 2041. The card shows power against right-handers up 7 points and contact against right-handers up 7. Against that, his discipline rating fell 9 points. He drew 37 walks in 138 games in 2041 after 48 in 81 games in 2040. His 2040 OBP was .403 and his 2041 OBP was .349. The best-looking piece of 2041 is the power, and power is what regresses first.

**The positives.** His clutch rating is 99. His arm is 84, so right field is the right place for him. At 27, with a five-year term, he is paid through his prime and not past it. The deal ends after 2046, so it carries no long tail. The raise from $2.7M is $21.1M a year, almost exactly the $21.4M that left with Mark Aitken, who signed with Colorado for $21.0M (feed). The club swapped a 33-year-old on a one-year deal for a 27-year-old on five.

**The outfield.** The extension settles right field and leaves the rest crowded. Per the feed, Frank Reyna, Desmond Morton, JR Rosado and Kevin Clark are now competing for left field and the bench. The A's just paid $23.8M to one corner and have a bench of cheap bats for the other. Danny Horn is in center on $37.8M through 2044. His 13.3 WAR in 2041 is the best value on the roster by a wide margin. The shape is sound: stars at two spots, young bats on the minimum-scale deals at the third. But Gonzalez's contract is only fair if he holds 4.7 WAR. If he reverts toward 3.0, the A's have an expensive right fielder who cannot be replaced from the bench.

**The verdict.** The A's bought a 27-year-old at the top of his curve. The price is fair on one year and rich on three. The downside is not a disaster, because the cheap alternatives are already in the building. But "he earned it" is only half the sentence. He earned it once.

What to watch in 2042 is the walk rate. If it stays near 37 walks a year, the .349 OBP is the real floor and the power has to carry everything.
