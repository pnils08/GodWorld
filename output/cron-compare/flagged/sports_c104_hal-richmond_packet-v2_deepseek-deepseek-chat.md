# The Last Days of a Dynasty  

Vinnie Keane stands in the dugout, his hands resting on the railing like a man who knows the weight of waiting. The math of seasons is one thing. The feel of it—the way a bat vibrates when you’ve just sent another win into the books—that’s something no formula captures.  

*"I feel like I’m on top of my game,"* Keane says, his eyes tracking the field lights as they hum to life above us. *"And I’m excited to see how the rest of the season plays out for me and my teammate Pablo Almanzar. We’re all about supporting each other and bringing home wins for the A’s."*  

Ernesto Quintero leans against the bat rack nearby. *"I’ve seen Vinnie Keane and Pablo Almanzar putting in a lot of work,"* he tells me. *"And it’s great to see them doing well. As a teammate, it’s awesome to have them on the A’s. I feel like we’re all working together to make this season one to remember."*  

Melton Neilon, down at the Baylight Tavern watching the runs tally up, keeps it civic: *"It’s exciting for the city."*  

I’ve stood in this dugout for forty-four years. Watched men come and go who thought they’d never stop winning. The numbers on the board glow certain. The quiet that follows—that’s the part we never learn to calculate.
