# Jack London, in their own words

## Mark Aitken — First Baseman, Colorado Rockies

> Yeah. I saw the feed. Hard not to, with the phone blowing up the way it is. Here's what I'll say about the Angels thing — it's real, in the sense that they're interested. I'm not going to sit here and pretend I don't know my own market. But interest is a two-way street, and I've got three kids who sleep in this time zone and a Little League program with my name on it that I'm not walking away from because somebody in Anaheim thinks I'd look good in red. The trade stuff? That's business. Giancarlo's a good glove, and Ernesto can play anywhere you put him. I've been around long enough to know that when a GM starts moving pieces, it doesn't mean he's moving you. Sometimes it means he's trying to build something worth staying for. I'm not going to beg. I've never begged for anything in this game, and I'm not starting now. But I'll tell you this — if Mike Paulson wants me back, he knows my number. And if he doesn't, I'll shake his hand, thank him for the memories, and I'll still be at the Little Leaguers' opening day next spring. That's not a negotiation tactic. That's just the truth.

## Elliot Abraham — General Manager, Oakland Oaks

> Look, I’m not going to sit here and pretend that 0-9 didn’t happen. It did. And I’m not going to pretend it was all part of some grand, four-dimensional chess plan, because that would be disrespecting the intelligence of every fan in this building. What I will say is this: we told you we weren’t panicking. We told you we were evaluating. We told you the real product would look different. And now you’re seeing it. We’re 9-1 in games that count. Number one in the power rankings. We went into Detroit, OKC, New York, and Philly—four of the best teams in this league—and we beat them all on their home floors. That’s not a fluke. That’s a foundation. The 0-9 was ugly. It was uncomfortable. But it gave Wilson and his staff a chance to see every combination, to figure out who fits next to who, and to drill the defensive principles that are winning us games right now. I said in C111 that we were prioritizing rest and reps over results. That was true. But I’ll be honest with you: we also tweaked some things. We adjusted the lineups, we tightened the rotations, and we asked Adash and T. to be more aggressive in transition. That’s on the staff. That’s on the players. That’s not on me. The Draymond situation is settled. He’s been a professional, he’s been in the building, and he’s been a voice in that locker room that these young guys need

> Numbers don't lie, and we've got the wins to back that top spot, but that San Antonio loss was a good, honest gut-check. Teams are absolutely circling us differently now — you don't get to be the hunted without earning it, and the scouting reports are going to come at us harder than any preseason. As for Wilson, that's his job and he's lived it: he keeps them focused on the next possession, not the standings, because a young group can read its own headlines and start believing the work is done. My job is to give him cover from the noise and make sure the front office doesn't get cute when the schedule tightens. We stay humble, we stay hungry, and the rankings take care of themselves.

## Kevin Clark — Third Baseman, Oakland A's

> *(Kevin looks up from his phone, rubbing the back of his neck with a wry smile)* Yeah, I saw the feed. Hard not to, with my name right there next to "trade chip" and "looking over his shoulder." That's the business, though. I've been in this game long enough to know the same front office that gives you a shot can just as easily take it away. *(He pauses, glancing out toward the field)* But here's the thing — Giancarlo Hernandez? Good glove, I'll give him that. A potential, they say. But I didn't come up from Vegas to just hand over the bag. I hit .327 last year, stole five bags, put up a WAR in 55 at-bats. I'm not a placeholder. I'm a ballplayer. If they want to platoon us, fine. If they want competition, even better. I've never backed down from a fight, and I'm not about to start now. *(He chuckles, shaking his head)* Besides, Deacon Seymour knows what I bring. Mike Paulson made the deal, but Deacon's the one writing the lineup card. And Ernesto sliding to first? That tells me they're planning for life after Aitken. So yeah, there's uncertainty. But that's baseball. You show up, you work, you let the bat and glove do the talking.
