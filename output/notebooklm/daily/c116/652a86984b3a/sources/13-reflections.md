# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Deacon Seymour — Manager, Oakland A's

> *leans back in the chair, letting the coffee steam rise before speaking* Yeah. I was there for both. On Clark and Hernandez — I'll say this: Kevin's got the right head on him. "Just here to help" — that's not a line he rehearsed. That's who he is. But he's not naive. He knows what a potential Gold Glove third baseman in the system means. Giancarlo's not here to replace anyone either. He's here to make us better. And if that means Kevin moves to a utility role or we find a way to get both bats in the lineup, that's a conversation Mike and I are having every day. *stirs coffee slowly* On Benji — *pauses* — that one's heavier. He's earned the right to take his time. Five Cy Youngs. Three no-hitters. Seven rings. When a man says "you realize you don't have to do it again," he's not being dramatic. He's being honest. The door being open isn't indecision. It's him figuring out if he can still give us what we need — and if he can, whether that's worth what it costs him. I've watched Benji pitch through things most guys would've shut down for. If he comes back, it won't be for the money or the legacy. It'll be because he still believes he can help. *quietly* That's the job. We watch. And we're ready either way.

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> Robert lost his glasses again this evening. They were on his head, as always. He laughed and I let him have it. What I keep turning over is something Rosario Medina said on the Fruitvale platform. The visioning phase has closed, the planners have their boards and their sticky notes, and she said it changed little for the people she sees every day. The riders are the ones who live there. They were waiting for trains before anyone drew a vision of them, and they'll be waiting after. I think about how often a city finishes a process and calls it a result. Luis is still circling the apprenticeship promise, twelve and a half million dollars for kids in West Oakland, East Oakland and Fruitvale. He's careful, and he's right to be. Quiet isn't the same as nothing happening. But a kid who wants a trade can't live on a careful sentence. I'd like the analysis to land and say something plain, even if it's unwelcome. KONO nags at me too. A street can be loud, full and fraying at once, and nobody owns the fraying. I know that feeling from newsrooms. Everything's busy, and no one is holding the thing that's slipping. Scout is on the rail between our glasses, warm and unbothered. I'll go down the stairs slowly tonight, knee and all. — Mags

## Leonard Briggs — DEPUTY CHIEF OF COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT

> Look, Downtown's number is eight, and that's the most on the table. But the number that keeps me up is the clearance rate — 20 cents on the dollar. That means five out of every six incidents in the heart of the city don't get closed. That's not a statistic, that's a person who called us and didn't get an answer. When we sit around that 22-neighborhood table, I want us to stop talking about who's up and who's down and start talking about who's accountable for closing the loop. I want the answer to be: every one of us, starting with me.

## Edmund Alegero — Construction laborer

> Getting my own place in Lake Merritt is a big deal for me. It's a step up, you know? I've been working hard, and it's nice to have a spot that's just mine. I've been following the A's news, and it's tough to see Mark Aitken leave. He's from around here, and he's always been involved in the community. But I'm trying to focus on my own game, you know? I've got my own things to work on.
