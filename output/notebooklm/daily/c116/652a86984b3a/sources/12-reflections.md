# KONO, in their own words

## AJ Dybantsa — SF / The Oaks

> Man. I was just starting to feel untouchable after that Denver game, and then San Antonio came in and reminded us we’re human. That 80-percent night, that was like a dream. I don't think I've ever been part of something that efficient — 40 assists on 5 turnovers, 19 threes. Everything just clicked, the ball moved like it had a mind of its own. But that Spurs game? That's the one that stings. Not because we lost — that happens — but because we gave it away in the fourth. We were even at 90, and then it just... slipped. I had looks I normally knock down, and they weren't falling. 6-for-14, that's not me. I know I finished with 19, but I left at least eight on the board. The frustrating part is that I know what this team is capable of. I've seen it. In Denver, we looked like a championship squad. Against San Antonio, we looked like a group that still hasn't learned to handle success. Wilson's been preaching about staying even-keeled, not letting the highs get too high. I get it now. I really do. Ty's been the bright spot though. 23 off the bench, six threes? That's the kind of spark that changes a rotation. I see the minutes he's earning. That's the standard. We got 9-2, but nobody in that locker room is satisfied. We lost one at home, and that's not who we are. Next game, I'm back to 8-for-8. Count on it.

## Harry Hayward — Oakland Oaks - Power Forward

> Yeah, I saw the feed. Both games. They're real, they happened, and I was there for both of them. The Denver night was special. That's the kind of game you dream about as a kid, where every shot you put up feels like it's got a magnet on it. Five-for-five, four-for-four from deep, 14 points in 19 minutes. But the real story wasn't me — it was the whole team. Forty assists, five turnovers, 80 percent from the floor. That's not just a hot night, that's a statement. We looked like a machine out there. Then San Antonio happened. That one stings more than the stat sheet shows. I went 2-for-9, which is ugly, and I know it. But 10 boards? I competed. I tried to make up for the misses with the dirty work. Still, when your shot isn't falling and the team needs a spark, you feel every miss weigh on you. Coach kept me in there down the stretch, which tells me he still trusts me, but I left that locker room frustrated like everyone else. Here's the thing though — you can't get too high on Denver or too low on San Antonio. That's the trap. We're 9-2, still one of the best records in the league. The loss is a gut check, not a crisis. We had a night where everything fell, and we had a night where it didn't. The difference between good teams and great teams is how they respond to the second one. I'll be ready for the next game.
