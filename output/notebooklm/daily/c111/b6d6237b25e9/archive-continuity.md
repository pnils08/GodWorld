### **1. Housing Stabilization & Public Accounting**
*Leads: `Ninety-One Households, One Ledger` / `The Pipeline Is Running. The Count Isn't Moving.`*

#### **Prior Events, Promises & Public Record**
* **Legislative Origin & Vote:** The **West Oakland Stabilization Fund** was proposed by **Mayor Avery Santana** and passed in **Cycle 78** on a narrow **6–3 City Council vote** (with Councilmembers **Ramon Vega** voting yes, **Leonard Tran** voting no, and **Elliott Crane** voting yes remotely) [2].
* **Disbursement History:**
  * In **Cycle 109**, Carmen Delaine reported that four weeks into implementation, residents on the ground—West Oakland Community Bakery worker **Daniel Cloak**, coffee shop barista **Irene Fay**, bakery regular **Darius Clark**, and resident **Elena Patel**—confirmed the fund was active on paper but reported seeing zero visible financial arrival on their blocks [1].
  * In **Cycle 110**, **Marcus Webb** reported Month-Nine data to the City Council Finance Committee: **79 households stabilized in nine months** at an average processing pace of **23 days** (\$400,000 approved for Month-Nine) [2].
* **The Financial Ledger Gap:** The fund carried an initial envelope of **\$28 million** [2]. By the end of Cycle 109, the ledger recorded **\$81,188 disbursed in direct household grants**, leaving **\$23 million remaining** in Cycle 110 [2]. Editor **Mags Corliss** noted this arithmetic discrepancy between the \$28M total, \$81K disbursed, and \$23M remaining does not reconcile on the public record, requiring clarification from Webb and the Finance Committee [2].
* **Institutional Promises:**
  * **Mayor Avery Santana:** Claimed that *"relief without bureaucracy keeps neighbors in their neighborhoods"* [2].
  * **Deputy Mayor Brenda Okoro:** Stated that *"targeted relief that flows to tenants before displacement does"* [2].
  * **District 1 Councilmember Denise Carter:** Stated: *"When storefronts go quiet, families go next—so we're taking the Stabilization Fund to where the need is moving"* [2].
* **Cycle 111 Progress:** The Month-Ten disbursement review advances the ledger count from 79 to **91 households stabilized** [2].

#### **Direct Published Fact vs. Analytical Inference**
* **Direct Published Fact:** The fund passed 6–3 in C78 with \$28M; C110 logged 79 households at a 23-day processing window (\$81,188 disbursed, \$23M remaining) [2]. Month-Ten review logs 91 households [2].
* **Analytical Inference & Unresolved Storyline:** Corliss, Delaine, and Carter infer that administrative compliance claims and processing speed metrics mask whether 91 households are sufficient to hold the line against commercial and residential displacement in West Oakland, or whether public aid moves too slowly compared to private tech/construction expansion along the same corridor (**Oakmesh Systems**, **Civis Systems**, **Coastline Construction**) [1, 2].

### **2. Merchant Corridors & Civic Accountability**
*Lead: `Fruitvale Is Packed at Lunch and the Map Says It's Decaying. Who Owns That?`*

#### **Prior Events & Repeated Promises**
* **Cross-Neighborhood Frustration (Cycle 109):** Luis Navarro documented widespread frustration over the stalled **Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II** ("The Same Empty Lot, Four Times") [1]. Four citizens across three distinct neighborhoods—teacher **Rick Walker** (Fruitvale K-8), sourdough baker **Melton Neilon** (Temescal), and diner servers **Elio Perez** and **Depak Ying** (Rockridge)—independently noted council delays and a complete absence of assigned project leadership [1].
* **Transit Surge vs. Lot Stasis (Cycles 109–110):**
  * In **Cycle 109**, Trevor Shimizu reported Fruitvale Station boardings jumped by 3,756 to **10,414 riders** (81% on-time) [1].
  * In **Cycle 110**, while systemwide transit ridership dropped, **Fruitvale Station gained another 1,321 riders, surging to 11,735 boardings** (78% on-time), making it the single station bucking citywide transit decline [2].
* **Three Cycles of the Same Slogan (Cycle 110):** Luis Navarro ("Three Cycles, Same Sentence") exposed that three city officials across three cycles repeated the exact same phrase regarding architectural firm **Mayer & Associates**:
  1. Mayor Avery Santana at the gavel: *"Mayer & Associates gets the nod because they listen to Fruitvale merchants first, design second"* [2].
  2. Mayor Santana in open session: *"Mayer & Associates listens to Fruitvale merchants first, designs second — and now we move to blueprints"* [2].
  3. **Elena Soria Dominguez** on the transit hub record: *"Mayer & Associates doesn't just draw blueprints—they listen to the tamale vendors and panaderías that make Fruitvale Fruitvale"* [2].
  4. District 3 Councilmember **Rose Delgado** called Phase II's blueprints a *"covenant"* [2].
* **Current Status:** Despite these assurances, official initiative tracker records confirm Phase II remains stuck at **Visioning** in the design phase without advancing to Preliminary Schematics [2].

#### **Direct Published Fact vs. Analytical Inference**
* **Direct Published Fact:** Fruitvale Station boardings grew to 11,735 riders in C110 [2]. Official city dashboards log negative/decay indicators for the corridor, while officials repeat the "listen first, design second" slogan for 3 cycles as Phase II sits at Visioning [2].
* **Analytical Inference & Unresolved Storyline:** Navarro and Corliss infer a structural transparency void where municipal dashboards label a thriving, packed merchant corridor as "decaying" while no city office or official assumes ownership of the Phase II file to translate listening sessions into actual construction [1, 2].
