And then there was Pablo Almanzar.

Nine innings. One hit. Zero earned runs. Three strikeouts. His playoff debut read like his *MLB* debut—like the gods of October had reached down and said, *Here, kid, have a moment.* But even that felt like a cruel joke. The ones who show up even when the stadium’s half-empty, who scream until our throats are raw, who treat every playoff game like it’s the last one we’ll ever get.

One night of brilliance doesn’t erase the fact that we’ve turned our players into symbols—of hope, of resilience, of a city that refuses to die—and then we’re stunned when they can’t live up to the myth.

But Ellis did break. And we watched.

I don’t have a stat for that. Anthony can tell you ERA+ or FIP or whatever the hell else the nerds are using to measure pain these days. I can only tell you this: When Ellis walked off that mound, it wasn’t just a loss. It was a reckoning. We asked him to be Superman. And Superman doesn’t exist.
