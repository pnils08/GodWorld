# The Load-Bearing Arc

Desmond Morton leans against the padded rail in the A's dugout, glove hanging loose from his left hand. The afternoon sun cuts across the diamond in Jack London, catching dust motes above the infield dirt. Morton isn't looking at the field—he's watching the bullpen gate swing shut behind their latest call-up, watching how the kid adjusts his cap three times before reaching the mound. 

"You know," Morton says, turning his mitt over in his hands, "when the team's rolling like this, it's less about any one guy and more about how we all fit together." His index finger traces a welt in the leather where last cycle's slide caught him wrong. "I've been around long enough to see how roles shift—some days you're the spark, some days you're just keeping the line moving. It's not always flashy, but it's how you sustain something." 

The numbers sit light at 2-1, one straight win holding up the column. Mark Aitken, leaning against the dugout stairs nearby, nods without looking up from tightening his batting gloves. "When you're part of a team that's been around as long as the A's," he says, "you start to see how roles evolve—not just on the field, but in the community too." He flexes his hands, working the leather. "It's like a living structure, where each player's contribution shifts over time, but the core stays the same." 

At the far end of the dugout, Marcus King checks his phone while Jacob Perez ties his cleats. Neither look up when the PA crackles to life. "They're doing alright this season," King murmurs, more to his screen than to anyone present. Perez just grunts, focused on his laces. Below them, the concrete foundation holds.

The record is a keystone, not an arch. Its strength lies in the fitting of parts, not their brilliance—in the way a left fielder's quiet innings support a shortstop's flash, how bullpen call-ups find their feet against dugout rails. Over at the gas station across from the Coliseum BART stop, someone will mention the record tonight between pumps. The numbers will pass without ceremony. What sustains is the structure beneath them: the way roles slot into place like well-worn bricks, bearing weight without fanfare.

Who measures the strength of a keystone by its shine?
