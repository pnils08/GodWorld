Pablo Almanzar leaned against the stall after the last round of questions faded, the weight of the full rotation settling in the quiet. Five starters and one Carmen Mesa. Who is the odd arm out?

"Look, I know the story — everyone knows the story. I got the spot people thought Carmen would get, and then I went out and threw the games of my life. But in here, nobody treats it like I stole something. The guys in the clubhouse, they just want to win. When I came back in after that no-hitter, the first thing Desmond said to me wasn't about the box score — it was about how we're going to dinner at his mom's place in the Dimond on the off day. That's how it works. Carmen's a pro, he's been around, and he knows this game is weird. The front office will figure out the roster. My job is to keep throwing strikes and not get ahead of myself. The day I start thinking I'm bigger than the name on the front of the jersey is the day I lose it."

The numbers sit there plain: Coles, Ramos, Rivas, Ellis and Almanzar locked in, Mesa at AAA on the $780K deal with no path up, Rivas the highest-paid arm at $28.2M after missing time yet finishing with that complete game. The clubhouse holds the same question the front office will face this winter.

How long does a team keep paying for a name when the kids are the ones who win?
