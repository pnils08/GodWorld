# The Fund Paid Out in C117. The Closeout That Gates Its Renewal Is Still Unverified.

By Carmen Delaine, The Bay Tribune

**By Carmen Delaine | Bay Tribune Civic Ledger**

Marcus Webb, the OEWD program director who runs the West Oakland Stabilization Fund, put it this way for the record this cycle: "The closeout is published, the ordinance is on the calendar with the scope that works and the 23-day clock still running — the hearing record carries the proof, and the roll call belongs to the Council."

The Initiative_Tracker for INIT-001 says something narrower. Its last disbursement is dated Cycle 117. Its next scheduled action, set for Cycle 118, reads: "Verify C114 closeout, then conduct Council charter-renewal consideration with scope and 23-day standard intact." The C117 milestone note says renewal support "is contingent on verified C114 closeout and Council action." So money went out in the same cycle that the check on the prior money is still listed as a future task.

**What the tracker shows**

The Fund is a $28 million authorization. Remaining balance is $20,200,000, so $7.8 million has left the account since the Council passed it at Cycle 78 and the mayor signed. Avery Santana proposed it.

For five straight cycles the notes carry a figure. C110 through C113 each record $400,000 approved. C114 records $400,000 "executed under the 23-day standard." Then the notes change character:

- **C115:** the closeout publication was "ordered" and the renewal ordinance "transmitted."
- **C116:** renewal "kept on Council calendar," with final action requiring a verified closeout.
- **C117:** renewal support "contingent" on that verification.

The C117 note carries no amount for the C117 disbursement. I read the tracker row and searched the paper's prior coverage for one. Neither returned an amount for C117, and neither returned the name of the office that signed off on it. The C108 note says the earlier 47 checks, about $4.2 million, were disbursed "under Deputy Mayor Okoro's authority" in C84 through C90. Nothing comparable is on file for C117.

**The sequencing question**

The paper raised this in C116, when Webb said the closeout was published and the tracker still listed it as unverified. "Published" and "verified" are different verbs. A published closeout is a document. A verified one has been checked by someone other than its author. The tracker gates renewal on the second. A disbursement that follows publication but precedes verification may be routine, or it may be the order of operations the charter was written to prevent.

I cannot say which. I have no record of a standing disbursement schedule that would make the order moot, and none of internal correspondence about the closeout's timing. The tracker's last-disbursement field says only that money moved in C117.

**Where the officials stand**

Deputy Mayor Brenda Okoro's statement this cycle does not address the disbursement. It addresses the gate: "West Oakland gets a verified closeout and a faithful charter renewal — not a shortcut around the businesses and residents this fund serves." Read plainly, that makes verification a precondition of renewal. It leaves open whether it was also a precondition of the C117 payment.

Councilmember Denise Carter, who represents District 1 and proposed the Tenant Defense Fund in C109, spoke to scope: "The Fund was never hood-wide relief, and it was never housing — it is West Oakland businesses and neighbors, with the 23-day clock kept honest. I verified nothing by wishing it." Her last sentence reads as a statement about her own work. The tracker's C109 note, which corrected the Fund's scope to businesses and residents, supports the first half.

Webb's second statement shifts to the Tenant Defense Fund, which the tracker now lists as operational: "The rent does not wait for the ordinance and the vote doesn't come before the analysis — West Oakland gets the legal-support record first, and the 23-day clock keeps running while it does." That is an argument for letting work proceed before the paperwork closes. It may well be correct. It is also the argument a disbursement-before-verification sequence would need, and nobody on the record has made it about the Stabilization Fund.

**The vote that has not happened**

Renewal goes to the full nine-seat Council. The seats are:

- Denise Carter (D1)
- Leonard Tran (D2)
- Rose Delgado (D3)
- Ramon Vega (D4)
- Janae Rivers (D5)
- Elliott Crane (D6)
- Warren Ashford (D7)
- Nina Chen (D8)
- Terrence Mobley (D9)

No roll call is in the tracker. For the original Cycle 78 passage at 6-3, the tracker's note names only three positions: Vega yes, Tran no, and Crane yes remotely. The positions of the other six seats are not in the Packet. Counting votes for renewal on that history would be guessing.

**Why it matters beyond sequence**

The same Council calendar holds $2.5 million for Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake, vote-scheduled for C118 with "no Clerk-certified roll call or outcome," and $15 million for Downtown Economic Revitalization, which the tracker calls stalled. The Council is being asked to renew the largest of these funds, $28 million, on the strength of a closeout it has not been shown verified. The Council rather than the program director decides the rest.

**What Cycle 118 has to answer**

Three things are still open:

- Who authorized the C117 disbursement, and in what amount?
- Whether verification of the C114 closeout comes before that roll call or after it.
- Who does the verifying. The tracker names the task and no owner.

Webb says the hearing record carries the proof. The hearing record is where the answers will have to appear.
