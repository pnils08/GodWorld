**"The A's winning season really brings Uptown together."**

That is Priya Narang, the EMS director, who lives in Uptown. She was answering what the A's season has done to her neighborhood, and she kept going: "When they're doing well, the whole neighborhood feels it. The bars and galleries stay packed, and you can feel the energy."

She said it to a reporter on a Wednesday page and left it there.

**"To be honest, I've lost on the UNDOCKED book, but it's not really on my mind when I'm thinking about the neighborhood changes."**

Ingmar Jordan is a spoken word poet in Uptown. Lives carried his wager this cycle, so that part is told. What he said to me was about the block. Asked about the changes, he came back to the question twice, once on Wednesday and once on Thursday, in nearly the same words: "I don't know if my losses on the book have affected my views on the neighborhood changes."

He left it as a question and did not answer it. A poet's pause is longer than most people's. I let it run.

**"To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy."**

Omar Amici is a construction laborer. He has lived in Uptown long enough to have watched it change before. Lives carried his wager too.

He kept going, the way people do when the first sentence was only the door. "The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days. I've lived here long enough to see the neighborhood transform, but this feels different. It's like the neighborhood is shifting away from the people who've been here all along."

Priya sees full bars and galleries and calls it a neighborhood together. Omar sees the same busy streets and counts the for-rent signs. Ingmar says the rent signs go up and come down just as fast, and the bars and galleries stay packed. I'm not going to tell you which of them is looking at the real Uptown. All three live there and all three said so.

Omar's last page that week was a question, not the complaint. I'll leave the floor to him:

"Will the people who've been here all along still be able to stay in Uptown as the for-rent signs keep multiplying?"
