**The Weight of One IP**  
*Anthony Raines*  

The first thing that jumps out on the card is the line Benji Dillon put up in his farewell start: five and a third innings, eight strikeouts, just two hits. It’s a quiet, workmanlike outing, the kind of pitching performance that doesn’t scream dominance but whispers steadiness. And in a game where Vinnie Keane went four for five with a home run, a double, and three RBIs, it’s easy to miss Dillon’s methodical command.  

What’s harder to miss is the season line writ large: one hundred and twenty-seven wins, thirty-five losses. Two straight victories heading into the late stretch. Mark Aitken’s two home runs and six RBIs this cycle alone. The A’s are a machine, and Dillon’s one IP is a single cog in that machine.  

But here’s the other card to consider — the one that’s harder to quantify, but that Isley Kelley alludes to in a quiet moment post-game.  

“I’ve been watching Vinnie and Benji closely this season,” the veteran shortstop says, “and their performances have been electric — they’re carrying the team with heart and grit, and it’s exactly what Oakland deserves for this farewell.”  

Carrying the team. That’s an intangible claim, but one that’s hard to argue against when you look at the numbers. Keane’s four hits this cycle are a microcosm of his season-long consistency at the plate, his ability to turn rallies into runs. And Dillon’s eight strikeouts over five-plus innings speak to a command that’s been there all year, even in short bursts.  

The question, then, is whether the season line fully encapsulates the legacy of Keane and Dillon — or whether there’s more to uncover. The record suggests dominance. The individual lines suggest reliability. And Kelley’s words suggest something deeper: a synergy between two players who’ve been linchpins of this farewell run.  

The card doesn’t lie — but sometimes it doesn’t tell the whole truth, either.
