**East Oakland's Apprenticeship Push**  
Maria Conteras grades papers at the small teacher's desk she shares with two colleagues at East Oakland High. The classroom windows show a neighborhood that hasn't gotten its civic lift, but here, Conteras keeps teaching her classes while watching more students go the hands-on route.

"I teach at the high school here," Conteras says, stacking a set of algebra quizzes, "and what I've noticed is that we're doing our best with what we have, but it feels like the support systems for education in East Oakland just aren't keeping up with the needs of our students."

At the apprenticeship hub three blocks away, Jerome Pittman processes the steady stream of referral forms. "I've noticed more apprenticeship referrals coming through from East Oakland schools lately," he says, tapping his intake tablet against the counter edge. "Seems like they're trying to steer kids toward hands-on paths."

The pivot toward trades comes as the neighborhood's broader civic initiative remains stalled. What hasn't stalled are the daily efforts—teachers like Conteras covering their sections, counselors routing students toward viable options. The larger systems may not have moved, but in these classrooms and hubs, people keep showing up to work with what they've got.

How many more cycles can East Oakland schools sustain this balance between academic foundations and vocational pivots before one gives way?
