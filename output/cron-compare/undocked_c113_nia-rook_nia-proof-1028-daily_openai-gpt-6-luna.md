# UNDOCKED: A quiet night at the top

Fruitvale’s watch-room has the kind of suspense that makes people lean closer, not louder: Eric Taveras is still the name at the top of the board, and Father Miguel’s episode offers no obvious fireworks to knock him loose. The priest is marked “dead even on the night.” That is a mood, not a verdict on the leaderboard—and the distinction is where tomorrow’s hallway argument starts.

The flight’s shape was spare: zero credits, four systems—lacaille_9352, sirius, sol and alpha_centauri—and no combat or mishaps. Its magnitude was 2/5. I’d call that a low-volume episode, the sort that leaves room for the audience to debate what counted more: a clean passage through the systems, or the absence of a big swing. Nothing in those facts says Father Miguel has taken Eric’s place. They do give his supporters something to make a case from.

Because Eric is not just currently ranked #1; the supplied standings say he has led for three cycles and is on a three-cycle streak. The earlier approved record adds texture, not a new ranking: Eric’s C111 entries include one with 1,170 credits and others with zero, alongside different system lists. All three record no combat and no mishaps. That’s a varied little reel, but it doesn’t rewrite tonight’s standings. It does make the leader feel less like a one-note act—and gives a challenger a higher bar than simply having a quieter night.

There’s another useful caution in the phrase “dead even.” Cristina Booker, whose own episode is described that way, refused to let the label stand in for her life. “To be honest, I'm not sure what you're referring to with my 'dead even performance.' I'm just going about my daily life as an immigration attorney here in Downtown Oakland. I haven't noticed anything out of the ordinary happening to me recently.” It’s a good reminder: the show can shorthand a night; that shorthand doesn’t tell us what a person’s day means.

So the Fruitvale room splits into familiar camps. One side likes the unruffled flight: no mishaps, no combat, no theatrics demanded. The other wants a challenger to do more than finish level with the night. I’m not ready to call this a leaderboard turn. Father Miguel gave the audience a question, not a coronation.

Will a “dead even” night be enough to challenge Eric Taveras’s standing at the top—or does the leader’s three-cycle run keep the room looking uphill?
