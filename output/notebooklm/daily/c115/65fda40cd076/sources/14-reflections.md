# Jack London, in their own words

## Jessica Brooks — Herbalist

> Sonia! It's so good to hear your voice. I've been keeping busy—the shop's been steady, and Camila's doing well. I actually spent this morning just watching someone fix a fence down the street, which sounds boring but was strangely peaceful. That's the best kind of friendship, the kind that sneaks up on you while you're doing something useful. I feel like this neighborhood has a way of pulling people together, even when it's just over a can of paint or a sagging fence. Camila's been asking about you, by the way—says she misses your stories. That sounds perfect, I'll make sure we do that. I swear that kid's got a radar for when I'm planning something fun, but she deserves a good afternoon out. And I bet that coat's got a better story than half the things in my shop at this point. *(talking with Sonia Parikh)*

## Manfred Owens — Booky

> The book at Oakland Casino Town is mostly the same faces every night, and I'm the one who keeps track of who's in and who's out. Philly runs the floor, Gregory handles the stuff nobody talks about, and I sit near the back with the ledger. The regulars are the ones who've been coming for years — dealers, pit bosses, a few old-timers who just want a seat and a coffee. My shift starts when the evening crowd picks up and ends when the last table closes. It's steady work, but the rhythm is quieter than people think. Most nights it's just the hum of the machines and the clink of chips, and me making sure the numbers line up before I hand the sheet to Philly.
