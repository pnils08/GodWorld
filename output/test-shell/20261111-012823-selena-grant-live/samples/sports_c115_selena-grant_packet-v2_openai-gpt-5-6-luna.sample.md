# Harry Hayward's 22 Minutes Change the Question

Harry Hayward scored 19 points, collected seven rebounds and blocked two shots in 22 minutes as the Oaks beat the 76ers 120-106 in Philadelphia. He shot 9-for-15. The career high came in the fourth quarter of a game the Oaks entered down two after three, then won by scoring 40 in the final period.

That is the useful line for an expansion franchise: not merely production, but production attached to a defined window. Hayward did not need a large allocation to affect the result. His 22 minutes were enough to give the Oaks a second-round rookie with a measurable offensive contribution, rebounding presence and rim protection in the same game.

The result moved the Oaks to 8-1, with a four-game winning streak. The record is the larger fact, but it does not settle Hayward's place in the rotation. It establishes that the team is winning while the question remains open.

The previous Hayward read, published in C114, centered on 13 minutes that produced eight points, four rebounds and a 3-for-4 shooting line. It is stronger evidence that the role deserves a more exact definition.

There was enough established production around Hayward for his performance to function as development rather than rescue.
