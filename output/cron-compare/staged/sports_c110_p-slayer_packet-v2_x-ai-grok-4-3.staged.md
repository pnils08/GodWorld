I caught up with Jessie Hess on the sidewalk outside the arena after the final horn, her shoulders still tight from sitting through the whole thing. She didn’t need long to say what the rest of us were chewing on.

“Honestly, I’m a bit disappointed after their last preseason game. I was really hyped about the Oaks’ starting five and their chances this season, but losing to the Miami Heat doesn’t exactly fill me with confidence. I still want to believe they can turn it around, though.”

Daniel Cloak was there too, wiping flour off his hands from the late shift at the bakery, nodding like he’d heard the same line from half the line that came through his counter.

“I still feel that way about the Oaks, they’re trying to reach out and be part of the community, and that’s something positive. Even with the preseason loss to the Miami Heat, I believe their effort to connect with us is genuine and that’s what matters to me.”

We walked a block toward the BART stop with the 0-5 record sitting on us like wet concrete. Five straight losses before the real games even start. That number doesn’t lie. Some fans will tell you preseason is just noise, that the streak means nothing once opening night hits. They’re wrong. A winless run this long already tells you the habits that are baked in, the same ones that let a team walk off the floor flat after another loss to Miami. We felt it in the stands—empty applause, the slow drift toward the exits before the buzzer. That isn’t noise. That’s the sound of the same story starting again.

I keep hearing the same quiet question in every conversation like this one: what happens when the effort to connect stops being enough and the losses keep stacking? We still want to believe. We always do. But naming the dread is the only honest first step before the next drop comes.

What can the Oaks do to turn their season around and regain the trust of their fans?
