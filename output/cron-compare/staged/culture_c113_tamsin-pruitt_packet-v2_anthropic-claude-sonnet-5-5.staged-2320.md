# The Page: Lives of Cycle 113

**Brady Lopez** moved into a place of their own on Piedmont Ave this cycle. It is a rental, and the keys are theirs.

"I've been playing to climb — a place of my own first," Brady said. "The last stretch threw a money week that mattered. Now I'm renting a place on Piedmont Ave."

The record can say what came just before. In Cycle 112 it logged a line under money: *the last debt cleared — the ledger finally reads clean.* In the same cycle Brady saved the Tribune clipping with their neighborhood in it, "the good one," and painted over graffiti on the recreation center wall alongside five neighbors they barely knew. The Tribune had also named Brady in a Business piece that cycle, "Uptown's Two Speeds: The Block Moves Faster Than the Store."

One line from this cycle sits oddly beside the move. Brady noticed the calendar squares all look alike lately and couldn't decide whether that was peace. A person who has just cleared a debt and signed a lease might be expected to mark the day. The record has Brady unsure whether the quiet is a good sign. It does not say how Brady came down on it. Brady is a dishwasher, and the record does not say where the lease came from or how long it runs.

**Kwame Fitzgerald** also moved into a place of their own this cycle, in Uptown, renting. Kwame is a craft brewery owner. The record does not say whether Uptown is where the brewery stands.

The record is split on how the move sits. In Cycle 112 Kwame waved at the regulars on the usual route, "all present and accounted for," and engaged with urban arts education. In Cycle 113 the log has them drinking the free cold brew in the company kitchen and feeling "completely disconnected from the neighborhood outside." Another entry reads: *limited social activity due to winter mood.* A third has Kwame navigating the stroller traffic on College Avenue and silently judging the parking jobs.

Back in Cycle 111 the log had Kwame unpacking a box that had stayed sealed since the last move. Two cycles later there is another move. The Tribune last named Kwame in Cycle 99, in "The Foundation and the Pillars: Baylight Phase II Steps Into the Light." The record does not say what is in the boxes this time.

**Solis Walmart** won on the UNDOCKED book in Downtown this cycle. The stake was 336 and the book paid 615.

"The window paid, that's all I can say," Solis said. "The week changed color. I was on cloud nine for a bit."

It was a week with a lot in it. The log has Solis at the Downtown Volunteer Boost Rally the night before, and seeing an Oakland Alternative Response Initiative team de-escalate a situation quietly on the corner. It also has Solis feeling "quietly lucky to call Downtown home." In Cycle 112 Solis had logged that the initiative was making waves, with residents feeling safer and more connected. A Housing Authority inspector by trade, Solis has kept the Tribune's front page close. In Cycle 110 the log had Solis reading it twice, once for the news and once to see who was in it. The record does not say what Solis did with the 615.

**Reggie Soto** lost on the same book. Reggie, who owns an auto body shop in Fruitvale, staked 247 and the book kept it.

"I wasn't too surprised when the slip came back empty," Reggie said. "I'd been thinking about it, and it just didn't feel right. Losing 247 isn't great, but it's not the end of the world either. I've been around here long enough to know that sometimes things just don't go your way."

The record has what Reggie did afterward. They walked a different street home, just to see it. That fits a Cycle 112 run of entries: Reggie was recognized by a stranger who had read about them in the Tribune, read about something unfamiliar and kept pulling the thread near a corner market, and noticed another new spot opening in Fruitvale. The Tribune named Reggie in Cycle 99 in "The Slot and the Street: Fruitvale's Transit Bargain Comes Due." The record does not say which street Reggie took.

**Naomi Sterling**, senior pastor in Rockridge, caught herself this cycle narrating the day as if telling it to someone gone. The record keeps only that line. It does not say who the someone is, or how long it has been going on.

The city's initiative work reached seven neighborhoods this cycle, and Rockridge is not among them. Naomi is one of four people the cycle's city turn reached anyway. Her last entry before this one was a better day. In Cycle 112 she attended a community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II. "I attended a community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II and actually felt heard," she said. "That's the kind of initiative work that resonates with me."

Two entries sit side by side in the thread, one of being heard and one of talking to someone who isn't there. The record does not connect them, and neither does this page.

**Matra Woven** stepped over a puddle in Chinatown that was reflecting the Tribune Tower neon and thought about taking a picture. Matra is one of four people the cycle's safety turn reached. Its entry for the cycle is: *a letter ends the biggest account on the books — the owner reads it twice before telling the staff.* The record does not say what Matra's part in that was.

The rest of the cycle's log is small and local. Matra painted over graffiti on the recreation center wall alongside five neighbors they barely knew, noticed the busy morning markets, and noticed the blend of old and new storefronts. The same graffiti line sits on Brady's and Kwame's logs in Cycle 112, and the record does not say whether it was one wall or three. The Tribune named Matra in Cycle 112 in the Civic piece "Six Names for 142 Sick Residents."

**Howard Yell** is the other person the safety turn reached in Rockridge. The turn was a train dead on the track between stations, with riders walking the tunnel, and a morning that never recovered. The one line the record holds for Howard is of the street above: Rockridge, bustling with shoppers and diners. The record does not say whether Howard was on the train.

**Tina Kozlov** moved from Temescal to Grand Lake this cycle, and the record calls it moving up. In Cycle 112 the schedule at Piedmont Avenue Medical Center came out with her name in the slot that used to belong to someone senior. That cycle she also drove the church van on the senior trip and listened to three hours of unfiltered neighborhood gossip. Before the move she reorganized a closet and found something thought lost. The record does not say what it was.

**Linda Chow** left Rockridge for West Oakland this cycle, sold up and bought, for what the record calls a better fit. Her log for the same cycle still has the Rockridge streets bustling with shoppers and diners. It also has her watching the ferry cut across the bay at sunset and feeling lucky to live this close to the water, and noting that the Oakland Alternative Response Initiative is making waves. The Tribune has named her three times before. In Cycle 86, "The Blocks" was a Culture piece. In Cycle 84 she was in "The Footprint" (Business) and "The Oakland Stack" (Social Trends).

**Austin Reaves** moved from Downtown to West Oakland this cycle, sold up and bought. He came home to find the neighbors had left a case of something cold on the porch. The record does not say whether it was the new porch. He drove home with the radio off, letting the win settle. At dinner he tried to keep the streak out of the conversation and failed, and the week's wins carried dinner. In Cycle 112 the Tribune had named him three times in Sports: "Oaks' First Win Offers a Useful Opening Read," "The Weight of One," and "The First Win Has Made the Room Louder, Not Certain."

**Amara Osei** moved from Jack London to West Oakland this cycle. The record says "moving up," and no more. The week's entries are of a person in motion who keeps stopping. Amara found a quiet minute at a venue doorway and let the week's noise settle. Amara stood by the bar at a ferry-adjacent corner, nodding along to the DJ's set. On Sunday Amara put on the good suit for the service, because some traditions still matter. The Tribune last named Amara in Cycle 84, in "The Oakland Stack." The cycle's last line in the record is that Amara had a quiet moment at home.
