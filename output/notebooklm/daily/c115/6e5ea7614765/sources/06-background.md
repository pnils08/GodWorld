# From the Tribune archive: Chinatown's Sick Are Counted by the Hundred and Named by No One

What The Bay Tribune published before today about the people and places in Dr. Lila Mezran's story "Chinatown's Sick Are Counted by the Hundred and Named by No One". Earlier coverage, not today's news.

In Cycle 105, hospital ledgers recorded that Deacon Seymour of Lake Merritt and Yu Zen of Rockridge were both admitted to inpatient care following severe workplace injuries, with Seymour facing the loss of his hand and Zen facing months of physical rehabilitation. In Cycle 107, Dr. Lila Mezran reported that Chinatown was carrying a heavy respiratory illness burden, with Zen listening from a hospital bed to nurses describing the toll on multigenerational families. In Cycle 112, civic reporter Noah Tan and OARI director Vanessa Tran-Muñoz noted that Chinatown had reached 142 sick residents, with Tran-Muñoz warning that municipal emergency response crews faced severe dispatch delays reaching Chinatown addresses compared to faster dispatches in West Oakland.

In Cycle 113, Dr. Mezran reported that Chinatown’s sick count stood at 140 residents out of 2,201, or 6.4 percent, with elder care coordinator Marisio Curazo managing care for the vast majority of sick residents directly in their homes. Official hospital records logged only three admissions and six Chinatown patients occupying staffed hospital beds, leaving 134 homebound residents dependent on local prescription deliveries and family caregivers commuting from districts like Fruitvale. Cab driver Omarion Farah confirmed that passengers frequently discussed rearranging family work shifts to care for relatives, while Zen observed from care that uncounted, unpaid household labor carried the true weight of the illness cluster. Public hospital ledgers published through these cycles omitted patient names and discharge dates for Chinatown’s admissions, tracking only long-term patients hospitalized outside the neighborhood, including Seymour and Zen.

By Cycle 114, Chinatown’s sick count eased slightly to 137 while continuing to lead the city, and Athletics outfielder Desmond Morton relocated into the neighborhood for what the ledger called a better fit. Across each reporting cycle, hospital beds accounted for only a handful of patients, leaving the home care provided behind kitchen counters unrecorded on municipal dashboards.
