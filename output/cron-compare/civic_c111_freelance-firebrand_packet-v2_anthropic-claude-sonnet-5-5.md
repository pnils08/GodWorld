# Fruitvale Is Packed at Lunch and the Map Says It's Decaying. Who Owns That?

I stood outside a storefront office in Fruitvale at noon, reading transit hub plans through the glass. The pages curl at the corners where the sun has been working them. Nobody inside looked up.

Down the block the taqueria was slammed. Work boots at the counter, blazers in line behind them, one door and one register for both. I ate standing up. I counted two people leaving with their lunch for every one coming in, and I counted nothing else, because nobody there was telling me anything and I made up nothing.

Then I went and read the map.

The map says Fruitvale is in decay. Mood is down by about a sixth of a point. Retail vitality is down by two full points. Housing pressure is up a full notch, to 4.5, in a city where the median is zero.

Now the part that doesn't compute. On mood, Fruitvale still ranks first of twenty-two neighborhoods. On retail, fifth of twenty-two. The mood number is more than half again the city median, and retail is well above it. Housing pressure, on the other hand, sits near the bottom of the pack. So this isn't a dead block. It's a block that tops the table on feeling good and is still sliding.

The sliding is what I keep staring at. A lunch line out the door is exactly what a place looks like right before the people in it can't afford to stay. I'm not saying that's what's happening. I'm saying the map can't tell me it isn't, and neither can anyone I could find with a title.

Here's what I could verify. Two programs on the city's roster list Fruitvale in their footprint: the Oakland Alternative Response Initiative and the Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline. Both are running. Neither one owns this break. The record is plain on that: no active initiative, no mitigator, nothing with a name on it that answers the housing squeeze or the retail slide.

Translate that. A neighborhood can be on the roster and still be on its own. Putting a place in a program's footprint doesn't mean anyone is working the problem that is actually hurting it.

I also don't know what came before. The map gives a direction but not the earlier readings I'd need to say how long this has been going on, or whether last week's dip was a blip. That's missing. And nobody from any office has said a word to me about the mismatch. I can't tell you whether that's silence or whether nobody was ever asked. Somebody's job should be to know.

Meanwhile the transit hub plans sit in a window. Pages curling. A hub means new people, new money, new rent. I won't claim any of it is coming for these blocks, because the record doesn't say that. But a plan in a window and a pressure number going up on the same street is a coincidence somebody at City Hall should be able to explain without a press officer.

So I'll ask the way I'd ask the guy next to me at any bar in town. Who is watching Fruitvale's housing? Not who's funded in the footprint. Who is watching the actual rent pressure, today, with a name and a phone number?

The taqueria will be packed again tomorrow. That's not an answer.

The map says decay, and the program roster doesn't answer it. So whose name goes on the answer?
