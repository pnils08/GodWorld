# Deputy Mayor Brenda Okoro — Weekly Report, Cycle 116

**West Oakland Stabilization Fund.** The charter renewal sits where I said it would sit: on Council's calendar, not in my hands. I will not call it before they do. The condition of that vote is verifiable and I want it verified in public: the C114 closeout published, the business-and-resident scope intact, and the 23-day processing standard held. The C114 tranche executed under that standard, $400,000 out the door, and with $20.6M remaining against a $28M authorization there is real money still to move and real households still waiting. The strings stay visible — I'd rather have Council on record than anyone whispering later.

**What the map tells me, and it's the hard part of this report.** West Oakland is booming — momentum 10 of 10, housing pressure at 5.5, median rent $3,669 against a median income of $146,748, sentiment 0.39, the strongest in the city. That is not uncomplicated good news. A rising West Oakland with pressure climbing is exactly the condition the Fund was chartered for: long-tenure residents priced out as the flats build out. The boom is the *reason* for the Fund, not a reprieve from it.

The same story, sharper, in Fruitvale, Jack London and Uptown: pressure at 9.5 and 10 out of 10, mass exodus signals — five residents each in Jack London and Uptown planning to leave for a better income-to-rent fit this Cycle alone. Growth without a floor under it is displacement with a nice paint job.

**The neighborhoods nobody puts on a press release.** Ivy Hill: pressure low, but migration −4, momentum 0, sentiment −0.06. Brooklyn: −5 migration, sentiment −0.04. Dimond −0.01, San Antonio −0.03. These are the rows I don't look away from. Ivy Hill and Brooklyn do not care about a charter renewal — they care whether I show up. Three portfolios is triage, and triage means not fooling myself into thinking a press line fixes a broken sidewalk. My commitment from last Cycle stands: feet on the ground in Ivy Hill and San Antonio, public-works answers in Glenview, health answers on Piedmont Avenue. Piedmont Avenue is genuinely rising — retail busy, five net in-migration, the Keane and Okoro households among them — and it gets its attention without being mistaken for the whole story.

**Faith and community.** The seniors lunch program at New Covenant Pentecostal Assembly in West Oakland marks its milestone this Cycle — that is the stabilization work with a human face, and it is West Oakland. St. Esperanza in Fruitvale opens another job-training session; Or Hashalom in Grand Lake does the same. Food pantries at Temple Beth Or and B'nai Tikvah are serving neighbors this Cycle — that is the quiet infrastructure holding the decaying edges up. The Grand Lake block party catalyzed by the ice cream truck did its small good: sentiment up a tenth. Small, and real.

**What's next.** Council consideration of the charter renewal on verified closeout terms; outreach reset in the boom neighborhoods where exodus signals are landing; and my feet in Ivy Hill and Brooklyn before a single camera is anywhere near them.

*— Brenda Okoro, Deputy Mayor (Community)*
