Colin Phillips arrives at Nino's before first light, the bakery section already warm from the ovens. He measures flour by the scoop, works it into dough on the long table, and lines the trays for proofing. The work settles into a rhythm that fills the racks by the time the morning shift starts.

"Most days at Nino's start before sunrise - mixing dough, shaping loaves, that kind of thing," he says. "It's steady work, not glamorous, but there's something satisfying about seeing the racks fill up before the morning rush. Lately I've been training on the pastry side too, which keeps things interesting."
