# Downtown's Eight: Two Arrests, Three Still Held, and a Clearance Rate That Counts Something Else

Along the Downtown corridor, the storefront shutters go up one at a time. A shop owner who finds a pried door or a missing register drawer makes a report, gets a number and goes back to opening. That kind of report is most of what Downtown's incident count is made of. Dolores Nwachukwu, an Oakland Police captain, says so herself.

Downtown logged 8 incidents this cycle, according to the C116 Crime_Metrics sheet. That is the most of any of the 22 neighborhoods, and 3 more than C115. The paper's C115 daily put Downtown at 5. Grand Lake and Lake Merritt followed with 7 each. Fruitvale and Eastlake had 6 each. Chinatown logged the fewest, at 4.

The Downtown row shows an average response of 10.1 minutes, a clearance rate of 20 percent, a property index of 37 and a violent index of 20. The sheet marks the trend "steady."

"Downtown's number being the highest doesn't surprise me," Nwachukwu said. "It's a busy corridor with a lot of foot traffic and storefronts, and that's where a lot of the petty stuff gets reported. The clearance rate is a different problem. We're stretched thin, and a lot of those cases are property crimes where there's no witness and no easy lead. Two arrests out of eight incidents — that's not a failure of effort, it's a reality of the caseload. We're doing what we can with the staff we have, and I'd rather be honest about that than pretend otherwise."

## Three numbers that look alike and aren't

The Care_Justice_Census row for Downtown reads 8 charges, 2 arrests, and 3 people in custody at the close. All 3 are listed as other residents. None are tracked individuals.

The captain's "two out of eight" is a count of arrests against charges. The 20 percent clearance rate on the crime sheet is a different measure. Neither sheet says how clearance is calculated, so the two figures cannot be reconciled from the record. A reader who sees "two of eight" and "20 percent" will assume they are the same thing. They are close, but the paper cannot confirm that.

The custody number is also not the arrest number. Downtown shows 2 arrests and 3 held at the close. The sheet does not say why the second figure is larger. One reading, offered here as analysis and not as a fact from the record, is that custody can include people arrested in earlier cycles who are still held. The sheet does not say which people are in the 3, and the paper will not guess.

The same pattern shows up across the table. Grand Lake, the one neighborhood marked rising, shows 7 charges, 2 arrests and 4 in custody. San Antonio shows 5 charges, 1 arrest and 3 in custody. Adams Point shows 5, 1 and 2. The Baylight District shows 6, 1 and 2. In each, the number held at the close is larger than the number arrested. Citywide, the census records 24 arrests this cycle, all of them other residents, with 48 in custody at the close and 43 left.

## The slowest wait is not Downtown's

Downtown has the most incidents, but it does not have the slowest response. Jack London does, at 10.9 minutes. That is 0.8 minutes slower than Downtown's 10.1. Jack London logged 5 incidents, one fewer than in C115, with a 22 percent clearance rate and a property index of 37, the same as Downtown's. Its violent index is 22, against Downtown's 20.

The two neighborhoods share a boundary in the Baylight District's running story. The sheets do not link the response gap to that project, and nothing here suggests they do.

## Who answers for it

Nwachukwu pointed to staffing. The ledger lists 39 public-safety staff on the roster. Police Chief Rafael Montez and Deputy Chief of Operations Kimberly Cho sit above her in the department. Neither is quoted this cycle. The record does not show how many of the 39 are assigned Downtown, or whether any were moved there after the count rose from 5 to 8. Those are questions for the chief's office, and the paper has put them to no one yet.

## What a resident can check

The table cannot tell a Downtown shop owner whether a case is moving. A resident who reports an incident can ask for the report number at the time of the call, then write down when they called and when someone arrived. Those two times are the only part of the 10.1-minute average that a person can test against their own experience. A clearance rate of 20 percent, and a count of 8, only matter once they are tied to a call that someone remembers making.
