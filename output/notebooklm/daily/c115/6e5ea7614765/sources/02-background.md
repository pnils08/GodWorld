# From the Tribune archive: The Sea-Level Desk Is Down to Three

What The Bay Tribune published before today about the people and places in Jordan Velez's story "The Sea-Level Desk Is Down to Three". Earlier coverage, not today's news.

In Cycle 83, the Oakland City Council approved the \$2.1 billion Baylight District redevelopment initiative on a 6–3 vote, formalizing a massive waterfront transformation effort intended to deliver jobs, commercial development, and housing.

In Cycle 106, Bay Tribune reporting introduced Mina Linas as a sea level monitoring technician stationed at Lake Merritt, where she tracked water levels and observed neighborhood residents withdrawing indoors across weeks of unbroken overcast skies.

Following the initial development approval, municipal milestone records tracked construction contracts executed in Cycle 107, Phase II environmental remediation commencing in Cycle 109, and soil test verifications completing in Cycle 110. In Cycle 112, Keisha Ramos, Director of the Baylight Redevelopment Authority, voiced her anxieties over community accountability, noting that she lay awake at night waiting on pending soil test results for the upcoming Cycle 115 review because the outcome would dictate the entire remediation calendar and prove whether the project genuinely benefited local residents.

In Cycle 113, municipal trackers showed Baylight haul routes operating with active dust control and a live 48-hour construction hotline, even as economic development officials warned that the district's final council approvals had sat stalled across three cycles while waiting on the scheduled Cycle 115 construction milestone review.

In Cycle 114, city ledgers highlighted a widening economic divergence across Oakland, documenting that while corporate technology and major construction projects surged, smaller specialized businesses began shedding staff, with Marine Systems losing its first worker in the Baylight District.
