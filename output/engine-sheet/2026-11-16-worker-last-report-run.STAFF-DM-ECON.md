# Deputy Mayor's Weekly Report — Cycle 116

Let me say this plainly, because the numbers don't lie and I'm not going to dress them up.

**City-wide, the headline looks fine.** 283 businesses, 31,399 staff, median pay $52,000, and not one closure on the ledger this Cycle. I'll take that.

**But Chinatown — my neighborhood — is being left behind.** Eleven businesses, 118 staff, median pay $46,000. Median income $84,843. Retail vitality 9.74 and a boom index of flat zero. Eighty-three residents out of work out of 1,502 adults. That's not a dip; that's a neighborhood stuck in neutral while the rest of the city moves. I've been saying it since Cycle 106 — I got banged up in that accident and walked these streets and saw how many of our people are struggling worse than me, and the hospital records had five names on them just that cycle. Nothing's changed enough since.

And it's not just us. **Downtown** — 22 businesses and 6,819 staff, the engine of this city — still has 56 residents out of work and a boom index of just 0.6. **Eastlake**, **Glenview**, **Dimond**, **Ivy Hill** are all sitting at 90-plus unemployed, and San Antonio's at 105. Those are the quiet neighborhoods nobody puts on the news, and that's exactly my worry: the economic development plans have to help people on the ground, not just the ones with the loudest voices.

**Two things I want my office tracking this Cycle:**
1. The mail truck brought an **insurance premium hike to a business** — a half-point drag on growth. Small businesses in this city can't absorb that. I want to know who it was and whether it's the first of many.
2. **Adams Point United Methodist Church lost two employees** — a services/retail sector hit, an 8-point drag lasting four cycles. Faith institutions are employers too, and when they shrink, the neighborhood feels it.

And those **carryovers** — Baylight District and Ivy Hill — are still working through. Ivy Hill has taken two back-to-back 8-point hits across the last three cycles. I want eyes on Ivy Hill before that becomes another Chinatown.

Bottom line for the record: no closures is good news, but flat is not growth. I'll be bringing a Chinatown small-business and hiring item to the Mayor this week, and I want the council looking at Eastlake, Glenview, Dimond and San Antonio alongside it. We don't wait for the headlines to tell us who's hurting.
