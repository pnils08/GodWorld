### Ridership Surge at 12th St Oakland City Center Tests Transit Systems  

At 12th St Oakland City Center, the fares click louder and more often. Eleanor Rivera, a BART station manager, has felt the surge in her bones—23,848 riders this cycle, up 5,472 from the last. “It’s not just numbers,” Rivera says. “It’s people adjusting their routines, and us adjusting right along with them. The team just... adapts. No big announcements, just everyone doing their part to keep things moving smoothly.”  

The station isn’t alone. Across Oakland, ridership has jumped nearly 30% this cycle, with 99,582 boardings logged across eight stations and ten corridors. Coliseum Station saw 9,724 riders, a spike of 4,859 from the last cycle, while Fruitvale Station recorded 10,414 boardings, up 3,756. Even MacArthur Station, quieter by comparison, saw a 24% increase, with 12,030 riders passing through its gates.  

The reasons aren’t hard to spot. Downtown hosted a game day, while Lake Merritt saw two major events. The Baylight District’s ongoing construction planning has also redirected traffic, funneling commuters toward 12th St. Joel Wren, an AC Transit mechanic who works near the station, has noticed the uptick firsthand. “You know, I’ve been seeing more folks hopping on the buses lately—especially down at 12th Street,” Wren says. “It’s good to see people out and about, trusting the system again. But what really gets me is the crew keeping those buses running. Those mechanics and drivers? They’re the ones putting in the long hours to make sure everyone gets where they need to go, especially with all these extra riders.”  

Jerald Jensen, a janitor at the station, sees the surge in the wear and tear. “More coffee spills to mop up, more trash cans overflowing by noon,” Jensen says. “Used to be you could catch your breath between rushes, but now? It’s constant. Folks pouring in for work, for games, for whatever’s happening downtown.”  

Coen Cartier, a transit operator, echoes the sentiment. “It’s been busy, but it feels good to know we’re helping people get where they need to go,” Cartier says. “The team’s been working hard to keep things running smoothly, and it’s paying off.”  

On-time performance has improved at most stations, with 12th St hitting an 85% on-time rate, up four points from the last cycle. Fruitvale Station, despite its own surge, managed an 81% rate, up three points. Even Coliseum Station, often a laggard, saw its rate climb to 83%, up five points.  

The question now is how long this surge will last—and whether the system can sustain it.
