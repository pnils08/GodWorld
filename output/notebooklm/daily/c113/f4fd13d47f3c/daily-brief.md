#### **Temescal’s Ledger Is Growing — The Question Is Who It’s Growing For**
*By Jordan Velez, Business Desk*

In Temescal, every named business on the ledger closed the cycle in the black, but local storefronts and tech firms are moving at drastically different speeds [1, 2]. At **Da Dough**, sourdough baker **Melton Neilon** works a five-person counter where revenue added \$128 to reach \$371,000 [1, 3]. A few doors away at **OakTown Social**, baristas **Vanessa Treary** and **Vanessa Ramas** added \$308 on \$521,000 in sales [3]. 

By contrast, tech and trade firms saw substantial gains:
*   **Gridiron Analytics** (founded by Daniel Yoon and Christine Nakamura) added \$16,000 to hit \$2.9 million in revenue—a 27.9 percent growth rate led by data analysts **Ariana Reyes** and **Jade Soy**, alongside sports analytics leads **Dawson Nguyen** and **Wesley Clark** [2, 4, 5].
*   **Calderon-Nishi** contractor added \$14,000 to reach \$9 million in revenue with 34 employees, including plumber **Jett Walker** and electricians **Ronald Silk** and **Cristina Norwood** [5].
*   **Temescal Community Health Center** holds 14 employees—including physical therapist **Clearence Leighton**—and reports \$45.1 million in revenue [6].

Project Director **Bobby Chen-Ramirez** emphasized that corporate growth means little if neighborhood needs remain unaddressed: *"Look, I run a health center project, not a spreadsheet... the numbers on the wall don't mean a damn thing if Mrs. Okafor still can't get her blood pressure checked without taking two buses. What creates accountability for me is simple: I've got 14 employees on my roster, and every one of them lives in this neighborhood... If growth just means the ledger gets fatter while the sidewalk stays the same, then we're building a ghost town with good accounting"* [6, 7].

#### **The Quiet Platform Still Has a Job**
*By Trevor Shimizu, Transit Desk*

Citywide transit ridership dropped across eight stations and 10 corridors to 46,520 passengers over a quiet weekend, but schedule punctuality and platform conditions varied station by station [8, 9]. **12th St Oakland City Center** experienced the largest drop, losing 4,821 passengers (down to 9,341) while on-time performance stood at 84 percent [8, 9]. Meanwhile, **19th St Oakland** maintained a 90 percent on-time rate despite falling to 10,012 riders [9, 10]. **Coliseum** (5,595 riders, 82% on-time) and **Fruitvale** (81% on-time) also felt the weekend dip [10].

Station operators and drivers noted how quiet days change the system's feel:
*   **Eleanor Rivera** (Station Manager, 12th St): *"The platform echoes more. You hear the announcements bounce off the tile instead of getting swallowed up by the crowd. And you see the regulars more clearly... We just adapt, keep things moving, make sure the trains run on time"* [9, 11].
*   **Evan Lewis** (AC Transit Driver): *"When the system runs light on a weekend, you feel it in your bones... 12th Street is the heart of downtown... When it's quiet there, it's not just empty platforms. It's the whole city catching its breath"* [11, 12].
*   **Coen Cartier** (AC Transit Operator): *"Riders don't see that part. They just see the bus showing up. But for us, it's the difference between moving a crowd and moving people"* [11, 13].

*   **Desmond Morton** (.291 AVG, 7 HR, 44 RBI, 28 SB, .348 OBP over 285 ABs in 80 games): Morton provides the most reliable full-season sample, pairing solid contact with team-leading speed [14, 15, 17].
*   **Frank Reyna** (.317 AVG, 14 HR, 34 RBI, .683 SLG over 164 ABs in 46 games): Reyna boasts high power rates (1 HR every 11.7 ABs), but his numbers come from a smaller sample size [14, 16, 18].
*   **JR Rosado** (.298 AVG, 7 HR, 27 RBI over 184 ABs in 54 games): Rosado sits between Morton and Reyna in rate metrics, creating direct positional overlap with Morton in left field [14, 19].

The front-office decision hinges on whether Paulson trusts Morton’s proven everyday workload or Reyna’s high-slugging potential [14, 20].
