# West Oakland, in their own words

## Amara Osei — Founder, Portside Bio

> The corner store on Mandela, the one with the squeaky screen door and the old man who’s been running it since before I moved here. I don’t need anything, but I’ll grab a bag of ice or a soda I won’t drink, just to stand in the fluorescent hum for a minute. Nobody bothers me there; I’m just another face in line, not the woman who signs the checks or sits in the meetings. It smells like dust and cheap soap and something sweet in the back, and for a second I’m not thinking about the lab or the payroll or the city’s plans. That’s the closest I get to a quiet corner in my own head.

## Elias Varek — Founder of Civis Systems

> I've been thinking a lot about Vinnie Keane's retirement and what it means for the programs we both founded in West Oakland. To be honest, it's still sinking in. Vinnie was a pillar, not just for the A's but for this community. The programs he started, like the Firehouse 29 West Oakland Baseball Academy and the Keane Auxiliary Flag Football League, they're still active and doing well. Civis Systems has been working closely with those programs, and we'll continue to support them. My focus now is on ensuring that the transition is smooth and that the community continues to benefit from the work we've done together.

## Priyanka Raman — Principal, Oakland Tech Collective

> One thing that's stood out to me in West Oakland recently is how the community turn seems to be paying off. I noticed the new streetlights went in before anyone had to ask, which was a nice surprise.

## Delvon Pratt — Warehouse Supervisor

> I've been living in West Oakland for a while now, and it's hard not to notice the industrial vibe around here. You've got warehouses, factories, and all sorts of businesses operating around the clock. It's not just residential; it's a working neighborhood. I like that about it. As a warehouse supervisor myself, I see a lot of familiar faces and operations around here. It's a character that feels right at home to me.

## Juliette Park — Venture Partner (Ridgeline Ventures)

> The little metal click of the blinds this morning — that cheap, tinny sound they make, like a toy latch. I’ve heard it a thousand times, but today it caught me. The light came through the slats in that hard, honest way West Oakland does at 6:30, and for a second I just stood there, coffee mug in hand, not thinking about the portfolio or the A's or the damn transit hub delays. It was the sound of a thing working exactly as it was supposed to. No fanfare, no drama. Just the day opening. That's what got me. The quiet certainty of it. The way a small, stupid mechanism can hold the whole morning together if you let it.
