# Jack London, in their own words

## Xing Patel — Ship Repair Foreman

> The first thing I notice is I don't have to check the time. That's still strange. Forty years of waking up to a shift, and now the day just... sits there, waiting on me. Celo's already up, making noise in the kitchen, and I can hear the grandkids stirring somewhere in the house, and it hits me that I get to be here for that now. Not rushing out the door, not missing the mornings. I keep waiting for the guilt to show up, the feeling that I'm supposed to be somewhere, but it hasn't come yet. Maybe that's the part nobody tells you about retirement — it's not the freedom that takes getting used to, it's the quiet.
