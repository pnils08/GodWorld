# Ninety-One Households, One Ledger

"Ninety-one households stabilized means ninety-one West Oakland families who won't face displacement this month," Marcus Webb said on the record this cycle, "and every case file meets compliance standards that will survive audit." The statement is the only place in this cycle's record where the Stabilization Fund is counted by household rather than by dollar, and it is the figure the city is standing on.

The money is real and it is moving. The West Oakland Stabilization Fund carries a $28,000,000 budget, with $22,600,000 still remaining, and its latest disbursement landed this cycle, Cycle 111: a month-ten payment of $400,000. Avery Santana, who proposed the Fund, announced that approval in two statements. "Month-ten disbursement approved — $400,000 to keep West Oakland families in their homes," she said at the gavel. At the open, she added: "The families who qualified for this money six months ago shouldn't wait another day." Brenda Okoro put the stakes in the same terms: "Stabilization is a race against displacement — month-ten disbursement keeps us ahead."

The Fund's path to this point runs through a single vote. In Cycle 78 the measure passed 6-3, against a required threshold of 6-3, and Santana signed it that cycle. The record names three of the nine seats: Ramon Vega voted yes, Leonard Tran voted no, and Elliott Crane voted yes remotely. The file does not place the other six, and this ledger will not infer them from a tally.

Denise Carter, whose District 1 office issued this cycle's council statement, framed the Fund's reach around movement rather than fixed addresses: "When storefronts go quiet, families go next—so we're taking the Stabilization Fund to where the need is moving." That sentence points at a geography the tracker does not map. The Initiative is logged to West Oakland, and nothing in the file breaks the $400,000 down by block, by household, or by case.

The clock is the strongest part of the record. Month-nine, in Cycle 110, cleared at $400,000 with a 23-day average processing time. Cycle 111 held the 23-day processing standard again. The next action is the month-eleven disbursement review in Cycle 112. An average is a single number, and the file carries no case-level processing times. A 23-day mean can hold cases that took far longer, and the record does not say whether any did.

Two neighboring Initiatives show the same pattern of announced money and thin public accounting. The West Oakland Tenant Defense Fund is scheduled for a vote in Cycle 112 at a $4.2M budget. Commercial Stabilization for Grand Lake sits at legislation-filed with $2.5M. Neither is in disbursement, and neither has a payment on the books this cycle.

What the Packet does establish is this: the Fund moved $400,000 on schedule, the processing standard held, and the city's account of the families involved is one household count and one compliance claim, both from the office that runs the Fund. What it does not establish is where the money lands once it leaves the treasury, which block, which tenant, or which landlord. The city treasury took in $5.1M this cycle and closed at $110.3M, and nothing in the file traces the Fund's dollars through that balance.

Webb's ninety-one families are a real number in a real statement. They are not yet a place on a map or a name in a file the public can read. Ask who should answer the question the disbursement invites. The Stabilization Fund's office signed the audit claim. The mayor's office announced the payment. The council seat that moved the Fund toward "where the need is moving" has not said where that is in the record this cycle.

If the money is moving on time, why can't the city show us where it lands?
