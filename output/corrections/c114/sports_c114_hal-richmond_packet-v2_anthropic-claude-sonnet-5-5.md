# Fifty-Six, and the Road Behind It

Danny Horn's 2041 line, as his dossier prints it: 148 games, 621 at-bats, 210 hits, 56 home runs, 113 RBI, 80 stolen bases, a .338 average, a .382 on-base, a .715 slugging. The dossier's own worked rate says one home run every 11.1 at-bats. His career reference puts the season's value at 13.3 WAR.

That is the line at the head of a Tribune feature on the A's this off-season, which names three men: Isley Kelley at shortstop, Darrin Davis at designated hitter, Danny Horn in center field. It is spring in the city's calendar, Y3C10, with no ballgame to report. It is a good time to read the ledger.

## The Ground

I went to the Archive for Horn's hometown, his family, and the name the city might have given him. It returned a dossier and a ledger. It holds no origin for him, no nickname, and no recorded words. So this piece does not supply them. Where the archive is silent, the man is allowed to be silent too.

What the archive does hold is the road, printed one season at a time.

## The Long Road

His dossier opens in 2033, with a team listed as MIN. He played 58 games, had 199 at-bats, and hit .166 with 2 home runs and 17 RBI. The 60 walks against 5 strikeouts are the oddest figures in the whole file. They read like a young hitter waiting on a pitch he could not yet hit.

In 2034, still MIN, he played 79 games and hit .192 with 17 home runs. Then the dossier shows Athletics in 2035: 122 games, .214, 24 home runs, 105 strikeouts.

The next three seasons don't read like a rise. 2036 was 96 games and .198. In 2037 he played 159 games and hit .246, with 31 home runs and 81 walks. In 2038 he played 159 games again and hit .259, with 29 home runs and 92 RBI. A man doesn't play 159 games in two straight years unless somebody keeps writing his name on the card.

Then the line turns. 2039 was .322, with 198 hits, 43 home runs, and a .391 on-base. His career reference lists All-Star selections in 2038, 2039, and 2040, and a Silver Slugger in 2039. In 2040 the average dipped to .280 on 32 home runs and 121 strikeouts. Then came 2041, the 56.

His 2041 season carries the awards too: the MVP, the Hank Aaron Award, the batting title, the home run title and a Silver Slugger.

The record doesn't say how a .166 hitter in 2033 became a .338 hitter in 2041. It only says he was there for each season in between.

## The Other Two

Horn doesn't stand alone on the feed's line. The men beside him are the ones the Archive knows best.

Isley Kelley's career reference prints MVP awards in 2032, 2034, and 2035, a Rookie of the Year in 2026, and All-Star selections. His dossier holds two seasons. In 2040 he hit .285 with 21 home runs and 74 RBI. In 2041 he played 136 games, got 181 hits, hit 31 home runs, drove in 103, and hit .321. His 2041 WAR is 7.4. His strikeouts also changed. He had 92 in 2040 and 54 in 2041, and the dossier's worked rate puts the second at one every 10.4 at-bats.

Darrin Davis's career reference prints MVP awards in 2037 and 2040, and the Hank Aaron Award in 2033 and 2037. The city's ledger lists him as an A's legend, born in Ohio in 2009. His dossier also holds two seasons. In 2040 he played 137 games and hit 38 home runs with 107 RBI. In 2041 he played 95 games, hit .305 with 24 home runs, and stole 26 bases on a .594 slugging. His 2041 WAR is 3.6.

I compiled the Origins of the Dynasty series, and Kelley and Davis are two of its five chapters. Those chapters hold the grounds these men came from. I won't borrow from them here. The Archive tells me the shelf is full, and this piece is about the man who isn't on it yet.

## What He Became

The feed calls this a player feature in the off-season, which means no scoreboard is attached to it. The city's tracker doesn't say what the line means. A feature is where a paper lets a number sit still long enough to be read.

Kelley's MVPs sit in the early part of the record. Davis's sit in the middle and in 2040. Horn's MVP is the 2041 one, and his 2041 line stands next to the other two men's best seasons. Whether the three of them are one era or three eras sharing a clubhouse is not a question the dossiers settle.

The feed's closing question, taken as a question and nothing more: what does the roster look like in the years after this one? The record doesn't answer it. It doesn't answer who should say so, either, and it doesn't say who decides. All it holds is who is on the card this spring, and what each man hit last year.

Some careers get a name from the city because the city watched them happen. Horn's is still unnamed. The Archive holds no nickname, and nobody can hand out one for him. Years from now, if the city does give him one, it will come from this stretch of seasons. It will be earned from games, not from a feature.

The record doesn't say how he got here. It says he stayed, and then it says 56.
