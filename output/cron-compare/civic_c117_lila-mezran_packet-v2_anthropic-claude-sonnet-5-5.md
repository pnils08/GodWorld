# Chinatown's Illness Count Is 133. The Hospital Count Is 6. The Record Can't Explain the Gap.

**Oakland, Cycle 117.** Chinatown has the most sick residents of any neighborhood this cycle. The census counts 133, up one from Cycle 116. East Oakland is next at 113 (down one), then Ivy Hill at 109 (down three) and Fruitvale at 108 (up one). Citywide the count is 1,717 across 22 neighborhoods, and the illness rate is 3.9 percent.

The hospital side of Chinatown's record has not moved. Three residents were admitted this cycle and three left. The neighborhood opened with six in care, closed with six, and has six beds occupied. None of the six is tracked by name. The census lists them as "other residents." Citywide the hospital took 34 admissions, held 68 in care in 68 beds, and discharged 34.

Chinatown's covered population is 2,219, so 133 sick residents is about 6 percent of the neighborhood. Six hospital beds is about 4.5 percent of those who are sick.

## What the EMS Director says

I asked EMS Director Priya Narang whether the hospital count reflects the real load. She said it does not.

"It has been sitting at 3 admissions and 6 in care every cycle since at least Cycle 110, flat, eight cycles running," Narang said. "That flatness tells me we're seeing a steady baseline of illness in that neighborhood that our intake pipeline is not capturing, not a spike."

She added: "If 133 people are sick and we admit 3, either the threshold for admission is set too high for that neighborhood or people aren't getting to us."

She noted that Chinatown has about 398 seniors in a population of roughly 2,200. She said Ivy Hill, San Antonio and East Oakland show the same pattern: more than 100 sick and 2 admissions each. She has no confirmed cause and declined to guess before she has one. She said she will flag the pattern in her weekly report.

On the 68 in care citywide, Narang said none is named in the ledger. "Zero named, 68 unnamed," she said. "That's a data gap, not an accounting detail... and that's a problem I own."

## The three named patients

The hospital ledger entries I was given list three residents as in care and active:

- Yu Zen (Rockridge) and Deacon Seymour (Lake Merritt), admitted in Cycle 105 after a workplace accident.
- Maurice Franklin (Rockridge), admitted in Cycle 106 with severe seasonal flu.

Narang and Medical Examiner Jonas Patel both say the same three are closed as recovered. Patel gave these discharge cycles:

- Seymour, Cycle 107.
- Yu Zen, Cycle 109.
- Franklin, Cycle 111.

The ledger line reading "active" and the discharge dates cannot both be right. Yu Zen's own account, given for this cycle, describes him as still in a bed after twelve cycles. I can't reconcile those from the records in front of me. I am treating the discharge dates from the two offices as the official status and flagging the ledger entry as a possible stale or mis-keyed record. I have asked neither office to resolve it yet.

This matters because the 68 residents in care carry no names. If the three named records can't be trusted on status, the city has no named patients at all.

Yu Zen's account, as he gave it: "I know Chinatown's carrying the heaviest load in this city, and I know there are more sick people than there are beds with names attached." Seymour, who describes his own hospital stay as in the past, said: "If only a handful of those folks are getting named out loud, that's not a care trail, that's a gap."

Not one of the three lives in Chinatown.

Patel also said, unprompted and on the record, that he is "not comfortable closing the book on Yu Zen's file the way the paperwork wants me to." He said the lacerations "don't sit right with a fall, and the camera was blind." He called it "not a finding" but "an open curiosity." I have no further information and draw no conclusion.

## Open determinations

Patel's sheet this cycle holds three open determinations. None is a death. One is a Chinatown resident, 67, recovering from a car accident. Patel reported no new entries and no health events this cycle. He reported two ambulance runs, both minor injuries, in Piedmont Avenue and Grand Lake, and neither was in Chinatown.

## One resident's week

Coen Cartier, a Chinatown transit operator, spent the week acting on a health scare. He had his blood pressure checked on his day off and asked twice for a later shift start so he could walk ten blocks instead of driving. "The money part is the co-pay, which is what it is," he said. "The time part is the part that stings."

Cartier's care appears nowhere in the hospital counts. It is outpatient prevention, and the census does not measure it.

## What the record shows

- A flat hospital line against 133 sick residents points to a measurement gap. It does not show that those residents are well.
- The city has no named patient in a hospital bed.
- At least one of the three named records carries a status that two offices contradict.

In clinical terms, the signs are consistent across eight cycles and the cause is unconfirmed. Narang has said she will answer on cause when she has one. I will report it when she does.
