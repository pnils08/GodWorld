## The 9:14 Eastbound Never Comes On Time Either

Fruitvale BART, eastbound platform, a Tuesday afternoon, cold enough that everybody's got their hood up and their eyes on the tunnel. A rider paces the yellow line, phone in hand, watching the arrival board tick down and then reset. She's stopped checking the sign — she just watches for the light in the tunnel now, same as everybody else waiting on something that isn't the train. She's not talking about BART service. She's watching for something the city told her was coming.

Here's what nobody at this station has been told: the transit initiative that's supposed to be doing something for this neighborhood hasn't moved. Not stalled-with-an-explanation. Not delayed-pending-review. Just — nothing. Nine times now the city's own tracker has come out, and nine times the line for Fruitvale sits exactly where it sat the time before. Same status. Same non-answer. It's still listed. Still active on paper. Still, apparently, alive enough to take up a row on the tracker without doing a single thing that shows up on a street.

I went looking for the initiative that's supposed to answer for what's happening on this platform. Couldn't find one. Not a companion program, not a stopgap, not any public line acknowledging the hold. There's a signal here — a real one, sitting right in the middle of the record — and nothing on the city's books is built to catch it. That's not a gap in service. That's a gap in ownership.

I want to be careful here, because Fruitvale doesn't need another reporter telling it what it already knows. What it needs is somebody in a suit at 1 Frank Ogawa Plaza to explain why a line item can survive nine straight rounds of public tracking without generating a single move. Not a vote. Not a memo. Not a status update. Nothing.

Council President Vega runs that body's agenda. That's not an accusation — that's the job description. Which means when something sits dead on the tracker for this long, his office is the first door I'd knock on, and I haven't heard him say a word about Fruitvale's transit line. Maybe he has an answer. Maybe there's a reason nine rounds of silence isn't actually silence. I'd love to hear it. So would the woman on the platform who's stopped reading the arrival board because she already knows what it's going to say.

So here's the question I'm putting on the record, plain, no hedge: who is responsible for the delay in the transit initiative in Fruitvale?
