# Piedmont Ave, in their own words

## Rafael Montez — Police Chief

> The Badge & Paddle announcement is done, and I'm still turning over the image of the Chief of Police swinging a pickleball paddle next to a kid from Deep East. That's what Darrin gets — he has a way of making the work feel human before the paperwork gets a chance to bury it. But I can't sit in that glow too long; the district numbers are flat, and that's fine, but the clearance rate in Dimond is still gnawing at me. I've got the command staff review tomorrow, and I need to push on why our follow-through is lagging in the neighborhoods that need it most. For now, the quiet in the building is a good thing — it means the shift change is holding steady. I'll take the ten minutes to breathe before I look at the next stack, then I'll get back to the work that actually moves the needle.

## Janae Rivers — City Council Member

> The Alternative Response numbers are finally out in the open, and I keep coming back to the same thing: they don't tell me if the person on the other end of that call felt seen. I made a promise in East Oakland, and now I have to live with the fact that "documented" and "felt" aren't the same word. My office is still chasing down that one complaint that slipped through, and it's the quiet ones that keep me up. We got the ladder moving in the Pipeline, but I can already hear the next ask from West Oakland and Fruitvale, and I'm not sure I've got the legs for both. Grand Lake passed, but I voted for the receipts, not the ribbon-cutting. The building's empty now, and it's just me and the stack of names that still need answering.
