The fourth quarter belongs to us now. Eleven and two, and the only loss that truly stung was the one where the Spurs took the final nine after we were tied at 90. Every other night we have answered the bell when the game turns. Golden State gave us the 15-point hole at the half, the biggest we have faced, and we still walked out with the win. That is not luck. That is Wilson Shepard's young roster learning the one thing expansion teams usually have to earn the hard way: how to close.

Shepard said it plain after the San Antonio game and again after Denver: the fourth is the result of rhythm work they put in, not some accident of schedule. He is right. The numbers do not lie. Ten wins in the fourth, two losses, one tie. The losses came when the game was already decided or when the offense simply went cold for one night. The wins came when the hole was deepest. Indiana left us down 20 at the half and we still cut it to four. Golden State left us down 15 and we erased it. Philadelphia left us down two after three and we poured 40 in the fourth. That is not a streak. That is a habit.

Austin Reaves has made it personal. Highest-paid man on the roster at thirty million, and he is playing like the check clears every night. Thirty-two against Denver in twenty-two minutes, twenty-three at Golden State while we climbed out of the hole, and the pattern holds: after the bad night in Philadelphia he answered in Denver, after the San Antonio loss he answered again. He does not need the ball to himself. He just needs the fourth. Reaves told me the movement and the early rhythm are what let him bounce back. That is the same language Shepard uses. It is not coincidence.

Harry Hayward is the proof the identity is spreading. Second-round rookie, minutes still shifting, and he is already forcing the issue. Nineteen in Philadelphia, perfect shooting in Denver, double-doubles and blocks when the starters need air. He said the role is something he has taken, not something handed. Coach gave him the chance. He is making it his. That is how you build a closer's mentality when you have never played an NBA game before this season.

The soft read says this is still just young legs and easy early tests. The soft read is wrong. The Warriors used to call this building home. They left. We came back on their floor from fifteen down and took the win. That is not schedule luck. That is the city demanding its due and the roster answering. The hard tests will come. We are not waiting on them to decide who we are. We are already finishing games.
