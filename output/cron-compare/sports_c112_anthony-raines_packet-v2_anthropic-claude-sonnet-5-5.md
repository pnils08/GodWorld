# Aitken's Line Is Earned, Clark's Is Not Yet Tested

That is the second-best rate among his 11 seasons on the card, behind only the one every 16.3 at-bats of his 637-at-bat 2034. ## What the sample can carry

The Is-It-Real test is about which signals need volume. A batting average over 64 at-bats rests on 19 hits, and a handful of hits either way moves it a lot. The slugging figure is no firmer, because it rests on three home runs. The stable signals in his line are the ones that barely register in a sample this small. The card prints one strikeout every 21.3 at-bats, three strikeouts in all, and one walk. The average is the figure that gets quoted, but the strikeout and walk columns are the ones that will show whether the process holds. At 64 at-bats, three strikeouts and one walk are a description, not a trend.

Nothing on the supplied record says who pitched to Clark across those 18 games, or whether the at-bats came in one stretch. That cannot be assumed either way, and this piece does not assume it. The feed also holds no defensive line for him at third. The case for Clark rests on a hitting line over a small sample, and the record does not support more than that.

## The harder question is Aitken's

Before asking whether Clark can replace Aitken, it helps to ask what Aitken is. His 2041 line is a different hitter from 2040. The 2040 card shows 550 at-bats, 30 home runs, a .260 average, 64 walks and 110 strikeouts. The 2041 card shows 33 home runs, a .283 average, 25 walks and 65 strikeouts. His strikeout rate on the card went from one every 5.0 at-bats to one every 8.3. The walks fell by more than half, and the on-base percentage went from .342 to .317 even as the average rose.

Aitken made more contact and swung at more of what he saw. That produced a better average and a better slugging percentage but a worse on-base percentage. His 2038 season is the useful comparison. It had a .284 average with 78 walks, and the on-base percentage was .373. The averages nearly match, but the 2038 line reaches base far more often. So .283 in 2041 is not the same production as .284 in 2038. It is the power and the contact without the patience.

That makes the free-agency question less simple than a choice between veteran and youngster. The 2041 line is real in volume. With 540 at-bats it is the kind of sample the method trusts. But it is also the narrowest on-base profile on his card among his full seasons. Whether the new contact rate is a durable change or a one-year swing in approach is the thing his 11 seasons cannot yet answer. His 2041 strikeout rate is the best of any 500-plus at-bat season on the card. That could be a repeatable adjustment. It could also be one season on the high end of his own range.

On the home-run rate alone, Clark's one every 21.3 at-bats trails Aitken's one every 16.4. The 12 RBI in 64 at-bats sit beside Aitken's 87 in 540, and the record gives no basis for treating that as a projection.

## What would settle it

The front office's reading comes down to one question. Is the .297 a promise, or are the 64 at-bats a warning? The record supports neither answer yet. It would take another full season of plate appearances for Clark, with his strikeout and walk columns scaled up, to say whether the line is architecture. For Aitken, it would take a second season at the 2041 contact rate to show whether the 25 walks were a trade-off he chose or a loss he cannot get back. Until then, the cleaner fact is that one of these lines has 540 at-bats behind it and the other has 64.
