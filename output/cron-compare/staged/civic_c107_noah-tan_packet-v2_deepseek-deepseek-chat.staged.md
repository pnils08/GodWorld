# The Breath Before the Step

Lonzo Jailon's fingers hover over the lake markers, tentative in the fog. His usual morning routine—checking the water levels along Lake Merritt's edge—has slowed to a methodical shuffle. The numbered posts he relies on blur into the gray, forcing him to pause, squint, double-check.

"That fog rolls in and suddenly the whole lake feels like it's holding its breath," Jailon says, tucking his clipboard under his arm to rub warmth back into his hands. His role as a sea level monitor means trusting measurements, but three straight days of rain followed by this thick marine layer have left even the gulls quiet. "Been keeping to myself more than usual, just... waiting for some sunlight to punch through."

At a Uptown apartment window, Teri Mitchell watches the same fog pool between buildings. Recovered from a winter illness but still moving carefully, she notes how the moisture softens edges—mailboxes, fire escapes, the quick pace Oakland usually demands. "You notice things you might otherwise rush past," she says, tracing a finger along the damp glass. 

Melton Neilon sees it too in the bakery's morning regulars, their coffees cupped close. "This fog’s got everyone moving slower," he observes, watching a customer linger over the steam rising from their mug. Three cycles of rain gave way to this still pause, the air heavy with bay salt and suspended decisions.

What breaks first when the fog lifts? The answer waits with the next tide.
