# The Page of Lives, C114: A Door of One's Own, Twice

**Elle Lilo** moved into a place of their own in Jack London this cycle, renting. The Household Ledger holds the move at C114. It does not hold the building, the floor, or the day.

The record had a mover's truck a cycle earlier. At C113 it logged one idling outside a newly renovated building near a bar patio. It does not say whose truck that was, so this page does not say either.

The ledger describes Elle as a Harbor Tugboat Captain, retired, born in 1973, with Jack London as their neighborhood. At C114 the record gave them "renewed social energy." It also says they lent a neighbor a tool and "got a story back with it," which is how a first week on a new block tends to go. They closed their eyes during a choir solo and felt the tension leave their shoulders. The record gave that same moment to Clarence Moody on the same cycle. It does not say whether they shared a room.

Two lines on Elle's page don't agree. The Tribune named Elle in its sick-residents coverage at C112 and again at C113, in "Six Names for 142 Sick Residents" and "Chinatown's 140 Sick Are Mostly at Home, and Home Is Doing the Work." Elle's own lines in those cycles are quieter. At C112 they cooked healthier meals all week. At C113 they finally shook a cold that had been hanging around. The record does not connect the two, and neither does this page.

One more line from C114: Elle read the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline "and wondered who was actually going to get those spots." They had a key in their pocket that week and a question about someone else's door.

**Cee Lee**, an Autonomous Systems Engineer, also moved into a place of their own at C114, renting, in Lake Merritt. Cee Lee talked about Elle's move on the lake path.

"I've seen Elle Lilo move into a place of their own in Jack London," Cee Lee said. "It made me think about when I finally got my own place in Lake Merritt. It's a big step, you know? I was dealing with some health stuff around that time too, so I feel for them. I'm just glad I could get back on track and enjoy the lake path again."

The health stuff is on the record. At C113 Cee Lee "dealt with a seasonal health concern" and tried a new recipe that mostly worked. The Tribune named Cee Lee in the same C113 pieces it named Elle in. The dates are the part that doesn't line up. The record has Cee Lee unpacking a box "that had stayed sealed since the last move" at C112, two cycles before the lease was logged. It does not say where that box was opened. At C114 Cee Lee recognized the OARI van idling outside the corner store "and knew better than to ask who they were waiting for."

**Amear Thompson**, a Jade Jewelry Artisan, retired and self-employed, moved from Chinatown to West Oakland at C114. The record calls it moving up. The household is two people, and the record names only Amear.

The line just before the move is a plain one. Amear "ran the numbers on a place in Chinatown and this time they did not work." After that came the move. At C114 Amear smelled the incense from the steps of Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple and watched a stranger running for a bus make it, "and felt weirdly invested." One cycle earlier, at C113, Amear took the long way home to check the Baylight fence line's progress. The record does not say which way home it is now.

**Aanya Rivera**, a Paramedic in Lake Merritt, won on the UNDOCKED book at C114. They staked 200 and were paid 366. The record keeps nothing else about the bet.

Around the win, the record kept small things. At C113 Aanya counted cranes on the skyline from the bus stop and got a different number than the day before. They also read the Apprenticeship Pipeline flyer and wondered who would get those spots. At C114 they checked the Civis Systems Field livestream to see whether the grass had been laid down yet. Then, passing a shop window, they caught their reflection and stood a little straighter. The record does not say what they did with the 166.

**Pamela Amelia** lives on Piedmont Ave. At C114 she read the Tribune's front page twice, once for the news and once to see who was in it. The city turn that cycle put initiative work in six neighborhoods: Downtown, Temescal, West Oakland, Fruitvale, Jack London and East Oakland. At C113 she carried three boxes of canned goods off the pantry truck and felt it in her lower back. She also walked a different street home just to see it. The record does not say whether she found herself in the paper.

**Jose Johnson** was in Temescal at C114. A group of fans huddled under an awning, arguing about the night's game over the sound of rain on the pavement. Near a small gallery, the crack of a bat came from a nearby screen and a few pedestrians stopped to watch. Jose observed art enthusiasts wandering the streets. At C113 a stranger hummed Jose's song, and Jose "felt briefly, wrongly, known." The ledger does not say what the song is or what Jose does. The record put the Temescal Community Health Center in operation that cycle, and Temescal feels that.

**Shawn Neighbor**, in Fruitvale, "felt quietly lucky to call Fruitvale home" at C114. At C113 Shawn walked the Mandela Parkway median under the trees and took the long way home. Shawn also haggled over a flat of bruised peaches at the Saturday market "just to have a reason to talk to someone," and lent a neighbor a tool and got a story back, the same line Elle's page has a cycle later. At C114 the record has Shawn knowing the rules cold: "two nights in the chair, the galaxy keeps the score, the board adds it up." Shawn had never been drawn. The Tribune named Shawn at C98 in "Four Years to the Floor: Apprenticeship Pipeline Passes 9-0 — and What It Binds."

**Billy Storip**, a Hall of Famer from the old basketball league MUBA, lives in Rockridge. At C114 he called an old coworker just to catch up and talked for an hour. He found a quiet minute at a bookstore corner and a favorite song on the jukebox. He attended a community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II and "actually felt heard." At C113 he fixed a hinge that had been squeaking since the kids left home. The ledger calls him retired but also keeps coworkers on his page. This page prints both lines as the record gave them. Lives wrote him once before, at C113.

**Clarence Moody**, a retired machinist in Dimond, swapped seed packets with a neighbor over the fence at C114. That same cycle he set up the chairs in the fellowship hall "because if they didn't do it, no one would." The record has that line for him twice, at C110 and again now. During the choir solo he closed his eyes and felt the tension leave his shoulders. At C113 he shared a small piece of history with someone who needed it. He also heard that Jack London had been packed the night before, though the record does not say what the crowd was for. Lives wrote him at C113 in "A Place of Their Own." The Tribune first named him at C87, in "In Montclair, the Night of Krishna's Birth."

**Gloria Liu**, in Rockridge near College Avenue, reorganized the garage at C114 into a system only he understood. He also wrote a letter by hand "because email felt like cheating." At C113 he read the Apprenticeship Pipeline flyer and wondered who would get the spots, the same question Elle and Aanya carried. He also found a quiet bench near the pergola after dodging the running groups at the lake. The Tribune named him at C108 in "Rockridge Schools Maintain Excellence Amid Citywide Shifts." The record does not say who the letter was to.
