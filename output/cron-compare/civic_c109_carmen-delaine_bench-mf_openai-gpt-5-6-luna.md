# West Oakland Fund Moves, but Its Reach Is Still Defined by the Charter

In West Oakland, $400,000 moved in the month-eight tranche, carrying two household grants worth $81,188. The payment is the visible action in Cycle 109 for a fund designed not as hood-wide relief, but as stabilization grants for long-tenure West Oakland residents priced out as the flats built out.

That distinction is the fund’s governing fact. It narrows the question from whether money has moved to whom the money is meant to hold in place. The West Oakland Stabilization Fund is in its disbursement-active phase, with $23.4 million remaining from its $28 million budget. Its next scheduled checkpoint is the month-nine disbursement review in Cycle 110.

The dollars arrive after a long authorization trail. Avery Santana proposed the fund, and the mayoral action was signed in Cycle 78. The initiative passed that cycle on a 6-3 vote; Ramon Vega voted yes, Leonard Tran voted no, and Elliott Crane’s yes was recorded remotely. The approval opened the account. It did not, by itself, settle what stabilization would look like on the ground.

By Cycle 108, the charter’s scope had been recorded: the remnant named in the policy was the group of long-tenure residents facing displacement as the flats built out. At that point, 47 checks totaling approximately $4.2 million had been disbursed under Deputy Mayor Okoro’s administration. In Cycle 109, the fund added its month-eight tranche, releasing $400,000 in West Oakland while identifying two household grants worth $81,188.

The ledger therefore shows two things at once. The fund is no longer an approved promise waiting for an opening payment. It is sending money. But the current movement is also bounded by a charter that does not claim to stabilize every household or every pressure in the neighborhood. The distinction matters because a broad public label can make a targeted program appear broader than its operating terms.

Santana remains the mayor associated with the proposal, with an approval rating of 69 in the Civic Office Ledger. The council action that established the fund is now several cycles behind the payment schedule, while the next review is one cycle ahead. That is the accountability clock: not whether the initiative passed, but whether each review makes the disbursement pattern legible against the population the charter named.

The latest tranche supplies a payment, a grant count, and a remaining balance. It does not supply a public accounting of the households still outside the fund’s reach, nor does it answer whether the targeted grants are producing stabilization beyond the checks themselves. Those are not facts to fill in with assumption. They are the questions the next review must face.

For now, West Oakland has a fund in motion, a defined remnant to protect, and most of its budget still unspent. Cycle 110 will show whether the month-nine review deepens that accounting—or leaves the foundation visible only as a balance and a boundary.
