## The Geometry of Grit: Decoding the Oaks' Draft Board

The first seven picks of the Oakland Oaks' inaugural expansion draft did not look like the birth of an entertainment product. They looked like the blueprints for a barricade. 

While the city screamed for a franchise savior to put on posters outside the new Jack London Square practice facility, Head of Basketball Operations Mike Paulson executed a cold, mathematically ruthless strategy. He drafted Wendell Carter Jr., Donte DiVincenzo, Herbert Jones, Draymond Green, Max Strus, Jose Alvarado, and Dorian Finney-Smith. 

If you look at raw scoring averages, you panic. If you look at the geometry of Wilson Shepard's offensive motion system and the "99 Hustle" metric, you realize Paulson is building a switchable, defensively suffocating machine designed to exhaust opponents before the fourth quarter even begins.

But the masterstroke wasn't the defense. It was the leverage.

Paulson knew the Los Angeles Lakers were desperate to extend their fading championship window with win-now perimeter defense. He didn't draft Herbert Jones to play in Oakland; he drafted him to hold Los Angeles hostage. Minutes after making the pick, Paulson traded Jones, the Oaks' upcoming #5 overall entry pick, and a future first-round pick to the Lakers for the #1 overall pick in the upcoming entry draft.

And he did it all while preserving maximum cap space for free agency.

For weeks, the rumor mill assumed Paulson would trade massive draft capital to Chicago to acquire Adash Stanley—the 19-year-old point guard who won 6th Man of the Year as an undrafted rookie last season while playing under Paulson's previous front office regime. But Paulson understood the contract structure: Stanley is an unrestricted free agent. You don't have to draft him. You just have to write him a check. 

By drafting Jose Alvarado as a point-of-attack disruptor, Paulson secured his defensive baseline at the guard spot. By preserving cap space, he can now aggressively pursue Stanley *and* a veteran scorer in free agency. And by executing the Lakers trade, he now holds the #1 overall pick to draft a true, blue-chip difference-maker to tie it all together.

Elias Varek promised the city a competitive franchise. Paulson is delivering a fortress, and he just bought the artillery.
