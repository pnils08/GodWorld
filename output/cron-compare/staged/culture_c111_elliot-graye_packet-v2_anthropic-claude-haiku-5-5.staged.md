# The Room Stays Open: Faith on Lake Merritt This Cycle

Kenji Tanaka was asked what brought people through the doors of the Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple in Chinatown, and he did not answer quickly. He paused, then said it plainly: "I'm not sure. I know the Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple is a place of peace for our community, but I haven't seen their crisis response efforts in action myself, so I can't say what specifically brought those people in."

The record shows that on C109 the temple logged a crisis response in which its congregation opened its doors for community support, with 41 people in attendance. The count is the whole of what the record holds about that evening. It does not say who asked for the doors to open, what was needed inside them, or how the 41 came to be there. Tanaka, a faith leader in Chinatown, is the one voice in this cycle's record who speaks to that building directly, and he is careful not to speak past what he has seen.

Two cycles earlier, across the lake's western edge and downtown, the Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship recorded its own crisis response. On C107 the congregation opened its doors for community support, with 72 people in attendance. Eunice Marston, a senior pastor downtown, put the two weeks of work in the same breath:

"It is a balance we have to maintain. We had those 72 people come to us for crisis support, and then we had 237 for Epiphany. As a faith leader, you just keep your focus on the people and their needs, whether that means providing emergency aid or coming together for a holy day. It's all part of the same work."

Her account is the closest the record comes to showing what a single congregation holds at once. On C109 the Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship's Epiphany observance drew 237. Earlier in the cycle, its doors had opened for people who came in need. Marston does not separate those two things, and the ledger, which tallies them in separate columns, does not force her to.

Across Lake Merritt, the Cathedral of the Living Word, a Catholic congregation, recorded the largest Epiphany counts in this cycle's faith ledger: 1,185 on C107, 1,077 at traditional Epiphany rituals on C108, and 1,265 on C109. St. Esperanza Parish in Fruitvale recorded 427 at traditional Epiphany rituals on C107 and 374 at its congregation's Epiphany celebration on C108. These are the holy-day counts. The record does not say how many of those who came for Epiphany also came for crisis support, and it does not connect the two sets of numbers.

It is in this setting that the Lakeshore Unitarian Universalist Fellowship comes into focus. The Fellowship is a Unitarian congregation on Lake Merritt, founded 153 years ago, with a congregation of 350. It is led today by Rev. Eleanor Bishop. The record names its work as interfaith dialogue and a humanist welcome. Across the 8 faith events on the record since C105, the Fellowship is the Lake Merritt institution the faith ledger returns to most often, with 6 of those events named.

Manny Ellen, who works for the Elections Office in Lake Merritt, has become part of that Fellowship's life. He described one afternoon in his own words:

"I've joined the congregation there. I was just over at the church recently when the lunch program was wrapping up; I saw the volunteers stacking chairs while some of the seniors stayed behind to finish their coffee."

Ellen's description is an observation of a room at the end of a program, not an account of what the program did or who ran it. The chairs were stacked, the coffee was still being drunk, and the seniors stayed. Whether the people who stayed to talk were members of the congregation, guests, or neighbors, the record does not say.

The Fellowship's own record gives the shape of the place: a congregation of 350, an interfaith dialogue, a humanist welcome. It does not give the names of the volunteers Ellen saw stacking chairs, the date of that lunch program, or any count for it. What the room was like on a weeknight, who set up, and who stayed late are not on the record.

The question the record leaves open is the one the faith ledger itself raises. The crisis-response doors opened at Bay Pure Land on C109 and at Telegraph Presbyterian on C107. The Epiphany counts swelled across the lake on C107 through C109. Is the surge in crisis-response attendance a temporary spike, or a shift in how these congregations are viewed by the neighborhood? Tanaka, who has not seen the crisis work himself, cannot say. Marston says it is one work. Bishop has not yet put her answer on the record. Until someone who sat in those rooms says what changed, the count of who walked in is all the city has.
