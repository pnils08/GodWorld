# The Lives of C114

**Tyler Filet** was awake when the wind got to the window frame by the front porch, in the first weeks of the place in Lake Merritt that is theirs alone. The household ledger has Filet, the Biotech Lab Director at Portside Bio, moving in this Cycle as a renter. The record keeps the night as its own line: Filet couldn't sleep for the rattling, and wondered whether a decision from years back had been the right one.

"I moved into my place in Lake Merritt and it's been a real adjustment," Filet said. "The other night, the wind was rattling the window frame near the front porch and I couldn't sleep. It got me thinking about some of the decisions I've made, wondering if they were the right ones. I've been seeing some familiar faces around the neighborhood, like Vinnie Keane at Grand Avenue Wine Bar. It's nice to have some continuity."

The earlier lines are small ones. In C109 Filet carried three boxes of canned goods from the pantry truck along the lakeside path and felt it in the lower back. That same Cycle they drank the free cold brew in the company kitchen and felt disconnected from the neighborhood outside. In C111 they wondered when exactly they had become the adult in the room. In C113 they read the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline and wondered who would get the spots, and a stranger hummed their song and they felt, briefly and wrongly, known. The wind came in C114. The record does not say whether the decision from years back was ever named, and it holds nothing yet on the sleep.

**Derek Obi** sold the house in Downtown this Cycle and now rents in Rockridge. The record labels it moving up, and the record's own line on the sale reads "renting in Rockridge until buying there is in reach," with $161,251 in the bank. Both are true on the page, and they do not sit easily together: a house owned became a place rented, and the ledger calls it a step up. The Tribune has named Obi before: in the Arts pages of C70, and in the Business piece "Uptown's Two Speeds" in C112. In C113 Obi felt a lift at Miso Metro after the win, then stayed thirty minutes after the service catching up in the parking lot. The record names no date for the next purchase.

**Graciela Foster** moved from Uptown to East Oakland in C114. The record gives the reason in its own words: a better fit between household income and housing costs. It was not a sudden choice. In C113 the line was that the for-rent signs outnumbered the welcome mats, and in the same Cycle Foster bought a new A's jersey. Just before the move, Foster checked out the First Friday galleries and caught a coffee order coming out of their own mouth before deciding to order it, by a venue doorway. Foster is also one of four people the Cycle's safety turn reached, the turn marked by an accident on the job floor and a stretcher out the loading door. The record does not say what reached Foster or whether Foster was hurt, and this page will not fill that in. Foster works as an IT support technician.

**Patrice Rao** moved from Chinatown to West Oakland this Cycle, a move the record calls moving up. The line beside it is the one a household might have kept: Rao wondered when exactly they became the adult in the room. Rao also discovered new local artists. The record holds one more thing for C114: Rao knew the rules cold, two nights in the chair, the galaxy keeps the score, the board adds it up, and had never been drawn. It gives no more than that.

**Kenji Okafor**, a patent attorney, moved into a place of their own in Rockridge this Cycle, renting. Okafor says the move has settled well. "I've been feeling pretty settled in Rockridge," Okafor said. "I attended a community feedback session for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II and actually felt heard, which was a positive experience. It's nice to be involved in the community." The record puts that session near a bookstore corner. It also has Okafor reorganizing a closet this Cycle and finding something thought lost, then catching the Oaks chatter at the gym and joining in like a regular. Earlier lines in the thread: a $5,200 windfall, banked, in C109, and in C113 the stroller traffic on College Avenue and a silent judgment of the parking jobs. The record does not say what was found in the closet.

**Jett Walker**, a plumber in Rockridge, was reached by the stalled train there last week, one of four people the Cycle's civic turn touched. The record has Walker rewinding the belt haul on the walk home and smiling at nothing in particular. Walker's own telling is plainer. "To be honest, the BART train disruption was just a minor frustration for me. I've gotten used to it around here. I rewound the belt haul on my walk home and didn't think much of it." The record has the smile and the quote has the shrug, and the page keeps both. In C114 Walker kept a spare trap gasket in every pocket and used one before lunch. At First Friday, Walker discovered new local artists. Lives wrote Walker once before, in C113, and the Tribune named Walker that Cycle in the Business piece "Temescal's Ledger Is Growing. The Question Is Who It's Growing For." In C113 Walker also spent the Cycle playing to climb, the house first, watched an old man feed pigeons and wondered what Walker's own ritual was, and made a real friend of Jenna Comer.

**Tobert Lewis**, the civil rights attorney, badged into the Baylight office early in C114 and tried not to think about last month's protestors. Downtown is where the Cycle's safety turn reached Lewis, with the transit snarl as its name. One line of the record says Downtown is thriving, with events drawing crowds and new faces; the other says Downtown is under strain. Both are in the ledger, and neither is Lewis's word. Lewis's word is about the flyer. "I read the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline and honestly, I wondered who was actually going to get those spots," Lewis said. "It's great they're offering opportunities, but I've seen programs like this before where the benefits don't always reach the kids who need them most." In C113 Lewis cleared the last debt, and the ledger finally reads clean.

**Jerome Lindqvist** graduated with their class this Cycle, in a joyful spring celebration in Fruitvale. In the same Cycle the record has Lindqvist watching the morning shuttles roll along the waterfront into the Baylight District and timing their own walk to beat them. At a kitchen table near a vendor by the transit stop, Lindqvist kept score by hand, the way their father taught them. In C113 Lindqvist read the flyer on the Fruitvale Transit Hub delays and shook their head at the timeline. The C114 record says Lindqvist enjoyed Fruitvale's community atmosphere.

**Priscilla Chang** walked across the stage at their commencement ceremony in Fruitvale this Cycle. In C113 Chang saved the Tribune clipping with their neighborhood in it, the good one. The record for C114 holds one more line: Chang ran the numbers on staying put another year. It does not say what the numbers came to.
