# Piedmont Ave, in their own words

## Marguerite Salcedo — Managing Partner, Anchor Build

> Look, I run the numbers every day. Anchor Build is growing — 13.7%, 181 people on the payroll. That's real. But I'd be lying if I said I don't see the squeeze. My crews — the electricians, the laborers, the folks swinging hammers — they're the ones building these new units, and half of them can't afford to live in the buildings they're putting up. That's not a talking point, that's what I hear at the job site. I've got guys driving in from Antioch, from Stockton, because Jack London rent went up again. We're not pricing out strangers — we're pricing out the people who pour the concrete. And that keeps me up at night, because I've been in this business long enough to know you can't build a neighborhood on the backs of people who can't stay in it.

## Marguerite Kimball-Ostrow — Elementary Teacher

> I didn't really do anything special on Sunday. I just flew through the systems one by one, and it happened to come out even on credits. I wasn't really focusing on that; I was just trying to explore and see where the show would take me.
