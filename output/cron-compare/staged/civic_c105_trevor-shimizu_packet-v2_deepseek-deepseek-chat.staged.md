# Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II Slips Back Into Visioning Stage

At the corner of East 12th Street and 35th Avenue, where Fruitvale's existing transit hub merges buses and BART lines into a tangled daily ballet, Manjit Singh watched a group of surveyors pack up their tripods at 4:30 PM. They'd been measuring the same stretch of sidewalk all week—twice now in the past eighteen months. 

The Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II project, officially listed as "visioning-complete" in city tracking systems as recently as last month, has quietly reverted to "active visioning" status—an administrative backslide without explanation or announced timeline adjustment. Public records show no halted funding or cancelled contracts, just a procedural regression in a project originally slated to begin construction by late next year.

Nina Reyes, who commutes through the existing hub three times a week, leaned against a concrete planter near the BART escalators. "I heard they were making progress on the transit hub, but now it sounds like they're back to square one. That's Oakland for you—always planning, never doing."

The Phase II expansion was designed to add three new bus boarding islands, a covered bike transit center, and pedestrian connectors to address chronic crowding at the existing transfer points. Early renderings suggested the redesign could cut transfer times—a critical buffer in a hub where late buses frequently strand riders missing their connections.

Two infrastructure patterns emerge here without city confirmation: First, this marks at least the third visioning reset for a major Oakland transit project in the past five years. Second, every reset has occurred during the winter months—a season when underground utility mapping becomes more difficult but also when fewer public meetings are scheduled to question delays.

"I heard about the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II moving back to active visioning, and it feels like we're stuck in a loop," Singh said. "I just hope they figure it out soon—it's been a long wait for something that could really help our community."

Neither AC Transit nor BART officials would confirm whether new engineering challenges prompted the regression or if earlier designs failed to accommodate known utility conflicts. The greater risk may be precedent: Projects that slip from visioning-complete back into active visioning tend to face extended delays before reaching construction.

At the survey site, a worker loading equipment into a van declined to comment on their current measurements. Rain began dotting their blueprint tubes—a winter detail irrelevant to schedules but palpable in the delaying of things.
