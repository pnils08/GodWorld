**Temescal Initiative Stalls at the Usual Step**  

Melton Neilon stood at the corner of 49th and Telegraph, hands in pockets, watching traffic inch past the same pothole that had been there since summer. The civic initiative meant to address it—and dozens of other neighborhood fixes—hadn’t moved forward in over a month.  

*"I’ve noticed that a lot of folks here in Temescal care deeply about our neighborhood,"* Neilon said, *"but it feels like we’re all waiting for someone else to take the lead—maybe that’s why things stall."*  

The pattern isn’t unique to Temescal. Across Oakland, neighborhood-level projects—street repairs, crosswalk upgrades, even basic traffic calming—tend to stall at the same stage: after community input but before concrete action. The engine audit shows no movement in a full cycle, though no formal halt or rejection has been issued.  

Without a clear next step—or a named office responsible for pushing stalled items forward—the delay risks becoming permanent. The question isn’t just about this corner or this pothole. It’s about what happens when a city’s system for handling local concerns defaults to waiting.
