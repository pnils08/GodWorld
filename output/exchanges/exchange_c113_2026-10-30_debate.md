# Exchange — debate (Cycle 113)

- participants: POP-00143 Clarissa Dane | POP-00009 Lena Carrow
- topic: Downtown Economic Revitalization (INIT-008) — vote at cycle 114 (initiative)

---

**Clarissa Dane:** Look, Lena, I know we've had our disagreements on how to fund community programs, but Downtown Revitalization isn't just about facades—it's about safety and foot traffic that makes our streets livable after dark. Every shuttered storefront is a corner where folks hesitate to walk their kids home. I've seen the case files from this district; empty blocks attract the wrong kind of attention, and this initiative puts real eyes on the street.

**Lena Carrow:** I hear you, Clarissa, and I don't think anyone's arguing against the goal of safety. But when I walk my kids past those boarded-up windows on 14th, the last thing I'm thinking about is whether the facade gets a fresh coat of paint—I'm thinking about whether the families who've been pushed out can even afford to come back once the rents climb. More foot traffic sounds good until it's priced out the people who've kept this neighborhood alive through the rough patches.

**Clarissa Dane:** That's a real tension, and I won't pretend it's easy. But the families I've watched scrape by in West Oakland didn't leave because of a fresh coat of paint—they left when the jobs vanished and the corners got dark. We need the foot traffic to make those storefronts viable again, and that's what brings jobs and services back within walking distance for the folks who never left. The revitalization fund includes a clause for workforce training and small-business support, so it's not just money to polish windows—it's money to keep the neighborhood's own people in on the ground floor.

**Lena Carrow:** You're right that jobs are the backbone—I've watched too many neighbors pack up because there was nothing left to hold onto here. And I'll give you this: if the training programs are real and not just a line on a grant application, that changes the math for me. But I've seen initiatives like this before, and the small-business support always seems to favor the folks with connections, not the ones who've been running their shops through the hard years. If you can show me how this keeps the corner taqueria and the family repair shop in the picture, not just the new coffee spot, then I'll stand with you on this.
