Brian Adams stayed close to home in Jack London this cycle, the winter mood pressing on him until he kept his own company more than usual. 

"The winter mood's been weighing on me, making me want to stay in more," he said. "It's like a fog that doesn't lift, you know? I've been keeping to myself, not going out as much as I usually do. My wife and kids are used to it by now; they know when I'm like this, it's best to just let me be."

Joseph Cramp noticed the change while he moved through Downtown. The sidewalks carried more people than they had in years, and the air felt different after the day's events.

"I felt the social atmosphere shift today, it's like the whole town is buzzing now," Cramp said. "I've been around Downtown a lot, and it's been dead for years, but now it's like people are finally coming out. I saw a choir performance at Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship that drew a small crowd, and there were volunteers gathering near City Hall. Even the sidewalks feel busier than ever with people spilling out of Midnight Bistro and SpeedyBurger. It's a good vibe."

Jorry Cula, who runs the record shop in Uptown, came home to his child and found the rooms steadier than the rest of the day had been.

"To be honest, I've been running on fumes lately, and it's been a strain," Cula said. "But when I'm home with my kid, things feel more grounded. I think that's what made the atmosphere feel positive inside the home. We were just being together, you know? No big drama, just us hanging out. It's those moments that make all the stress worth it."
