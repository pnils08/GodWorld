# Deputy Mayor's Weekly Report — Economic Development, Cycle 116

Let me say what I see, and I'll be blunt about it because the numbers deserve it.

**The headline nobody's going to like: we have 10 businesses city-wide carrying 13,675 jobs. That's it. Ten.** I don't care how you slice it—that concentration is the whole story. One bad quarter at any one of those and we're not talking about a rough week, we're talking about a cliff. Downtown alone has 22 establishments but 6,819 staff, and Baylight's 12 businesses hold 2,804 at a median pay of $136,839. Meanwhile my Chinatown has **11 businesses and 118 staff at $46,000 median**—right on the bottom of the pile with East Oakland, San Antonio and Laurel. Folks in Chinatown aren't just struggling in the abstract. The numbers say it plain.

**Unemployment, and I want this read out loud:** Temescal leads at 166 out of work. Hard on its heels: San Antonio 105, Dimond 98, Glenview 97, Eastlake 92, Ivy Hill 90, East Oakland 87, Laurel 86, **Chinatown 83.** Those are names, not statistics. Four hundred, five hundred people across the flatlands and the hills who aren't bringing home a paycheck, and the retail vitality in the very neighborhoods where they live—San Antonio 4.84, Dimond 6.51, Glenview 6.15, Ivy Hill 5.54—is the weakest on the map. The places where people most need work are the places where the storefronts are thinnest. That's not a coincidence, and I won't pretend it is.

**Downtown, and I mean this:** I said before that thirteen people making headlines is thirteen too many. Downtown's got 56 out of work and the lowest boom feeling on the institutional side of things, and my predecessor's plans didn't fix it. Our economic development strategy has to help the people on the ground, not the loudest voices. I'll keep saying it until the budget reflects it.

**What moved this Cycle:** Baylight lost 8 on a carryover hit, Ivy Hill lost 8 two cycles running, and Adams Point United Methodist lost 2 employees—a services/retail sector impact of 8. Small potatoes against 13,675 jobs, sure. But Ivy Hill's already at 90 out of work and 5.54 vitality, so two cycles of bleed in a row is not nothing.

**The one bit of good news, and I'll take it:** No business closures on the ledger this Cycle. None. Building inspector passed BIZ-00174. One mail-truck insurance premium hike on BIZ-00092, which is friction I'll want watched, but it's a hike, not a shuttered door. I'd rather report a flatline than a funeral.

That's the report. It's not pretty, but it's honest—and I'd rather be rattled and tell you the truth than smooth it over.
