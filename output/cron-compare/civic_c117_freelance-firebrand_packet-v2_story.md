Three neighborhoods are sliding—Fruitvale, Grand Lake, East Oakland—and City Hall has nothing aimed at any of them. ## §3 INTERVIEWS (wake 2 — real citizens, real quotes)
- Rose Delgado (POP-00503): "Jax. Good to see you. You want to know who owns the fix? That’s me. That’s this office. District 3, Fruitvale and San Antonio — that’s my desk, that’s my neighborhood, and I don’t get to pass the buck when the numbers go sideways. The sentiment decay you’re reading about? I’ve been watching it too, and it’s not some abstract index to me. It’s the shopkeeper on International who’s been holding on by her fingernails. It’s the family who’s been priced out of a two-bedroom and is now sleeping on a cousin’s couch in San Antonio. Housing pressure — nobody in this building owns that alone, and I won’t pretend otherwise. But what I *do* own is the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II. That’s the lever I’ve been pulling. That’s $230 million of design and site work that’s supposed to bring real jobs, real foot traffic, real stability — not just another rendering in a storefront window. The community review is set for the first Thursday of next month, and the design team will be in the room, because I am not letting construction outrun the people’s say. So if you’re asking who owns the fix — it’s me. And I’m asking the city to back it up. If that vote goes sideways, you’re not gonna find me hiding behind a staff report. You’ll find me on International, taking the questions. That’s the job."
- Avery Santana (POP-00034): "Jax — you know I don’t duck questions, so let me give it to you straight. You’re right to push. And I’m not going to sit here and tell you that every street in this city gets the same attention at the same time. That would be a lie, and you’d smell it from a mile away. But here’s what I’ll say: the noise isn’t what moves me. It’s the data, and it’s the people who show up with receipts. Fruitvale — I was down there last month with the OARI team, not for a press conference, just to watch them work a corner. East Oakland — I know exactly where the potholes are on MacArthur because I drive them. Grand Lake — that’s not sliding, that’s creeping, and it’s just as unacceptable. The problem isn’t that we only respond to noise. The problem is that the system is built to respond to noise — and I’ve spent the last two years trying to rewire it so that we respond to *need*. That means community boards that report to me directly, not through twelve layers of city hall. It means funding formulas that don’t reward the loudest ZIP code. So no, I won’t pretend every neighborhood has gotten its fair shake. But I’ll also tell you this: the day I stop showing up in Fruitvale without a camera crew is the day you can call me out. Until then, keep asking. That’s your job. Mine is to make sure you don’t have to ask twice."
