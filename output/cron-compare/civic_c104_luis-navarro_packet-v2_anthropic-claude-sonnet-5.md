## The Corner That Stopped Moving

Elio Perez crosses the same intersection twice a day, coming and going from work in West Oakland. He says he stopped noticing the chain-link around the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II site months ago, the way you stop noticing a scar. It's just there now — the fencing, the idle equipment, the faded flags marking utility lines that no one has moved since he can remember.

"I've heard people talking about the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II being stalled, and honestly, it feels like no one's stepping up to take responsibility," Perez says. "It's frustrating because this project could really help our community, but it's just sitting there, waiting for someone to move it forward."

Nobody has told him it's stalled. He's just watched it not change.

Across town in Rockridge, Depak Ying hears the same complaint from a different set of mouths. Ying works the floor, close enough to the register to catch what regulars say between orders. The Fruitvale project comes up often enough that he can recite the shape of the frustration without needing to think about it.

"I hear people at the diner grumbling about the Fruitvale project all the time — everyone wants to know who dropped the ball, but nobody steps up to take responsibility," Ying says. "Feels like the city keeps spinning its wheels while we wait."

Two servers, two neighborhoods, the same unanswered question passed across two different counters. That's what we have. What we don't have is a name attached to the silence — no office identified as the one holding the file, no statement, no denial, no acknowledgment that there is a file at all. Nothing in the record this cycle shows a civic initiative addressing the signal Perez and Ying are both describing independently, on opposite sides of the city, without comparing notes.

That absence is itself worth sitting with. Not because it proves neglect — a stalled site can mean permitting, funding, engineering, a dozen ordinary bureaucratic reasons that never make it to a diner counter. But when the same complaint surfaces in West Oakland and in Rockridge in the same breath — *who dropped the ball* — it stops being one person's frustration and starts being a pattern with no name attached to it.

Perez and Ying aren't accusing anyone specific. They can't. Nobody has told them who's supposed to be answering. That's the gap: not a scandal, not a confirmed failure — just a project sitting visibly still, and a city's residents left to guess, on their own, who's supposed to be moving it.

What is still unfinished for the people living this? Right now, that's the only honest answer there is.
