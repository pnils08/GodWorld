# The Council That Moves Around

Adams Point United Methodist keeps its folding chairs stacked along the fellowship hall wall. After an evening there, the last person out is usually whoever notices the stack leaning. This time the lean was a small one. A tall stack of chairs says a room filled up, and last cycle a room did.

The Faith Ledger holds the row. In C115 the Interfaith Council held a prayer breakfast in Adams Point, and the row records 123 people attending. The council's earlier rows show a body that travels. In C78 it met Downtown, with 275 on the row. In C114 a dialogue series on shared concerns met in Chinatown, with 53. Then it came to the lake. The council has no building of its own in these rows. It borrows other people's rooms and leaves them with the dishes done.

Rev. Daniel Han leads Adams Point United Methodist. The registry lists a congregation of 300, a progressive, LGBTQ-affirming Methodist church, founded in 1892. The Tribune has been in the building before. In C94 Maria Keen found Han in the aisle on a Tuesday morning, watching teenagers carry a folding table toward the kitchen. That piece was about a church opening its doors. This one asks what the doors have connected to since.

Han does not claim more than the record supports. When I asked him about job training, which began new sessions this cycle at other congregations, he was exact about what his church does and doesn't do.

"I've seen firsthand the impact of job training programs in our community," he said. "While Adams Point United Methodist isn't directly running such a program, our involvement in the Interfaith Council has shown me the value of faith organizations coming together to support local initiatives. I recall the interfaith prayer breakfast we had last cycle, where leaders from various faith backgrounds gathered to discuss ways to serve the community. It's this kind of collaboration that can lead to meaningful support for job training and other vital programs."

The sentence is worth reading slowly. He says his own church isn't running the program. He puts the credit in the council and the rooms the council borrows. A pastor who says "we don't do that" and then says what he thinks the gathering made possible is describing a division of labor.

The Faith Ledger shows where that labor sits this cycle:

- **St. Esperanza Parish, Fruitvale.** A job training workshop began a new session, with 86 attending.
- **Or Hashalom Renewal Community, Grand Lake.** A job training workshop also began a new session, with 58.
- **New Covenant Pentecostal Assembly, West Oakland.** Its seniors lunch program marked a milestone, with 153.
- **Temple Beth Or, Rockridge.** A food pantry served neighbors, with 89.
- **B'nai Tikvah Synagogue, Piedmont Ave.** A food pantry also served neighbors, with 32.

In C115 the Cathedral of the Living Word in Lake Merritt expanded its after-school tutoring, with 250 on the row.

None of these rows says the prayer breakfast caused any of it, and I won't say so either. The ledger records that people gathered and that these programs ran. It does not record who left the breakfast and phoned whom.

The other pastors on the council see a link. Ramon Solano, who leads in Fruitvale, said it this way: "I've heard that Adams Point United Methodist is involved with the Interfaith Council, and that they had a prayer breakfast that brought leaders together. As a faith leader myself, I appreciate efforts to build bridges between different communities."

Robert Jaston, in West Oakland, was warmer. "I've seen Adams Point United Methodist participate in the Interfaith Council's prayer breakfast, bringing leaders together in Adams Point," he said. "It's heartening to see different faith communities come together like that."

Naomi Sterling, in Rockridge, put the date on it. "I've seen Adams Point United Methodist play a role in the Interfaith Council's efforts, particularly during their interfaith prayer breakfast in C115, which brought together leaders from various faith backgrounds," she said. "As a faith leader myself, I appreciate the work they do in fostering dialogue and cooperation among different communities."

All three describe seeing the church take part, and all three are generous about it. That is not the same as showing a program that started because of it. I should be honest that the ledger gives me the first and not the second.

The council's rows are 275, then 53, then 123, which is a lot of room to fill. The numbers swing, and the council does not stay put, so the question is who carries the dialogue between meetings. Nothing in the rows I pulled names a next meeting, a host, or a date. It is the Spring Equinox on the calendar, a threshold of the year, and a council that has met Downtown, in Chinatown and now by the lake has no stated address for the next one.

A chair-stacker at Adams Point United Methodist can say the room held 123. Nobody on the record can yet say where the chairs go next. The people who would know are the council's host churches, so the question belongs to them and to the pastors who have said they appreciate the bridge.
