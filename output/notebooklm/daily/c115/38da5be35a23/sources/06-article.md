By P Slayer, The Bay Tribune

Adash Stanley stood in the tunnel after the final horn and said the energy around the Oaks is electric, like the whole city is waking up with the team. I heard it on the replay at the counter where the old screen still hangs in KONO, the sound turned low enough that the room had to lean in. The Oaks had just put Orlando away early, 125-109, and the third straight win left them at 7-1. The place stayed loud long after the final score flashed.

Some will tell you this streak is still early noise, that the disrespect the city has been hearing is just the league doing what it always does to an expansion club. They are wrong. That line is a softer way of saying the same thing the front office used to sell patience last fall: wait and see. We waited. The record is not waiting anymore.

Dillon Brooks spoke next on the same feed. It is a great feeling to be contributing to the team’s winning streak, he said. We are all just trying to stay focused and keep pushing through, and it is awesome to see the community’s energy behind us. My wife loves seeing the wins too. The room at the counter answered him with the same low growl it used to give when the losses stacked up. The difference now is nobody is pretending the growl is only about one game.

Austin Reaves kept it plain. The team’s vibe is locked-in, he said. We have been playing well, and the energy in the room is good. We are on a three-game winning streak, and everyone is feeling confident. We are still figuring some things out, but we are clicking on a lot of levels right now. That last sentence is the one that used to get used against us. Now it lands like proof the figuring is finally pointed the right direction.

Harry Hayward, the rookie who played sixteen minutes and still came away with two steals and a block, talked about what he saw walking the neighborhood. I have seen more people around the neighborhood, and they seem excited, he said. The energy’s good. Retail’s been busy, and I have recognized a few faces from years ago. It is like everyone is feeling the win streak. That is not a boardroom talking point. That is a second-round kid noticing the same thing the rest of us have been waiting to feel since the first preseason loss.

The disrespect is still out there. It always will be until the wins stop being a surprise. But the stands are not answering with slogans. They are answering with the same three straight nights of staying past the final horn, the same low growl that used to mean frustration now meaning something else. The front office can call it whatever they want in the next press release. The city already answered.

We are not done waiting. We are just done pretending the wait is the story.
