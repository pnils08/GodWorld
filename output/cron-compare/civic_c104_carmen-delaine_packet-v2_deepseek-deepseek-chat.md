Elio Perez walks past the same empty storefronts on Seventh Street every morning on his way to the diner where he works the breakfast shift. The plywood sheets haven’t budged since last cycle, and the commercial vacancy signs still hang in the windows. "I’ve heard about the Stabilization Fund, and I hope it helps," he says, pulling his jacket tighter against the chill. "But I still see empty storefronts and people struggling to keep their businesses open. It feels like we need more than just money—we need real support and community action."

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund, now in its disbursement-active phase, was meant to address precisely this kind of decay—empty storefronts, struggling businesses, and the slow erosion of neighborhood vitality. But on the ground, the impact is harder to measure. Daniel Cloak, a bakery worker a few blocks over, echoes Perez’s sentiment. "I've heard about the Stabilization Fund at the bakery - some folks say it's helping, but I still see empty storefronts on my way to work every morning." 

At Queen’s Coffee, a small shop two blocks from the BART station, barista Irene Fay hasn’t noticed much change either. "I heard about the Stabilization Fund at work—some regulars were talking about how it's supposed to help small businesses, but honestly, I haven't noticed much change around here yet," she says, wiping down the counter. Darius Clark, another bakery worker, agrees. "I heard about that fund at the bakery - some folks say it's helping, but I still see empty storefronts on my block. Hard to tell if it's making a real difference yet."

The question hangs in the air: Is this stabilization fund hitting the right pressure points, or is it just keeping seats warm while West Oakland bleeds?
