**Simon Leary:** **"Patricia, what does a quiet morning in Temescal sound like to you?"**  

(Thoughtful, grounded, inviting her to paint the neighborhood’s texture — keys into the theme of stability beneath Oakland’s noise.)

**Patricia Nolan:** "Oh, it’s the freight trains first—that low rumble and clank near the port, steady as a heartbeat. Then the cafe grinders kick on at Temescal Alley, and you’ll hear someone’s wind chimes down the block if the breeze is right. It’s not silent, but it’s the kind of noise that lets you breathe."

**Simon Leary:** **"Which of those sounds reminds you most of the Temescal you knew thirty years ago?"**  

(Leaning into contrast/continuity — invites reflection on how the neighborhood’s essence persists beneath surface changes.)

**Patricia Nolan:** "The trains, absolutely. Those rhythms haven’t changed—still the same metallic groan and echo. The cafes might’ve swapped owners three times over, but those tracks? They’ve been scoring the neighborhood’s hours since before I retired. Some things just settle into the bones of a place."
