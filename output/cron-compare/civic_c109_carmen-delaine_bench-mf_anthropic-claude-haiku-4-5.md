# $23.4 Million Remains, Two Households Received Grants, and West Oakland Waits for Month Nine

The West Oakland Stabilization Fund sits at a crossroads it was never meant to reach. When Mayor Avery Santana signed the initiative into law in Cycle 78—a $28 million commitment passed by the council 6-3, with Ramon Vega and Elliott Crane voting yes and Leonard Tran voting no—the charter was narrow: stabilization grants for long-tenure West Oakland residents priced out as new construction climbed. Not hood-wide relief. Not a rush. Targeted money for residents the flats had already pushed toward the edge.

Thirty-one cycles later, the fund has released $4.6 million. Month eight of disbursement-active moved $400,000 out in West Oakland this cycle. Of that tranche, two household grants totaled $81,188. The remaining $23.4 million sits unspent, and the Month-Nine disbursement review lands in Cycle 110.

The math is not mysterious. In Cycle 108, when Deputy Mayor Okoro's office logged the scope as officially chartered, forty-seven checks had gone out to long-tenure residents—roughly $4.2 million of the budget anchored to a specific thing: people who had been named by the fund's own criteria and had already waited through construction, rent increases, and the slow erasure of the neighborhood they knew. Those forty-seven households were the remnant. The fund's design assumed more would follow. 

This cycle adds no new clarity to why the pace has slowed. The tracker records only that month eight released $400,000, that two grants went out, and that the next review is scheduled for Cycle 110. No explanation is logged. No named deputy, no office dispatch, no official statement appears in the Initiative_Tracker. The fund is not stalled—it is disbursement-active, moving in the register. But at two grants per month in the eighth month of operation, a $28 million fund reaches full dispersal sometime after Cycle 147. Residents priced out wait for their turn. The fund waits for Month Nine.

Who decides when to move more money? The tracker does not say. Who is authorized to account for the pace? That, too, is absent from the record. The West Oakland Stabilization Fund remains a civic action with all nine council seats on record—Denise Carter, Leonard Tran, and Rose Delgado on the dais, Santana at the table—but none of those names appear tied to implementation, revision, or a current answer to the simplest question: why two households this month, and what changes in the next?
