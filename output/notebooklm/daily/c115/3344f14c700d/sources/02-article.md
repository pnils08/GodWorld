By Ines Sorensen, The Bay Tribune

## Voices: The Quiet Block, the Coffee Machine, the Fourth Quarter

"The block's been weird quiet this week, like everyone's holding their breath. Foot traffic's down — even the corner store guy said so, and he never says much."

That is Hazel Lanning, a student in Laurel, on her morning page. She goes on: "I keep hearing people mention someone from the neighborhood in conversations around town, but nobody says the name loud enough for me to catch it. It's got that feeling before a game, when you know something's about to happen but the lineup hasn't been posted yet. I just hope it's not another store closing."

She is the same Hazel who, on a page back in C111, was testing a lemon pound cake with thyme in it while her mom called it "fancy." Today she carries one question: "Who's the someone everyone's whispering about, and is this going to be another store closing?"

---

"It's the little things, honestly. The way the community center's coffee machine drips all night—I can hear it from my office."

Brooklyn Nguyen wrote that on her night page. She is a community programs coordinator in Fruitvale. She goes on: "I know it's just a coffee machine. But it's become this tiny, constant reminder that I never leave this place at a decent hour, and the place never really goes quiet either. I keep the plant I brought home on my desk, and I watered it today, and I thought, 'When did I start keeping a plant at work because I'm here more than I'm home?' That's the grating thing."

Her page at C112 had Martin in it. She wrote then of "a quiet distance that's crept in since I switched over to the Stabilization Fund," and ended it: "I have to believe that's not the same as drifting apart." Tonight Martin is on the page again, at the end:

"And I know it doesn't matter—it's a coffee machine and a plant. But I think I'm just worn down, and my brain is pointing at the smallest things because the big ones—Martin, the distance, the neighborhood shifting under our feet—are too heavy to hold at 1 a.m. So I fixate on the drip. It's easier."

---

"The Philly game keeps playing back in my head. Nineteen on nine-of-fifteen, and the funny part is I didn't feel hot — I just took what they gave me and didn't rush."

Harry Hayward plays power forward for the Oakland Oaks and lives in KONO. This is his only page so far, written after work. The paper's C114 daily had him as a second-round rookie with eight points in 13 minutes. His own page picks up the next game. "Two blocks, seven boards, all in 22 minutes. That's the version of me that earns trust, and I know it. Coach Shepard put me out there in the fourth when we were down two, and I didn't make him regret it."

He doesn't leave it at the box score. "Eight-and-one feels like a dream, but the stat sheet says it's real. I keep waiting for the other shoe, for the minutes to dry up, but right now I'm just trying to hold onto this feeling."
