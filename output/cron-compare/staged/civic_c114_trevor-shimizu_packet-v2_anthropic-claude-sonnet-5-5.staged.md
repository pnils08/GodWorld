# Coliseum Lost 799 Riders on a Weekend With No Event on Its Row

Jerald Jensen reads the Coliseum platform by what the trash cans hold. He is a janitor with AC Transit. "When the Coliseum empties out like that on a weekend, I see it in the trash cans — fewer cups, fewer wrappers, fewer folks lingering after the last train," he said. "The platform records don't catch the quiet. They don't catch the feeling that people aren't just missing a train, they're missing a reason to be there."

The ridership row agrees with him on the count. Coliseum logged 4,796 riders in cycle 114, down 799 from the 5,595 on the cycle 113 row. That was the largest drop of any station on the record this cycle.

**What the two rows say**

The cycle 113 row listed events in the Baylight District ×2 among its causes. The cycle 114 row lists no event and no construction. Its entries are mild weather, weekend, the ledger base, and crowded platforms. Crowded platforms is on every station row this cycle, so it does not set Coliseum apart. The weekend rule applies citywide, so it does not either. The station moved more than the weekend rule alone would explain, and the row gives no reason of its own.

Earlier rows show how much this station swings with its causes. Cycle 112 carried "events in East Oakland ×3" and logged 9,062 riders. Cycle 113 logged 5,595. Cycle 114 logged 4,796. The row cannot say whether the station is settling toward a baseline or sliding past one.

Coliseum's loss also cancelled the system's gains. Four stations rose: 19th St Oakland by 740, 12th St Oakland City Center by 670, Fruitvale by 510 and MacArthur by 156. Four fell: Coliseum by 799, Lake Merritt by 729, Rockridge by 219 and West Oakland by 10. Total ridership across the eight stations was 46,839, up 319 from 46,520. That is roughly what the gains left over after Coliseum's loss. The stations that rose each carry listed causes: First Friday, game day, events, a hub in design.

**The on-time column**

Coliseum's on-time performance was 81%, down one point. Every other station fell by seven to thirteen points: West Oakland by 13, Lake Merritt by 11, 19th St Oakland and Rockridge by 10, Fruitvale and MacArthur by 9, 12th St Oakland City Center by 7. The platform that lost the most riders held its schedule best. That fits a station with room on it. It does not explain why the room appeared.

Evan Lewis drives the line past the Coliseum for AC Transit and does not treat the pattern as routine. "On a weekend, yeah, the whole city thins out a bit — that's just how it goes. But when you pull up to that station and it's quieter than it ought to be, even for a Saturday, you start wondering."

He did not offer a cause. He offered a gap. "They don't tell you about the family that used to catch that train to a game and decided it wasn't worth the hassle this month," he said. "But nobody's asking the folks who aren't there anymore why they stopped. That's the part the numbers can't say."

The sports feed this cycle logged games in Indiana and Toronto. It also logged a sold-out NBA Cup opener at KONO, where the Oaks beat the Oklahoma City Thunder 101-95. The Coliseum row carries no game-day entry for any of them. The feed notes the rider lift is real only where a station row says game day, and this one does not.

**What the platform managers can and can't say**

Eleanor Rivera, a BART station manager in the Oakland Division, was asked about the drop. She described her own platform first. "At 7:40 on a weekend morning at 12th Street, the shift is quiet in a different way than a weekday. The crowds aren't there, so you hear the announcements bounce off the tile more." She declined to speak for Coliseum. "I wasn't there, and I don't have that platform's view."

She did offer a rule of thumb. "When a station drops more than the usual weekend pattern, it's rarely one thing. It's usually a rhythm stacking up — something at the station, something on the line, something in that neighborhood. But I'd be guessing if I told you where those riders went."

Her three layers are the places to look. The Packet has no entry for the station, none for the line, and none for the neighborhood. The three BART incidents on the record this cycle are a medical hold at Fruitvale, a signal failure in West Oakland and a stalled train in Rockridge, all from last week. None involves Coliseum. No chaos entry touched BART or AC Transit this cycle.

Elena Soria Dominguez leads planning for the Fruitvale Transit Hub Authority. Her description of Phase II applies in reverse here. "The documents show sightlines and path widths," she said. "They don't show the woman who sets up her cart at the panadería corner every morning." Fruitvale gained 510 riders with a hub in design and three events in the neighborhood. Coliseum has neither a hub project nor an event line. The row has nothing human attached to it except Jensen's trash cans and Lewis's empty stretch of platform.

**What stays open**

The record holds 14 transit workers on its roster and 8 citizens who have lived a transit line. None of them has been asked about Coliseum's cycle 114 numbers except through these four voices. Whether the 799 riders went to another station, another mode or nowhere at all is a question no row answers. The next Coliseum row will show whether the drop was a one-weekend gap or the start of a lower floor. If the next row also names no cause, Coliseum has a rider count and no reason, and that is a harder condition to maintain around than a broken signal.
