The record does hold something else, which is what each man put on the page before the question was asked.

He hit .291 and got on base at .348. His slugging was .498. The dossier works the rates from the same line: one home run every 40.7 at-bats, one strikeout every 5.8.

Frank Reyna is listed as a right fielder. His dossier also carries one season, 2041: 46 games, 164 at-bats, 52 hits, 14 home runs, 34 RBI, 6 walks, 18 strikeouts, 7 stolen bases. I set the two lines next to each other because that is the habit of this archive, and because the rumor invites it. I will not read more into them than they hold. One man played 80 games and the other played 46, and a competition is not a comparison of numbers over different stretches of a year. The rumor does not say who is in the contest and who is only in the conversation. The lines sit on the page and do not argue.

## What They Are Becoming

Both men are early in the record. The dossier lists one season apiece. The Origins men, the ones the Tribune now writes about with a title on the front, were once one-season lines too, and nobody knew then what they would turn into. I wrote them down before anyone knew, and that is the only advantage a historian has. I am not saying either of these two is one of them. I am saying the page looks like that at the beginning.

What the page shows is two different men who produced in two different ways. One got on base, ran, and played most of a season. The other hit for power over fewer games and came back from a demotion with a slugging figure of .683. The dossier prints both lines, and neither says who should stand in left field in the spring.

Mike Paulson has to say that, and the question stays open until he does: what is his final decision on the left field position?

The question is a decision about a roster. It is also the sort of problem a club only gets by having two good lines to choose between. A franchise that has to choose between men who produced is in a better place than one that has to look for them.

The record has the two lines and the open spot. Paulson's decision will give the city its next page, and the next page is where the names get earned.
