## Who's Steering the Fruitvale Transit Hub — Or Is Nobody?

The platform at Fruitvale BART is overcast and fifty degrees give or take, the kind of gray that sits on your shoulders all evening. A rider waiting for the next train — a regular on this line, didn't want her name in it — points across the tracks at the fenced lot where the hub is supposed to be going up. "That fence has been there since I had a different job," she says. "Whatever's happening back there, it isn't happening fast."

That's the whole story, honestly. I could stop typing right there. But let's do the math anyway, because the city sure isn't doing it out loud.

The initiative on the books is called "Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II — Visioning." Visioning. That word alone should tell you something — it's the stage where people draw pictures and hold meetings, not the stage where concrete gets poured. Except the record says this thing has been parked in a phase called "construction-planning" for nine cycles running. Nine. That's not a hiccup. That's a project that walked into a room and forgot why it came in.

Here's the part that actually burns: nobody downtown has said a word about the stall. The official line, as far as anyone's told the public, is that this initiative is still live, still moving, still on track. On track toward what? A fence that's aged past the point where a regular commuter remembers a different job? You can't be "on track" and "parked for nine cycles" at the same time. Pick one. The record can't have it both ways, and neither can whoever's signing off on the status updates.

I don't have a name for you yet on who's actually holding this file. That's not me being coy — that's the problem. Somebody in this city is responsible for moving "Visioning" out of the drawing-board stage and into a stage where a shovel touches dirt in Fruitvale. Nine cycles of stillness didn't happen because the file cabinet ate the paperwork. Somebody's desk it's sitting on. Somebody's phase gate it's stuck behind. And whoever that is has let the public-facing story stay rosy while the tracker underneath it hasn't moved a single phase.

The rider on the platform doesn't care about phase gates. She cares that the lot's still a lot. That's the gap right there — the story City Hall tells about this hub and the story the fence has been telling for nine cycles are not the same story. One of them is lying, and it's not the fence.

So here's the question, and I'm not letting it go quiet: who is responsible for the stalled progress of the Fruitvale Transit Hub Phase II initiative? Somebody owns that phase gate. Somebody's job it is to explain nine cycles of nothing while the public line stayed "on track." I'd love to hear the explanation. I'm not holding my breath for one to show up unprompted.
