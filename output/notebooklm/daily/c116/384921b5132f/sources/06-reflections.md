# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Jade Orion — Musician

> Hey, Tank. Good to see you out here. Was just thinking about you the other day—all this Aitken stuff got me remembering those long nights breaking down contracts at the bar. We did, yeah. I kept telling myself Oakland would find a way to keep him, the way it always seemed to. But I guess the city can't hold everybody forever, no matter how much we want it to. I know exactly what you mean. I've been feeling that too—like all the roots I've put down here could get pulled up just as easy if the numbers stop working out. But then I walk by the lake and see the same faces I've seen for a decade, and I think maybe it's the people, not the contracts, that make a place home. *(talking with Tank Fiester)*

## Tank Fiester — Biotech Lab Director (Portside Bio)

> Jade. Good to see you too. Yeah, that news hit different—feels like we lost a piece of the city itself, not just a first baseman. Remember when we'd argue about whether he'd ever actually leave? I think we both got that one wrong. That's the part that gets me. He did everything right—showed up at every meeting, built that league for the kids, made himself part of the block. And still, the math didn't work out. Feels like a warning sometimes, you know? Like nothing's safe just because it's been there a long time. That's true. The lake's got a way of grounding you, reminding you what's actually real. But I still catch myself watching my neighbors a little closer these days, wondering who's going to be here next year and who's already looking at moving on. It's a strange time to be from a place. *(talking with Jade Orion)*

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> I did it. Two inches, at the chair on Grand, nothing dramatic. Robert noticed at the rail before I said a word, which is the best review a haircut can get. He said I looked like someone who had made up her mind. I told him it was only hair. The Oaks are 8-1, and I keep doing the arithmetic backward. Ten cycles ago they were 0-9 before a single game counted, and the room in C109 wanted Wilson Shepard to explain himself. Elliot Abraham says the energy is infectious, and he's right. But I'd like the paper to remember who sat in those seats
