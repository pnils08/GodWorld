# Harry Hayward’s 22 Minutes Change the Oaks’ Rotation Question

Harry Hayward scored 19 points, grabbed seven rebounds and blocked two shots in 22 minutes as the Oaks beat the 76ers 120-106 in Philadelphia. He made 9 of 15 shots, and the performance came with the Oaks down two after three quarters before they scored 40 in the fourth.

That is the useful line for an expansion team: not merely a career high, but production attached to a defined opportunity. Hayward played six more minutes than he had against Orlando, when he scored five points with two steals and a block. The Oaks won that game 125-109 for their third straight victory. They are now 8-1 with a four-game winning streak.

The result matters. The rotation question matters more.

Hayward’s path through the early season has not been linear. The feed records 4 points in 13 minutes against Oklahoma City, 8 points and five rebounds in 23 minutes against Toronto, 8 points on 3-of-4 shooting in 13 minutes against Indiana, 10 points and 10 rebounds against New York, and 12 points against Detroit. The Philadelphia line is the first entry that combines scoring volume, rebounding and rim protection at this level of efficiency.

That does not settle his place in the lineup. It establishes a stronger case for examining it.

The Oaks have enough established production to avoid making Hayward the entire explanation for their start. AJ Dybantsa scored 25 points against Philadelphia on 10-of-16 shooting, including 3-of-3 from three-point range. Adash Stanley had 23 points and seven assists while shooting 8 of 13, and Wendell Carter Jr. supplied 12 points, 15 rebounds and three offensive rebounds. T. Jerome added nine assists. Against Orlando, Stanley had 24 points, eight assists and no turnovers, while Dybantsa shot 11 of 13 for 23 points and Dillon Brooks scored 23.

Hayward’s value is therefore not dependent on the Oaks lacking alternatives. It is visible because the team has alternatives and still found 22 minutes for him in a game that required a fourth-quarter correction.

The previous read, published in C114, treated Hayward’s 13-minute, eight-point performance as enough to identify a possibility but not enough to settle it. Philadelphia moved that possibility forward. It did not turn a second-round rookie into a finished player, and it did not prove that one large scoring night should dictate the rotation. It did show that the developmental argument now has a fuller sample: Hayward can contribute in limited minutes, and those minutes can expand without the offense losing its balance.

That is the broader stability signal in an 8-1 start. The Oaks are not relying on one narrow formula. Stanley is creating and shooting, Dybantsa is converting efficiently, Carter Jr. is controlling the glass, and Hayward has supplied a new pressure point for the rotation.

The front office’s unanswered question is no longer whether Hayward can produce. The record has answered that for one night. It is how the Oaks will measure the next increase in his role against the production already keeping an expansion roster together.
