# Faith & Quiet Work | Cycle 109

Bhai Manjit Singh has tended the langar kitchen at Gurdwara Singh Sabha Fruitvale since the congregation's founding in 1982. The work is steady: peeling, stirring, offering. On the nights when the city's crisis rooms fill and the phone lines at other congregations start to move, he knows what his kitchen is for.

The Gurdwara stands in Fruitvale with a congregation of 350. It is not the largest faith house in the Bay. It does not make the cycle's biggest announcements. What it carries is persistent—a kitchen that feeds, a practice that holds, a place where the work of gathering does not pause between holy days and ordinary Tuesdays.

This cycle, the record shows eight faith events across Oakland since C103. Bay Pure Land Buddhist Temple in Chinatown opened its doors for community support in C109, holding 41. Telegraph Presbyterian Fellowship in Downtown marked Epiphany with 237 present. Cathedral of the Living Word at Lake Merritt held 1,265 for the same observance. The numbers move up and down; the gesture—a congregation standing open to its neighborhood when crisis arrives—has become a pattern worth naming.

Eunice Marston, a faith leader in Downtown, speaks without claiming expertise she does not have. "I haven't had any direct involvement with the Gurdwara Singh Sabha Fruitvale," she said, "but as a faith leader in Downtown, I appreciate the various initiatives and organizations that bring people together and foster a sense of community."

The Gurdwara sits alongside other steady work in Fruitvale. Lucia Polito founded the Postpartum Oakland Network there in C101. The Fruitvale Transit Corridor Mutual Aid has been running since the same cycle. The Fruitvale Summer Speed Series moves through the neighborhood for youth. St. Esperanza Parish, Catholic, keeps its own observance—374 present for Epiphany in C108.

What binds these institutions is not doctrine but function: they are places that stay open, that practice care when they are asked, that do not disappear when the crisis passes. The langar kitchen does not close between cycles. The phone lines at Telegraph Presbyterian and Bay Pure Land do not go quiet. The emergency assistance fund at Lakeshore Unitarian Universalist Fellowship, activated in C104 with 53 present, remains activated.

The question is not whether these congregations know what their neighborhoods need. The record shows they do. The unanswered matter is whether the city's neighborhoods—Fruitvale, Chinatown, Downtown, Rockridge, Lake Merritt—know that these houses are still standing, still open, still holding the work of gathering on the nights when the crisis rooms fill.
