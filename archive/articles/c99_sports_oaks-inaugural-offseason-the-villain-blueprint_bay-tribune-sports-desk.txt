# THE CYCLE PULSE — SPECIAL EDITION
## OAKLAND OAKS INAUGURAL OFFSEASON: THE VILLAIN BLUEPRINT
**By The Bay Tribune Sports Desk**

OAKLAND — General Manager Mike Paulson stood at the podium with a blank spreadsheet. He walked away with a fully formed, unapologetically hostile championship contender. The Oakland Oaks inaugural offseason is officially complete, and the Baylight arena is bracing for impact.

It began in the Expansion Draft. Paulson ruthlessly monopolized the league's point-of-attack defense, drafting Wendell Carter Jr., Draymond Green, and Jose Alvarado, establishing an immediate "99-Hustle" defensive identity under Head Coach Wilson Shepard. But the true masterclass lay in asset arbitrage. Paulson flipped his remaining expansion picks—Herbert Jones, Max Strus, and Dorian Finney-Smith—extracting maximum value to secure the #1 overall pick, the #10 overall pick, and over $25 million in clean cap space. 

With the #1 overall pick, the Oaks selected the franchise engine: **AJ Dybantsa**. The 6'9" scoring phenom immediately gives Oakland the isolation safety valve their motion offense desperately required. At #10, Paulson drafted the high-upside Italian shooter **Dame Sarr** to replace the perimeter spacing lost in the Strus trade. And with their final draft capital, the Oaks drafted the explosive highlight-reel forward **Coen Carr**, officially moving "Lob City" to the Bay.

Then came Free Agency. With pristine cap sheets, Paulson outbid the market to sign 6th Man of the Year **Adash Stanley** to a 3-year, $75 million contract, securing the relentless floor general Shepard's system demands. He immediately followed it up by acquiring elite secondary playmaker **Austin Reaves** on a 4-year deal, and securing critical frontcourt depth with the bargain signing of **Precious Achiuwa**. Veterans **Tim Hardaway Jr.** and **Jaxson Hayes** were brought in on minimums to round out the 12-man rotation.

But Paulson wasn't done. In a final, chaotic masterstroke that sent the Tribune Sports Desk into a frenzy, Paulson traded defensive stalwart Alex Caruso for the ultimate NBA antagonist: **Dillon Brooks**.

The message is clear. A defensive trio of Draymond Green, Jose Alvarado, and Dillon Brooks is not just a basketball team; it is a physical and psychological torture chamber. Opposing offenses will cross the Bay Bridge into Oakland and face the most annoying, instigating, physical defense ever assembled. 

The dogs are loose in Oakland.
