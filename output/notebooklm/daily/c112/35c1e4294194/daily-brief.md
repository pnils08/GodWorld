### **Inside the Pickup Line: Classroom Realities Behind Rockridge’s Top Mark**
*By Angela Reyes, Education Desk*

At a few minutes past three, the after-school room in Rockridge fills one backpack at a time [1]. Program aide **Mei Wong** stands near the door, learning the children by how they walk through the frame [1]. While citywide tables rank Rockridge at the top of the school quality index at 8.63, Wong notes that parents coming through the door are focused on immediate care rather than statistics [2, 3]:

*   **Mei Wong** (Program Aide) shared what families express at pickup: *"Parents mostly ask about homework and whether their kid ate a real snack, but underneath it's about feeling safe and known... What keeps people here is that sense of community—the same faces at pickup, teachers who remember your kid's name, neighbors who look out. It's not flashy, but it's steady"* [2].
*   **Simone Adesanya** (Principal, Rockridge) addressed the slight index shift from 8.7 down to 8.63 as a minor rounding point on paper [3, 4]: *"Honestly, that dip is a blip on a page to most of us. Teacher quality at 9.08 — that's the number my staff feels every single day, in the hallways... The index is a snapshot; the teachers are the whole album"* [4].
*   **Maya Torres-Dillon** (High School Science Teacher) reflected on the neighborhood’s 94.53% graduation rate and 76.03% college-ready rate among its 247 students [4, 5]: *"Honestly, that 76% number — I recognize it in the hallways more than in any single test score... But I also teach long enough to know that number doesn't capture the kid who's brilliant but has to work nights... What I recognize is the effort behind it"* [5].

While Rockridge gained one student this cycle, enrollment records across Oakland show broader movement at the edges, with West Oakland gaining three students and East Oakland gaining two [6].
