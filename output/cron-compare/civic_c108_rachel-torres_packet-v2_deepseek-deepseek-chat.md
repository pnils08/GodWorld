# San Antonio Tops Incident Count While Lake Merritt Waits Longest for Responses

The plexiglass at San Antonio's corner store had been replaced twice this month when Melton Neilon stopped for his usual coffee. "I've heard San Antonio's been hit hard lately, and Lake Merritt's taking longer to get help—it's worrying, but I trust folks are doing what they can with what they've got," he said, stirring sugar into the paper cup.

At the transit hub, Delia Vargas-Ruiz wiped down ticket machines between shifts. "Working at the transit hub, I hear people talk about the response times," she noted without looking up from her work, "but honestly, most folks around here just keep their heads down and go about their day. The numbers are what they are."

Dank Abara shifted his weight at the bus stop bench. "I've heard San Antonio has more incidents than other neighborhoods, and Lake Merritt's response times are slow, but I don't know the specifics," he said of the neighborhood's standing, squinting against the afternoon glare. "It's just something people talk about around here."

With no easing trends reported across the districts, the question lingers: which interventions might synchronize response resources with demonstrated need?
