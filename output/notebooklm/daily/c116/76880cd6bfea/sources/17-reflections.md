# West Oakland, in their own words

## Mike Paulson — General Manager, Oakland A's & The Oaks

> Look, the record is the record. Eight and one is eight and one, and nobody's giving it back because the lineup card changed. But the question of whose record it is — that's the wrong way to look at it. The architecture is the organization. The players are the load-bearing walls. You take one out, you brace it, you move on. Quintero sliding to first, Clark and Hernandez at third — that's us bracing. The wall doesn't fall because the hands change. It holds because the foundation was laid right. That's what I tell myself on the days it doesn't hold.

> As a resident of West Oakland, I've seen the mood around the Oaks shift dramatically. The team was 0-9 to start, and now they're 8-1. The fans are excited, and you can feel the confidence in the air. It's not just about the wins, though; it's about the team's performance and how they've gelled. I've heard people talking about whether the early struggles were by design, and now they're curious to see if this is a sustainable change. The energy is positive, and it's great to see the community rallying around the team.

## Wilson Shepard — Head Coach, Oakland Oaks

> Structure is what holds when the names change. I've seen it in my own locker room — we went 0-9 before anyone counted a game, and the structure didn't wobble because the people in it kept doing their jobs. Same thing with the A's right now. Quintero sliding to first, Clark and Hernandez competing at third — that's not a rebuild, that's a system that trusts the next guy in. The record is just the wall. The hands change, but the wall doesn't move if the foundation's real. That's what I tell my guys when they start reading their own headlines: the ranking is a mirror, not a load-bearing beam. What holds is the work, day in and day out.

> The team's performance so far has been great, we're 8-1 and the guys are playing with confidence. I think we've found our rhythm, and it's a testament to the hard work they've been putting in. The offense is clicking, and we're getting good contributions from everyone. Of course, we had a hiccup against San Antonio, but that's to be expected. We're still a young team, and we're learning as we go. Overall, I'm proud of how we're performing, and I'm excited to see how we'll do as the season progresses.

> To be honest, my new living situation in West Oakland hasn't really affected my coaching. I've been focused on the team's performance, and my daily routine hasn't changed much. I'm still the same person, with the same goals for the Oaks.

## Elias Varek — Founder of Civis Systems

> The recent financial returns for Civis Systems show a minimal change, $489,017 this week, which is just slightly up from the previous week's $488,443. For Varek Systems, the returns are $35,112 this week, compared to $34,932 last week. These numbers indicate a stable, but not growing, revenue stream for both companies. I'm concerned that Civis Systems is flat where it matters, and I'm still trying to understand the implications of this stability.

> Numbers don't lie, but they do withhold. Civis moved a tenth of a percent. That's not growth, that's a heartbeat. Flatlining. And the Oakland Oaks — 11.28% growth on $62.7M? That's a solid, honest number. A real business. The kind that pays for the city's lights. But it's Civis that's the problem. I built this company to read the city, and it's stopped telling me anything new. We went from $60.3 to $60.4. That's noise. I don't fund noise. Here's the thing — the signal in West Oakland. It's moved. It's not just sitting unanswered anymore, it's traveling. I wrote last week I wouldn't wait for it to become someone else's emergency, and it hasn't. It's gotten worse. The instrument reads it, but nothing downstream responds. That's not a city failure anymore. That's a Civis failure. My product is reading a problem we can't solve. So we're going to do this: I'm pulling 20% of Civis's engineering resources off the Baylight contract and putting them on the signal. I want to know why it moves, where it's going, and what's causing it. Not what it correlates with. What it *is*. We built the best instrument in the country. Time to prove it. And Varek Systems — $9.1M, 11.8% up. That's my growth engine. That's where I'm doubling down. I'm putting $2M of Civis's cash into Varek's R&D.

## Selena Grant — Journalist - The Oakland Oaks Beat Reporter

> The Aitken signing keeps sitting wrong with me. It's not the money — he got his money — and it's not even that he left, because guys leave. It's that I wrote that piece about the city holding on to its own, and I believe it, and then it didn't. That's a story I told myself one way and the universe told it another. Does it matter? In the grand scheme, no. But I keep circling back to Kevin Clark, how he's about to be handed the weight of third base and the legacy that comes with it, and I wonder if anyone's actually prepared him for what that means off the field. Maybe that's the real thing grating on me — not the loss, but the quiet before the next kid gets tested.

## Robert Jaston — Senior Pastor / Faith Leader

> I've seen Adams Point United Methodist participate in the Interfaith Council's prayer breakfast, bringing leaders together in Adams Point. It's heartening to see different faith communities come together like that.

## Denise Carter — City Council District 1

> Denise leans forward, her elbows on the table, voice low and steady. She doesn't wave off the numbers, but she refuses to let them be the whole story. "That -0.04 is a start, not a finish. I said it plainly on the record, and I'll say it to you here: I'm not going to call a two-step climb out of a ditch a victory lap. Brooklyn's still the lowest in the city, and I carry that. But I also know the difference between a slide and a stand. We're standing now. The decay has stopped, and that matters to the folks who never left, the ones who watched empty storefronts multiply while the rest of the city talked about 'revitalization' like it was a weather report." She pauses, letting the weight settle. "What I won't do is let that number get used against my people. When the city sees -0.04, I see Kaila's produce stand finally getting a consistent supplier. I see Drago's nephew getting a steady shift at the new café. I see Sefu's block getting its streetlights fixed on time. The metrics are climbing, but the measure that matters to me is whether a grandmother can walk to the market after dark and whether a kid can point to a future here without leaving the neighborhood. So yes, we're moving in the right direction. But my office isn't in the business of celebrating inches while the city counts miles. We're pushing for the full block, and I'll keep saying that until the numbers and the lived reality match."
