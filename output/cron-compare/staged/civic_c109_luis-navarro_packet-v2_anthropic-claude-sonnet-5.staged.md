## The Same Empty Lot, Four Times

Rick Walker stands at the chain-link fence at the corner lot two blocks from his school, waiting for the crossing guard to wave the line of kids through. It's the same lot it was last year. He teaches K-8 in Fruitvale, cooked line before that, and he's got maybe four minutes before the bell before he has to be back inside.

"Honestly, I hear teachers and parents at school talking about how the city keeps promising things for Fruitvale but nothing ever moves forward," he tells me, one eye on a kid untying his shoe mid-crosswalk. "We see the same empty lots year after year, and nobody ever explains why."

I don't have an explanation for him. There isn't one on the record.

Three miles north, Melton Neilon is bagging sourdough loaves at the Temescal farmers market table, flour on his forearms, half-listening to the vendor next to him complain about parking. He's not from Fruitvale. He doesn't need to be to have heard about it.

"I've heard folks at the farmers market talking about the Fruitvale Transit Hub delays," Neilon says, "but honestly, I'm not sure who's supposed to be steering it forward. It feels like everyone's waiting for someone else to take charge."

That's the sentence that stuck with me. Not the complaint — the shrug inside it. Nobody in this cycle's record names who's steering.

Over in Rockridge, servers Elio Perez and Depak Ying are working the same lunch rush at the same diner, six tables apart, and somehow talking about the same lot in Fruitvale neither of them has a stake in.

"Honestly, I hear folks at the diner grumbling about how nothing ever moves forward with the Fruitvale plans," Perez says between orders, "but nobody seems to know who's actually holding the reins or why it's stuck."

Ying, wiping down a table Perez just cleared, doesn't disagree. "I've heard people talking about the Fruitvale Transit Hub delays, and it feels like no one's stepping up to take responsibility — it's frustrating because we need that progress."

Four people. Three neighborhoods. Zero names.

Here's what the record gives me and what it doesn't. What it gives me: the city signal this cycle flagged a civic gap — accountability, or the absence of it, tied to Fruitvale. And it gives me this: no civic initiative on the books this cycle addresses that signal. Not a placeholder, not a stalled line item. Nothing pointed at it at all.

What it doesn't give me is a name. Not an office, not a person, not a duty assigned to anyone who could pick up a phone and explain the lot. I don't have a request on file. I don't have a response, or a refusal, or a date stamped on either. There isn't one to report — which is different from saying nobody's ever going to answer. It just means nobody's on record answering yet.

So I'm left with what four residents in three different neighborhoods noticed without prompting each other: a stalled thing with no fingerprints on it. That's not proof of wrongdoing. It's proof that the story of who's responsible hasn't been told to the people standing at the fence.

Walker's bell rings. He heads back in without finishing the thought. Neither did I.

What's still unfinished, for the people living next to that lot, isn't the transit hub. It's the answer to Neilon's question — who's supposed to be steering it forward — and right now, that answer doesn't exist anywhere I can find it.
