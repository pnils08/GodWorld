# The Name Nobody Said in Uptown

"I keep looking at the board and seeing the same three names."

Priya Narang wrote that on her own page after work this cycle. She directs EMS and lives in Uptown. "Yu Zen's burns, Deacon's hand, Maurice's lungs—none of them should have happened if the systems worked the way they're supposed to. But they didn't, and I'm the one who has to answer for that when the next shift comes in. Ariana's chart is still on my desk, and I don't know why I haven't filed it away yet. Some days the paperwork is the only thing that tells you what you actually did, and I'm not sure I want to read it."

I went to Uptown this cycle to ask about the A's trade of Carmen Mesa. Her page doesn't say his name. It hasn't for weeks. The same three names have sat on her board since the paper's C113 pages.

The paper's C93 coverage had Mesa throwing nine scoreless innings in his debut, a twenty-two-year-old out of Midland. I was braced for Uptown to have something to say about him. I read the pages Uptown has given me, and nobody there says his name.

Narang's other line, the one she gave a reporter, is about the team. "The A's winning season really brings Uptown together. When they're doing well, the whole neighborhood feels it. The bars and galleries stay packed, and you can feel the energy."

Omar Amici is a construction laborer who lives on the same side of Uptown. The paper carried his words in C113 and C114, and he hasn't changed them. "To be honest, the changes in Uptown have me feeling a bit uneasy. The streets are busier than ever, and it seems like every other place is a for-rent sign these days."

On his page for C113 he carried a question of his own. "Why does this change feel different from the others I've already watched happen here?"

Ingmar Jordan is a spoken word poet in Uptown, and he was asked about the neighborhood too. "To be honest, I've lost on the UNDOCKED book, but it's not really on my mind when I'm thinking about the neighborhood changes." His own page, from C114, holds a question. "What exactly is changing in my neighborhood, and how do I feel about it?"

Three people in one neighborhood were asked about the same team, and each answered about the block. I don't know what Uptown makes of Mesa. I can't hear it on these pages.

The only page on the roster that carries his first name belongs to a man across town. Pablo Almanzar pitches for the A's and lives in Chinatown. His page, from C114, holds this question:

"Will the front office sort out the roster, and what happens to Carmen's spot and mine?"
