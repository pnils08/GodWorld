- **A's — roster-move (off-season):** Mark Aitken (1B)
  - StoryAngle: Mark Aitken signs with Colorado, one year and $21 million. The city didn't hold on to its own this time
  - Notes: The free agency question this page has carried since C112 has its answer, and it is not Oakland and it is not Anaheim. Mark Aitken has signed with the Colorado Rockies, one year for $21 million. The Angels were the team with the reported interest in C116, the A's were the team with the history, and Colorado is the team that closed. The money is almost exactly what he made in 2041, $21.4M, so this was never about Aitken being priced as a player in decline. He is coming off a .283 season with 153 hits, 33 homeruns, 87 RBI and 3.2 WAR in 540 at bats, a 93 overall first baseman with 3 Gold Gloves and the 2037 Playoff MVP, and he homered in the World Series that clinched the seventh title. The length is the detail that will be argued over. One year is a short commitment for a player at the top of the market, and it puts Aitken back in free agency after 2042. Whether that reads as a door left open or simply the deal that was there, he has not said. What it leaves behind in Oakland is bigger than a lineup spot. Aitken is an Oakland native who grew up swinging a bat in East Oakland. He owns his place in Jack London, where he lives with his wife and three kids. He has been the A's player rep and community liaison, and he runs Aitken Little Leaguers, free baseball for East Oakland kids heading into its sixth year. He showed up at city council and planning hearings, brought Benji Dillon to the DEIR session, was a major voice in the Baylight negotiations, and was the veteran who reached out to Kevin Clark the day Clark was called up. In C114 he wrote that "the number on the calendar means more than the number on my back," and that "the city has a way of holding on to its own, and I've been its own for a long, long time." This time the city did not hold on. For the A's, the move this page laid out in C116 now happens: Ernesto Quintero slides across the diamond to first base. That opens third base outright to the bat-or-glove contest between Kevin Clark, .327 in 55 at bats with 83 discipline and 87 clutch, and Giancarlo Hernandez, the 96 fielding glove from the Carmen Mesa trade. It also comes in the same off-season Vinnie Keane retired, which means the A's go into 2042 without the two longest-standing local voices in their clubhouse. Mike Paulson's choice is now on the record: Aitken walks, and the club trusts its young infield.

- **A's — re-signing (off-season):** Vladimir Gonzalez (RF)
  - StoryAngle: Vladimir Gonzalez gets five years and $119 million. The A's put Aitken's money into their right fielder
  - Notes: The same cycle Mark Aitken left for Colorado, the A's locked up a player they intend to keep. Vladimir Gonzalez, the A's right fielder, has agreed to a five-year, $119 million contract, $23.8 million a year, avoiding arbitration. It is the biggest raise on the roster this off-season. Gonzalez made $2.7M in 2041, so the new deal adds $21.1M a year, almost exactly the $21.4M that came off the books when Aitken signed with the Rockies. Mike Paulson let a 33-year-old walk on a one-year deal and put the same money into a 27-year-old for five. He earned it. Gonzalez, born in the Dominican Republic, is a 92 overall right fielder coming off the best line of his career: .306 with 161 hits, 28 homeruns, 76 RBI, 17 steals and 4.7 WAR in 527 at bats, with only 60 strikeouts. That 4.7 WAR is more than Aitken's 3.2 and more than any A's hitter this page has written up this off-season. The deal also settles a question Gonzalez raised himself. In C113, with the outfield crowded behind him, he said he was not sure how his clutch performance would affect his chances, that "it's up to Paulson to decide," and named Desmond Morton and Frank Reyna as the players pushing him. He told the young guys, "you can't control what Paulson decides. You just control how you show up," and added, "I earned my spot, sure, but I remember what it was like trying to crack a crowded room." Five years answers who plays right field. It also means Reyna, Morton, JR Rosado and Kevin Clark, whose secondary positions include right field, are competing for left field and the bench.
