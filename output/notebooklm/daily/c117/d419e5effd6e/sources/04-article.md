# The Lives Page, Cycle 117

By Tamsin Pruitt, The Bay Tribune

## Serg Cerrano, Fruitvale

Serg Cerrano welcomed a new child into the family this cycle. Cerrano is a mover who lives in Fruitvale. The record gives no name for the baby, no date and no hour, and it does not yet say what came next.

It does keep the days around the birth. One evening Cerrano set the table for one, though a sister had said she might stop by. The record does not say whether she came. On another day Cerrano walked past the new place on the corner, near a taqueria patio, and decided from the sidewalk that it wasn't for them. On a third, Cerrano ran the numbers on staying put another year. Nobody wrote down what the numbers said.

Those three lines are not a mood. They are what a household does with a new person in it: it counts the table and looks at the rent. Two cycles earlier the record has Cerrano feeling quietly lucky to call Fruitvale home. This cycle it has Cerrano pricing out another twelve months there.

Cerrano's own words are plain about it. "After welcoming my new child, I felt the city's pace pulling me forward. Life's been busy, and I've been close to family a lot lately."

The record has no earlier Tribune piece on Cerrano that I could find. This is the first line the page holds.

## Joseph Cramp, Downtown

Joseph Cramp was promoted at the City of Oakland after 28 years as a janitor. He lives Downtown.

Twenty-eight years is a long time to push a wide broom. The record shows the broom in the cycle before last, when Cramp went down a school hallway after the bell and, in the record's words, enjoyed the total silence. The paper's C112 coverage had him moving through the Downtown sidewalks. The record has him disagreeing aloud with a Tribune column to an empty kitchen, so he reads us. In the C116 entry a DJ was spinning records at a crowded bar near Broadway, the bassline thumping through the foggy night. It does not say whether Cramp was at the bar.

The record names no new title and no new office. I will not supply them. It gives his own account.

"I've been a Janitor for 28 years, and I just got promoted. To be honest, it's still sinking in. It means more responsibilities, more fires to put out before noon, but it's a win for me. I'm not sure what comes next, but I'm ready to take it on."

Two other lines from the same cycle do not sit easily together. In one, he started an application and closed the tab after the first page. In the other, he held a door and got a whole conversation for it. The promotion is not in question. The record does not say what the application was for, or whether he went back to it. It does say a door held open brought him talk.

## Travis Paiz and Jocelyn Santo, Lake Merritt

Travis Paiz and Jocelyn Santo married this cycle and set up a household in Lake Merritt. They are renting.

The record has been watching this for some time. In C113 it logged Jocelyn Santo as a real friend, not just a colleague, to Travis Paiz, and the same line ran the other way. In C116 the record said something had shifted between them, more than friends now. That same cycle Paiz wrapped the good dishes twice and labeled the box in block letters. Santo had spent C113 and C114 with a lease renewal letter that sat on the table for three days, and with rent taking the biggest bite of the month again.

Paiz is a mover. Santo is a yoga instructor and works for herself.

The wedding cycle also has Paiz on the porch, listening to the ninth inning on the radio and letting the street hear it too. Santo, in the same cycle, saw the sign-up sheet for the recreation center clean-up and walked past it a second time. She also ordered in for the third night running and asked for it to be left at the door. A household being set up looks like that: one person on the porch, and the other behind a door with the boxes.

Paiz said: "Setting up the household with Jocelyn was a whirlwind, to be honest. We just got married and were eager to start our life together. It's been a lot of work, but we're getting into a groove. We've got our routines down, and it's nice having someone to share the responsibilities with. Our little one is the light of our lives, and it's been great watching them adjust to our new setup."

The record names no child in this household. I take it from his words that there is one in the house, and I cannot say more.

Santo said: "Setting up a household with Travis has been a whirlwind, to be honest. We just got married and are still figuring things out. It's been a big adjustment, but I'm excited to start this new chapter with him in Lake Merritt."

## Alma Lewis, Rockridge

Alma Lewis moved into a place of their own in Rockridge, renting. The record gives no street and no date.

In the days that followed, Lewis brought home a new plant and picked its spot carefully. The record also keeps a Port of Oakland manager putting Lewis's name forward for the open lead role. It does not say whether the role was filled or whether Lewis got it. Lewis also grabbed coffee from the cart outside the hospital before the morning rush hit.

## Benji Dillon, Maya Torres-Dillon and Rick Dillon, Piedmont Ave

The Dillon household of three left Rockridge for Piedmont Ave. The record's reason is a better fit. It does not say what the old place failed to fit.

The page has followed this family. Benji Dillon appeared in the paper's C115 and C116 Lives pages and in the C116 Culture piece headlined "Benji Dillon Doesn't Think It's a Big Change, and That's the Story." Rick Dillon was on the C116 page. Maya Torres-Dillon was in the paper's Civic coverage in C112 and C114.

The record keeps small things from the move cycle. Rick traded the good sticker for the rare one and called it a fair deal. Maya wore the green-and-gold cap to the corner store and got three nods for it. She read the Tribune's front page twice, once for the news and once to see who was in it. She also took the long way home to check the Baylight fence line.

Benji cancelled the standing coffee date and sent nothing in its place. He also took the job at the back of the fellowship hall, the one nobody talks during. That line follows him from C116 and is not mine to explain. The page will note only that a standing coffee date went unanswered in the week the family moved house.
