# 12th Street Added 1,962 Riders and Lost 4 Points of On-Time. The Logged Causes Don't Fully Explain It.

*By Trevor Shimizu*

12th St Oakland City Center carried 14,175 riders in Cycle 117, up 1,962 from 12,213 in Cycle 116. Its on-time performance fell from 81% to 77%. Most of the system went the other way. Lake Merritt, West Oakland and MacArthur each gained seven points. 19th Street gained three, to 81%, even though it carried its own game-day crowd.

The Transit Hub planning lead, Elena Soria Dominguez, made the same point when I asked: "the slip is not a system-wide slide." She said it is concentrated at 12th Street, which takes the heaviest event traffic.

## What the sheet logs

The city's record for the 12th Street row lists five things:

- A Downtown and Jack London game day. The Oaks beat Denver 137-113 at KONO in front of a 94 percent crowd.
- Events in Lake Merritt and West Oakland.
- Baylight District construction, now active.
- A citywide platform-crowding factor of 0.14.
- A breeze at 59°F. That is the seventh straight clear cycle, so weather is not the cause.

There was one chaos car on the line, and it ran clean. It was a BART train at Lake Merritt with a transit delay of zero. Any hit it carries would show up on next cycle's rows, not this one.

## The mechanism, and where it falls short

Eleanor Rivera, the BART station manager at 12th, described the mechanism. "The crowd comes in waves, not a steady stream, and that's what stretches the dwell time," she said. "Trains are there on time, but everyone's squeezing on at once, so the doors hold longer and the schedule slips a little."

Dwell time is the minutes a train sits at the platform with its doors open. On a busy line, a few extra seconds per stop add up, because each train inherits the delay of the one ahead of it. That is how a crowd turns into a late schedule. Ximena Mehta, BART Oakland Division's PR contact, put it this way: "every little delay compounds." She called the congestion "the price of the city actually showing up."

That explanation fits this cycle. It doesn't fit the whole record. Compare recent 12th Street rows:

| Cycle | Riders | On-time | Logged congestion factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 112 | 14,162 | 89% | none |
| 115 | 14,526 | 86% | 0.12 |
| 117 | 14,175 | 77% | 0.14 |

C112 and C117 carried almost the same number of riders, within 13 of each other. On-time was 12 points apart. The congestion factor moved by only a couple of hundredths. Volume alone doesn't set the on-time number at this station. The crowd's shape matters too, and the record doesn't capture that. Rivera's "waves" are one candidate. Event timing is another. The sheet doesn't say which.

The 77% also isn't new. 12th hit it in C107 on 8,191 riders, in C114 on 10,011, and again now on 14,175. A station that lands on the same number at three very different loads has a ceiling somewhere other than its headcount. I'd want the dwell logs for those three cycles, and I've asked for nothing the city hasn't published. Until someone shows them, "crowds" is a description, not a diagnosis.

An AC Transit driver who works the corridor, Evan Lewis, gave the plainest summary: "It's not one thing; it's the construction, the events, the whole city moving at once."

## The rest of the system

Total ridership across the 8 stations was 71,090, up from 68,505. Riders moved around more than they grew:

- **Gains:** 12th Street, +1,962. Rockridge, +1,388 to 5,945, with events in Rockridge and on Piedmont Ave. Lake Merritt, +716. West Oakland, +327. MacArthur, +196. 19th Street, +31.
- **Losses:** Fruitvale, down 1,336 to 8,007, though its on-time rose two points to 85%. Coliseum, down 699 to 8,209, at 82%.

Soria Dominguez was blunt about Fruitvale. The Phase II visioning moved into design this week, but "nothing has been built." She said she won't claim a gain as the hub's credit, and she won't dress up the drop.

## The roads

The corridors are running hot, and two of them have no logged cause beyond the base ledger:

- **I-880 North:** index 100, up 11. The row cites game day and Baylight construction.
- **I-980:** index 100, up 14. It cites the same two causes.
- **International Blvd:** 84, up 11, with no event or construction attached.
- **MacArthur Blvd:** 77, up 7, also with none.
- **Other corridors:** I-880 South is at 93. I-580 East and West are at 82 and 80. Broadway, Telegraph and Grand are at 73, 79 and 65.

A rise of 11 points on International with no named cause usually means something unlogged: a lane closure, a signal fault, or spillover from a neighboring route. I can't tell which from the sheet, and I won't guess. Soria Dominguez said she is watching that corridor. So am I.

## What to watch

12th Street is the one station where more riders and worse service are arriving together. If the on-time number stays at 77% on a quieter week, the crowd isn't the cause. If it recovers, the crowd is. Next cycle's rows will show which, and they will also carry any delay from the Lake Merritt train.
