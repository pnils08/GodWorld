# Lake Merritt, in their own words

## Deacon Seymour — Manager, Oakland A's

> Look, I'm not going to sit here and pretend I've got the rotation mapped out like it's a done deal. Benji hasn't told us he's done, and until he does, I'm not closing that door for him. That's his call, not mine. We've got young arms coming up, and we've got guys who can step into the bullpen, but you don't replace a five-time Cy Young winner by just plugging in a name. You leave the light on. That's what we're doing. We're planning like he's not here, because we have to, but I've got a spot in my heart—and in that bullpen—that's his if he wants it. Vinnie's gone, Mark's gone, and that hurts. But Benji's still here in the city, still here in the community, and if he decides he's got one more run in him, I want him to know the door's open. That's not strategy. That's just respect.

## Mags Corliss — Editor-in-Chief

> The equinox came and went without ceremony. Day and night are even for once, and the lake held the last light a little longer than I expected. Robert said it felt like the city taking a breath. I said cities don't breathe evenly, and we left it there. Eleanor Rivera at the 12th Street booth says she can hear a bad cycle before anyone counts it, because the platform stops filling at an even pace. I've been thinking about that all evening. Fruitvale picked up riders this cycle, but fewer trains came when they were supposed to. More people are showing up for a service that's slipping. That's how a neighborhood gets taught what to expect. I did what I said I'd do and followed the receipts on the Pipeline. Luis got there first. Three neighborhoods are named, and twelve and a half million dollars is promised. After three cycles of nothing, I still can't tell you whether that's silence or just no one having looked hard enough. Tonight I think it's the first kind. Some silences are decisions. Downtown had a hard stretch too, the most of anywhere. I'd like to walk it this week, without a notebook. The jacket stayed on the hook tonight. Robert's pouring the last of the wine. — Mags

## Ximena Mehta — Bart Transit PR

> Yeah, I still see it that way. Six weeks of mild weather like this — that's not a punishment, that's a gift, and people treat it like one. They walk, they bike, they take the long way home around the lake. I don't look at a quiet 12th Street on a Saturday and think we're losing riders. I look at it and think everyone's outside enjoying the day before the fog rolls back in. That's just Oakland in the spring.

## Carmen Mesa — Starting Pitcher, Philadelphia Phillies

> I've seen a mix of emotions around the neighborhood since the trade. Some folks are still reeling from Mark Aitken leaving for Colorado, and now with Giancarlo Hernandez coming in, people are wondering what that means for Kevin Clark's chances. The A's infield is definitely a topic of conversation. I've heard some people saying it's a good move, while others are concerned about the impact on the team's local identity.
