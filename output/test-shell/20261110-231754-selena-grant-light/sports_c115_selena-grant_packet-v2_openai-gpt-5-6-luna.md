# The Oaks’ latest win made Harry Hayward’s minutes the next decision

**Selena Grant | Bay Tribune Sports**

Harry Hayward needed 22 minutes to produce 19 points, seven rebounds and two blocks in Philadelphia, shooting 9-for-15 as the Oaks beat the 76ers, 120-106. The career high matters less as a single scoring line than as a pressure point in an expansion rotation that is already producing wins: Oakland is 8-1, with four victories in a row.

The game offered the clearest version yet of Hayward’s case. The Oaks were down two after three quarters, then scored 40 in the fourth. Hayward was part of the group that turned a close game into a 14-point win, and his efficiency gave the second-round rookie a role beyond the usual developmental shorthand. He did not need a high-volume night to affect the game. The 19 points came on 15 shots, alongside seven rebounds and two blocks.

That performance also has to be read against the minutes that came before it. Hayward played 16 minutes against Orlando, scoring five points with two steals and a block in a 125-109 Oaks victory. He played 13 minutes against Oklahoma City, scoring four points and making two of three shots. He had eight points, five rebounds and three assists in 23 minutes against Toronto, eight points on three-of-four shooting in 13 minutes against Indiana, 10 points and 10 rebounds against New York, and 12 points against Detroit.

The pattern is not a straight line. It is a player moving through different workloads while the team keeps winning. That distinction matters. A 19-point game can create a demand for more opportunity; it does not, by itself, settle what that opportunity should look like.

The Oaks’ broader structure is making the question more difficult, not less. Adash Stanley had 23 points and seven assists in Philadelphia, while AJ Dybantsa scored 25 points. Wendell Carter Jr. collected 15 rebounds, including three offensive rebounds, and T. Jerome added nine assists. In the earlier Orlando win, Stanley had 24 points, eight assists and no turnovers; Dybantsa shot 11-for-13; Dillon Brooks scored 23 points; and Austin Reaves added 15 points and seven assists.

There is no obvious need to force Hayward into a larger role simply to rescue a struggling team. The Oaks are stable enough to make the decision on basketball grounds. That is the useful test for an expansion franchise: not whether a young player can seize one night, but whether the rotation can convert that night into a repeatable responsibility without disturbing what is already working.

Hayward’s recent stretch supplies evidence for more trust, but not a predetermined answer. The coaching staff still has to decide whether 22 minutes was an exception, a ceiling, or the beginning of a larger assignment. The next board read is simple: how will the Oaks respond to the best game of Hayward’s young season?
