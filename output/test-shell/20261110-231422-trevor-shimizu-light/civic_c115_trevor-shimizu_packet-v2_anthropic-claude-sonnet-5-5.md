# On an ordinary weekday, every station on the line filled at once

At 19th Street the Packet has no stalled train, no short crew and no closure. The station recorded 16,076 riders this cycle, up 5,324 from C114, on a mild 55°F day. That is the biggest gain of any station on the record. On-time performance was 81%, up one point. The platform did what it does on a game night, on a day that carried no game-night excuse.

Day type this cycle was an ordinary weekday, with no citywide cut. The Transit_Metrics rows treat that as the baseline. A station that moves more than the baseline allows has to name its own reason on its row, and 19th Street's row names several. There was a game day in Downtown. There were events in Downtown, Lake Merritt, Temescal and KONO. The Baylight District, which is construction-active, was on the row. So were crowded platforms.

## What the rows show

Coen Cartier, an AC Transit operator, drives a route that feeds that platform. He said he did not feel a jump on his own runs. "I did see more folks waiting at 19th than I'd expect on a regular afternoon," he said. "Most of them got on heading toward the Lake Merritt and downtown stops, not so much out to the edges."

That direction of travel matters. Lake Merritt was up 2,684 riders, to 6,780, with events in Lake Merritt and Chinatown on its row. 12th St Oakland City Center was up 4,515, to 14,526, and its on-time performance rose nine points to 86%.

The lift was not confined to downtown. Fruitvale carried 8,943, up 3,880. Its on-time performance rose 15 points to 87%, the largest gain in the set. West Oakland carried 9,519, up 3,491, and held 77% on time, up three. Coliseum carried 7,776, up 2,980, at 81%. Rockridge carried 5,684, up 2,397, and MacArthur carried 4,634, up 1,828.

Eight stations and ten corridors are on the record, and total station ridership was 73,938 against 46,839 in C114. That is a rise of about 58 percent.

The roads moved too. I-880 North and I-980 each read a traffic index of 100 this cycle, up 37 and 38. I-880 South read 98, up 44. I-580 East read 89, up 51. Even Grand Avenue, with no event on its row, was up 38. No chaos car touched BART or AC Transit this cycle, so none of this traces to a transit-side disruption.

## The comparison base

One more fact from the station history matters here. The 19th Street row for C114 carries a weekend tag in its factors. This cycle's row does not. The tag does not explain a 58 percent swing across eighteen rows. A weekend week against a weekday week is a different kind of comparison, though, and it makes the "+5,324" a harder number to read than it looks. This reporter has not seen a weekday-to-weekday series that would settle it.

## The people on the platform

Ximena Mehta, who handles public relations for BART's Oakland Division, said the explanation sits in the stacking. "It's not one big thing — it's a bunch of little things stacking up," she said. She listed the Oaks game downtown, the Baylight District vote going through, and events at Lake Merritt, Temescal and KONO. "The operators were saying 19th Street felt like a game day crowd even though it wasn't a game day for them."

Elena Soria Dominguez, planning lead at the Fruitvale Transit Hub Authority, declined to credit her own project. "I can't say the visioning is what's pulling those extra riders in," she said. The hub is still in its design phase, "no shovels in the ground." She pointed instead to events in East Oakland. She said the people who watch the station most closely are the construction workers on their breaks under the overpass, who "see it from the platform every day."

## The reading

The rows give each station a reason. The reasons are local, and they don't add up to a number. Fruitvale's events are in East Oakland. Rockridge's are on Piedmont Avenue and in Montclair. MacArthur's are in Temescal. Three of the five largest gains carry a game-day tag, and the other two carry only events and crowding. Taken one at a time, each row is explained. Taken together, they describe a system where every part ran heavier at once.

The on-time numbers are the part worth watching. They did not fall with the load. Fruitvale, 12th Street, Lake Merritt and Rockridge all improved. 19th Street and Coliseum held roughly flat, at 81%. Nothing on the record says that reflects slack in the system. It could just as easily mean one good week. Platform crowding was listed on every station row. No row says how many people a platform holds before dwell times stretch and a schedule starts to slip.

If every station moved together on an ordinary weekday, something changed in the city that no single station row can name. The rows to watch are the next two cycles. If the gains hold on a weekday, 19th Street has a new baseline. If they fall back, this was a week of stacked events, and it won't tell the agencies how much the line can carry.
