# The Oaks’ First Win Is Still a Question Asked Along the Fence

At the Baylight District fence line, the metal gives back a dry rattle when I press my notebook against it. Beyond the blocks, the Oaks have finished another preseason game without giving Oakland its first victory. The room around the team is quiet in the particular way a room gets when nobody wants to be the first person to decide whether disappointment is permanent.

The Oaks hosted game two of the preseason against the Warriors and lost. They lost again after strong efforts from Austin Reaves, leaving the team at 0-3 with a seven-game losing streak in the feed’s running context. The numbers belong elsewhere. Here, the question is simpler and harder: what does a new club have to show before people make room for it?

Reaves did not dodge the feeling.

“Honestly, I think fans are getting a little frustrated with the team's performance so far. I mean, we're still finding our footing, but it's hard not to worry when we're not seeing the wins. I trust that we'll get it together, we just need to keep working and supporting each other.”

That is the sentence hanging over the fence: not anger, not abandonment, but the thin patience of people waiting for a reason to return. The Oaks’ fans are low this cycle, and the room mood is frustrated. Those are feed words, but they have a physical shape here. They sit in the pause after the final whistle. They sit in the space where a first victory would have gone.

Austin Reaves scored 24 points in the loss. Dillon Brooks added 21, and Wendell Carter scored 12, according to the Oaks’ game notes. Those efforts are evidence that the team can give a neighborhood something to watch even when it cannot yet give the neighborhood a win. They are not, by themselves, a tradition.

Wilson Shepard, the head coach, put the larger question plainly: “To be honest, I think the team's performance can definitely affect the neighborhood's mood, but I haven't really seen that play out yet this season. I mean, we're still finding our rhythm, and I'm confident we'll tighten up and be ready for the real deal.”

The confidence is his. The waiting belongs to everyone outside the line.

I walk the edge of the Baylight blocks with the loss still fresh, past blank stretches of fence and the darkened glass that throws my own reflection back at me. A franchise can ask for support before it has earned belief. It can ask people to keep working, keep watching, keep showing up. But the first person to decide this is their team will need more than promise. They will need a night that sounds different when the crowd leaves.

For now, the fence keeps rattling in the same low air.
