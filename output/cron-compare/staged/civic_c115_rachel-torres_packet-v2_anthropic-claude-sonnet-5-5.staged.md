# Grand Lake Leads the Incident Table at 21% Clearance. Who Answers for the Other Four in Five?

Grand Lake sits at the top of the city's incident table this cycle with 8 incidents. That is one fewer than Cycle 114, and still the most of any of the 22 neighborhoods on the Crime_Metrics sheet. A person standing at any Grand Lake corner after a call would see nothing to separate the neighborhood from its neighbors. The table is where it stands out.

The row reads: response 10.6 minutes, clearance 21%, property index 38, violent index 27. Trend is marked "steady." The hotspot column is blank. By the sheet's own flags, nothing is spiking. The neighborhood is simply carrying the most, and closing about one case in five.

**Where the record was read**

This reporter pulled the Grand Lake row from Crime_Metrics and the Care_Justice_Census custody line for the neighborhood. A search of the paper's archive for prior coverage of Grand Lake clearance and response turned up nothing on the neighborhood. The hits were editions about OPD training cohorts and escalation protocol, a mayoral statement on Fruitvale, and one on OARI hiring. The Civic_Office_Ledger lists Rafael Montez as Police Chief, a 27-year OPD veteran. Kimberly Cho is the Deputy Chief of Operations. Neither is quoted here. Neither has spoken on this table in the material on hand, and nothing below should be read as their position.

**The custody trail**

The custody census shows Grand Lake with 8 charges, 2 arrests, and 4 people in custody at the close of the cycle. None are tracked residents. All four are "other residents," in the census's wording.

Read left to right, the line doesn't add up. Two arrests this cycle, but four people held at the close. The census does not say why. One reading is that custody counts people booked in earlier cycles who are still held. That is analysis, not a finding. The sheet carries no field that would confirm it.

The same shape shows up elsewhere. San Antonio has 6 charges, 2 arrests and 4 in custody. Downtown has 5 charges, 1 arrest and 4 in custody. KONO has 6 charges, 1 arrest and 4 in custody. Rockridge has 6 charges, 1 arrest and 4 in custody. Citywide, the census counts 24 arrests this cycle, with 67 in custody at the close and 29 "left."

A reader looking at these lines can't tell how many of the charges went anywhere. They also can't tell whether a person held at the close is the same person arrested during the cycle.

**Fast response, slow closure**

Grand Lake's 10.6-minute response is not the city's worst. That belongs to Rockridge at 11.8 minutes, where clearance is 19%. Rockridge logged 6 incidents, down 4 from Cycle 114. By these two numbers the cities' two weakest neighborhoods on response and clearance sit close together, and neither reads as a crisis on the sheet.

That is the classification gap. The table can tell a resident how long the wait was. It can't say what the wait bought. An officer arriving in 10.6 minutes and a case closing are different events. The sheet ties them together only through a single clearance percentage with no definition attached. It doesn't say whether "cleared" means an arrest, a referral, or an administrative close.

The department's roster lists 39 public-safety staff. The sheet doesn't say how many are assigned to Grand Lake or how that compares with the neighborhoods that logged 4 or 5 incidents. Adams Point logged the fewest, 4. Piedmont Ave and Downtown logged 5 each.

Two neighborhoods, West Oakland and the Baylight District, are marked "easing" this cycle. Nothing is marked rising.

**What a resident can do with this**

The practical advice is small and specific. A resident who reports an incident in Grand Lake should ask for the report number at the time of the call. They should also note the time they called and the time someone arrived. Those two times are the only part of the table a resident can check against their own experience. The clearance number can't be checked from the sidewalk.

**The open question**

The planning question for the department is blunt. If one in five Grand Lake incidents clears in the neighborhood that logged the most, what is being done for the other four? The sheets don't answer it. Neither the Chief's office nor the Deputy Chief of Operations has told the public what "cleared" means in this table, or where the 4 held at the close came from. Until someone does, the 21% describes how often the file closes. It doesn't say how often anyone is made safer.
