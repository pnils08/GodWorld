# Rockridge, in their own words

## Avery Santana — Mayor of Oakland

> The quiet is strange. Seven petitions, seven responses — that ledger is the one thing I can point to and say we didn't let it rot. West Oakland's renewal, Fruitvale's schematics, Baylight's evidence: all of it is out there now, and the building feels lighter for it. But I keep thinking about Laurel. One number up, another down, and the residents still want to see someone on their block. That's the gap between a report and a sidewalk, and I won't pretend it's closed. Grand Lake's vote and the tenant-defense decision are still pending — I want them recorded before the week ends, not carried into the next one. I'm not tired; I'm just aware of how much of this depends on follow-through I can't delegate away.

## Warren Ashford — City Council Member - D7

> The Grand Lake vote sits right where it should—tied to milestones, not promises. That's the only way I can justify spending a dime of commercial money. But the Rockridge sentiment number, that -0.06, it keeps nagging at me. The vitality is there, the foot traffic is there, yet the merchants are telling us something we aren't fully hearing. I can audit the dollars, but I can't audit a mood. And the health center's fifteen million in operational funds—that's a lot of care, but I need to know it's *delivered* care, not just budgeted care. I'll push for that clinical proof next cycle, before the corridor conversation gets any more expensive.

## Brenda Okoro — Deputy Mayor (Community Affairs)

> The closeout is out, the renewal is in council's hands, and I meant what I said — I won't call it before they do. That discipline cost me nothing but patience, and it's the only thing that keeps the fund's integrity intact when every block wants a piece of it. Grand Lake got its $2.5 million with the strings visible to everyone — I'd rather have the council on record than have them whisper later. The uneven mood map is what keeps me up, though; Ivy Hill and Brooklyn don't care about a charter renewal, they care if I show up. Three portfolios is triage, and triage means I'm not fooling myself into thinking a press line fixes a broken sidewalk. I'll take the quiet hour to breathe, then I'll be back on the street where the proof lives.

## Jem Steele — Athlete

> This week felt pretty uneventful for me. I didn't really do anything out of the ordinary.
