# Who Gets Through the Door: Artists, Curators and the Money Question

By Kai Marston | Bay Tribune Arts

Access to Oakland's arts scene doesn't come with a sign on the door. It comes through referrals, open calls, a friend who says *you should show them this*, and sometimes just walking in with a portfolio under your arm. The question I keep coming back to is who ends up on the other side of that door, and who never finds it.

I started with the people who open it for a living.

Mei Chen runs a gallery in Chinatown and curates work from the outside in. The way new artists reach a show, Chen says, is a mix of routes. "For me, artists get in front of the gallery through a mix of referrals, open calls, and sometimes just walking in with their work. I've been doing this for a while, so word gets around. To be honest, it's not always easy for newer artists to get noticed, but I try to keep an eye out for fresh talent."

Chen's answer to what would make the door feel more open is about capacity, not charity. "What would make the door feel more open? More opportunities for them to show their work, I think. Maybe more collaborations with other local art spaces or programs that can help bring new artists to the forefront."

Chen pointed to Chinatown's cultural events as a sign of momentum. "I've seen some great initiatives around Chinatown lately, like the cultural center's events, which are always well-attended. It's a good sign that there's energy here."

Energy is the easy part to see. Pathways are harder.

Marin Tao, a musician who lives in Chinatown, came at the question from the other side of the street. Tao read the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline and kept thinking about the spots on it. "When I saw the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline, I wondered who was going to get those spots because it made me think about the opportunities available to young people in our community."

Tao has noticed new faces around the neighborhood, some probably drawn by events like the Winter Lights festival. But Tao isn't sure those faces are getting what the programs promise. "I'm not sure if they're getting the same opportunities as others. It makes me question whether the programs are reaching the kids who need them most."

That doubt was shared by Lena Cross, an actor who lives on Piedmont Ave. Cross read the same flyer and had the same question. "I read the flyer for the Oakland Youth Apprenticeship Pipeline and wondered who was actually going to get those spots. To be honest, I don't know anyone who's applied, so I can't say much about the process."

Three working artists and arts-adjacent people, three different corners of the city, and the same blank spot: nobody they know has walked through the program's door. That's an anecdote, not a census, and I'm not the civic desk. I can't tell you the placement count or how many applicants made it through intake. What I can tell you is what the people closest to the arts economy are saying they can see from where they stand.

The arts side has its own named institutions. The Keane Gala Arts Fund, based in the Baylight District and founded by Vinnie Keane, is one of the arts programs on the city's community record. The West Oakland Mural Collective and the Oakland Sound Archive, in Uptown, are two more. Being on the record isn't the same as being easy to reach. An artist who hasn't heard of a program can't apply to it, and an artist who hasn't been to a gallery can't be noticed by the curator behind it.

Chen's answer is the one I'd put on the wall of every arts office in town: more places to show, and more collaborations that carry new artists forward. Tao's question is the one the city has to answer: whether the kids who need a spot know there's a spot at all.

I'll keep going to the openings, the readings and the Chinatown events. I'll keep asking who's in the room. And if you're a young artist in Chinatown, West Oakland, Fruitvale or East Oakland who has walked into a gallery with your work, or who has read a flyer and wondered whether it was meant for you, tell me. The door is only open if people know it's there.
