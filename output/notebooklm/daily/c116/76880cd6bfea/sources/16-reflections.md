# Uptown, in their own words

## Elliott Crane — City Council District 6

> That’s a fair record, and I’ll own every line of it. The through-line is simple: I’m not going to buy a promise with money we haven’t verified yet. My constituents on Piedmont Avenue don’t need slogans—they need the sidewalk power-washed and the permit counter moving. And the health spike affecting 93 residents? That’s not a talking point, that’s a number I can’t shake. I want the public health folks to give us a straight answer and a clear timeline, not a new layer of city process that takes six months to tell us what we already know. So if you’re asking whether I’m open to a charter extension or a new investment package—yes, but only if the closeout audits are clean and the milestones are enforceable. I’ll cross lines when the numbers hold up. That’s not stubbornness, that’s just how I sleep at night.

## Terrence Mobley — City Council District 9

> That's the record, and I'll stand by every line of it. The cleanup isn't a press release—it's a scope, a resident list, and a Clerk entry that holds up to scrutiny. If someone wants to verify, they can pull the file. The four complaints are what they are, and the next date belongs to whoever's responsible, not to a promise about six million dollars. Uptown's rhythm is steady. Laurel's still finding its footing. I'm not going to spin either one—overstating helps nobody. And the metrics tell a mixed story: sentiment ticking up, crime down, but decay momentum doesn't just vanish because we had a good month. Retail vitality at 5.7 is a number I can't wave away, and missing the C116 filing deadline is a miss, full stop. Now, the Planning Director's directive—I'll answer it with the record, not with rhetoric. That's the honest part. The quiet after a vote is where I feel the weight of it, not during the debate. Laurel's stability, Uptown's pace—I keep telling people I won't blink, but privately I have to ask myself if steady is enough when the clock's still running. That's where I land this week.
