# Coles' Line Is the Only One of the Four That the Rates Fully Back

Travis Coles finished 2041 at 22-4 with a 2.72 ERA, 254 strikeouts and 6.2 WAR, for $780K, per the club's feed line. The question this cycle is whether the A's should extend him, John Ellis, Ernesto Quintero and Vladimir Gonzalez before the price moves. The four lines are not equally solid. Win-loss records and counting stats tend to wobble. Strikeout-to-walk rates, large samples and multi-year consistency tend to hold. Sorted that way, the four separate into one clear case, one good case with a caveat, one case that leans on a record, and one with two full seasons that disagree.

**Coles.** His TrueSource card shows 215 innings in 2041, with 39 walks and 25 home runs allowed. The worked rates are 10.6 strikeouts per nine, 1.6 walks per nine and 6.51 strikeouts per walk. The two earlier seasons on the card, both with Washington, read differently. In 2040 he threw 219.1 innings at a 2.05 ERA, but with 8.5 strikeouts per nine, 2.5 walks per nine and 3.39 strikeouts per walk. In 2039 he went 9-15 with a 3.96 ERA, 9.0 per nine and 3.88 strikeouts per walk. His best ERA came in 2040, and the process behind it was weaker than this year's. The 2041 ERA is a good number, but the walk and strikeout columns are the better evidence. A pitcher who gets his strikeouts up while cutting walks over a 215-inning sample is showing a change in how he works. That is harder to dismiss as luck than a 22-4 record. The card has three seasons, so the improvement is one year old. The line is large-sample and clean, but it is a single season of this version of him.

**Ellis.** John Ellis is 20-3 with a 3.42 ERA at $780K, per the feed. His card has 189.2 innings in 2041 and 153 strikeouts against 54 walks, which works out to 7.3 per nine, 2.6 walks per nine and 2.83 strikeouts per walk. The year before, he threw 185.0 innings at 3.89 with 6.0 per nine and 1.97 strikeouts per walk. The direction is encouraging. But 20-3 is the loudest number on his card and the least reliable one, because wins depend on run support and bullpen work. A 3.42 ERA with 2.83 strikeouts per walk describes a solid mid-rotation starter who improved. That is worth a team-friendly deal. It is not an ace's deal, and the record should not be allowed to price it as one.

**Quintero.** The feed has Ernesto Quintero at .282 with 46 home runs, 92 RBI and 5.8 WAR for $980K. His card lists 145 games and 554 at-bats, which is enough volume to take the power seriously: one home run every 12.0 at-bats, with a .587 slugging mark. The caveat is on the same card. He has 27 walks, a .318 on-base percentage, and a strikeout every 6.2 at-bats. The batting average is respectable, but the on-base number shows a hitter who gets his value from damage rather than from getting on base. He has played one season on the dossier, so the power has no earlier line to confirm it. A long extension would be built on 554 at-bats. The case is good, but it is not yet a case for a long, expensive commitment.

**Gonzalez.** Vladimir Gonzalez's feed line is the most expensive of the four at $2.7M, and it reads .306, 28 home runs and 4.7 WAR. The roster has that as his 2041 season: 527 at-bats, 161 hits, 76 RBI and 17 steals, one home run every 18.8 at-bats. His TrueSource card stops at 2039, when he hit .271 with 14 home runs in 506 at-bats, one every 36.1. Two full seasons, two very different power rates. The 2041 line is the bigger sample and the better one, but it is one year old, and his case rests on whether the next season repeats it.

**What the board says.** The four players combine for four bargain salaries and four useful lines, but the record does not call for four equal decisions. Coles is the one whose rates carry his headline, and he is the one the A's would regret losing. If the front office lets him reach the open market after a 6.2 WAR season at $780K, the question is what it values, the production or the payroll. Mike Paulson sits as general manager on the feed row, and the feed shows no extension talks or contract terms for any of the four. That absence leaves the real question open.

**What would settle it.** The case needs three things the current file lacks. The first is another season of Coles at a walk rate near 1.6 per nine, which would show that 2041 was a new baseline. The second is a second full year of Quintero at one home run every 12.0 at-bats or close, with an on-base percentage that moves. The third is another full season of Gonzalez near 2041's power rate, so it reads as his level rather than a jump over 2039. Until those arrive, the lines support extending Coles first and treating the other three as decisions still waiting on more evidence.
