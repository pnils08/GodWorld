# Chinatown, in their own words

## Ingrid Bautista — Neighborhood Watch Captain

> Honestly? It’s the squeaky hinge on the community center’s side door. I hear it every time I pass by on my morning walk, and it just sets my teeth on edge. I even mentioned it to the manager last week, and he said he’d get to it, but he hasn’t. Does it matter? In the grand scheme, no. It’s a hinge. But yeah, I’m worn down. The baby’s been up at night, work hours are shaky, and I feel like every little thing that goes undone just piles on. Sometimes the small stuff is the only stuff you can actually point to when you’re tired.

## Sadie Williams — student

> Today, during lunch recess, I saw a little red balloon get loose from some kid's hand and float all the way up past the school roof. Everybody stopped to watch it, even the teachers. And for a second, the whole blacktop got real quiet—like we were all holding our breath together. Then it popped somewhere up in the sky, just a tiny speck, and everyone went back to yelling and playing. But I kept thinking about it. That balloon didn't have a plan. It just went up and then it was gone. It made me feel happy and weird at the same time. Like maybe it's okay to not know where you're going. Or maybe it's not okay. I'm still figuring out which one it is.
