# The Oaks’ Second Board Read Is Harry Hayward

Harry Hayward’s 19 points in 22 minutes, on 9-of-15 shooting, are the clearest new number in an Oaks season that has moved to 8-1 and a four-game winning streak. The second-round rookie also had seven rebounds and two blocks as Oakland beat Philadelphia 120-106, coming from two down after three quarters by scoring 40 in the fourth.

That line matters less as a career-high anecdote than as pressure applied to the rotation. Hayward had played 16 minutes against Orlando, scoring five points with two steals and a block, in the Oaks’ 125-109 win. Before that, his listed game lines were 4 points in 13 minutes against Oklahoma City, 8 in 23 against Toronto, 8 in 13 against Indiana, 10 points and 10 rebounds against New York, and 12 against Detroit. The pattern is not a settled role. It is a player producing in changing portions of one.

The Philadelphia game supplied the strongest argument yet for more responsibility without answering how the minutes should be redistributed. Hayward’s 22 minutes were enough for the career high, but the feed does not identify a new starting assignment, a permanent rotation change or a front-office decision. That distinction is important for an expansion franchise. A good night can establish usefulness; it cannot, by itself, establish hierarchy.

The rest of the board explains why the question is difficult. AJ Dybantsa scored 25 points, made 10 of 16 shots and went 3-for-3 from three-point range. Adash Stanley added 23 points and seven assists while shooting 8-for-13, including 5-for-7 from three. Wendell Carter Jr. supplied 12 points, 15 rebounds and three offensive rebounds. T. Jerome had nine assists, and J. Champagnie made three of five threes. Austin Reaves scored 12 points, though he was 4-for-14.

This is not a single-player rescue of a thin roster. It is a winning group with enough production to make the development question real. The Oaks buried Orlando early for their third straight win before surviving a tighter Philadelphia game with a fourth-quarter surge. The record is now 8-1, and the franchise stability line is growing. Those facts make Hayward’s progress consequential, but they do not prove that every promising performance should trigger a larger role immediately.

The next board read is therefore narrower than “can Hayward play?” The record has answered that. He can score efficiently, rebound, defend at the rim and contribute in limited minutes. The unanswered question is how the Oaks will value that evidence when the rotation is set: whether 22 minutes was an isolated opportunity or the first durable adjustment in the expansion build. The numbers have made the decision harder. They have not made it for the front office.
