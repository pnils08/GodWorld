# The Room Is Starting to Believe in the Oaks

**Talia Finch | Bay Tribune Sports**

KONO is loud before I get through the door, the kind of loud that makes the glasses tremble when somebody drops a hand on the counter. The game is over, but nobody has released it yet. A replay catches Dillon Brooks rising for another three, and the room leans with him as if the shot is still in the air.

The Oaks opened their NBA Cup run at home by beating the Oklahoma City Thunder, 101-95, in front of a sold-out crowd. Brooks scored 22 and hit six threes, enough small flashes of daylight to keep a close game from turning. The Oaks led after every quarter. They are 6-1, winners of two straight, and the people in this room have begun to speak about them without lowering their voices.

Trenton Nawan is working the counter, moving a glass out of the way with one hand while keeping the replay in sight. He has been checking the scores twice a day, he says, and the wins kept making him happy.

“I'm still feeling pretty good about the Oaks. That 4-0 start has really got me hopeful. I checked the scores twice a day all week and was happy every time they won. Maybe they're finally getting their act together.”

That last sentence lands differently now. It is not a declaration. It is a door left open.

The room mood is locked-in, the feed says, and I can see what that means here: people are not asking who is supposed to take the shot. They are watching to see who does. Brooks supplied the threes against Oklahoma City. Adash Stanley added 21 points and six assists, making all six of his free throws while also turning the ball over six times. Wendell Carter Jr. scored 19 and pulled down 13 rebounds. The margins stayed narrow enough that every cheer sounded like a warning.

Stanley has his own description for what is happening around the team.

“The mood among fans is electric. I've felt the excitement building around the Oaks, it's like the whole city is waking up with the team. There's a buzz in the air, and I think people are really looking forward to seeing what they can do this season.”

At the far end of the room, somebody says the next win will settle it. Somebody else says there is no settling anything yet. That is still the Oaks' actual neighborhood: excited, confident, and ready to be convinced again.

Trevor Henderson, whose voice carries the player's view of the same night, puts it plainly: “I've seen the energy around the Oaks lately, and it's electric. The crowd was locked-in during the NBA Cup opener against OKC, and you could feel the excitement. As a player, it's motivating to see the fans so engaged. We're on a two-game win streak, and the vibe is great.”

The screen goes dark between replays. For a moment, the room keeps making its own light—glasses on wood, chairs scraping, a low argument about whether six threes can become a habit. Then the replay starts again, and everyone turns toward it.
