**Rockridge's Retail Pulse Quickens**  

The line at HotSlice Pizza stretched halfway to College Avenue on Thursday evening—a sight Jacob King hadn't seen in years. "I've been working the line at a restaurant near Cozy Pot for years," said King, wiping flour from his arms, "and lately there's just more energy—more people out at night, more folks sticking around after dinner to chat. Feels like the whole block's waking up again."  

Signs of life cluster along the Rockridge storefronts this cycle. At Cozy Pot, dinner service runs later than last season's ledger predicted, while Festive Fireside's weekend brunch crowd spills onto sidewalks usually quiet after morning church services. Howard Yell, grinding beans at a café two blocks east, tracks the shift in foot traffic: "I’ve noticed more people out and about, especially around places like Cozy Pot and HotSlice Pizza—it feels like the neighborhood’s got a bit more energy lately."  

The question lingers like the scent of baking dough—is this sustained momentum or seasonal flicker? Lease renewals along this strip still tilt month-to-month, a hedge against the last downturn's scars. For now, the ovens stay hot.
