Marc Allie adjusted the thermostat in his Laurel kitchen for the third time that morning, his hands lingering on the dial as the fog pressed against the windows. Outside, the neighborhood felt cloaked, the usual morning bustle reduced to muted footsteps and the occasional soft hum of a car engine. Inside, the table was cluttered with textbooks and half-finished assignments—homework the kids couldn’t seem to focus on.  

"The fog in Oakland? It’s like a quiet blanket that slows everything down," Allie said, pouring himself a cup of coffee. "You feel it in your bones, especially early mornings when I’m heading out for my shift. It’s not just the chill—it’s the way it makes the neighborhood feel smaller, more intimate. Like the world’s holding its breath."  

Across town, Tyrie Groin pulled her coat tighter as she walked to her retail job. The fog lingered, draping familiar streets in a muted gray. "It’s introspective, you know?" she said. "Makes you appreciate the warmth of family and the comfort of routine even more."
