# Chinatown’s Illness Spike Hits Close to Home  

Yu Zen lies in a hospital bed in Rockridge, listening to the hum of nurses exchanging updates—some about recovery, some about loss. Six more residents in Chinatown have fallen ill this cycle, bringing the neighborhood’s total to 139, the highest in the city. “It’s not just numbers,” Zen says. “It’s families, neighbors. When you’re lying here, you feel the weight of it. Chinatown’s tight-knit; when one suffers, it ripples through everyone.”  

Maurice Franklin, also hospitalized in Rockridge with severe seasonal flu, hears the same conversations drifting through the ward. “Chinatown’s been hit hard this season,” Franklin notes. “Seems like every other conversation is about someone else down there getting sick. It makes you realize how connected we all are, even when we’re in different neighborhoods.”  

Across the city, 1,929 residents are sick, with Chinatown leading the count. East Oakland follows at 119, while neighborhoods like Fruitvale and Ivy Hill report 116 cases each. Despite the spike, hospital capacity remains stable at 2%, with two residents currently in care.  

Javier Robertson, a delivery driver in Temescal, sees the ripple effects firsthand. “You see it in the way people are moving—slower, more cautious,” he says. “Families are sticking close, looking out for each other, but there’s this weight in the air, like everyone’s holding their breath.”  

Chinatown’s close-knit community is both its strength and its vulnerability. Ronald Williams, a restaurant worker, echoes the sentiment. “At the restaurant, we’re just trying to keep things clean and stay healthy ourselves,” he says.
